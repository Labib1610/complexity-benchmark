<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory's automated rostering system operates in three stages. In the first stage, it gathers all pairwise scheduling constraints into a centralized database. In the second stage—which is the component you must implement—the system must process a roster that is already known to be hopeless. The factory has only two shifts. Your job is not to assign shifts; rather, it is to generate a certificate of impossibility to pass to the third stage. The third stage will then consume this certificate to automatically trigger an expansion request for a third shift.

Each conflict record between two workers implies a hierarchical dependency. By factory policy, this dependency always flows from the worker with the numerically smaller ID to the worker with the larger ID, regardless of the order the two workers are listed in the database.

To prove impossibility to the third stage, your certificate must consist of three workers $a$, $b$, and $c$, such that $a < b < c$. Furthermore, you must verify that there is a directed dependency from $a$ to $b$, a directed dependency from $a$ to $c$, and a directed dependency from $b$ to $c$.

It is guaranteed that the database contains at least one such valid group of three workers. You must output one such group. If there are multiple valid groups, output the lexicographically smallest such triple (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The integer $n$ represents the factory headcount, which is at least 3 and at most a maximum facility capacity of 2000. The integer $m$ represents the total number of conflict records, which is at least 3 and at most the system limit of 200,000 (or the maximum possible number of unique pairs $n(n-1)/2$, whichever is smaller).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — representing a conflict record between worker $u$ and worker $v$. 
It is guaranteed that at least one valid triple exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that the directed dependencies $a \to b$, $a \to c$, and $b \to c$ all exist. Output the lexicographically smallest such triple.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Changed the conflict relation from implicitly mutual pairs to directed hierarchical dependencies that always flow from the smaller ID to the larger ID. The certificate explicitly requires directed arcs $a \to b$, $a \to c$, and $b \to c$. This forces models to handle the directed logic correctly; models relying on surface pattern matching for undirected triangles may incorrectly add reverse arcs or misinterpret the orientation, leading to invalid certificates or missed solutions. The numeric bounds have been restored to the Input section as exact prose to satisfy the gate while adhering to the indirect bounds strategy.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because the dependencies are defined to always flow from the smaller to the larger ID, any undirected triangle with sorted vertices $a < b < c$ will naturally form the exact directed configuration $a \to b$, $a \to c$, and $b \to c$ required, making the set of valid certificates identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The core logic remains equivalent to finding a triangle (K3) in an undirected graph, keeping it in the exact same complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>

</RISK>
