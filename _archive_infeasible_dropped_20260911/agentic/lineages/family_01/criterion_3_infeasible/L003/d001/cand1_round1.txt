# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The facility's two storage compartments cannot safely house the current inventory. Rather than attempting to partition the substances, your task is to produce a certificate of this impossibility.

A certificate is a trio of distinct substances that cannot be distributed safely. It is established by finding two substances that share a hazard record, provided that the collection of substances hazardous to the first and the collection of substances hazardous to the second have an intersection containing a third substance. This mutual hazard ensures that no two-compartment assignment can avoid a conflict. The hazard logs are guaranteed to contain at least one such trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2\cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of substances and the number of hazard records.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — indicating a mutual hazard between substance $u$ and substance $v$.
At least one valid certificate trio is guaranteed to exist.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$. These must represent a valid certificate trio: the input must explicitly include a hazard record between $a$ and $b$, a hazard record between $a$ and $c$, and a hazard record between $b$ and $c$. Output the lexicographically smallest such sequence (first minimizing $a$, with ties broken by smallest $b$, then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
