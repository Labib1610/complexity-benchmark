# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The safety board has determined that dividing the laboratory's inventory into two isolated storage zones is insufficient to prevent accidents. Your task is to provide the required documentation to certify this failure.

To prove that two zones cannot safely house the inventory, you must identify a combination of three distinct chemicals where every chemical in this combination has a documented incompatibility with both of the other chemicals in the same combination. The safety board guarantees that at least one such combination exists within the records.

If multiple such combinations exist, you must report the one that is prioritized by the filing system. The filing system requires the three chemical identifiers to be listed in ascending order, and it prioritizes the combination with the lowest first identifier. If there is a tie, it prioritizes the lowest second identifier, and finally the lowest third identifier.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the inventory size and the log count respectively. The inventory size $n$ is constrained by the facility's baseline requirement of 3 chemicals and its maximum structural capacity of 2000 chemicals. The log count $m$ is bounded below by 3 and above by the auditing limit of 200000 entries (or the theoretical maximum of all possible distinct pairs of chemicals in the inventory, whichever is more restrictive).

Following this are $m$ incompatibility logs, one per line. Each log consists of two integers $u$ and $v$ representing the identifiers of the incompatible chemicals.

## Output
Print three space-separated integers on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three chemicals in the chosen combination, ordered from smallest to largest. The chosen combination must be the one prioritized by the filing system.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
