Cubic Frequency Sum
</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
Replaces the parity condition with a Diophantine constraint on the degree sequence of the chosen logs. By requiring the sum of the cubes of the frequencies to be 24, we mathematically force the degree sequence to be exactly (2, 2, 2), which uniquely identifies a triangle in a simple graph. This defeats models that rely on surface-level keyword matching for parity or even-degree, forcing them to either solve the Diophantine equation to discover the triangle structure or fall back to an O(m^3) brute force that will TLE.
</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals are in conflict and cannot share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific failing configuration. They require a proof of impossibility consisting of exactly three distinct incompatibility logs chosen from the provided records. 

For any chosen set of three logs, we can count how many times each personnel member appears in them. Let $f(x)$ be the number of chosen logs that individual $x$ is involved in. This selection of three logs is considered a valid proof if the sum of the cubes of these frequencies over all individuals equals 24. In other words, a valid proof satisfies $\sum (f(x))^3 = 24$.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such selection of logs. Your task is to identify the unique individuals involved in this proof.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of incompatibility logs, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating an incompatibility log involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one valid selection of three logs exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the individuals involved in the valid selection of logs.

If there are multiple valid groups of individuals that can be formed this way, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I replaced the parity condition with a Diophantine constraint on the frequencies of individuals in the chosen logs. Because three logs contain exactly 6 personnel endpoints in total, the only non-negative integer solution to $\sum f(x)^3 = 24$ subject to $\sum f(x) = 6$ is three 2s and zero 1s. This uniquely identifies a triangle, preserving the problem exactly, but forces models to genuinely reason through the mathematics rather than pattern-matching "even number of times" to a parity check or graph cycle.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition $\sum f(x)^3 = 24$ for 3 edges in a simple graph is mathematically satisfied if and only if the 3 edges form a triangle (frequencies 2, 2, 2), exactly matching the seed's parity condition.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying task is still finding a 3-clique (triangle) in a graph, which remains solvable in $O(nm/64)$ using bitsets or adjacency intersections.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
