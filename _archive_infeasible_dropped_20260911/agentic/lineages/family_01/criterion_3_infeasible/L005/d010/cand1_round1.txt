<METHOD_NAME>Incidence Matrix Dot Product</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the parity condition on edges with a condition on the pairwise dot products of binary sequences constructed from the logs. Since each log sets exactly one unique position to 1 for its two individuals, the dot product of any two individuals' sequences is 1 if they share a log and 0 otherwise. A sum of three pairwise dot products equaling 3 uniquely identifies a triangle. Models failing to recognize this equivalence will attempt to construct and multiply $O(m)$-length sequences, leading to TLE or memory exhaustion.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals are in conflict and cannot share a working period. 

To formally declare the two-period assignment impossible, management uses a sequence-based validation system to pinpoint a specific failing configuration. 
First, each of the $n$ personnel is assigned a binary sequence of length $m$, initially containing all zeros. 
The $m$ incompatibility logs are then processed in the order they are provided. For the $k$-th log ($1 \le k \le m$), which indicates a conflict between individual $u$ and individual $v$, the $k$-th element of both individual $u$'s sequence and individual $v$'s sequence is set to 1. All other individuals' sequences retain a 0 at position $k$.

A configuration of exactly three distinct individuals $a$, $b$, and $c$ is considered a valid proof of impossibility if the sum of the dot products for the three pairs of these individuals (i.e., the pair $a$ and $b$, the pair $b$ and $c$, and the pair $a$ and $c$) is exactly 3. The dot product of two binary sequences is calculated as the number of positions where both sequences have a 1.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such valid configuration of three individuals. Your task is to identify the unique individuals involved in this proof.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of incompatibility logs, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating an incompatibility log involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one valid selection of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the individuals involved in the valid configuration.

If there are multiple valid groups of individuals that can be formed this way, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
Replaced the parity/XOR condition on logs with a pairwise dot product condition on binary sequences. This forces the model to deduce that the dot product of two individuals' sequences is exactly 1 if they share a log and 0 otherwise, meaning a sum of 3 for three pairs corresponds exactly to a triangle. Models that fail to make this structural deduction will simulate the $O(m)$-length sequences and TLE.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because all logs are distinct, the maximum dot product between any two sequences is 1, so the sum of three pairwise dot products is exactly 3 if and only if all three pairs share a log, which is mathematically identical to the original triangle condition.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
