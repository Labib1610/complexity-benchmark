<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the parity condition with a requirement that every individual mentioned in the 3 logs appears in at least two of them. By the handshaking lemma on a simple graph, 3 edges whose vertices all have degree >= 2 must form a triangle. This avoids the "even number of times" phrasing that triggers Eulerian/cycle heuristics.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals are in conflict and cannot share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific failing configuration. They require a proof of impossibility consisting of exactly three distinct incompatibility logs chosen from the provided records. 

This selection of three logs is considered a valid proof if every individual who is involved in at least one of the three chosen logs is actually involved in at least two of them.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such selection of logs. Your task is to identify the distinct individuals involved in this proof.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of incompatibility logs, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating an incompatibility log involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one valid selection of three logs exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the individuals involved in the valid selection of logs.

If there are multiple valid groups of individuals that can be formed this way, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The parity condition ("even number of times") was replaced with a lower bound condition ("at least two of them"). Because the graph is simple and exactly three edges are chosen, requiring every involved vertex to have degree >= 2 mathematically forces the three edges to form a triangle. This avoids the parity wording that acts as a strong semantic trigger for models to search for cycles.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that every vertex in the 3 chosen edges has degree >= 2 implies exactly 3 vertices with degree 2, which perfectly corresponds to a triangle, preserving the exact same valid sets as the original parity condition.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains finding a triangle (a 3-clique) in a simple graph, which is in the same certificate-style infeasibility class solvable in O(n*m/64) using bitsets.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
