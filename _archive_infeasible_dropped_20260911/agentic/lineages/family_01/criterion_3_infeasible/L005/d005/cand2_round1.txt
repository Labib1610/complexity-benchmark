# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of preliminary friction reports, each indicating that two specific individuals should ideally not share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a minimal failing subset. Specifically, they require a group of exactly three individuals that meets a specific threshold regarding their friction footprint.

To evaluate any group of three individuals, management calculates a *footprint weight* across all $m$ reports. Each report is assigned a weight based on its overlap with the group:
- 3, if both individuals involved in the report are members of the group.
- 1, if exactly one individual involved in the report is a member of the group.
- 0, if neither individual involved in the report is a member of the group.

A group of exactly three individuals justifies the roster failure if the total sum of these weights across all $m$ reports is exactly 6 greater than the number of reports that have a weight strictly greater than 0.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group. Your task is to identify one such group to justify the roster failure.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the number of preliminary reports, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating a preliminary report involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All reports are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the minimal failing subset.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The previous inclusion-exclusion formula (`union_size = degree_sum - 3`) explicitly handed the models the sum of degrees, making it mathematically trivial to deduce that exactly 3 internal edges were required. I replaced this with a procedural weight function where reports score 3, 1, or 0. The condition `sum_weights = count_nonzero + 6` simplifies algebraically to `3*E2 + E1 = E2 + E1 + 6`, which means `2*E2 = 6`, or `E2 = 3`. This forces the model to either perform the algebraic reduction to discover the triangle structure, or implement the literal O(N^3 * M) simulation which will Time Limit Exceed.
</RATIONALE>
