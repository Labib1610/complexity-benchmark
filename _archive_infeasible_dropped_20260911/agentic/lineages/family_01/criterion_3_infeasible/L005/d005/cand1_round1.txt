# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a stack of preliminary friction reports. Each report is a document listing exactly two specific individuals who ideally should not share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a minimal failing subset. Specifically, they require a group of exactly three individuals that meets a specific density condition regarding these documents.

During the audit of any proposed group of three individuals, the committee performs the following procedure:
1. They retrieve every single friction report document that lists at least one of the three individuals.
2. They ask each of the three individuals to review the retrieved stack and place their signature on every document that contains their own name.

A group of exactly three individuals $\{a, b, c\}$ justifies the roster failure if the total number of signatures applied by the three individuals is exactly 3 greater than the total number of documents retrieved in step 1.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group to justify the roster failure.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of preliminary report documents, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating a preliminary report document listing individual $u$ and individual $v$. All reports are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed on more than one document).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the minimal failing subset.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
