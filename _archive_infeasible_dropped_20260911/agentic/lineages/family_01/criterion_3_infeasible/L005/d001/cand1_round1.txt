# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. Personnel frequently submit candidate incompatibility reports against one another. 

According to HR policy, a candidate report between two individuals is only considered a *confirmed incompatibility* if it passes an additional validity rule: the two individuals must be part of a 'deadlock'. A deadlock is defined as a group of exactly three individuals where all three possible candidate reports among them exist in the records. Reports that do not pass this validity rule are considered misunderstandings and are ignored.

It is a known fact that if there is a group of three individuals where every individual has a confirmed incompatibility with both of the other two, it becomes logically impossible to place them into just two periods. The management guarantees that the provided records of candidate reports contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers n and m (3 ≤ n ≤ 2000, 3 ≤ m ≤ min(n·(n−1)/2, 2·10⁵)), representing the number of personnel and the number of candidate incompatibility reports, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to n.

Each of the next m lines contains two integers u and v — indicating that a candidate report exists between individual u and individual v.

It is guaranteed that at least one group of three mutually incompatible individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers a, b, and c on a single line, satisfying a < b < c, such that a has a confirmed incompatibility with b, a has a confirmed incompatibility with c, and b has a confirmed incompatibility with c.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize a; in case of a tie, minimize b; and finally minimize c).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I replaced the direct-edge statement with a scenario where input pairs are "candidate reports", which only count as "confirmed incompatibilities" if they pass an additional validity rule (being part of a 3-person "deadlock"). This forces the model to reason about the relationship between the validity rule and the final structure. A model that genuinely reasons will realize that any triangle of candidate reports automatically satisfies the validity rule for all its edges, making the problem logically identical to finding a triangle. A model relying on surface pattern matching may get confused by the circular-sounding definition or attempt to implement a redundant and potentially buggy multi-pass filtering algorithm.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Any group of three individuals with confirmed incompatibilities between all pairs must have candidate reports between all pairs, which by definition makes them a deadlock, meaning the valid outputs are exactly the triangles in the input graph.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle (a 3-clique) in an undirected graph, which remains in the same complexity class (polynomial time certificate-style infeasibility).
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "part of a deadlock" as requiring the corroborating third person to be outside the target group, though the text clearly defines deadlock as just "a group of exactly three individuals".
</RISK>
