# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of preliminary friction reports, each indicating that two specific individuals cannot share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a minimal failing subset. Specifically, they require a group of exactly three individuals that fundamentally cannot be distributed across two working periods. 

For a group of three individuals to justify the failure, their preliminary reports must actively block any two-period assignment. This happens when selecting any one of the three individuals for the first period and any second individual from the three for the second period reveals a preliminary report between them, meaning their separation was inherently forced. Concurrently, the remaining third individual is blocked from both periods because they share a preliminary report with the individual in the first period and a preliminary report with the individual in the second period.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group to justify the roster failure.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the headcount and the number of preliminary reports, respectively. The headcount $n$ requires at least 3 personnel, up to a maximum facility capacity of 2000. The number of reports $m$ is at least 3, capped at a maximum of $2 \cdot 10^5$, and will never exceed the total possible unique pairs $n(n-1)/2$. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating a preliminary report involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All reports are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the minimal failing subset.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
