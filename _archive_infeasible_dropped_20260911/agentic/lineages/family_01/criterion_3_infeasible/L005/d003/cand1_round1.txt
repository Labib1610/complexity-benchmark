# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of preliminary friction reports, each indicating that two specific individuals should ideally not share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a minimal failing subset. Specifically, they require a group of exactly three individuals that fundamentally cannot be distributed across the two working periods without conflict. A group of three individuals justifies the roster failure if, for every possible way to choose one of them to work in a different period from the other two, the two individuals who end up sharing a period always have a preliminary friction report between them.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group to justify the roster failure.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the number of preliminary reports, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating a preliminary report involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All reports are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the minimal failing subset.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The models were using the phrase "every possible way of assigning them to the two periods forces at least two individuals sharing the same period to have a preliminary report" as a direct trigger to recognize the 2-coloring conflict of a triangle (K3). I replaced this with an equivalent structural description: "for every possible way to choose one of them to work in a different period from the other two, the two individuals who end up sharing a period always have a preliminary friction report between them." This forces the models to logically deduce that all three pairs must have reports, rather than relying on the familiar bipartite-coloring phrasing.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition requires that for all 3 ways to partition the 3 individuals into sets of 1 and 2, the set of 2 has a report, meaning all 3 pairs must have a report, which is mathematically identical to finding a triangle.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
