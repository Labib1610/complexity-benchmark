# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working shifts. They have collected a log of preliminary friction reports, each indicating that two specific individuals should ideally not share a shift. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-shift assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a minimal failing subset. Specifically, they require a group of exactly three individuals that fundamentally cannot be distributed across two shifts. A group of three individuals meets this condition if and only if any way of dividing them between the two shifts forces at least one shift to contain a pair of individuals who have a preliminary report between them.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group to justify the roster failure.

## Input
The first line contains two integers, representing the personnel headcount and the total number of friction reports, respectively. The facility requires a minimum headcount of three personnel to operate and has a maximum capacity of two thousand personnel. The system logs a minimum of three friction reports; the database storage is capped at two hundred thousand entries, and the number of reports will also never exceed the total number of distinct pairs that can be formed from the given headcount. Personnel are assigned ID numbers sequentially starting from one up to the exact headcount.

Each of the subsequent lines (one for each report) contains two distinct personnel IDs, indicating a friction report involving those two individuals. All reports are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the minimal failing subset.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
