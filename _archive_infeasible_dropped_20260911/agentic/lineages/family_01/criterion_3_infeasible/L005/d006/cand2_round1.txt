<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods (Shift X and Shift Y). They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals should ideally not share a working period. 

Management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific failing group. They require a group of exactly three individuals $\{a, b, c\}$ that meets a specific structural condition regarding the logs.

To evaluate a proposed group of three individuals, management considers all possible ways to assign each of the three individuals to either Shift X or Shift Y. There are $2^3 = 8$ possible shift assignments for the group. 

An assignment is considered *conflict-free* if there is no incompatibility log between any two individuals in the group who are assigned to the same shift. (Logs involving individuals outside the group of three are ignored for this evaluation).

A group of exactly three individuals $\{a, b, c\}$ is certified as a failing group if exactly 0 of these 8 possible assignments are conflict-free.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such failing group. Your task is to identify one.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of incompatibility logs, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating an incompatibility log involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the group.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
