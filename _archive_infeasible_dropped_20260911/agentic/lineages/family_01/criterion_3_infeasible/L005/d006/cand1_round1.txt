<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all employees to two distinct shifts. They have collected a list of conflict reports, each indicating that two specific employees should ideally not share a shift. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-shift assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific "critical trio". They require a group of exactly three employees $\{a, b, c\}$ that meets a specific parity-based checksum condition regarding the reports.

For any group of three employees $\{a, b, c\}$, we evaluate its interference with the reports as follows:
For each of the $m$ reports, count how many of its two involved employees belong to the group $\{a, b, c\}$. Let this count be $k$ (which will be 0, 1, or 2). We then compute the bitwise XOR of $k$ and 1 (i.e., `k ^ 1`).
The *interference checksum* of the group is the sum of these bitwise XOR values over all $m$ reports.

A group of exactly three employees $\{a, b, c\}$ is a critical trio if its interference checksum is exactly equal to:
$m - (C_a + C_b + C_c) + 12$
where $m$ is the total number of conflict reports, and $C_x$ represents the total number of reports involving employee $x$.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such critical trio. Your task is to identify one.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of employees and the total number of conflict reports, respectively. Employees are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating a conflict report involving employee $u$ and employee $v$. All reports are distinct (no pair of employees is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one critical trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three employees in the critical trio.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Replaced the linear subset sum condition with a mixed bitwise-arithmetic equation ($\sum (k \oplus 1) = m - \sum C_x + 12$). This forces the model to evaluate the XOR operator on the finite domain $\{0, 1, 2\}$, express the sum in terms of intersection counts ($E_0 + 3E_2$), and algebraically solve for $E_2=3$. Models relying on pattern matching will fail to simplify this and will generate literal $O(n^3 m)$ simulations that Time Out.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The checksum equation mathematically simplifies to $E_2 = 3$, meaning all three possible edges between the three employees must exist (a triangle), which is exactly the same condition as the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains equivalent to finding a triangle in an undirected graph, which is solvable in $O(nm/64)$ time using bitset intersection.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret 'its two involved employees' as referring to the total pool rather than the specific report's endpoints, though the phrasing 'each of the m reports... its two involved employees' strongly binds them.
</RISK>
