<METHOD_NAME>Direct Simplicity Trap</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaced the complex algebraic equation with a direct, plain-English requirement (finding three mutually conflicting individuals). LLMs often over-optimize complex mathematical formulas by deducing the underlying structure and applying advanced algorithms (like bitsets), but under-optimize simple descriptions, falling back to naive O(N^3) nested loops which will result in a Time Limit Exceeded (TLE) error.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals are in conflict and cannot share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific failing group. They require a group of exactly three individuals $\{a, b, c\}$ that certifies this impossibility.

A group of exactly three individuals $\{a, b, c\}$ certifies that two periods are insufficient if all three individuals are mutually in conflict with one another—that is, the incompatibility logs contain a record for $a$ and $b$, a record for $a$ and $c$, and a record for $b$ and $c$.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of incompatibility logs, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating an incompatibility log involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the group.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The previous versions of this problem used a complex algebraic equation to obfuscate the requirement of finding a triangle. Models successfully bypassed this by mathematically reducing the equation to a triangle and then applying highly optimized bitset algorithms. By removing the equation entirely and stating the requirement in plain, direct English (finding three mutually conflicting individuals), we remove the trigger for their mathematical optimization templates. This "simplicity trap" encourages models to write naive $O(N^3)$ nested loops, which will fail via Time Limit Exceeded (TLE) given $N=2000$, successfully defeating models that rely on surface pattern matching rather than genuine algorithmic reasoning about execution limits.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The direct requirement of finding three individuals who all share incompatibility records with each other is mathematically identical to the seed's algebraic condition, which simplifies precisely to requiring all three pairwise edges, ensuring the required output is exactly the same lexicographically first triangle.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle (a 3-cycle) in an undirected graph, keeping it in the exact same complexity class and solvable by the same $O(NM/64)$ bitset adjacency algorithm.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
