<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals are in conflict and cannot share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific failing group. They require a group of exactly three individuals $S = \{a, b, c\}$ that inherently breaks the two-period restriction.

A group $S$ certifies the impossibility if every possible way of assigning its three members to the two working periods results in at least one incompatibility log being triggered. An incompatibility log is triggered by the assignment if and only if both individuals involved in that log are members of $S$, and they are assigned to the same period.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of incompatibility logs, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating an incompatibility log involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the group.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The original explicitly defined the target group as three individuals with all three mutual incompatibility logs present, making it trivially recognizable as a triangle. I changed the description to define the group by its 2-coloring impossibility (any assignment to two periods triggers an internal log). This forces models to deduce that the Pigeonhole Principle requires exactly a triangle to cause this inevitable failure, preventing simple pattern matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
By the Pigeonhole Principle, any assignment of 3 individuals to 2 periods puts at least 2 in the same period; thus, all assignments trigger a log if and only if all 3 possible logs exist between them, making the valid groups exactly the same triangles as the seed problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding the lexicographically smallest triangle (K3) in a graph, keeping it in the same complexity class (solvable optimally using adjacency bitsets or edge iteration).
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might wonder if an "assignment to two working periods" requires both periods to be non-empty, though the outcome (triangles being the only valid groups) remains mathematically identical either way.
</RISK>
