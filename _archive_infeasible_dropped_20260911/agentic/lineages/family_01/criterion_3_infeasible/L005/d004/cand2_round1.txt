<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of preliminary friction reports, each indicating that two specific individuals should ideally not share a working period. 

For any individual $x$, let $F(x)$ be the set of individuals with whom $x$ has a preliminary friction report. 

Management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a minimal failing subset. This subset must be a group of exactly three distinct individuals; let us call this group $G$. The group $G$ justifies the roster failure if, for every individual $x$ in $G$, the number of individuals who are present in $G$ but not present in $F(x)$ is exactly one.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group to justify the roster failure.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the number of preliminary reports, respectively. Personnel are numbered from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating a preliminary report involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All reports are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three individuals exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the minimal failing subset.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I replaced the bipartite-coloring impossibility description (which models easily pattern-matched to "minimal non-bipartite graph / triangle") with an equivalent set-theoretic condition. The condition states that for every element $x$ in the 3-person group $G$, the set difference $G \setminus F(x)$ must have a size of exactly 1. Models that rely on surface pattern matching will implement this set difference literally and TLE in $O(n^3)$, whereas models that genuinely reason about the structure must algebraically deduce that since $x \notin F(x)$, the other two elements of $G$ must be in $F(x)$, which is identically the triangle requirement.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because an individual cannot have a friction report with themselves, $x$ is always the single element in $G \setminus F(x)$, forcing the other two members of $G$ to be in $F(x)$; this requires all three pairs in $G$ to have a report, which exactly preserves the original triangle condition.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
