<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals are in conflict and cannot share a working period. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific failing group. They require a group of exactly three distinct individuals $\{a, b, c\}$ that certifies this impossibility.

A candidate subset of exactly three distinct individuals $T = \{a, b, c\}$ is considered a certifying group if it satisfies the following structural condition: there must exist a combination of exactly three distinct logs from the provided records such that the union of the individuals involved in these three logs is exactly the set $T$.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such group of three individuals. Your task is to identify one such group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The facility issues sequential ID badges starting from 1 up to the total headcount $n$. A minimum of three staff members are required to form a group, and the building's maximum occupancy restricts the headcount to no more than two thousand. The log registry requires at least three entries to trigger an audit, and its storage is physically capped at two hundred thousand records. Naturally, the number of logs $m$ never exceeds the total possible unique pairs of personnel.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$. These represent the ID badges of the two individuals involved in the log, meaning they are distinct valid badge numbers from the active headcount.

It is guaranteed that at least one certifying group exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three individuals in the group.

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I replaced the subset-density condition (finding 3 edges within a 3-vertex subgraph) with a mathematically equivalent set-union condition: finding exactly 3 distinct edges whose combined endpoints form exactly the target set of size 3. This forces models to deduce that three pairs of size 2 can only have a union of size 3 if they form a triangle. Pattern-matching models will likely attempt an $O(m^3)$ simulation by iterating over edge triplets and fail due to Time Limit Exceeded.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Three distinct pairs have a union of exactly three elements if and only if they represent all three possible pairs among those elements, making the condition identical to finding a triangle in the incompatibility graph.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
