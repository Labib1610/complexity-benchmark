<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Parity Checksum Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management is auditing personnel incompatibility logs using parity checksums. There are $n$ personnel, numbered 1 to $n$.

Each recorded log between individual $u$ and individual $v$ is encoded as a parity state: a binary string of length $n$ (indexed 1 to $n$) where the bits at positions $u$ and $v$ are `1`, and all other bits are `0`. 

A "parity failure" is formally defined as a situation where there exists a set of exactly three distinct logs whose bitwise XOR sum evaluates to a string of all zeros. 

The management requires you to pinpoint the specific personnel involved in such a failure. You must find a set of three logs that XOR to zero, and determine the positions of the `1`s in their bitwise OR sum. 

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such set of three logs. Your task is to identify the involved personnel.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of personnel and the total number of incompatibility logs, respectively.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating a log between individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one parity failure exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the positions of the `1`s in the bitwise OR sum of the three logs.

If there are multiple such sets of three logs, output the one whose resulting positions $(a, b, c)$ come first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I reframed the problem from finding a triangle in a graph to finding three 2-hot binary strings that XOR to zero. This forces the model to mathematically deduce that the only way three 2-hot strings can XOR to zero is if they share `1`s in exactly three positions, which is structurally identical to a triangle. Models that rely on surface pattern matching will attempt a generic 3-XOR algorithm and fail the time limit, while reasoning models will recognize the graph equivalence and use the intended $O(nm/64)$ adjacency intersection algorithm.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The bitwise XOR sum of three distinct 2-hot strings is 0 if and only if they represent the three edges of a triangle, and their bitwise OR sum yields exactly the three vertices of that triangle, preserving the output identically for all inputs.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly the detection of a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, maintaining the INFEASIBLE certificate complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "binary string of length n" as literal strings that must be constructed in memory rather than an abstract mathematical property, leading to memory/time limit exceeded if they don't realize the sparse nature of the problem, but this abstraction penalty is part of the intended difficulty.
</RISK>
