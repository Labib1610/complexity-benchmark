<METHOD_NAME>Semantic Property Substitution (Local 2-Coloring Failure)</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the explicit structural definition of a triangle ("three edges over three vertices") with its semantic equivalent in the problem's domain: a subset of 3 vertices that cannot be 2-colored. Models must logically deduce that a 3-vertex set is un-2-colorable if and only if all 3 edges are present, forcing them to reason about the condition rather than pattern-matching the definition of a triangle.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Justifying the Roster Failure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The management wants to assign all personnel to two distinct working periods. They have collected a list of incompatibility logs, each indicating that two specific individuals are in conflict and cannot share a working period. 

However, management will only formally declare the two-period assignment impossible if they can pinpoint a specific failing configuration. They require a proof of impossibility consisting of a subset of exactly three personnel. 

This subset of three personnel is considered a valid proof if it is intrinsically unassignable: that is, no matter how you assign each of these three specific individuals to one of the two working periods, at least one of the periods will end up containing a pair of individuals who share an incompatibility log.

The management guarantees that the provided records contain at least one such subset of three personnel. Your task is to identify the three individuals involved in this proof.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The headcount $n$ represents the number of personnel (numbered 1 to $n$). The headcount is at least three, but never exceeds the building's maximum fire-code capacity of 2000. The value $m$ represents the total number of incompatibility logs. It is at least three, and capped at either the total number of possible pairs among the personnel, or the database limit of 200000, whichever is smaller.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating an incompatibility log involving individual $u$ and individual $v$. All logs are distinct (no pair of individuals is listed more than once).

It is guaranteed that at least one valid subset of three personnel exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, representing the three unique individuals in the valid subset.

If there are multiple valid groups of three individuals that can be formed this way, output the one that comes first lexicographically (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and finally minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit structural hint ("three distinct logs ... exactly three unique individuals") has been removed, as it allowed models to immediately pattern-match the requirement to a triangle. Instead, the condition is defined purely by its semantic effect in the problem's domain: a subset of 3 personnel that cannot be validly distributed into 2 periods. Models must now logically deduce that the only way a 3-element set fails all 2-partitions is if every pair within it is restricted (i.e., it forms a triangle). The bounds have also been rewritten according to strategy S07 to require careful reading.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
A set of 3 items can be partitioned into 2 sets in ways that only create groups of size 2 and 1 (or 3 and 0); a partition avoids internal conflicts if and only if at least one pair among the 3 items is not logged, meaning the subset is "intrinsically unassignable" if and only if all 3 pairs are logged, which is exactly the original triangle condition.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle (a 3-clique) in an undirected graph, keeping it in the same complexity class with the same optimal bitset-adjacency algorithm.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "assign each of these three specific individuals to one of the two working periods" as implying that a period could be left empty, but since an empty period just means the other period has all 3 individuals (guaranteeing a conflict if all pairs are logged), the mathematical equivalence holds regardless of whether empty periods are considered valid assignments.
</RISK>
