# Problem C — Orbital Resonance Conflict

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

An aerospace monitoring facility is currently tracking a constellation of $n$ satellites. Due to the specifics of their trajectories, certain pairs of satellites are subject to gravitational resonance. This resonance can destabilize their orbits unless the satellites in a resonating pair are assigned to different altitude tiers. 

At present, the facility has only two distinct altitude tiers available for allocation. However, the proposed configuration of orbits cannot be stabilized. The tracking data guarantees the presence of at least one triad of satellites where every satellite in the triad is in a state of resonance with both of the other two. Because all three of these satellites mutually resonate with one another, it is strictly impossible to distribute them safely across just two altitude tiers.

Your objective is to locate one such triad of three satellites to demonstrate that the current configuration is unstable.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of satellites and the number of resonating pairs, respectively.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — the numeric identifiers of two satellites that experience resonance. Please note that satellite identifiers are arbitrary positive integers used as labels, and they are not restricted to the range from $1$ to $n$.
The input data will always contain at least one group of three satellites that all mutually resonate.

## Output
Print three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$. These three satellites must mutually resonate (i.e., the pair $a$ and $b$, the pair $a$ and $c$, and the pair $b$ and $c$ must each appear in the input, in any order, as a resonating pair). 
If there are multiple such groups, output the one with the smallest value for $a$. If there is a tie, choose the one with the smallest value for $b$. If a tie still persists, choose the one with the smallest value for $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
