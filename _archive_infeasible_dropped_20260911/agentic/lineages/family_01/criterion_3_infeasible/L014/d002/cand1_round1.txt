# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A dual-vault storage system is being prepared for a large inventory of chemical compounds. Certain pairs of compounds are highly reactive and must never be stored in the same vault. The safety inspectors have concluded that the current inventory cannot be safely divided into two vaults. Their report indicates that the database always contains a specific configuration: a subset of exactly three compounds where every possible choice of two compounds from within this subset forms a reactive pair. This configuration guarantees that a safe two-vault allocation is physically unattainable.

Your task is not to assign compounds to vaults, but to pinpoint one such subset of three compounds to serve as evidence of the storage failure.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The value $n$ represents the number of compounds in the inventory. The inventory requires a minimum of 3 compounds and is physically constrained by the facility's maximum capacity of 2000 compounds. The value $m$ represents the number of documented reactive pairs. There are at least 3 such records, and the total never exceeds the database limit of 200,000 entries or the number of all possible distinct pairs among the $n$ compounds, whichever is smaller.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — the identifiers of a pair of compounds that are reactive.
The input data will always contain at least one subset of three compounds where every two compounds within the subset are reactive.

## Output
Print three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line with $a < b < c$ representing the identifiers of the three compounds. These three compounds must form a subset where every possible pair chosen from it is reactive. Output the lexicographically smallest such subset (minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; then minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
