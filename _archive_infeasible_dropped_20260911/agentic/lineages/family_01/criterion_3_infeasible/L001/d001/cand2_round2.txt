<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Core Committee

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory is analyzing its workforce to form a core committee. You are given a log of past collaborations.
Let $C(u, v)$ be a symmetric condition that evaluates to true if and only if the pair of workers $u$ and $v$ (in either order) appears as an entry in the collaboration log.
Your task is to identify a set of exactly three distinct workers $a$, $b$, and $c$ such that the conditions $C(a, b)$, $C(b, c)$, and $C(a, c)$ all evaluate to true.
It is guaranteed that the provided log contains at least one such group of three workers. Output one such group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The factory headcount $n$ requires a minimum staffing level of 3, up to a maximum facility capacity of 2000. The number of log entries $m$ is at least 3, and is bounded to not exceed the maximum possible number of distinct pairs $n \cdot (n-1)/2$, nor the database limit of 2·10⁵.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of workers who have collaborated.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid set of three workers exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that $C(a, b)$, $C(a, c)$, and $C(b, c)$ are all true. Output the lexicographically smallest such triple (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Changed the direct graph-like "conflict pairs" representation to an algebraic symmetric boolean condition $C(u, v)$ based on a collaboration log. This forces models to evaluate the condition logically rather than immediately pattern-matching to graph adjacency and neighborhood intersection, increasing the likelihood they fall back to a naive $O(n^3)$ loop which will exceed the time limit. Numeric bounds were updated to use the exact digits required by the gate while still functioning as capacity limits.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition $C(a, b) \land C(b, c) \land C(a, c)$ is mathematically identical to finding a triangle in the undirected input graph, meaning the exact same outputs remain valid and lexicographically ordered for all inputs.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The core problem is still finding a 3-clique (triangle) to act as a certificate of non-bipartiteness, which operates in the exact same time complexity and remains in the same class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "appears as an entry in the collaboration log" if they assume the log requires a specific ordering, but the text explicitly mitigates this by stating $C(u, v)$ is a "symmetric condition" and applies to the pair "in either order".
</RISK>
