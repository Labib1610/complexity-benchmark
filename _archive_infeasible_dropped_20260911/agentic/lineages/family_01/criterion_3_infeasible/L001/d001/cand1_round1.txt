# Problem C — Worker Roster

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory management is analyzing its worker roster of $n$ employees. You are provided with a log containing $m$ distinct unordered pairs of employees that have been flagged for review.

Your task is to identify a subset of exactly three employees such that the provided log contains exactly three pairs where both employees in the pair belong to this subset.

It is guaranteed that the log contains at least one such subset of three employees. Output one such subset.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2\cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a flagged pair of employees. The pairs in the log are distinct, and $u \neq v$.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid subset of three employees exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the identified subset. Output the lexicographically smallest such subset (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I changed the explicit request for "three workers who all mutually conflict" (which directly describes a clique/triangle) to a set-based counting condition: finding a subset of three employees that completely contains exactly three logged pairs. This forces the model to deduce that since a 3-element set has exactly $\binom{3}{2}=3$ possible pairs, this condition is mathematically identical to requiring every pair to be present. Without making this structural deduction, models will likely attempt to iterate over all $\binom{n}{3}$ subsets to count their pairs, which will fail the time limit.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because any subset of 3 employees can contain at most 3 internal pairs, requiring it to contain exactly 3 pairs is logically equivalent to requiring all of its internal pairs to be present, preserving the exact same valid answers as the original problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a $K_3$ (triangle) in an undirected graph, maintaining the exact same algorithmic structure and computational complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
