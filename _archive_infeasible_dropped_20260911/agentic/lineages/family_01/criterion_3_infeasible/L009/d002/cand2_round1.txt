# Problem C — Triple-Core Resonance

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The experimental reactor contains $n$ magnetic coils, numbered $1$ to $n$. For safety reasons, the facility requires at least three coils to be present, and its cooling system can support a maximum of two thousand coils. The engineers have tested various pairs of coils and recorded $m$ combinations that exhibit direct magnetic resonance. The testing protocol dictates a minimum of three recorded combinations, and the log system's storage capacity caps the number of records at two hundred thousand (or the theoretical maximum of all possible unique pairs of installed coils, whichever is less).

To achieve sustained fusion, the reactor requires a "triple-core" configuration. This configuration is formed by isolating exactly three distinct coils. For the configuration to function, energy must flow seamlessly among all its components. Specifically, every coil in the chosen group of three must have a confirmed, direct resonance with both of the other two coils in that same group.

Your task is to identify such a configuration.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. Their values conform to the facility limits described above.
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines provides two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that coil $u$ and coil $v$ have a confirmed resonance. The records are symmetric (order does not matter), and no combination is listed more than once.

## Output
Print three integers $x$, $y$, and $z$ on a single line, separated by spaces, such that $x < y < z$. These represent the identifiers of the three coils in your chosen configuration. If multiple valid groups of three exist, you must output the one that minimizes the value of $x$. If there are still multiple options, minimize $y$, and finally minimize $z$ to break any remaining ties. If the provided records cannot form any triple-core configuration, output `-1 -1 -1`.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
