# Problem C — Tri-Symbol Seal Decipherment

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An archaeological team is studying a forgotten language consisting of $n$ distinct symbols, identified by integers from $1$ to $n$. Through extensive analysis of recovered tablets, they have cataloged $m$ pairs of symbols that are "contextually compatible"—meaning they have been observed adjacent to one another in valid grammatical constructs.

The team has uncovered a prominent artifact known as a tri-symbol seal. A valid seal always consists of exactly three distinct symbols. According to the grammatical rules of this language, a group of three symbols can only form a seal if they are mutually compatible; that is, any two symbols chosen from the three must form a contextually compatible pair.

You are given the total number of symbols and the complete catalog of compatible pairs. It is a known historical fact that at least one valid tri-symbol seal can be formed using the cataloged symbols. Your task is to identify the three symbols that make up the seal.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing that symbol $u$ and symbol $v$ are contextually compatible. Compatibility is a symmetric property (order does not matter), and no pair is listed more than once in the input.
It is guaranteed that the provided data contains at least one valid tri-symbol seal.

## Output
Print three integers $x$, $y$, and $z$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $x < y < z$. These integers represent the identifiers of the three symbols forming the seal.
If there is more than one valid seal, you must output the one that minimizes the value of $x$. If multiple options share the same $x$, minimize $y$, and finally minimize $z$ to resolve any remaining ties.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
