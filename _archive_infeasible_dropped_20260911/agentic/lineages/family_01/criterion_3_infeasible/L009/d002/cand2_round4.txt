# Problem C — Triple-Core Resonance

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An experimental reactor contains a set of magnetic coils, sequentially numbered starting from $1$. The engineers have tested various pairs of coils and recorded specific combinations that exhibit direct magnetic resonance.

To achieve sustained fusion, the reactor requires a "triple-core" configuration. This configuration is formed by isolating exactly three distinct coils. For the configuration to function, energy must flow seamlessly among all its components. Specifically, every coil in the chosen group of three must have a confirmed, direct resonance with both of the other two coils in that same group.

Historical test data confirms that the reactor has successfully achieved fusion, meaning the current records are certain to contain at least one such configuration. Your task is to identify it.

## Input
The first line contains two integers: the total number of coils and the total number of recorded combinations.
The reactor's safety protocols require a minimum of three coils to be installed, while the physical chassis restricts the installation to a maximum of two thousand coils.
The monitoring system is configured to log at least three resonant pairs. The system's memory buffer imposes a strict upper limit of two hundred thousand on the recorded pairs, though the number of records will naturally never exceed the total possible number of distinct pairs among the installed coils.

Each of the subsequent lines (one for each recorded combination) provides two integers, indicating that the two corresponding coils have a confirmed resonance. The records are symmetric (order does not matter), and no combination is listed more than once. The logs always represent a state where at least one valid triple-core configuration exists.

## Output
Print three integers on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the identifiers of the three coils in your chosen configuration, sorted in strictly increasing order. If multiple valid groups of three exist, you must output the one whose first identifier is as small as possible. If there are still multiple options, minimize the second identifier, and finally minimize the third identifier to break any remaining ties.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
