# Problem C — Triple-Core Resonance

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An experimental fusion facility houses $n$ magnetic coils, assigned unique integer IDs from 1 up to $n$. Engineers have logged $m$ successful two-coil resonance tests.

A sustained reaction requires isolating a primary operating group of exactly three distinct coils. For the reaction to be stable, mutual resonance is required across the entire group. This means that if you look at the three coils in the isolated group, every possible pairing of those three coils must have been logged as a successful resonance test.

It is guaranteed that the logged tests contain at least one such stable trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The facility requires a minimum of 3 coils and can support an architectural maximum of 2000 coils. The testing logs contain at least 3 records, and the total number of records is limited by either the theoretical maximum of $n \cdot (n-1)/2$ possible pairs, or the database capacity of $2 \cdot 10^5$, whichever is smaller.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — representing a pair of coils that exhibit direct magnetic resonance. The pairs are unordered, and no pair is listed more than once.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$, representing the IDs of the three coils in the stable group. All three internal pairs ($a$ and $b$, $a$ and $c$, $b$ and $c$) must be present in the logs. Output the lexicographically smallest such trio (the one with the smallest $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest $b$; and if still tied, the smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

</RATIONALE>
