# Problem C — Triple-Core Resonance

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An experimental reactor contains a number of magnetic coils, sequentially numbered starting from 1. The engineers have tested various pairs of coils and recorded those that exhibit direct magnetic resonance. 

To achieve sustained fusion, the reactor requires a "triple-core" configuration. This configuration is formed by isolating a set of exactly three distinct coils. When activated, energy must be able to flow directly between any two coils within this isolated set. Energy can only flow directly between two coils if they have been recorded as a resonant pair. Consequently, every possible pairing that can be formed from the chosen three coils must be present in the resonance records.

It is guaranteed that the recorded data contains at least one such configuration. Your task is to identify it.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the total number of coils and the total number of recorded resonance pairs, respectively. A reactor requires at least three coils to operate, and physical space limits it to a maximum of two thousand coils. The log of resonant pairings will contain at least three entries, up to a strict limit of two hundred thousand entries (or the maximum number of unique pairs possible for the installed coils, whichever is smaller).

Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers, identifying a pair of coils that exhibit direct magnetic resonance. The pairs are unordered, and no pair is listed more than once.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid triple-core configuration exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three coils in the configuration. Output the lexicographically smallest valid triple (the one with the smallest $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest $b$; and if still tied, the smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
