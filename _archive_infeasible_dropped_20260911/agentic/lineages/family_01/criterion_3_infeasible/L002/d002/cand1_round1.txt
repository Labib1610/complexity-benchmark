<STATEMENT>
# Conflict Resolution Certification

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility has a roster of $n$ technicians. Over time, there have been $m$ formal conflict reports logged. Each report involves exactly two technicians who refuse to operate on the same shift. The management originally intended to partition the staff into two distinct shifts, but the logged conflicts make any such two-shift partition impossible.

To formally document this failure, you must identify a minimal irreconcilable group. Specifically, you must find a set of exactly three conflict reports from the log that collectively involve exactly three distinct technicians. 

Because the number of possible combinations is large, you must systematically search the conflict logs to find such a trio of reports. Once found, you must extract the IDs of the three technicians involved. If there are multiple such groups of three technicians, you must report the one that is ordered first according to technician ID. That is, compare the lowest ID of the groups, then the middle ID, and finally the highest ID, selecting the group with the smallest values.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2\cdot 10^5)$), indicating the total number of technicians and the total number of conflict reports.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), representing a pair of technicians who have filed a mutual conflict report. The order of these reports in the log is historical, and each specific pair of technicians is recorded at most once.
It is guaranteed that at least one such set of three conflict reports exists.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the IDs of the three technicians involved in the identified reports. If multiple such groups exist, output the one that is lexicographically smallest.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Changed the definition of the target from "three technicians where every pair has a conflict" to "three conflict reports that collectively involve exactly three distinct technicians." This removes the explicit description of a clique. It forces the model to mathematically deduce that three edges spanning exactly three vertices in a simple graph must form a triangle, preventing surface-level pattern matching to triangle-finding algorithms.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
In a simple graph with no parallel edges or self-loops, the only way for three distinct edges to span exactly three distinct vertices is if those edges form a triangle, so the valid sets of three technicians are identical to the original problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying task remains finding a triangle in an undirected graph, which has the same time complexity and requires the exact same bitset or vertex-iteration algorithms to pass within the time limit.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might attempt a literal $O(m^3)$ search over all combinations of three reports instead of deducing the graph property, which would lead to a Time Limit Exceeded, but this is the intended penalty for failing the reasoning step.
</RISK>
