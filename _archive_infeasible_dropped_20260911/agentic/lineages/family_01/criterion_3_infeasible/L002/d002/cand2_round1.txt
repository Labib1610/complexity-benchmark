# Conflict Resolution Certification

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility has a roster of $n$ technicians. Over time, there have been $m$ formal conflict reports logged. Each report lists exactly two technicians who refuse to operate on the same shift. The management originally intended to partition the staff into two distinct shifts, but the logged conflicts make any such two-shift partition impossible.

To formally document this failure, you must identify a minimal irreconcilable group. Specifically, you must find three technicians $a$, $b$, and $c$ (with IDs satisfying $a < b < c$) such that the conflict logs contain:
- A report involving technician $a$ and technician $b$.
- A report involving technician $a$ and technician $c$.
- A report involving technician $b$ and technician $c$.

Because the number of possible three-person groups is large, you must systematically search the conflict logs to find a trio that meets these criteria. If there are multiple such groups of three technicians, you must report the one that is ordered first according to technician ID. That is, compare the lowest ID of the groups, then the middle ID, and finally the highest ID, selecting the group with the smallest values.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2\cdot 10^5)$), indicating the total number of technicians and the total number of conflict reports.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), representing two technicians who have a conflict report. The order of these reports in the log is historical, and any specific pair of technicians is recorded at most once.
It is guaranteed that at least one group of exactly three technicians meeting the criteria exists.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the IDs of the three technicians. The conflict logs must contain a report for $a$ and $b$, a report for $a$ and $c$, and a report for $b$ and $c$. If multiple such trios exist, output the one that is lexicographically smallest.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I replaced the phrase "every possible pair within this group" with an explicit, itemized list of the three required reports ($(a,b)$, $(a,c)$, $(b,c)$). This removes the semantic trigger for "clique" or "triangle" pattern-matching, forcing the model to synthesize the graph structure from three discrete conditions and design an efficient search without relying on pre-memorized graph templates.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The criteria for the three technicians are logically identical to finding a mutually conflicting triple, so the exact same lexicographically smallest group is always chosen.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
