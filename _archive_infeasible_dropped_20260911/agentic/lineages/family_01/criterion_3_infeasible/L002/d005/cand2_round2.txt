# Conflict Resolution Certification

## Time and Memory Limits
Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description
A manufacturing facility has a roster of $n$ technicians, identified by integers from 1 to $n$. Over time, $m$ conflicting pairs have been recorded. Each record is a pair of technicians $u$ and $v$ who cannot be assigned to the same shift. The conflict relationship is symmetric; a record for $u$ and $v$ applies equally in both directions.

Management intended to divide the staff into two shifts, but the recorded conflicts make this impossible. To formally document this scheduling failure, you must locate a specific obstruction: a completely fractured triad. 

A completely fractured triad is a group of exactly three distinct technicians such that, according to the records, no two technicians within this group can be assigned to the same shift. 

If there are multiple completely fractured triads, you must report the one that is lexicographically smallest. To compare two triads, write their IDs in increasing numerical order ($a < b < c$); the lexicographically smaller triad is the one with the smaller first ID, or the smaller second ID if the first IDs are equal, or the smaller third ID if the first two IDs are equal.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2\cdot 10^5)$), indicating the total number of technicians and the number of conflicting pairs.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), representing a conflict between technician $u$ and technician $v$. It is guaranteed that at least one completely fractured triad exists.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the IDs of the three technicians in the chosen triad. If multiple such triads exist, output the one that is lexicographically smallest.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

Instead, the triad is defined naturally through the scenario's logic (no two members can share a shift). This strictly maintains the undirected nature of the problem, blocking the ambiguity that led to the previous rejection, while still requiring models to deduce that this implies the presence of all three pairwise edges.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that no two technicians in the group can share a shift means every pair within the three technicians must be a recorded conflict, which is mathematically identical to finding a 3-clique in an undirected graph, preserving all valid outputs.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
