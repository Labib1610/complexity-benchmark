# Problem C — Shift Assignment Pipeline: Stage 2

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

We are building the second phase of a scheduling utility for a factory that runs strictly on a day shift and a night shift. The utility processes workforce data in a three-step sequence.

**Step 1: Data Consolidation.** The initial step gathers all personnel profiles and grievance logs, yielding a deduplicated registry of pairs who cannot be placed on the same shift.

**Step 2: Viability Check (Your Task).** Before attempting to construct a full schedule, the software must quickly detect fatal blockages. Specifically, if there exists a group of three distinct workers where every member has a registered grievance against the other two, it is structurally impossible to divide them across just two shifts. 

**Step 3: Escalation.** If the viability check detects such a blockage, the software aborts the scheduling and generates an incident report for the human resources department.

You must implement the viability check. The system guarantees the provided data contains at least one group of three mutually incompatible workers. To maintain predictable incident reports, you must output exactly one such group. If multiple exist, apply a strict tie-breaker: select the group whose lowest worker ID is as small as possible; if still tied, minimize the middle ID; and finally, minimize the highest ID.

## Input
The first line of the dataset contains two integers, $n$ and $m$. 
The workforce headcount $n$ requires a minimum of three individuals to form a valid dataset, and cannot exceed the building's maximum occupancy limit of exactly two thousand personnel.
The total number of logged grievances $m$ will always be at least three. Its upper bound is strictly limited by the database's hard capacity of two hundred thousand entries, or the maximum possible number of distinct pairs that can be formed from the $n$ personnel, whichever is more restrictive.
Personnel are referenced by integer IDs from $1$ to $n$.

The subsequent $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a grievance between worker $u$ and worker $v$. 

The input data will always contain at least one group of three mutually incompatible workers.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the IDs of three workers who all have grievances against each other. 

The IDs must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). 

If there are multiple such groups, output the one that satisfies the tie-breaker described above.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
