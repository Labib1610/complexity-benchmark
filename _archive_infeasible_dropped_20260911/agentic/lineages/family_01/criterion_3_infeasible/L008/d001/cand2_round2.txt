# Problem C — Shift Assignment Pipeline: Stage 2

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are configuring a scheduling system for a facility that operates on exactly two daily shifts. You are provided with a list of pairwise incompatibilities between workers. If two workers have an incompatibility, they cannot be assigned to the same shift.

Before attempting to build the schedule, the system must check for configurations that are impossible to resolve. Specifically, if there is a group of three distinct workers where every pair among them has an incompatibility, it is impossible to assign them to just two shifts without placing at least one incompatible pair together on the same shift. 

Your task is to identify one such group of three workers to demonstrate the scheduling impossibility. You will receive the parsed list of incompatibilities. It is guaranteed that the provided data contains at least one group of three workers who are all mutually incompatible with one another.

To ensure reproducible escalation reports, you must output exactly one such group of three workers according to a strict tie-breaking rule.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of workers and the total number of conflicting pairs. Workers are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.

The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating a conflicting pair. Each conflicting pair is listed at most once. 

It is guaranteed that at least one group of three mutually incompatible workers exists in the data.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, representing the identifiers of the three mutually incompatible workers. 

The three identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). 

If there are multiple valid groups, output the lexicographically smallest triplet (smallest $a$; if tied, smallest $b$; if still tied, smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
