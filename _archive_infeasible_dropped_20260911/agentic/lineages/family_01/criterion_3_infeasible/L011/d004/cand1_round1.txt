<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Tripartite Cohesion

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A distributed network maintains an audit trail of bilateral synchronizations. Each record in the trail documents a successful handshake between exactly two distinct modules.

Due to a configuration flaw, the network has entered a deadlocked state. To diagnose the issue, you must isolate a specific pattern in the audit trail known as a complete tripartite resonance.

Consider any group of exactly three distinct module identifiers. We define the *cohesion score* of such a group as the total number of records in the audit trail where both modules involved in the handshake belong to this chosen group of three. A complete tripartite resonance is defined as a group of three modules whose cohesion score is exactly 3.

The audit trail is guaranteed to contain at least one such group. Your task is to identify it.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$. The number of modules $n$ is at least 3. The system allocates one dedicated channel per module, and the hardware supports a maximum of two thousand such channels, which strictly bounds $n$. The number of handshake records $m$ is at least 3. The audit trail is written to a storage buffer that holds up to two hundred thousand records, which acts as a hard limit on $m$. The number of records will not exceed the maximum possible number of distinct pairs of modules.

Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — the identifiers of the two modules in a single handshake record. Modules are identified by integers from 1 to $n$. All records represent distinct pairs (there are no duplicate handshakes in the log).

It is guaranteed that at least one group of three modules with a cohesion score of exactly 3 exists in the audit trail.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three modules in the resonance group. If there are multiple valid groups, output the one with the smallest value for $a$; in case of a tie, the one with the smallest value for $b$; and if still tied, the one with the smallest value for $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I removed the explicit framing of "finding three pairs" and replaced it with a subset-scoring definition. By defining a "cohesion score" as the count of records whose elements both fall within a chosen triplet, the problem mathematically still demands finding a triangle, but forces the model to recognize this equivalence rather than blindly translating the instructions into a naive, TLE-prone subset-scoring loop.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Since the graph is simple, a triplet of vertices can have at most 3 internal edges; thus, a cohesion score of 3 is strictly identical to the three vertices forming a triangle, preserving all valid outputs and tie-breaking logic.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The problem remains exactly the detection of a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, keeping it in the same complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>

<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
Instead of asking for a set of three mutually connected nodes by describing their pairwise edges directly, it defines a "cohesion score" as the number of edges whose endpoints both fall within a subset of size 3. Finding a subset with score 3 mathematically requires finding a triangle, but models strongly tend to translate the definition literally into O(N^3) or O(N^3 M) subset-scoring loops rather than recognizing the graph structure and applying fast triangle-finding algorithms.
