<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Collusion

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An auditor is attempting to divide regional suppliers (identified by integers starting from $1$ up to $n$) into exactly two independent oversight tiers. However, the records contain $m$ bilateral non-compete agreements. Two suppliers sharing such an agreement cannot be placed in the same oversight tier.

The auditor already knows that a two-tier division is hopeless for the given records. Your task is to provide a concrete proof of its impossibility by identifying a specific deadlock structure. You must find a sequence of three suppliers, $a$, $b$, and $c$, ordered such that $a < b < c$, which satisfies the following dependencies:
- Supplier $b$ shares an agreement with supplier $a$.
- Supplier $c$ shares an agreement with both supplier $a$ and supplier $b$.

This mutual restriction prevents the three suppliers from being split across just two tiers.

Output the identifiers of these three suppliers. It is guaranteed that the records contain at least one such sequence.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The number of suppliers $n$ is at least the minimum necessary to form the deadlock sequence, and at most two thousand. The number of agreements $m$ is at least three, and is capped at the maximum possible number of unique pairs among the $n$ suppliers, or two hundred thousand, whichever is smaller.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of suppliers who share a bilateral agreement. (Each pair appears at most once in the records).
It is guaranteed that at least one sequence of suppliers satisfying the proof condition exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three suppliers. Output the lexicographically smallest such combination (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The graph triangle condition was previously described as a trio of suppliers with exactly three internal agreements, which models easily identified as a 3-clique. This rewrite reframes the condition as an asymmetric sequence of dependencies ($b$ shares with $a$, $c$ shares with both), forcing the model to deduce the underlying symmetric structure. Additionally, numeric bounds are stated indirectly (Strategy S07) to test the model's ability to derive constraints from the scenario.
</RATIONALE>
