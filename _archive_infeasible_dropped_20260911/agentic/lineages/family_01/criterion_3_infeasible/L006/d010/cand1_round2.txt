Tensor Factorization Obfuscation
</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
Replaced the direct product of dot products with an algebraically equivalent triple sum over a 3D tensor, grouping terms by registry instead of by pulse. This hides the dot products entirely. Models must now either perform non-trivial symbolic algebraic factorization to recover the graph interpretation, or fail via TLE by directly computing the $O(m^3)$ tensor sums.
</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Registry Resonance

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a system of $n$ independent memory registries, numbered $1$ to $n$.
The system receives a sequence of $m$ synchronization pulses. Each pulse is assigned a sequential index $p$ from $1$ to $m$ in the order it is received.

Each registry $r$ maintains a state vector $V_r$ of length $m$. Initially, all elements of every vector are set to $0$.

The $p$-th pulse is defined by a pair of registry IDs $(u, v)$. Upon receiving this pulse, registry $u$ sets the $p$-th element of its vector to $1$ ($V_u[p] = 1$), and registry $v$ sets the $p$-th element of its vector to $1$ ($V_v[p] = 1$). All other elements of their vectors remain unchanged.

For any triplet of registries $(A, B, C)$, we define their *resonance tensor* $\mathcal{T}^{(A,B,C)}$ as a 3-dimensional array of size $m \times m \times m$. Its elements are computed by grouping the temporal states of each registry across three time indices $i$, $j$, and $k$:
$$ \mathcal{T}^{(A,B,C)}_{i, j, k} = (V_A[i] \times V_A[j]) \times (V_B[i] \times V_B[k]) \times (V_C[j] \times V_C[k]) $$

A critical resonance anomaly occurs if there exist three registry IDs $x_1, x_2$, and $x_3$ strictly increasing in value ($x_1 < x_2 < x_3$) such that the sum of all elements in their resonance tensor is strictly positive:
$$ \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^m \sum_{k=1}^m \mathcal{T}^{(x_1,x_2,x_3)}_{i, j, k} > 0 $$

You must find the IDs involved in this anomaly. It is guaranteed that the sequence of pulses produces at least one such configuration.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 200000)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — the two registries involved in the $p$-th synchronization pulse. The first of these lines corresponds to $p=1$, the next to $p=2$, and so on.
Each pulse involves two distinct registries ($u \neq v$), and no pair of registries is given more than once. It is guaranteed that at least one resonance anomaly exists.

## Output
Print three integers $x_1$, $x_2$, and $x_3$ on one line with $x_1 < x_2 < x_3$. Output the lexicographically smallest such combination (smallest $x_1$; ties broken by smallest $x_2$; then smallest $x_3$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The direct product of dot products $(V_A \cdot V_B)(V_A \cdot V_C)(V_B \cdot V_C)$ explicitly suggested checking pairwise connections (graph edges). By expanding this into a 3D tensor sum and grouping the factors by registry ($V_A[i]V_A[j]$) rather than by pulse ($V_A[i]V_B[i]$), the pairwise dot products are completely hidden. To succeed, the model must now symbolically factor the triple sum back into three independent dot products; otherwise, it will either fail to recognize the graph triangle structure or attempt an $O(m^3)$ brute-force evaluation and TLE. We also explicitly clarify that inputs do not contain self-loops or duplicate pairs, resolving the expert critique without changing the problem's mathematical reality.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The tensor sum algebraically factors exactly into the product of the three pairwise dot products, meaning the mathematical condition for an anomaly is identical for all inputs.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying problem remains finding a triangle in an undirected graph (detecting a 3-clique), which preserves the computational complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the triple sum as requiring $i, j, k$ to be distinct indices, though standard mathematical notation for independent nested sums strictly implies they iterate independently over all possible combinations.
</RISK>
