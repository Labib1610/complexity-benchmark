# Problem C — Registry Resonance

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a system of $n$ independent memory registries, numbered starting from $1$ up to $n$. The facility houses enough registries to allow for anomalies, meaning $n$ is at least three, up to a strict architectural maximum of two thousand.

The system receives a sequence of $m$ synchronization pulses. The number of pulses $m$ begins at a minimum of three. It cannot exceed the total number of distinct pairs of registries, nor can it surpass two hundred thousand, whichever is smaller. Each pulse is assigned a sequential index $k$ starting from $1$ up to $m$ in the order it is received.

Each registry $i$ maintains a state vector $V_i$ of length $m$. Initially, all elements of every vector are set to $0$.

The $k$-th pulse is defined by a pair of registry IDs $(u, v)$. Upon receiving this pulse, registry $u$ sets the $k$-th element of its vector to $-k$, and registry $v$ sets the $k$-th element of its vector to $-k$ (i.e., $V_u[k] = -k$ and $V_v[k] = -k$). All other elements of their vectors remain unchanged.

Let $V_a \cdot V_b$ denote the standard dot product of two state vectors, defined as $\sum_{k=1}^m V_a[k] \times V_b[k]$.

A critical resonance anomaly occurs if there exist three registry IDs $x_1, x_2$, and $x_3$ strictly increasing in value ($x_1 < x_2 < x_3$) such that:
$$ (V_{x_1} \cdot V_{x_2}) \cdot (V_{x_1} \cdot V_{x_3}) \cdot (V_{x_2} \cdot V_{x_3}) > 0 $$

You must find the IDs involved in this anomaly. It is guaranteed that the sequence of pulses produces at least one such configuration.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — the two registries involved in the $k$-th synchronization pulse. The first of these lines corresponds to $k=1$, the next to $k=2$, and so on.
It is guaranteed that at least one resonance anomaly exists.

## Output
Print three integers $x_1$, $x_2$, and $x_3$ on one line with $x_1 < x_2 < x_3$. Output the lexicographically smallest such combination (smallest $x_1$; ties broken by smallest $x_2$; then smallest $x_3$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I changed the vector update rule from a constant +1 to a pulse-index-dependent value (-k) and stated the numeric bounds indirectly via the scenario text (Strategy S07). This forces models to mathematically reason that the dot product is a sum of squares ((-k)^2 = k^2 > 0), thereby mapping the condition back to simple boolean graph adjacency. Models relying on surface patterns will attempt literal mathematical simulation, which will fail due to massive integer overflow in C++ or Time Limit Exceeded in Python.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because (-k)^2 = k^2 > 0 for all k >= 1, the dot product between any two registries is strictly positive if and only if they share at least one pulse, keeping the anomaly condition logically identical to finding a triangle in the underlying graph.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly equivalent to finding the lexicographically first triangle in an unweighted graph, which maintains its original complexity class and can still be optimally solved using the intended bitset adjacency algorithm.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>

</RISK>
