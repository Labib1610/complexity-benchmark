<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Storage Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A safety officer is attempting to divide $n$ chemical compounds (identified by integers $1$ through $n$) into exactly two isolated storage bunkers. However, certain pairs of compounds are highly reactive. Two compounds with a reactive restriction cannot be placed in the same bunker.

The officer already knows that a two-bunker division is hopeless. Your task is not to attempt the division, but to provide a concrete proof of its impossibility. You must find exactly three distinct compounds, $a$, $b$, and $c$, such that the records indicate a restriction between $a$ and $b$, a restriction between $a$ and $c$, and a restriction between $b$ and $c$. Because these three compounds mutually react with one another, they cannot be separated into only two bunkers without at least two of them ending up in the same bunker.

Output these three compounds. It is guaranteed that the records contain at least one such configuration.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of compounds that share a reactive restriction.
It is guaranteed that at least one configuration of three mutually reactive compounds exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three compounds. Output the lexicographically smallest such combination (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>

The phrase "subset of exactly three distinct suppliers such that every pair among them is bound" strongly triggered 3-clique/triangle recognition in models. This was rewritten to explicitly list the required internal relationships ("a restriction between a and b, a restriction between a and c, and a restriction between b and c"), forcing models to parse the structural constraints rather than matching the definition of a clique. The exact numeric bounds were restored in the input section to satisfy strict machine-validation checks.
</RATIONALE>
