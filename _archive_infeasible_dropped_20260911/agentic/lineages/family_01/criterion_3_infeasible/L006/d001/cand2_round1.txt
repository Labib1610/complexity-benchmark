# Problem C — Thermal Runaway

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A materials engineer is trying to distribute $n$ experimental catalysts into two separate thermal isolation chambers. However, certain pairs of catalysts are incompatible; if two incompatible catalysts are placed in the same chamber, they will undergo a runaway exothermic reaction.

The engineer knows that a safe two-chamber distribution is impossible. Your task is to provide a minimal proof of this impossibility. Specifically, the records guarantee the existence of at least one group of exactly three catalysts in which every catalyst is incompatible with both of the other two. Such a group inherently proves that two chambers are not enough, because at least two of the three would have to share a chamber. 

You must identify one such group of three catalysts.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of catalysts that are incompatible.
It is guaranteed that at least one group of three mutually incompatible catalysts exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$ $b$ $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that the pair $a$ and $b$, the pair $a$ and $c$, and the pair $b$ and $c$ are all listed as incompatible. Output the lexicographically smallest such group (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
