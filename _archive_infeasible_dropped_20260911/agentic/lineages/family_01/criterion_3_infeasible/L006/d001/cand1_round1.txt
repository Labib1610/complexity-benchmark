# Problem C — Proof of Collusion

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An auditor is attempting to divide regional suppliers into exactly two independent oversight tiers. However, certain pairs of suppliers share bilateral non-compete agreements. Two suppliers with such an agreement cannot be placed in the same oversight tier, as they would collude to manipulate the audit.

The auditor already knows that a two-tier division is hopeless. Your task is not to attempt the division, but to provide a concrete proof of its impossibility. It is guaranteed that the records contain at least one group of exactly three suppliers who all share bilateral non-compete agreements with one another. Any such trio guarantees that two tiers are insufficient, as at least two of them would be forced into the same tier. Output one such trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers representing the total headcount of suppliers and the total volume of agreements. The supplier headcount is bounded below by the minimal size of the sought group (three) and bounded above by the district's strict licensing cap of two thousand suppliers. The volume of agreements is also at least three, and cannot exceed the database limit of two hundred thousand records, or the natural maximum of one agreement per unique pair of suppliers, whichever is lower.

Each of the subsequent lines contains two integers identifying a pair of suppliers who share a bilateral agreement. The suppliers are identified by integers starting from 1 up to the headcount. It is guaranteed that at least one trio of mutually agreeing suppliers exists in the records.

## Output
Print three supplier identifiers on a single line, ordered from smallest to largest, such that each of the three suppliers has a bilateral agreement with both of the other two. If there are multiple such groups of three, output the group that is lexicographically smallest (the one with the smallest first identifier; in case of a tie, the smallest second identifier; and if still tied, the smallest third identifier).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
