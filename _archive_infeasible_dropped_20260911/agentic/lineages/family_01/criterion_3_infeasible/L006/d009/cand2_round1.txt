<METHOD_NAME>Ising-Model Polarity Transformation</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Transforms the boolean state (0/1) and logical AND operations into a spin-system with +1/-1 polarities and algebraic sums. The edge existence is represented by a polarity of -1 (achieved by multiplying by -1), and the triangle condition is represented by the sum of three polarities equaling -3. This forces the model to reason about parity and algebraic conditions rather than falling back on bitset/graph-matching heuristics.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Registry Resonance

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a system of $n$ independent memory registries, numbered $1$ to $n$. Each registry $i$ maintains a polarity field $P_i(j)$ with respect to every other registry $j$. Initially, all polarities are in a positive state, meaning $P_i(j) = 1$ for all $i \neq j$.

The system receives $m$ synchronization pulses. Each pulse is defined by a pair of registry IDs $(u, v)$. Upon receiving this pulse, the mutual polarities between $u$ and $v$ are inverted: $P_u(v)$ is multiplied by $-1$, and $P_v(u)$ is multiplied by $-1$.

A critical resonance anomaly occurs if there exist three registry IDs $x_1, x_2$, and $x_3$ strictly increasing in value ($x_1 < x_2 < x_3$) such that their mutual polarities satisfy:
$$P_{x_1}(x_2) + P_{x_1}(x_3) + P_{x_2}(x_3) = -3$$

You must find the IDs involved in this anomaly. It is guaranteed that the sequence of pulses produces at least one such configuration.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The facility limits the system to a maximum of two thousand registries, and naturally, at least three are required to form an anomaly. The number of pulses $m$ is at least three, and cannot exceed the number of unique pairs that can be formed from the $n$ registries, or two hundred thousand, whichever is smaller.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a synchronization pulse between registry $u$ and registry $v$.
It is guaranteed that at least one resonance anomaly exists.

## Output
Print three integers $x_1$, $x_2$, and $x_3$ on one line with $x_1 < x_2 < x_3$. Output the lexicographically smallest such combination (smallest $x_1$; ties broken by smallest $x_2$; then smallest $x_3$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The boolean state bits were replaced with +1/-1 polarities, and the "set to 1" operation was replaced with a parity-sensitive inversion (multiplying by -1). The anomaly condition was changed from a product of bits to an algebraic sum of polarities equaling -3. This forces models to derive the equivalence to a static graph triangle rather than triggering structural heuristics from bitwise operations.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because the input represents a simple graph without duplicate pulses, multiplying the initial +1 state by -1 exactly once yields -1 (equivalent to an edge), and a sum of three such values equals -3 if and only if all three are -1, preserving the anomaly condition perfectly.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains equivalent to finding a triangle (K3) in an undirected graph, keeping it in the same complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
