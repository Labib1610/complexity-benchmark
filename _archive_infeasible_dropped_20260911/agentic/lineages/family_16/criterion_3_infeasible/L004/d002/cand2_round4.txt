# Problem C — Impossible Deadline: Certify It

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A facility processes $j$ jobs through exactly two stations. Every job must be processed at station 1 first, and only after it finishes there can it be processed at station 2. Each station can process at most one job at a time.

There is a required deadline $T$: every job must be completely finished by time $T$.

For all inputs provided to your program, completing all jobs by the deadline is impossible. Your task is to output a short certificate that identifies the cause of this impossibility. Exactly one of the following reasons holds for every input; print the one that holds:

- A single job is structurally too long. Even in isolation, its sequential processing requirements at the two stations would cause it to exceed the deadline. Print `JOB i`, where $i$ (1-based) is the index of the job.
- A single station is overloaded. The total active processing time required at this station across all jobs exceeds the deadline, making it impossible to finish everything on time regardless of the schedule. Print `LOAD c`, where $c$ is the station number (1 or 2).

If the applicable reason is satisfied by multiple jobs or stations, you may print the certificate for any one of them.

## Input

The first line contains two integers $j$ and $T$ ($1 \le j \le 200000$, $1 \le T \le 1000000000$).
Each of the next $j$ lines contains two integers $p_1$ and $p_2$ ($1 \le p_1, p_2 \le 1000000$): the processing times of the job at station 1 and station 2.

## Output

Print a single line containing the certificate of impossibility: either `JOB i` (where $i$ is the job index) or `LOAD c` (where $c$ is the station number).

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, even if the facility only had to process the first job, it would still fail to meet the 100-unit deadline because of the time it must spend sequentially across the two stations (70 then 60). Thus, `JOB 1` proves the deadline is impossible. In Example 2, no single job violates the deadline on its own, but the first station is required to operate for a combined 200 time units overall. Since it processes tasks one at a time, it cannot finish within the 150-unit deadline, making `LOAD 1` the valid certificate.
