<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Impossible Polynomial Specifications: Certify It

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are given **j** jobs. For each job $k$ (where $k$ is a 1-based index from 1 to **j**), you must assign two non-negative integers $a_k$ and $b_k$. These integers define two polynomials in the variable $x$ for job $k$:
- $A_k(x) = x^{a_k} \sum_{m=0}^{p_{1,k}-1} x^m$  (belonging to collection 1)
- $B_k(x) = x^{b_k} \sum_{m=0}^{p_{2,k}-1} x^m$  (belonging to collection 2)

The values $p_{1,k}$ and $p_{2,k}$ are positive integers provided as input for each job.

A valid assignment of $a_k$ and $b_k$ for all jobs must satisfy three rules:

- **Rule 1 (Degree Limit):** The degree of every polynomial $A_k(x)$ and $B_k(x)$ must be strictly less than **T**.
- **Rule 2 (Coefficient Bound):** For collection 1, the polynomial $S_1(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{j} A_k(x)$ must have no coefficient strictly greater than 1. Similarly, for collection 2, the polynomial $S_2(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{j} B_k(x)$ must have no coefficient strictly greater than 1.
- **Rule 3 (Degree Separation):** For a given job $k$, the degree of $A_k(x)$ must be strictly less than the lowest exponent with a non-zero coefficient in $B_k(x)$.

For all test cases provided in this problem, it is guaranteed that no valid assignment can satisfy all three rules simultaneously. Your task is to certify this impossibility by identifying a localized contradiction. Exactly one of the following two types of certificates will be valid for the given input. Print the one that applies:

- **Job-specific contradiction:** There is a single job **i** (1-based index) such that Rule 1 (for job **i**) and Rule 3 (for job **i**) cannot be simultaneously satisfied, even if we completely ignore Rule 2 and all other jobs. Print: `JOB i`.
- **Collection-specific contradiction:** There is a single collection **c** (where **c** is 1 or 2) such that Rule 1 (for collection **c**) and Rule 2 (for collection **c**) cannot be simultaneously satisfied, even if we completely ignore Rule 3 and the other collection. Print: `LOAD c`.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the required number of terms $p_{1,k}$ and $p_{2,k}$ for the polynomials of job $k$.

## Output

Print a single line containing the certificate of impossibility: either `JOB i` (where i is the job index) or `LOAD c` (where c is the collection number).

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, consider job 1. Rule 1 requires the degrees of $A_1(x)$ and $B_1(x)$ to be strictly less than 100. Since $a_1 \ge 0$, $A_1(x)$ has a degree of at least 69. Rule 3 requires the degree of $A_1(x)$ to be strictly less than the lowest exponent with a non-zero coefficient in $B_1(x)$. This forces the lowest exponent of $B_1(x)$ to be at least 70. Since $B_1(x)$ has 60 terms, its highest exponent (its degree) would be at least $70 + 59 = 129$, which violates the requirement that the degree must be strictly less than 100 (Rule 1). Thus, `JOB 1` is the correct certificate. 

In Example 2, no single job violates the rules on its own. However, consider collection 1. Rule 2 requires that when we sum the four $A_k(x)$ polynomials (each having 50 terms with a coefficient of 1), the resulting polynomial $S_1(x)$ has no coefficient greater than 1. This means the non-zero terms of the four polynomials cannot overlap in their exponents. To accommodate $4 \times 50 = 200$ distinct non-negative exponents, $S_1(x)$ must have a degree of at least 199. However, Rule 1 restricts the degree of every $A_k(x)$ to be strictly less than 150. This is a contradiction, so `LOAD 1` is the correct certificate.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The problem was completely reframed from sets of consecutive integers into constructing polynomials with specific degrees and coefficients. The sizes of the sets (`p1` and `p2`) are now the number of terms in truncated geometric series, the domain bound `T` is a strict degree limit, disjointness is enforced by requiring the sum of polynomials to have coefficients $\le 1$, and strict precedence is a strict inequality between the degree of one polynomial and the lowest exponent of another. This forces the model to perform algebraic deduction to recover the identical `p1 + p2 > T` and `sum(p1) > T` checks, defeating surface-level pattern matching on set sizes.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The number of available non-negative exponents strictly less than T is exactly T, perfectly matching the original capacity of the integer universe {1..T}, so the thresholds for contradictions (`p1 + p2 > T` and `sum(p1) > T`) remain mathematically identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
