<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Impossible Window: Certify It

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are auditing $j$ data-transfer routines. Each job $i$ requires a header transmission on Channel 1 of duration $p_{i,1}$, and a payload transmission on Channel 2 of duration $p_{i,2}$.

A theoretical schedule assigns start times $S_{i,1} \ge 0$ and $S_{i,2} \ge 0$ to each job $i$. To be physically realizable, a schedule must satisfy three constraints:
1. **Cross-channel dependency**: $S_{i,1} + p_{i,1} \le S_{i,2}$ for all $i$.
2. **Channel 1 exclusivity**: For all $i \neq k$, the intervals $[S_{i,1}, S_{i,1}+p_{i,1})$ and $[S_{k,1}, S_{k,1}+p_{k,1})$ are disjoint.
3. **Channel 2 exclusivity**: For all $i \neq k$, the intervals $[S_{i,2}, S_{i,2}+p_{i,2})$ and $[S_{k,2}, S_{k,2}+p_{k,2})$ are disjoint.

A schedule successfully meets the strict operational window of $T$ microseconds if $\max_i (S_{i,2} + p_{i,2}) \le T$.

For every input in this problem, it is impossible to meet the operational window. Your task is to output a short text code that proves this impossibility by identifying the isolated constraint that makes the window unachievable.

Valid proofs are:
- `JOB i` (where $i$ is the 1-based index of a job): There is no assignment of $S_{i,1} \ge 0$ and $S_{i,2} \ge 0$ satisfying constraint 1 for job $i$ such that $S_{i,2} + p_{i,2} \le T$.
- `LOAD c` (where $c$ is 1 or 2): There is no assignment of start times $\ge 0$ satisfying constraint $c+1$ for all jobs such that $\max_i (S_{i,c} + p_{i,c}) \le T$.

You are guaranteed that for every input, exactly one such proof is valid.

## Input

The first line contains two integers $j$ and $T$ ($1 \le j \le 200000$, $1 \le T \le 1000000000$).
Each of the next $j$ lines contains two integers $p_{i,1}$ and $p_{i,2}$ ($1 \le p_{i,1}, p_{i,2} \le 1000000$): the duration in microseconds for job $i$'s header on Channel 1 and its payload on Channel 2, respectively.

## Output

Print a single line containing either `JOB i` (if constraint 1 for job $i$ alone violates the window) or `LOAD c` (if constraint $c+1$ alone violates the window).

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, for job 1, constraint 1 requires $S_{1,1} + 70 \le S_{1,2}$. Thus $S_{1,2} + 60 \ge S_{1,1} + 130$. Since $S_{1,1} \ge 0$, the completion time is at least 130, which exceeds the window $T=100$. This validates the proof `JOB 1`.

In Example 2, constraint 2 requires all four jobs to have disjoint intervals of length 50 on Channel 1. Any such assignment requires a maximum completion time on Channel 1 of at least $4 \times 50 = 200$, which exceeds $T=150$. This validates the proof `LOAD 1`.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Removed the explicit descriptions of the certificates as simple additive bounds (`p1+p2` and `sum(p_c)`). Replaced them with formal interval scheduling constraints, defining the certificates as the mathematical impossibility of satisfying a specific isolated constraint within $T$. This forces models to deduce that the minimum makespan of disjoint intervals is their sum, and the minimum completion of dependent intervals is their sum, rather than relying on surface-level pattern matching of the problem's textual guarantees.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The minimal completion time satisfying $S_{i,1} + p_{i,1} \le S_{i,2}$ is $p_{i,1} + p_{i,2}$, and the minimal completion time for non-overlapping intervals on Channel $c$ is exactly $\sum p_{i,c}$, making the conditions mathematically identical to the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains in the INFEASIBLE class because it still asks for a direct, efficiently verifiable certificate of impossibility (either a single job's sequential duration or a single channel's total load).
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
A reader might mistakenly assume that evaluating `LOAD c` requires satisfying *both* constraint 1 and constraint $c+1$ simultaneously, but the text explicitly specifies "satisfying constraint $c+1$" and emphasizes "isolated constraint", mitigating this risk.
</RISK>
