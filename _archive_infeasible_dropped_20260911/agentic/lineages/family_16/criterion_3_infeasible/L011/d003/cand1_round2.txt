<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Unattainable Target: Provide Evidence

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A ground station needs to route $j$ data transmissions through a relay satellite. Every transmission is sent on the uplink frequency first, and then broadcast on the downlink frequency. For each transmission, you are given its duration on the uplink ($p_1$) and its duration on the downlink ($p_2$). Each frequency can carry only one transmission at a time. Furthermore, after a transmission's uplink has entirely finished, the relay satellite requires a mandatory 1 millisecond transition before it can begin that transmission's downlink.

There is a hard time limit $T$: every transmission must be completely finished by millisecond $T$.

You do not need to find an order; for every input in this problem, no sequence of transmissions can possibly finish by the limit $T$. Your task is to output a certificate that proves the limit is unattainable. Every input is guaranteed to satisfy exactly one of the following explicit properties. Print the certificate for the one that holds:

- A fundamental delay in a single transmission: At least one transmission $i$ requires a total sequential time (uplink, plus the 1 millisecond transition, plus downlink) that strictly exceeds the deadline. That is, $p_{1,i} + 1 + p_{2,i} > T$. Print: `JOB i` — where $i$ (1-based) is such a transmission.

- A fundamental capacity shortfall on a single frequency: At least one frequency is assigned a total sum of work that strictly exceeds the deadline, even ignoring the other frequency and all transition times. That is, either the sum of all uplink durations strictly exceeds $T$ ($\sum_{i=1}^j p_{1,i} > T$), or the sum of all downlink durations strictly exceeds $T$ ($\sum_{i=1}^j p_{2,i} > T$). Print: `LOAD c` — where $c$ is 1 (if the uplink sum exceeds $T$) or 2 (if the downlink sum exceeds $T$).

## Input

The first line contains two integers $j$ and $T$ ($1 \le j \le 200000$, $1 \le T \le 1000000000$).
Each of the next $j$ lines contains two integers $p_1$ and $p_2$ ($1 \le p_1, p_2 \le 1000000$): the durations of a transmission on the uplink and the downlink.

## Output

Print a single line containing exactly one of the following:
- `JOB i` if transmission $i$ (1-based) satisfies $p_{1,i} + 1 + p_{2,i} > T$.
- `LOAD 1` if the uplink frequency satisfies $\sum_{i=1}^j p_{1,i} > T$.
- `LOAD 2` if the downlink frequency satisfies $\sum_{i=1}^j p_{2,i} > T$.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, transmission 1 requires 70 milliseconds on the uplink, a 1 millisecond transition, and 60 milliseconds on the downlink. Even without competing transmissions, it requires $70 + 1 + 60 = 131$ milliseconds sequentially. Since $131 > 100$, the limit is exceeded. Thus, `JOB 1` is the certificate.

In Example 2, no single transmission violates the limit in isolation (for instance, $50 + 1 + 1 = 52 \le 150$). However, the uplink frequency is assigned four transmissions of 50 milliseconds each. The sum of all uplink durations is $\sum_{i=1}^4 p_{1,i} = 200$. Since $200 > 150$, the raw volume on the uplink exceeds the limit, so `LOAD 1` is the certificate.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Introduced a mandatory 1 millisecond transition time between the uplink and downlink stages of a transmission, changing the isolated job requirement to `p1 + 1 + p2 > T`. To address the gate and expert feedback, the exact mathematical formulas for all certificates were explicitly included in both the description and output sections. This forces models to engage with the modified inequality rather than relying on a hardcoded, unadapted `p1 + p2 > T` check.
</RATIONALE>

The dataset contains no boundary cases where `p1 + p2 = T` while a machine overload also exists; thus the +1 modification preserves exactly one valid certificate per input.
