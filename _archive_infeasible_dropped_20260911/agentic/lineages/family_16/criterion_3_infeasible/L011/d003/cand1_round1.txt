<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Unattainable Target: Provide Evidence

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A ground station needs to route $j$ data transmissions through a relay satellite. Every transmission is sent on the uplink frequency first, and then broadcast on the downlink frequency. For each transmission, you are given its duration on the uplink and its duration on the downlink. Each frequency can carry only one transmission at a time, and a transmission can only start its downlink after its uplink has entirely finished.

There is a hard time limit $T$: every transmission must be completely finished by millisecond $T$.

You do not need to find an order; for every input in this problem, no sequence of transmissions can possibly finish by the limit $T$. Your task is to output a certificate that proves the limit is unattainable. Every input is guaranteed to satisfy exactly one of the following properties. Print the certificate for the one that holds:

- A fundamental delay in a single transmission: At least one transmission possesses an inherent end-to-end duration that strictly exceeds the deadline. Print: `JOB i` — where $i$ (1-based) is such a transmission.

- A fundamental capacity shortfall on a single frequency: At least one frequency requires a total raw operating time that strictly exceeds the deadline. Print: `LOAD c` — where $c$ is 1 (for the uplink) or 2 (for the downlink) and is such a frequency.

## Input

The first line contains two integers $j$ and $T$ ($1 \le j \le 200000$, $1 \le T \le 1000000000$).
Each of the next $j$ lines contains two integers $p_1$ and $p_2$ ($1 \le p_1, p_2 \le 1000000$): the durations of a transmission on the uplink and the downlink.

## Output

Print a single line: `JOB i` (where $i$ is a transmission that cannot meet the limit due to its inherent duration) or `LOAD c` (where $c$ is a frequency that cannot process its required volume by the limit).

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, transmission 1 requires 70 milliseconds on the uplink and 60 milliseconds on the downlink. Its inherent end-to-end duration is 130 milliseconds, which exceeds the 100 millisecond limit. Thus, `JOB 1` is the certificate.

In Example 2, no single transmission violates the limit. However, the uplink frequency is assigned four transmissions of 50 milliseconds each. The total raw operating time on the uplink is 200 milliseconds, which exceeds the 150 millisecond limit, so `LOAD 1` is the certificate.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The suggested removal (adding a 1-millisecond transition) would change the mathematical condition from `p1 + p2 > T` to `p1 + 1 + p2 > T`. This violates Constraint 1 because for an input where `p1 + p2 = T`, the answer would change from LOAD to JOB, breaking the unmodified reference solution.
Instead, I removed the explicit phrases "even if it were granted exclusive access" and "idealised relaxation" that models used to justify their simple arithmetic shortcuts. By replacing them with terms like "inherent end-to-end duration" and "total raw operating time", models must now independently deduce that these map to the isolated `p1 + p2` and aggregate `sum(p)` sums.
</RATIONALE>
