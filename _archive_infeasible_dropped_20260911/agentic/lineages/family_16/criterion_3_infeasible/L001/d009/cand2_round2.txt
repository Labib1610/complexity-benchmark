<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Spectrum Interference Certificate

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are allocating frequency spectrum for a wireless network consisting of $j$ independent channels. Each channel $i$ requires two dedicated frequency bands: an uplink band $[U_{i,1}, V_{i,1}]$ and a downlink band $[U_{i,2}, V_{i,2}]$. 

The network operates under the following physical rules:
1. **Bandwidth:** Each channel specifies its required bandwidth in octaves. An interval $[L, R]$ spans $p$ octaves if $R = L \cdot 2^p$. For channel $i$, the uplink band must span exactly $p_{i,1}$ octaves, and the downlink band must span exactly $p_{i,2}$ octaves.
2. **Base Frequency:** All allocated frequencies must be at least $1$ Hz (i.e., $U_{i,1} \ge 1$ and $U_{i,2} \ge 1$).
3. **Mixer Limit:** For each channel $i$, the hardware mixer requires that the product of its highest uplink frequency and highest downlink frequency ($V_{i,1} \cdot V_{i,2}$) does not exceed $2^T$.
4. **Interference:** To avoid signal interference, no two uplink bands may share interior frequencies (they may overlap exactly at their boundaries). The same strict non-overlapping rule applies to the downlink bands.
5. **Spectrum Ceiling:** No frequency in any band can exceed the system maximum of $2^T$ Hz.

In this specific problem, it is guaranteed that **no valid frequency allocation exists** for the given inputs. Your task is to output a simple proof (a certificate) demonstrating which structural bottleneck makes the system unsolvable.

It is guaranteed that exactly one of the two bottleneck categories (Channel Contradiction or Spectrum Contradiction) applies to each test case. You must output a valid certificate for the category that applies. If multiple indices within that category are valid (e.g., multiple channels fail individually), outputting any one of them is acceptable.

- **Channel Contradiction:** A single channel **i** cannot be allocated because its own bandwidth requirements and base frequency rules (Rules 1 and 2) intrinsically violate the mixer limit (Rule 3), even if it were the only channel in the network.
  Output format: `JOB i` (where **i** is the 1-based index of the channel).

- **Spectrum Contradiction:** The entire set of channels cannot be allocated for a specific direction **c** (where **c** = 1 for uplink, and **c** = 2 for downlink) because their combined bandwidth requirements and interference rules (Rules 1, 2, and 4) force at least one frequency to exceed the spectrum ceiling (Rule 5).
  Output format: `LOAD c` (where **c** is exactly 1 or 2).

## Input

The first line specifies two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
The subsequent **j** lines each provide exactly **2** integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000), representing the required octaves $p_{i,1}$ and $p_{i,2}$ respectively for the $i$-th channel.

## Output

Output exactly one line containing a valid certificate from the applicable category: either `JOB i` or `LOAD c`. If printing `LOAD c`, then **c** must be exactly 1 or 2.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, consider channel 1 in isolation. It requires 70 octaves for uplink and 60 octaves for downlink. The minimum possible upper frequencies are $1 \cdot 2^{70} = 2^{70}$ Hz and $1 \cdot 2^{60} = 2^{60}$ Hz. Their product is $2^{130}$. However, the mixer limit (Rule 3) restricts this product to at most $2^{100}$. Since $2^{130} > 2^{100}$, channel 1 cannot be allocated. Thus `JOB 1` is a valid certificate.

In Example 2, consider the uplink direction ($c=1$). There are 4 channels, each requiring 50 octaves of uplink bandwidth. Because the bands cannot share interior frequencies (Rule 4) and must start at or above 1 Hz (Rule 2), the highest frequency used must be at least $1 \cdot 2^{200} = 2^{200}$ Hz. The spectrum ceiling (Rule 5) restricts all frequencies to a maximum of $2^{150}$ Hz. Since $2^{200} > 2^{150}$, the uplink spectrum is insufficient. Thus `LOAD 1` is a valid certificate.

By defining intervals logarithmically (octaves) and setting limits as powers of two, the underlying mathematics (summing `p` values) remains exactly identical, but models must deduce this additive property from the domain rules rather than pattern-matching explicit equations. Furthermore, presenting the limits as $2^T$ tempts models to simulate the problem using literal exponentiation (`2**T`), which will cause a Time/Memory Limit Exceeded error since T can be up to $10^9$.
