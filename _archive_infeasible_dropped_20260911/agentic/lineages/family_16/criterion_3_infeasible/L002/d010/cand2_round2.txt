<METHOD_NAME>Physical Footprint Encoding</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the bare algebraic inequalities of the seed problem with physical footprint constraints on a 1D track. The variables w_{c,i} become center anchor points, and q_{c,i} become half-widths of a machine's footprint. The boundary 2T naturally emerges as the track length, and dispersion/separation become non-overlapping conditions. This forces the model to derive the additive packing bounds themselves rather than extracting them directly from parsed algebraic equations, while strictly preserving the mathematical model to satisfy the gate.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Infeasible Coordinate Assignments

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are given a target threshold $T$ and a set of $j$ tasks. Each task $i$ (from $1$ to $j$) is associated with two positive integers, $q_{1,i}$ and $q_{2,i}$.

Consider the problem of placing a specialized machine for each task $i$ for each stage $c \in \{1, 2\}$. The machines are placed on a linear track that starts at position $0$ and ends at position $2T$. Each machine must be assigned a real-valued center anchor coordinate $w_{c,i}$. 

The machine for stage $c$ of task $i$ has a physical footprint extending exactly $q_{c,i}$ units to the left of its anchor and $q_{c,i}$ units to the right of its anchor. Thus, its footprint covers the continuous interval $[w_{c,i} - q_{c,i}, w_{c,i} + q_{c,i}]$.

The placement must satisfy three conditions:
1. **Transition:** For every task $i$, the stage 2 machine's footprint must be located entirely to the right of its stage 1 machine's footprint, and the two footprints cannot overlap (they may touch at their boundaries).
2. **Repulsion:** For each stage $c \in \{1, 2\}$ and every pair of distinct tasks $i$ and $k$, their machine footprints cannot overlap (they may touch at their boundaries).
3. **Confinement:** For every task $i$, the stage 1 machine's footprint must not extend past the left end of the track (position 0), and the stage 2 machine's footprint must not extend past the right end of the track (position $2T$).

For every test case, it is guaranteed that no such full valid placement of anchors exists. Your task is to provide a directly checkable certificate of unsatisfiability by identifying a specific relaxed subsystem of these constraints that is already impossible to satisfy on its own. 

To formalize the certificates, we define the symmetric confinement margin for an anchor $w$ and footprint half-width $q$ as:
$$S(w, q) = \min(w - q, (2T - q) - w)$$

You must output a valid certificate of unsatisfiability from one of the two allowed forms below. It is mathematically guaranteed that at least one such certificate is valid for every test case.

- `JOB i` : For the task at 1-based index $i$, the subsystem consisting only of the Transition condition for $i$ and the Confinement conditions for $i$ cannot be simultaneously satisfied (ignoring all other tasks and conditions).
- `LOAD c` : where $c \in \{1, 2\}$. This certifies that the subsystem consisting only of the Repulsion conditions for stage $c$, along with the requirement that $S(w_{c,i}, q_{c,i}) \ge 0$ for all $i$, cannot be simultaneously satisfied (ignoring the other stage and all other conditions).

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **q1** and **q2** (1 ≤ q1, q2 ≤ 1000000): the values of $q_{1,i}$ and $q_{2,i}$ for the $i$-th task.

## Output

Print a single line containing a valid certificate: `JOB i` or `LOAD c` as described above.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, for task 1, the `JOB` subsystem requires the stage 1 footprint to not extend past 0 (so $w_{1,1} \ge 70$), the stage 2 footprint to not extend past $2T = 200$ (so $w_{2,1} \le 140$), and the stage 2 footprint to be entirely to the right of the stage 1 footprint ($w_{2,1} - 60 \ge w_{1,1} + 70$, which simplifies to $w_{2,1} - w_{1,1} \ge 130$). Combining $w_{1,1} \ge 70$ and $w_{2,1} - w_{1,1} \ge 130$ gives $w_{2,1} \ge 200$, which contradicts $w_{2,1} \le 140$. Thus, this subsystem is unsatisfiable for task 1, making `JOB 1` a valid certificate.

In Example 2, no single task violates the `JOB` subsystem. However, for `LOAD 1`, the subsystem requires four real-valued anchors $w_{1,i}$ to simultaneously satisfy $S(w_{1,i}, 50) \ge 0$. Evaluating the symmetric confinement margin, this means $\min(w_{1,i} - 50, 250 - w_{1,i}) \ge 0$, which confines all four anchors to the interval $[50, 250]$. This ensures all four footprints (each of length $100$) are completely contained within the track $[0, 300]$. The Repulsion condition requires these footprints to not overlap. It is physically impossible to place four non-overlapping footprints of length 100 within a total track length of 300. Thus, this subsystem is unsatisfiable, making `LOAD 1` a valid certificate.

<RATIONALE>
The rewrite replaces the explicit algebraic inequalities of the seed problem with physical footprint constraints on a 1D track. This removes the bare inequalities that models were parsing to trivially extract the additive bounds (`q1+q2 > T` and `sum(q) > T`), forcing them to mentally construct the interval packing math and derive the bounds themselves. Crucially, the mathematical model (coordinates, 2T boundary, symmetric margin) is strictly preserved to prevent task drift and satisfy the gate.
</RATIONALE>
