<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Impossible Deadline: Certify It

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A chef is preparing a banquet featuring **j** special recipes (`JOB`s). Every `JOB` must be processed in appliance 1 (the oven) and subsequently in appliance 2 (the broiler). For each `JOB`, you are given the exact minutes it requires in appliance 1 and appliance 2. Each appliance processes one `JOB` at a time, and a `JOB`'s broiler phase cannot start until its oven phase is entirely finished.

All `JOB`s must be completed by a strict deadline **T**. For every input, it is guaranteed that no valid schedule can meet this deadline. Your task is to certify this impossibility.

To do this, you must identify a hypothetical relaxed scenario that *still* fails to meet the deadline. For every input, exactly one of the following hypothetical scenarios will have a minimum possible completion time (the time when all required phases are fully processed) strictly greater than **T**. You must print the identifier of the unique scenario that fails:

- `JOB i` : The scenario where we cancel all recipes except `JOB i` (1-based index). Even with exclusive access to the kitchen, its sequential processing (its oven phase strictly followed by its broiler phase) cannot finish by **T**.
- `LOAD 1` : The scenario where we must process all recipes, but we completely remove the precedence requirement (the two phases of every recipe can be scheduled independently, even at the same time), and we upgrade the broiler to have infinite parallel capacity (it can process any number of recipes simultaneously, though each phase still takes its required minutes). The oven remains strictly sequential (processing one recipe at a time). Even with these upgrades, the deadline is impossible.
- `LOAD 2` : The scenario where we must process all recipes, remove the precedence requirement as above, and upgrade the oven to infinite parallel capacity. The broiler remains strictly sequential. Even with these upgrades, the deadline is impossible.

Print the identifier of the unique scenario that exceeds **T**.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the required minutes of the `JOB` in appliance 1 and appliance 2.

## Output

Print a single line: the identifier (`JOB i`, `LOAD 1`, or `LOAD 2`) of the unique relaxed scenario whose minimum completion time exceeds **T**.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, under the scenario for `JOB 1`, its oven phase (70 minutes) followed by its broiler phase (60 minutes) requires 130 minutes total. This exceeds the 100-minute deadline. The other scenarios do not exceed the deadline (for instance, in the `LOAD 1` scenario, the oven sequentially processes all jobs in 75 minutes while the broiler processes all its phases in parallel in 60 minutes; since phases are independent, both appliances can start at minute 0 and finish all work by minute 75). Thus `JOB 1` is the unique failing scenario.

In Example 2, no single `JOB` exceeds the 150-minute deadline. However, under the `LOAD 1` scenario, even though the broiler can process all its phases simultaneously in 1 minute and phases are independent across appliances, the oven must still process all 4 jobs sequentially. This requires 200 minutes, which exceeds the 150-minute deadline, making `LOAD 1` the unique failing scenario.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I removed the explicit wording that checks for aggregated appliance durations and isolated sums. Instead, I redefined the three certificates as three specific relaxations of the scheduling problem (e.g., removing precedence constraints and granting infinite capacity to one machine). This forces the models to deduce that the minimum completion time of these relaxed scenarios corresponds mathematically to `p1+p2`, `max(sum p1, max p2)`, and `max(max p1, sum p2)`.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because the inputs guarantee that exactly one of the original bottlenecks applies, and because `max(sum p1, max p2) > T` is logically equivalent to `sum p1 > T` when no single job exceeds T, the exact same unique condition triggers for every test case.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The class remains INFEASIBLE (a linear scan checking three simple conditions), as the mathematical bounds of the relaxed scenarios can still be computed in O(N) time.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>

Models might misunderstand "infinite parallel capacity" and attempt to simulate the relaxed schedule step-by-step rather than extracting the O(N) mathematical bound, though the definition of the relaxation is mathematically unambiguous.
</RISK>
