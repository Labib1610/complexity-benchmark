<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Linear Track Congestion: Sensor Placement Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are given a linear track of length $2T$ (extending from coordinate $0$ to $2T$) and a set of $j$ sensor pairs. Each sensor pair $i$ (from $1$ to $j$) consists of a primary transmitter and a secondary receiver. The transmitter requires an interference radius of $p_{1,i}$, and the receiver requires an interference radius of $p_{2,i}$.

Consider the problem of assigning a center coordinate $u_i$ for each transmitter and $v_i$ for each receiver along the track. The assignment must satisfy the following four conditions:
1. **Intra-pair separation:** For every sensor pair $i$, the distance from the transmitter's center to the receiver's center must be at least the sum of their required radii. The receiver must be positioned at a larger coordinate than the transmitter.
2. **Transmitter isolation:** For every pair of distinct transmitters, their interference regions (the segment of length $2p_{1,i}$ centered at $u_i$) cannot overlap in their interiors.
3. **Receiver isolation:** For every pair of distinct receivers, their interference regions (the segment of length $2p_{2,i}$ centered at $v_i$) cannot overlap in their interiors.
4. **Track boundaries:** For every sensor pair $i$, both interference regions must be completely contained within the track from $0$ to $2T$.

For every test case in this problem, it is guaranteed that no such valid assignment of centers exists. Your task is to provide a directly checkable certificate of unsatisfiability by identifying a specific relaxed subsystem of these constraints that is already impossible to satisfy on its own. 

Output a valid certificate of unsatisfiability from one of the two allowed forms below. For every test case, exactly one of the two certificate types (either a `JOB` or a `LOAD` impossibility) applies; print the one that applies.

- `JOB i` : For the sensor pair at 1-based index $i$, the requirement that its transmitter's region starts at or after $0$, its receiver's region ends at or before $2T$, and their intra-pair separation cannot be simultaneously satisfied (ignoring all other pairs and isolation constraints).
- `LOAD c` : where $c \in \{1, 2\}$. 
  - If $c=1$, it certifies that the requirement for all transmitters to be completely contained within the track from $0$ to $2T$, along with the transmitter isolation constraints, cannot be simultaneously satisfied (ignoring receivers and all other constraints). 
  - If $c=2$, it certifies that the requirement for all receivers to be completely contained within the track from $0$ to $2T$, along with the receiver isolation constraints, cannot be simultaneously satisfied (ignoring transmitters and all other constraints).

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the values of $p_{1,i}$ and $p_{2,i}$ for the $i$-th sensor pair.

## Output

Print a single line containing the applicable certificate: `JOB i` or `LOAD c` as described above (exactly one type is applicable for each test case).

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, for sensor pair 1, the transmitter's interference region forces its center to be at least 70 units from the start. The intra-pair separation requires the receiver to be placed at least 130 units further along the track. This would push the receiver's center to at least 200. However, to keep its interference region within the track of length 200, the receiver's center cannot exceed 140. This contradiction makes the subsystem unsatisfiable for sensor pair 1, so `JOB 1` is the applicable certificate.

In Example 2, no single sensor pair is impossible to place on its own. However, for `LOAD 1`, the subsystem forces all four transmitters to be completely contained within the track, restricting their centers to a segment of length 200 (from coordinate 50 to 250). The transmitter isolation condition mandates a distance of at least 100 between any two transmitter centers. It is geometrically impossible to pack four centers with mutual distances of at least 100 into a segment of length 200. Thus, this subsystem is unsatisfiable, making `LOAD 1` the applicable certificate.
