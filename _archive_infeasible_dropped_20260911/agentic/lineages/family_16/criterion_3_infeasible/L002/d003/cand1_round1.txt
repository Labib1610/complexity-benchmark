# Problem C — Impossible Deadline: Certify It

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A chef is preparing a banquet featuring **j** special recipes, tracked in the kitchen's order system as `JOB`s. Every `JOB` must be processed in appliance 1 (the oven) and subsequently in appliance 2 (the broiler). For each `JOB` you are given the exact number of minutes it must occupy appliance 1, and the exact number of minutes it must occupy appliance 2. Each appliance can only accommodate one `JOB` at any given moment, and a `JOB` can only transition to appliance 2 after its appliance 1 phase is entirely complete.

The kitchen operates under a strict deadline: all processing for all `JOB`s must be completely finished by time **T**.

For every input in this problem, it is **guaranteed that the deadline cannot be met**, no matter how the chef schedules the processing. Your task is not to search for a schedule; it is to **prove** the deadline is impossible with a short certificate that can be checked directly against the input.

Exactly one of the following structural bottlenecks is guaranteed to apply for every input. Print the certificate corresponding to the unique applicable bottleneck:

- **Isolated Recipe Bottleneck:** Even if granted exclusive access to the kitchen from the start of the shift, a recipe's uninterrupted end-to-end sequence (its oven phase immediately followed by its broiler phase) cannot be completed within the **T**-minute operational window.
  Print: `JOB i` — where **i** (1-based) is such a recipe.

- **Appliance Saturation Bottleneck:** An appliance cannot concurrently process multiple recipes. Consequently, its required active duration is the aggregation of all corresponding phases across the entire banquet. If this aggregated active duration for the oven cannot be contained within the **T**-minute operational window, the schedule fails. The broiler is subject to the identical saturation constraint.
  Print: `LOAD c` — where **c** is 1 (if the oven's aggregated duration overflows the window) or 2 (if the broiler's aggregated duration overflows the window).

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the required minutes of the `JOB` in appliance 1 and appliance 2.

## Output

Print a single line: `JOB i` (a `JOB` whose isolated sequence fails) or `LOAD c` (an appliance **c** whose aggregated duration overflows the window).

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, `JOB` 1 has an uninterrupted end-to-end sequence requiring 70 minutes in the oven and 60 in the broiler. This sequence cannot be completed within the 100-minute operational window, so `JOB 1` proves the deadline is impossible.

In Example 2, no single `JOB` fails the isolated recipe bottleneck. However, the oven's required active duration across all recipes aggregates to 200 minutes. Because the oven cannot concurrently process multiple recipes, this duration cannot be contained within the 150-minute operational window, making `LOAD 1` the certificate.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Replaced the direct mathematical descriptions of the certificates (e.g., subtracting oven time from T, or sequentially deducting budgets) with conceptual descriptions of scheduling bottlenecks (end-to-end sequence duration and aggregated active duration). This forces models to independently translate the physical constraints into the `p1 + p2 > T` and `sum(p) > T` inequalities rather than just transcribing the provided arithmetic.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The output is identical because an uninterrupted end-to-end sequence exceeding the window is mathematically equivalent to `p1 + p2 > T`, and an aggregated duration exceeding the window is exactly equivalent to the total sum exceeding `T`.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The complexity class remains linear-time certification, as the required checks are still single-pass summations and comparisons against T.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "aggregated active duration" as needing to account for scheduling gaps, though the phrase "aggregation of all corresponding phases" clarifies it refers strictly to the sum of processing times.
</RISK>
