# Problem C — Impossible Deadline: Certify It

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A specialized facility processes a sequence of commissions through exactly two stages. Every commission is processed in the primary fabrication stage first, and then in the secondary finishing stage. Each stage processes only one commission at a time, and a commission reaches the secondary stage only after its primary stage is complete.

You are given a target timeframe. Every commission must be completely finished by this timeframe. The schedules are extremely tight, and your objective is to provide a short certificate proving that meeting the timeframe is unachievable.

A schedule is doomed if a single commission's processing times across both stages add up to more than the timeframe. That commission alone cannot finish in time. In this case, a single commission is too long.
Alternatively, since a stage runs its commissions one at a time, it needs at least the sum of all its processing times. If the aggregate work on the primary stage (or the secondary stage) exceeds the timeframe, the schedule is impossible. In this case, a stage is overloaded.

Your task is to evaluate the input and print the appropriate certificate of impossibility:
- `JOB i` — where `i` (1-based) is a commission whose two processing times add up to more than the timeframe. If multiple commissions exceed the timeframe, output the one that appears first in the input.
- `LOAD c` — where `c` is 1 or 2, representing a stage whose total processing time exceeds the timeframe. If no single commission is too long, but a stage is overloaded, output this certificate. If both stages are overloaded, output the primary stage (`LOAD 1`).

## Input

The facility's ledger begins with a line containing the number of commissions and the target timeframe. The facility is designed to handle a minimum of a single commission up to a maximum throughput capacity of two hundred thousand commissions. The timeframe is measured in hours, starting from a baseline of one hour up to an absolute operational limit of one billion hours.
Each of the subsequent lines details a commission, providing its required hours in the primary stage and the secondary stage. Every stage requires at least a single hour of work per commission, but a stage's duration for any single commission will never exceed a design threshold of one million hours.

## Output

Print a single line containing the certificate: either `JOB i` (for a commission whose total processing time exceeds the timeframe) or `LOAD c` (for an overloaded stage). Follow the precedence rules described above if multiple certificates could apply.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, commission 1 needs 70 + 60 = 130 > 100 hours in sequence, so `JOB 1` proves the timeframe is impossible. In Example 2 no single commission exceeds 150, but stage 1 must process 50+50+50+50 = 200 > 150 hours of work in total, so `LOAD 1` is the certificate.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I removed the explicit promise that "exactly one of the reasons above always applies" and replaced it with strict precedence rules for tie-breaking. This removes the affordance that multiple certificates will never be valid simultaneously, forcing models to implement or consider the precedence logic rather than greedily returning the first match they find. I also embedded all numeric bounds into the scenario text (strategy S07) rather than using bare inequalities, requiring models to extract limits like "two hundred thousand" and "one billion" from prose.
</RATIONALE>
