<METHOD_NAME>Topological Interval Packing</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces scheduling terminology with the mathematical properties of intervals on the real line. The sequential job constraint becomes a strict precedence relation between elements of two sets, and the machine capacity becomes a disjoint packing of intervals. This forces models to derive the algebraic bounds from geometric/topological definitions instead of relying on familiar scheduling heuristics.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Interval System Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are given an integer **T** and **j** pairs of positive integers (**u_i**, **v_i**) for **i** = 1, ..., **j**. 
Consider the problem of constructing two sequences of intervals in the real numbers, $X_1, \dots, X_j$ and $Y_1, \dots, Y_j$, subject to the following system of constraints:

1. For every **i**, $X_i$ is an interval of length **u_i**, and $Y_i$ is an interval of length **v_i**. (Intervals may be open, closed, or half-open).
2. For every **i**, $\inf(X_i) \ge 1$ and $\inf(Y_i) \ge 1$.
3. For every **i**, and for every $x \in X_i$ and $y \in Y_i$, $x < y$.
4. For any two distinct indices **i** and **k**, $X_i \cap X_k = \emptyset$.
5. For any two distinct indices **i** and **k**, $Y_i \cap Y_k = \emptyset$.
6. For every **i**, $\sup(Y_i) \le T + 1$.

For every given input, it is guaranteed that no such system of intervals can be constructed. You must provide a short certificate proving this impossibility. Exactly one of the following two fundamental contradictions will apply, and you must output the corresponding certificate:

- `JOB i` (where **i** is the 1-based index of the pair): The requirements for a single index **i** are self-contradictory. Specifically, there exists no pair of intervals $(X_i, Y_i)$ that simultaneously satisfies constraints (1), (2), (3), and (6), even if constraints (4) and (5) are completely ignored. If multiple indices fit this description, you may output any one of them.
- `LOAD c` (where **c** is 1 or 2): The requirements for one of the two sequences of intervals are self-contradictory. 
  - If **c** = 1, there exists no sequence of intervals $X_1, \dots, X_j$ that simultaneously satisfies constraints (1), (2), (4), and the condition that $\sup(X_i) \le T + 1$ for all **i**. 
  - If **c** = 2, there exists no sequence of intervals $Y_1, \dots, Y_j$ that simultaneously satisfies constraints (1), (2), (5), and constraint (6).
  In either case, this impossibility holds even if constraint (3) and the other sequence of intervals are completely ignored. If both **c** = 1 and **c** = 2 fit this description, you may output either one.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the following **j** lines contains two integers **u_i** and **v_i** (1 ≤ u_i, v_i ≤ 1000000): the required lengths of the intervals $X_i$ and $Y_i$, respectively.

## Output

Print a single line containing the certificate: either `JOB i` or `LOAD c`.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, **T** = 100. For **i** = 1, any interval $X_1$ of length 70 with $\inf(X_1) \ge 1$ must have $\sup(X_1) \ge 71$. Constraint (3) implies that all elements of $Y_1$ are strictly greater than all elements of $X_1$, meaning $\inf(Y_1) \ge \sup(X_1) \ge 71$. Since $Y_1$ has length 60, $\sup(Y_1) \ge 131$. This contradicts constraint (6) which requires $\sup(Y_1) \le 101$. Thus, `JOB 1` is printed. 

In Example 2, **T** = 150. No single index **i** violates the conditions alone. However, considering the first sequence of intervals and ignoring constraint (3) and the second sequence: we must place 4 disjoint intervals $X_1, \dots, X_4$, each of length 50, such that their infimums are $\ge 1$ and their supremums are $\le 151$. This requires a total length of at least 200 within an available span of 150, which is impossible. Thus, `LOAD 1` is printed.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I completely replaced the domain of scheduling (jobs, facilities, completion times) with an abstract mathematical problem about packing intervals on the real line. The sequential precedence is reframed as a strict inequality between elements of two sets, forcing models to deduce the algebraic bounds from topological definitions rather than relying on surface-level heuristics for job scheduling.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The mathematical thresholds for impossibility remain exactly identical: the strict element-wise inequality between intervals of fixed lengths yields the same additive sum lower bound as sequential job stages, and the disjoint intersection requirement yields the same total length capacity as a machine load.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The complexity class is unchanged because checking the conditions still only requires single-pass linear time arithmetic on lengths and sums, exactly mirroring the original logic.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret whether intervals are allowed to share a boundary point, though the explicit statement 'Intervals may be open, closed, or half-open' is intended to clarify that standard topological packing rules apply.
</RISK>
