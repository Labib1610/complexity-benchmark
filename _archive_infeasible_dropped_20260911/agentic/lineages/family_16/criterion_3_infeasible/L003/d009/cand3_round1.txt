<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces straight sum-based capacity checks with a topological partition argument. The capacities and lengths are reframed as a telescoping sequence of segments (vehicles and gaps) whose true lengths must sum to exactly T. <STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Impossible Ferry Loading

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are the loading master for a cargo ferry. The ferry has two parallel parking lanes, Lane 1 and Lane 2. Each lane has a usable parking length of exactly **T** meters, with coordinates running from $0$ to $T$.

There are **j** transport teams that need to board. Each team $i$ (for $1 \le i \le j$) consists of a primary vehicle of length $p_{i,1}$ meters and a secondary vehicle of length $p_{i,2}$ meters. 

A valid parking configuration must assign a continuous interval to every vehicle such that all of the following physical rules are satisfied:
- **Lane Assignment:** All primary vehicles must be parked in Lane 1. All secondary vehicles must be parked in Lane 2.
- **Bounds:** Every vehicle's interval must be completely contained within the $0$ to $T$ coordinate bounds.
- **Non-overlapping:** Vehicles parked in the same lane cannot overlap (their intervals can at most touch at the endpoints).
- **Team Ordering:** For every team $i$, the primary vehicle must be parked ahead of its secondary vehicle. Specifically, the end coordinate of team $i$'s primary vehicle must be less than or equal to the start coordinate of team $i$'s secondary vehicle.

For every test input, it is guaranteed that no valid parking configuration exists. Your task is to output a short certificate that proves the geometry is contradictory. 

To do this, you must identify the fundamental bottleneck by finding which of the following logical collapses occurs. Exactly one collapse is guaranteed to hold. Print the certificate for the one that applies:

* `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$): The isolated timeline for team $i$ collapses. Consider the logical chain of five geometric segments for team $i$: the empty space in Lane 1 before its primary vehicle, the primary vehicle itself, the logical transit space (the secondary vehicle's start coordinate minus the primary vehicle's end coordinate), the secondary vehicle itself, and the empty space in Lane 2 after the secondary vehicle. Because of the bounds and the team ordering rule, the true lengths of these five sequential segments form a telescoping sum that must equal exactly $T$. Each segment has a rigid minimal allowable length dictated by the vehicle dimensions and the physical rules. If the sum of these five minimal allowable lengths strictly exceeds $T$, output `JOB i`.

* `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2): The spatial capacity of Lane $c$ is over-subscribed. Consider any hypothetical left-to-right sequence of all $j$ vehicles assigned to Lane $c$. The complete span from coordinate 0 to coordinate $T$ is partitioned into exactly $2j+1$ quantities: the $j$ vehicle intervals, and the $j+1$ interstitial empty gaps (which includes the boundary gaps at the absolute start and end of the lane). Because they partition the lane, their true lengths must sum to exactly $T$. Each of these $2j+1$ components has a rigid minimal allowable length dictated by the vehicle dimensions and the non-overlapping rules. If the sum of the minimal allowable lengths of all $2j+1$ components strictly exceeds $T$, output `LOAD c`.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000), representing $p_{i,1}$ and $p_{i,2}$ respectively for team $i$.

## Output

Print a single line containing either `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$) or `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2), corresponding to the unique logical collapse that occurs.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, `JOB 1` is the correct certificate. For team 1, the logical chain of five segments consists of: the start gap (minimal length 0), the primary vehicle (minimal length 70), the transit gap between primary and secondary (minimal length 0), the secondary vehicle (minimal length 60), and the end gap (minimal length 0). The sum of their minimal allowable lengths is 130. Since 130 strictly exceeds the available coordinate span of 100, this timeline collapses.

In Example 2, `LOAD 1` is the correct certificate. For Lane 1, the 4 primary vehicles and 5 interstitial gaps form 9 components that partition the lane. The vehicle components have minimal lengths of 50, 50, 50, and 50, while the 5 gaps have minimal lengths of 0. The sum of these minimal lengths is 200. Since 200 strictly exceeds the lane's 150-meter capacity, Lane 1 is over-subscribed.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit checks for `p1 + p2 > T` and `sum(p) > T` have been removed entirely. Instead, the impossibility conditions are framed as a topological partition of the coordinate space where the sum of the absolute minimal allowable lengths of the segments strictly exceeds T. To solve this, models can no longer just scan for "overlap" rules and translate them to sum inequalities; they must algebraically deduce that the minimal lengths of the gaps are 0 and the minimal lengths of the vehicles are p, reconstructing the same mathematical checks from first principles.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The segment sums mathematically reduce to exactly `p1 + p2 > T` for JOB i and `sum(p_c) > T` for LOAD c, which are the exact same conditions evaluated in the original seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The required algorithms to determine the valid certificate remain O(N) single-pass summations and comparisons, keeping the solution fully within the original linear time complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
