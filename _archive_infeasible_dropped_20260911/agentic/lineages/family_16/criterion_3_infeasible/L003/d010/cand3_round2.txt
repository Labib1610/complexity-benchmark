<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Impossible Ferry Loading

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are the loading master for a cargo ferry. The ferry has exactly two parallel parking lanes, Lane 1 and Lane 2. Each lane has a usable parking length of exactly **T** meters, with coordinates running continuously from $0$ to $T$.

There are **j** transport teams that need to board. Each team $i$ (for $1 \le i \le j$) consists of a primary vehicle of length $p_{i,1}$ meters and a secondary vehicle of length $p_{i,2}$ meters. 

A valid parking configuration must assign a single real-valued center-of-mass coordinate to every vehicle. Let $y_{i,1}$ be the center coordinate for the primary vehicle of team $i$, and $y_{i,2}$ be the center coordinate for the secondary vehicle of team $i$. All assignments must satisfy the following physical constraints:

1. **Length Representation:** Each vehicle is modeled by its center coordinate. The primary vehicle of team $i$ has a fixed length $p_{i,1}$, and the secondary vehicle has a fixed length $p_{i,2}$.
2. **Assignment:** All primary vehicles are assigned to Lane 1. All secondary vehicles are assigned to Lane 2.
3. **Bounds:** The entire body of every vehicle must be completely contained within the $0$ to $T$ coordinate bounds. This requires the center coordinate to satisfy $y_{i,c} \ge \frac{p_{i,c}}{2}$ and $y_{i,c} \le T - \frac{p_{i,c}}{2}$ for its respective lane $c$.
4. **Non-overlapping:** Vehicles assigned to the same lane cannot overlap. For any two distinct teams $i$ and $k$, their vehicles in lane $c$ must satisfy an absolute center distance of $|y_{i,c} - y_{k,c}| \ge \frac{p_{i,c} + p_{k,c}}{2}$.
5. **Team Ordering:** For every team $i$, its primary vehicle must be parked completely ahead of its secondary vehicle. This requires the directed distance between their centers to satisfy $y_{i,2} - y_{i,1} \ge \frac{p_{i,1} + p_{i,2}}{2}$.

For every test input, it is guaranteed that no valid parking configuration exists. Your task is to output a short certificate that proves the rules are contradictory. 

The test cases are constructed such that the impossibility always stems from exactly one of two fundamental bottlenecks. Print the applicable certificate:

* `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$): The subset of Rules 1, 2, 3, and 5 enforced exclusively on the vehicles of team $i$ has no valid mathematical solution.
* `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2): The subset of Rules 1, 2, 3, and 4 enforced exclusively on the vehicles assigned to Lane $c$ has no valid mathematical solution.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000), representing $p_{i,1}$ and $p_{i,2}$ respectively for team $i$.

## Output

Print a single line containing either `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$) or `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2), corresponding to the unique bottleneck that applies.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, `JOB 1` is the correct certificate. If we only enforce Rules 1, 2, 3, and 5 for team 1, their center coordinates must satisfy $y_{1,1} \ge 35$, $y_{1,2} \le 70$, and $y_{1,2} - y_{1,1} \ge 65$. This requires $y_{1,2} \ge 100$, which contradicts $y_{1,2} \le 70$, making it mathematically impossible.

In Example 2, `LOAD 1` is the correct certificate. If we only enforce Rules 1, 2, 3, and 4 for Lane 1, we must place four center coordinates $y_{1,1}, y_{2,1}, y_{3,1}, y_{4,1}$ in the domain $[25, 125]$ such that any two centers are separated by an absolute distance of at least $50$. This would require a domain of length at least $150$, but we only have $100$ units of space between the bounds, which is impossible.

<RATIONALE>
The problem was completely reformulated mathematically using interval midpoints instead of endpoints. This preserves the exact logical and geometric truth of the original 1D packing problem while replacing the simple `p1+p2>T` and `sum>T` conditions with fractional bounds and absolute differences. Models relying on surface pattern matching will fail to recognize the standard scheduling constraints, forcing them to algebraically deduce the contradiction from scratch.
</RATIONALE>

The midpoint inequalities are mathematically isomorphic to the original interval constraints, meaning the exact same configurations are impossible for the exact same localized reasons.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

Checking the algebraic contradiction for a single team still takes O(1) time, and checking it for a lane still takes O(N) time, preserving the O(N) linear scan complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
