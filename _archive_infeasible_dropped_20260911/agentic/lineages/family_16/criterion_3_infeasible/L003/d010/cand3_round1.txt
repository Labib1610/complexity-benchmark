# Problem C — Impossible Ferry Loading

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are the loading master for a cargo ferry. The ferry has two parallel parking lanes, Lane 1 and Lane 2. Each lane represents a one-dimensional space with coordinates running from $0$ to $T$.

There are **j** transport teams that need to board. Each team $i$ (for $1 \le i \le j$) consists of a primary vehicle of length $p_{i,1}$ meters and a secondary vehicle of length $p_{i,2}$ meters. 

A valid parking configuration must assign a footprint (a single closed interval of real numbers) to every vehicle, satisfying all of the following rules:
1. **Span:** The length of a vehicle's footprint (its maximum coordinate minus its minimum coordinate) exactly equals its given length.
2. **Assignment:** All primary vehicles are assigned to Lane 1. All secondary vehicles are assigned to Lane 2.
3. **Containment:** Every footprint must be a subset of the interval $[0, T]$.
4. **Independence:** For each lane, the geometric length of the union of all footprints in that lane must exactly equal the sum of their individual lengths.
5. **Progression:** For every team $i$, there must exist a real-valued threshold coordinate $X_i$ such that the primary vehicle's footprint is a subset of $[0, X_i]$, and the secondary vehicle's footprint is a subset of $[X_i, T]$.

For every test input, it is guaranteed that no valid parking configuration exists. Your task is to output a short certificate that proves the rules are contradictory. 

To do this, you must identify the fundamental bottleneck by finding a specific subset of these rules that is already impossible to satisfy on its own. Exactly one of the following isolated rule subsets is guaranteed to be contradictory (even when all other rules and vehicles are ignored). Print the certificate for the unique subset that fails:

* `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$): The subset consisting only of Span, Assignment, Containment, and Progression, enforced exclusively on the vehicles of team $i$. (All other rules, and all other vehicles, are ignored).
* `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2): The subset consisting only of Span, Assignment, Containment, and Independence, enforced exclusively on the vehicles assigned to Lane $c$. (All other rules, and all vehicles assigned to the other lane, are ignored).

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ (1 ≤ u, v ≤ 1000000), representing $p_{i,1}$ and $p_{i,2}$ respectively for team $i$.

## Output

Print a single line containing either `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$) or `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2), corresponding to the unique rule subset that remains impossible.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, `JOB 1` is the correct certificate. If we only enforce Span, Assignment, Containment, and Progression for team 1, there must exist a threshold $X_1$ to divide the 100-meter interval into a left section for the 70-meter primary footprint and a right section for the 60-meter secondary footprint. This is impossible given their lengths.

In Example 2, `LOAD 1` is the correct certificate. If we only enforce Span, Assignment, Containment, and Independence for Lane 1, the four primary vehicles (each 50 meters) must have footprints that are subsets of $[0, 150]$. By Independence, the geometric length of their union must be exactly 200, but the entire available interval is only 150 meters long. This is contradictory.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit inequalities for "non-overlapping" and "parked ahead of" were replaced with set-theoretic constraints: measure-independence of unions, and subset containment bounded by a threshold coordinate. This removes the surface patterns `p1 + p2 > T` and `sum > T` from the text, forcing the model to mathematically deduce that these geometric properties imply exactly the same additive bounds.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The Progression rule mathematically forces $p_{i,1} + p_{i,2} \le T$, and the Independence rule mathematically forces the sum of lengths in a lane to not exceed $T$, making the contradictory subsets identical to the seed problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
