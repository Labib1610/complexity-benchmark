<METHOD_NAME>Center-Coordinate Formulation</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces interval endpoints and lengths with a center-coordinate representation. The non-overlapping and bounded-domain rules are rewritten as distance constraints between centers and boundaries. This forces the model to algebraically derive the total length constraints (e.g. `p1+p2 > T` or `sum > T`) rather than pattern-matching for them in the text.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Impossible Ferry Loading

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are the loading master for a cargo ferry. The ferry has two parallel parking lanes, Lane 1 and Lane 2. Each lane has a usable parking length of exactly **T** meters, with coordinates running from $0$ to $T$.

There are **j** transport teams that need to board. Each team $i$ (for $1 \le i \le j$) consists of a primary vehicle of length $p_{i,1}$ meters and a secondary vehicle of length $p_{i,2}$ meters. 

A valid parking configuration must assign a real-valued center coordinate $x$ to every vehicle, satisfying all of the following physical rules:
1. **Span:** A vehicle of length $L$ assigned to center coordinate $x$ occupies the continuous interval $[x - L/2, x + L/2]$.
2. **Assignment:** All primary vehicles are assigned to Lane 1. All secondary vehicles are assigned to Lane 2.
3. **Bounds:** Every vehicle's span must be completely contained within the $0$ to $T$ coordinate bounds. This requires $x - L/2 \ge 0$ and $x + L/2 \le T$.
4. **Spacing:** Vehicles assigned to the same lane cannot overlap in their interiors. For any two vehicles in the same lane with centers $x_A$ and $x_B$ and lengths $L_A$ and $L_B$, the distance between their centers $|x_A - x_B|$ must be at least $(L_A + L_B)/2$.
5. **Team Ordering:** For every team $i$, its primary vehicle must be parked ahead of its secondary vehicle. Specifically, the secondary vehicle's center $x_2$ and the primary vehicle's center $x_1$ must satisfy $x_2 - x_1 \ge (p_{i,1} + p_{i,2})/2$.

For every test input, it is guaranteed that no valid parking configuration exists. Your task is to output a short certificate that proves the rules are contradictory. 

To do this, you must identify a specific subset of these rules that is already impossible to satisfy on its own. You must print a certificate for an isolated subset that fails:

* `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$): The subset consisting only of Rules 1, 2, 3, and 5, enforced exclusively on the vehicles of team $i$. (All other rules, and all other vehicles, are ignored). This certificate is valid if and only if no valid center coordinates can be chosen for team $i$'s vehicles to satisfy this subset of rules.
* `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2): The subset consisting only of Rules 1, 2, 3, and 4, enforced exclusively on the vehicles assigned to Lane $c$. (All other rules, and all vehicles assigned to the other lane, are ignored). This certificate is valid if and only if no valid center coordinates can be chosen for the vehicles in Lane $c$ to satisfy this subset of rules.

It is guaranteed that at least one such contradictory subset exists. If multiple valid $i$ or $c$ exist, any one is accepted.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000), representing $p_{i,1}$ and $p_{i,2}$ respectively for team $i$.

## Output

Print a single line containing either `JOB i` (where **i** is a 1-based index, $1 \le i \le j$) or `LOAD c` (where **c** is either 1 or 2), corresponding to a valid certificate as defined above.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, `JOB 1` is a valid certificate. If we only enforce the Span, Assignment, Bounds, and Team Ordering rules for team 1, their primary and secondary vehicles (lengths 70 and 60) must be assigned centers $x_1$ and $x_2$. The Bounds rule requires $x_1 - 35 \ge 0$ (so $x_1 \ge 35$) and $x_2 + 30 \le 100$ (so $x_2 \le 70$). Thus, the maximum possible difference $x_2 - x_1$ is $70 - 35 = 35$. However, the Team Ordering rule requires $x_2 - x_1 \ge (70 + 60)/2 = 65$. This is contradictory.

In Example 2, `LOAD 1` is a valid certificate. If we only enforce the Span, Assignment, Bounds, and Spacing rules for Lane 1, all four primary vehicles (each of length 50) must be assigned centers in Lane 1 such that the distance between any two centers is at least 50, and each center $x$ satisfies $x - 25 \ge 0$ and $x + 25 \le 150$. This requires packing four non-overlapping spans of length 50 into a bound of size 150, which is impossible.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The interval representation has been completely replaced with a center-coordinate representation, transforming the bounding constraints into algebraic distance inequalities. This eliminates the explicit `p1+p2 > T` and `sum > T` shortcuts from the text, forcing the model to mathematically deduce that the center-distance constraints are strictly equivalent to the aggregate length constraints.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The center-coordinate constraints are mathematically identical to the original non-overlapping interval constraints, meaning the sets of valid inputs and the conditions for rule subset contradictions (`JOB i` iff $p_{i,1}+p_{i,2}>T$, `LOAD c` iff $\sum_{Lane c} p > T$) remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires checking individual pairs and total lane sums against $T$, keeping it in the same linear-time feasibility-checking complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>

Models might struggle with the algebraic deduction required to prove the constraints are equivalent to sums, but the wording itself is rigorously unambiguous and mathematically precise.
