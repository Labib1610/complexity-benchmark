<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Impossible Geometry: Certify It

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A geometric configuration is built within an ambient real vector space $E$ of dimension **T**. The configuration consists of **j** independent pairs of linear subspaces. For each pair $i$, the configuration specifies two dimensions:
- $V_i$ must be a subspace of dimension **p1**.
- $W_i$ must be a subspace of dimension **p2**.

A valid configuration must be able to satisfy three independent structural properties:
1. **Transversality:** For any single pair $i$, it must be possible to choose $V_i$ and $W_i$ as subspaces of $E$ such that they intersect trivially (their intersection contains only the zero vector).
2. **Primary Independence:** It must be possible to choose all **j** subspaces $V_1, \dots, V_j$ within $E$ such that their sum is a direct sum (equivalently, the intersection of any $V_x$ with the span of the other $j-1$ primary subspaces is the zero vector).
3. **Secondary Independence:** It must be possible to choose all **j** subspaces $W_1, \dots, W_j$ within $E$ such that their sum is a direct sum.

Every provided configuration represents an impossible geometry—there is no valid way to choose these subspaces within $E$ of dimension **T** that satisfies all properties. Instead of attempting to construct them, your task is to identify the bottleneck that proves the configuration is unachievable, providing a short certificate the system can verify.

Exactly one of the following foundational requirements is broken. Output the applicable certificate:

- **Transversality Failure:** For some pair $i$, every subspace of dimension **p1** and every subspace of dimension **p2** in $E$ will always share at least one non-zero vector. Output `JOB i` (where $i$ is the 1-based index of the pair).
- **Primary Collision:** There do not exist **j** subspaces in $E$ of dimensions corresponding to the **p1** values that form a direct sum. Output `LOAD 1`.
- **Secondary Collision:** There do not exist **j** subspaces in $E$ of dimensions corresponding to the **p2** values that form a direct sum. Output `LOAD 2`.

You are guaranteed that exactly one of these requirements will be broken for the given input.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **j** and **T** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1000000000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the required dimensions for the primary subspace $V_i$ and the secondary subspace $W_i$ of a pair.

## Output

Print a single line: `JOB i` (for a transversality bottleneck) or `LOAD c` (for a collision bottleneck, where **c** is 1 for primary collision or 2 for secondary collision).

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3 100
70 60
2 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
JOB 1
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 150
50 1
50 1
50 1
50 1
```

**Output**
```
LOAD 1
```

## Note

In Example 1, for pair 1, the primary and secondary subspaces require dimensions of 70 and 60 respectively. In an ambient space of dimension 100, any subspace of dimension 70 and any subspace of dimension 60 must intersect in a subspace of dimension at least 30, meaning they will always share a non-zero vector. This breaks the Transversality property. Thus `JOB 1` is the correct proof of impossibility. 

In Example 2, no single pair breaks Transversality. However, the primary subspaces require dimensions of 50, 50, 50, and 50. There do not exist 4 subspaces of these dimensions in a 150-dimensional space that form a direct sum, because their required combined dimension would be 200, which exceeds 150. This breaks the Primary Independence property. Thus `LOAD 1` is the correct output.

<RATIONALE>
I changed the problem's domain from sets and injective functions to linear algebra and vector subspaces. The disjointness and causality conditions on sets were replaced with their exact linear algebra analogues: trivial intersections and direct sums. This forces the model to realize that the existence of such subspaces depends purely on dimension counting (via Grassmann's formula), preventing them from simply pattern-matching set cardinality bounds.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition for two subspaces of dimensions p1 and p2 to have a trivial intersection in an ambient space of dimension T is exactly p1 + p2 <= T, and the condition for a set of subspaces to form a direct sum is that the sum of their dimensions is <= T, making the failure conditions mathematically equivalent to the original.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The solution still requires a single linear scan over the j pairs to check if p1+p2 > T, sum(p1) > T, or sum(p2) > T, keeping it in the same O(N) complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might be confused by the continuous nature of real vector spaces, though standard linear algebra definitions make the dimension bounds unambiguous.
</RISK>
