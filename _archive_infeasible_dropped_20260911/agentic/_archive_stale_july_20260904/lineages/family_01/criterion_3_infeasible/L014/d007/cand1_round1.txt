<METHOD_NAME>Boolean De Morgan Obfuscation</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the structural bipartite/2-hold condition with a logically equivalent boolean compliance check based on the non-existence of safe pairings. By hiding the "triangle of prohibited edges" behind the negation of a disjunction of safe edges, surface-level pattern matchers fail to extract the graph structure and instead implement an unoptimized O(N^3) simulation.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Cargo Prohibition Protocol

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A logistics firm operates a shipping route for hazardous materials. Certain pairs of these materials are highly reactive and are strictly prohibited from sharing a vessel.

The proposed loading schedule is structurally impossible to fulfill. You must produce a definitive proof of this impossibility to trigger an automatic schedule rejection.

To establish this proof, you are required to isolate a "deadlock" configuration consisting of exactly three distinct materials. A configuration of three materials is deemed a deadlock if and only if it fails the internal compatibility audit.

During this audit, the three materials are evaluated to see if any two of them can safely share a vessel (meaning they are not explicitly listed as a prohibited pair). The audit passes if at least one such safe pairing exists among the three. If the audit discovers that zero safe pairings can be formed strictly between the materials within the configuration, the audit fails, triggering a deadlock.

Because the automated auditing system requires deterministic reporting, it will not accept an arbitrary deadlock configuration. The system demands the canonical proof, meaning you must systematically ensure no earlier configuration could have been reported.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), indicating the number of hazardous materials and the number of prohibited pairs. The materials are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.
Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$) — a pair of materials that are prohibited from sharing a vessel. 
It is guaranteed that the input contains at least one deadlock configuration of three materials.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ ($a < b < c$), representing the identifiers of the materials in the deadlock configuration.
To satisfy the deterministic auditing system, if more than one such configuration exists, you must output the lexicographically earliest one among all valid configurations. This means you must minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if still tied, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The previous wording ("impossible to assign each of them to either Hold A or Hold B") acted as a direct keyword trigger for bipartite graph and 2-coloring templates, allowing models to immediately deduce they were looking for an odd cycle (a triangle). This rewrite removes the 2-hold framing entirely, replacing it with a logically equivalent boolean condition (zero safe pairings out of the three possible). This forces the model to actually analyze the condition to realize it represents a clique of three prohibited edges, causing shallow pattern matchers to fall back to an unoptimized $O(N^3)$ simulation that will exceed the time limit.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition "zero safe pairings can be formed strictly between the materials within the configuration" means all three possible pairs among the three materials are prohibited, which is logically identical to the original bipartite impossibility condition (since 3 items in 2 holds forces at least one pair to share a hold).
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly the detection of a triangle (K3) in the graph of prohibited pairs, preserving the $O(NM/64)$ bitset complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
