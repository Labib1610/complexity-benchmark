# Problem C — Flight Manifest Rejection

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A cargo company relies on two transport planes to ship various industrial chemicals. Many of these chemicals are volatile when mixed, meaning certain pairs will react dangerously and must be transported on different planes.

The current shipping manifest is fundamentally flawed: it is impossible to divide all the chemicals between just two planes without risking a reaction. Your task is to generate an automated safety rejection report. You do not need to attempt a safe allocation; you only need to prove that no such allocation exists.

To provide this proof, you must identify a subset of exactly three chemicals such that all three possible pairs among them are listed as dangerously reactive in the manifest. Because every pair within this trio is reactive, they inherently cannot be safely distributed across two planes.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The inventory size $n$ ranges from a minimum of $3$ up to the warehouse capacity of $2000$. The log size $m$ is at least $3$ and is capped by the system's hard limit of $\min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$. Chemicals are numbered from $1$ to $n$.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$) — an unordered pair of chemicals that react dangerously with each other. Each reactive pair is listed at most once.
It is guaranteed that at least one such triplet of chemicals exists in the given data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line with $a < b < c$, representing the IDs of the three chemicals that cannot be safely divided.
If there are multiple such groups, output the one that is lexicographically smallest (i.e., smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The description of the impossibility proof was changed from a 2-coloring contradiction ("every possible way of assigning these three... forces at least one listed reactive pair to share a plane") to a direct structural requirement ("all three possible pairs among them are listed"). This forces models to parse the explicit requirement rather than pattern-matching the bipartite-impossibility framing to a graph triangle. The bounds were also rewritten to use narrative constraints (warehouse capacity, system limits) as per S07, while retaining the exact mathematical expressions to prevent contract drift.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The required output remains the lexicographically smallest triplet of mutually reactive chemicals, which is mathematically identical to finding the lexicographically smallest triangle in the original graph.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
