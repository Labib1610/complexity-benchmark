<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
Redefines the triangle (clique of size 3) not as a sequence of connected vertices ("a primary pair and a third material connected to both"), but as a subset of 3 elements where no two elements are permitted to be together (i.e., every pair within the subset must be in the prohibited list). This removes the sequential graph-traversal affordance and forces the model to synthesize the subgraph condition globally over the subset.
</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

# Problem C — Cargo Prohibition Protocol

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A logistics firm operates a shipping route for hazardous materials. Certain pairs of these materials are highly reactive and are strictly prohibited from sharing a vessel.

The proposed loading schedule is structurally impossible to fulfill. You must produce a definitive proof of this impossibility to trigger an automatic schedule rejection.

To establish this proof, you are required to isolate a "deadlock" configuration. A configuration of exactly three distinct materials is deemed a deadlock if it is impossible to choose any two materials from the configuration that are allowed to share a vessel. In other words, any two materials selected from the three must appear together in the explicit list of prohibited pairs.

Because the automated auditing system requires deterministic reporting, it will not accept an arbitrary deadlock configuration. The system demands the canonical proof, meaning you must systematically ensure no earlier configuration could have been reported.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$. The integer $n$ indicates the number of hazardous materials; this value is at least the minimum strictly necessary to form a deadlock, and is bounded above by the two thousand slots available in the testing registry. The integer $m$ represents the total number of prohibited pairs. This value is at least three, and will never exceed two hundred thousand or the theoretical maximum number of unique pairs among the $n$ materials, whichever is smaller. 

The materials are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.
Each of the following $m$ lines contains two distinct integers $u$ and $v$, representing a pair of materials (identified by their valid registry numbers between $1$ and $n$) that are prohibited from sharing a vessel. 

It is guaranteed that the input contains at least one deadlock configuration of three materials.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, sorted in strictly increasing order, representing the identifiers of the materials in the deadlock configuration.
To satisfy the deterministic auditing system, if more than one such configuration exists, you must output the lexicographically earliest one among all valid configurations. This means you must minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if still tied, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The definition of the deadlock was changed from a sequential graph-traversal description ("a primary pair... alongside a third material") to a global property of a 3-element subset ("impossible to choose any two materials... that are allowed to share a vessel"). Numeric bounds were also replaced with descriptive physical limits according to Strategy S07. This forces the model to holistically deduce that the required structure is a 3-clique, rather than relying on memorized pattern-matching for triangle finding.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that any two materials from a set of three must be prohibited from sharing a vessel is logically identical to finding three mutually prohibited materials (a triangle in the graph), so the valid configurations and lexicographical tie-breaking remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires identifying a 3-clique in an undirected graph, which maintains the same polynomial-time complexity for fixed subgraph sizes.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "theoretical maximum number of unique pairs among the n materials" if they fail to recognize it as n(n-1)/2, though this is a standard combinatorial definition and should not cause genuine ambiguity for a competent reader.
</RISK>
