# Problem C — Mechanical Jam

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

An analog computing engine is constructed from a series of gears. The blueprint dictates specific contact points where the teeth of two distinct gears interlock. By mechanical law, any two interlocking gears must rotate in strictly opposing directions.

The prototype of this engine was expected to run continuously, but it has completely seized upon its first test. An audit of the design reveals a critical structural flaw: there is at least one triplet of gears in which every gear directly interlocks with the other two. When this happens, motion is entirely blocked because each gear is simultaneously driven to turn in both clockwise and counterclockwise directions by its two interlocking neighbors.

To authorize a structural revision, the chief architect needs a formal defect ticket that singles out one such paralyzed arrangement. Your objective is to locate a trio of gears where any pair chosen from the three features a direct contact point.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$.
The number of gears $n$ is bounded below by the minimum number of gears required to form the described flaw. It is bounded above by the capacity of the mounting plate, which consists of exactly 40 rows and 50 columns of gear pegs, where each peg holds at most one gear.
The number of contact points $m$ is at least 3. It cannot exceed the maximum number of entries in the testing log, which contains exactly 800 pages with exactly 250 entry slots per page. Furthermore, $m$ cannot exceed the maximum possible number of unique contact points among the $n$ gears.

The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ (ranging from 1 to $n$, with $u \ne v$), indicating that gear $u$ and gear $v$ interlock. Each contact point is logged at most once. It is guaranteed that at least one paralyzed trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the IDs of three gears that form a paralyzed arrangement. The IDs must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$).
If there are multiple such arrangements, you must output the one whose sequence of IDs is lexicographically smallest. That is, minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
