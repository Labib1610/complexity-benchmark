# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing plant employs a number of individuals, identified by integers starting from 1 up to the total workforce size. The HR department maintains a registry of specific two-person combinations whose working styles severely clash. If two individuals' working styles clash, they cannot be assigned to the same daily shift.

Management initially proposed dividing the workforce into exactly two daily shifts. However, this proposal is fundamentally unworkable. Because there exists at least one trio of employees where any two members of the trio clash with each other, a two-shift system is guaranteed to fail.

To finalize the rejection of the two-shift proposal, you must provide a formal certificate of its failure. Your task is to identify one such trio of individuals.

## Input
The first line contains two integers representing the total workforce size and the total number of clash records. The workforce size is a minimum of 3, and is bounded by the plant's maximum legal occupancy of 2000 individuals. The number of clash records is a minimum of 3, and will never exceed 200,000 (the HR database system's hard limit for such records) or the total number of possible two-person combinations, whichever is smaller.

The subsequent lines each contain two integers, corresponding to the valid employee badges (ranging from 1 up to the total workforce size) of two distinct individuals whose working styles clash. Each clashing combination is listed at most once. It is guaranteed that at least one trio of employees, where any two members clash with each other, exists.

## Output
Print three integers on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the identifiers of the three individuals who form the trio. The identifiers must be printed in ascending order.
If there are multiple such trios, output the one that is lexicographically smallest. That is, minimize the first identifier; if there is a tie, minimize the second identifier; and if there is still a tie, minimize the third identifier.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
