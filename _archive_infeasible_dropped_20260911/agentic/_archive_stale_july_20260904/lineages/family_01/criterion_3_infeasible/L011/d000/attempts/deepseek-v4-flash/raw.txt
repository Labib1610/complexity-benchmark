 We need to find a triangle (three mutually connected nodes) in an undirected graph, and output the lexicographically smallest triple.

A direct brute force over all triples would be O(n³), which is too slow for n up to 2000.  
We can instead iterate over edges and check for a common neighbor, but we need to ensure we get the lexicographically smallest triple.

A simple approach:  
- Sort adjacency lists for each node.  
- Iterate over all edges (u, v) with u < v.  
- For each such edge, find the smallest common neighbor w > v (to avoid duplicates and ensure ordering).  
- If found, that gives a triangle (u, v, w). Since we iterate edges in sorted order and pick the smallest w, the first found triangle will be lexicographically smallest.

We can use sets for fast neighbor lookup.

Here’s the implementation:

```python
n, m = map(int, input().split())
adj = [set() for _ in range(n)]
edges = []

for _ in range(m):
    u, v = map(int, input().split())
    u -= 1
    v -= 1
    if u > v:
        u, v = v, u
    edges.append((u, v))
    adj[u].add(v)
    adj[v].add(u)

edges.sort()

for u, v in edges:
    # find smallest w > v that is neighbor of both u and v
    for w in sorted(adj[v]):
        if w > v and w in adj[u]:
            print(u + 1, v + 1, w + 1)
            exit()
```
