I applied a dense non-singular change of basis $P$ combined with a new diagonal scaling $D$ to completely scramble the coefficients. # Critical Interference

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A new communications protocol is being tested on a network of $n$ transceivers, which are uniquely identified by integers from 1 to $n$. During diagnostic evaluations, technicians discovered $m$ bidirectional interference anomalies. Each anomaly occurs between a specific pair of distinct transceivers, and no pair experiences more than one such anomaly.

To isolate the structural vulnerability in the network, the engineering team evaluates the *resonance instability* of groups of exactly three transceivers. When a triplet is selected, its members are sorted by their identifiers such that $u_1 < u_2 < u_3$.
The triplet is assigned three continuous tuning parameters, $x_1, x_2,$ and $x_3$.

Let $I_{1,2} = 1$ if an interference anomaly exists between $u_1$ and $u_2$, and $0$ otherwise.
Let $I_{2,3} = 1$ if an interference anomaly exists between $u_2$ and $u_3$, and $0$ otherwise.
Let $I_{1,3} = 1$ if an interference anomaly exists between $u_1$ and $u_3$, and $0$ otherwise.

We define $S = I_{1,2} + I_{2,3} + I_{1,3}$ as the total number of internal anomalies within the triplet.

The resonance instability of the triplet under these parameters is calculated using the following polynomial:
$$E(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (4 - 2I_{1,2}) x_1^2 + (16 - 8I_{1,3}) x_2^2 + (36 - 18I_{2,3}) x_3^2 + 4(2 - S) x_1 x_2 + 12(2 - S) x_2 x_3 + 6(2 - S) x_1 x_3$$

Your task is to identify a *critically unstable* triplet. A triplet is classified as critically unstable if there is at least one combination of real tuning parameters $x_1, x_2, x_3$ (where at least one parameter is non-zero) that results in a resonance instability of exactly $0$. 

## Input
The first line provides $n$, the number of transceivers, and $m$, the number of interference anomalies ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The subsequent $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating a pair of transceivers affected by a mutual interference anomaly.
It is guaranteed that the network contains at least one critically unstable triplet.

## Output
Output a single line containing the three identifiers $a$, $b$, and $c$ (with $a < b < c$) of the critically unstable triplet.
If multiple such triplets exist, output the one that minimizes $a$. If there is still a tie, minimize $b$, and finally minimize $c$.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The previous quadratic form was a simple diagonal scaling of the graph Laplacian, allowing models to easily factor the determinant or recognize the structure. I applied a dense, non-singular change of basis matrix to the Laplacian, followed by a new diagonal scaling. This completely scrambles the coefficients so they now depend on the total edge count $S$, hiding the fact that the matrix is positive semi-definite only when all three edges are present. Models must now either execute a rigorous symbolic determinant analysis over all 8 boolean states or fail to deduce the triangle condition.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The new quadratic form is $x^T (D P^T L P D) x$ where $L$ is the original Laplacian matrix, $P$ is a dense invertible matrix, and $D$ is an invertible diagonal scaling matrix; since Sylvester's Law of Inertia preserves the signature, the form has a non-trivial zero if and only if $L$ has a zero eigenvalue, which occurs strictly when the triplet forms a triangle.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying mathematical condition still strictly identifies 3-cliques (triangles) in the graph, keeping the problem solvable by the identical $O(n m / 64)$ bitset intersection algorithm.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
