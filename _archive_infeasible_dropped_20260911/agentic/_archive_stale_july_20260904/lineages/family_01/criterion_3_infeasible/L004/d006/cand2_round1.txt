<STATEMENT>
# Critical Interference

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A proposed communications standard attempts to allocate $n$ transceivers (labeled 1 through $n$) into a network. However, testing has revealed $m$ specific bidirectional interference links between distinct pairs of transceivers. 

To diagnose the core issue in the network's topology, engineers analyze the interference signatures of transceiver triplets. Your objective is to isolate a subset of exactly three transceivers that exhibit a *critical resonance loop*. 

A subset of three transceivers has this property if and only if it is possible to assign a non-zero real number (a "tuning value") to each of the three transceivers, such that for every transceiver in the subset, the sum of the tuning values of its interfering partners *within that same subset* is exactly twice its own tuning value. (If a transceiver has no interfering partners in the subset, this sum is considered to be 0). The existence of such a subset proves a critical flaw in the network. It is guaranteed that at least one such subset is present.

## Input
The first line contains the transceiver count $n$ and the interference link count $m$. The transceiver count $n$ is at least 3, and at most the number of millimeters in two meters. The number of tested interference links $m$ is at least 3, and is capped by either the maximum possible number of distinct pairs of transceivers, or the number of centimeters in two kilometers, whichever is smaller.
The following $m$ lines each detail a pair of transceivers $u$ and $v$ that share a mutual interference link.
At least one subset of three transceivers satisfying the resonance condition is guaranteed to exist.

## Output
Provide the labels of the three transceivers forming the subset, printed on a single line in ascending numerical order.
If multiple such triplets exist, return the one whose smallest transceiver label is as small as possible. If there is still a tie, minimize the second-smallest label, and finally the third.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The problem was previously solved by models recognizing that a 3x3 adjacency matrix with a determinant of 2 corresponds to a triangle (3-clique). To defeat this shortcut, I reformulated the condition into an equivalent eigenvalue problem (Mw = 2w), presented purely as an algebraic system of constraints on "tuning values". Only a fully connected triplet (triangle) allows a solution where all three weights are non-zero. Additionally, per strategy S07, the numeric bounds for $n$ and $m$ were replaced with derived physical equivalents (millimeters in two meters, centimeters in two kilometers).
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The algebraic condition is mathematically identical to finding a 3-clique, as a 3-vertex subgraph has an eigenvalue of 2 with non-zero eigenvector components if and only if it is a complete graph; thus the output for any input remains exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying task remains finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, preserving the O(n*m/64) complexity class and intended bitset intersection algorithm.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
A model might misinterpret "assign a non-zero real number... to each" as meaning they must all be assigned the *same* non-zero real number, though this misinterpretation harmlessly leads to the exact same correct structural requirement (a triangle).
</RISK>
