# Critical Interference

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A proposed communications standard attempts to allocate $n$ transceivers (labeled 1 through $n$) into a network. However, testing has revealed $m$ specific bidirectional interference links between pairs of transceivers. Each link connects two distinct transceivers, and no two links connect the same pair.

To diagnose the core issue in the network's topology, engineers analyze the *cross-talk isolation matrix* of transceiver triplets. For any chosen subset of exactly three transceivers, we order their labels as $u_1 < u_2 < u_3$. 
We then construct a $3 \times 3$ matrix $Z$, where the entry $Z_{i,j}$ (for $1 \le i, j \le 3$) represents the isolated load between $u_i$ and $u_j$:
- $Z_{i,j} = 0$ if testing has revealed an interference link between $u_i$ and $u_j$.
- $Z_{i,j} = u_i \cdot u_j$ if there is no interference link between them.

By definition, a transceiver's self-load is its own label, so $Z_{i,i} = u_i$ for all $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.

The *stability characteristic* of the triplet is defined as the determinant of the matrix $Z$.

Your objective is to isolate a subset of exactly three transceivers whose stability characteristic is exactly equal to the product of their labels (i.e., $u_1 \cdot u_2 \cdot u_3$). The existence of such a subset proves a critical flaw in the network. It is guaranteed that at least one such subset is present.

## Input
The first line contains the transceiver count $n$ and the interference link count $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The following $m$ lines each detail a pair of transceivers $u$ and $v$ that share a mutual interference link.
At least one subset of three transceivers satisfying the stability condition is guaranteed to exist.

## Output
Provide the labels of the three transceivers forming the subset, printed on a single line in ascending numerical order.
In the event that several such subsets exist, you must resolve the ambiguity by selecting the subset that includes the transceiver with the absolute lowest label. Should multiple subsets share this lowest-labeled transceiver, further narrow your selection to those whose second-lowest label is as small as possible. If ambiguity still persists, choose the subset whose highest label is minimal.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
Replaced the static trace(Z^5) condition with a label-dependent matrix determinant condition. Algebraically, the equation det(Z) = u_1*u_2*u_3 simplifies to a form that only holds when all off-diagonal elements are zero (since the labels are distinct integers >= 1, the maximum possible value of 1/u_1 + 1/u_2 + 1/u_3 is 11/6, which is strictly less than 2, preventing any non-triangle solutions). This forces the model to perform non-trivial algebraic derivation to discover the underlying triangle-finding problem, defeating models that rely on recognizing the trace of a fixed adjacency matrix.
</RATIONALE>
