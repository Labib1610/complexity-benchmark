<METHOD_NAME>Quadratic Form Definiteness Obfuscation</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the explicit matrix determinant condition with a continuous energy minimization problem (a quadratic form). The energy form is constructed such that its matrix is positive definite for any non-triangle triplet, and positive semi-definite with a non-trivial null space if and only if the triplet forms a triangle. Models relying on surface pattern matching of the matrix entries will fail to recognize the triangle condition, while models that naïvely simulate the condition will TLE. Only models that symbolically prove the condition's equivalence to a triangle will succeed.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Critical Interference

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A proposed communications standard attempts to allocate $n$ transceivers (labeled 1 through $n$) into a network. However, testing has revealed $m$ specific bidirectional interference links between pairs of transceivers. Each link connects two distinct transceivers, and no two links connect the same pair.

To diagnose the core issue in the network's topology, engineers analyze the *interference energy* of transceiver triplets. For any chosen subset of exactly three transceivers, we order them by their labels as $u_1 < u_2 < u_3$. 
We can tune their relative phase coefficients, represented by three real numbers $x_1, x_2, x_3$.
The interference energy of the triplet under these coefficients is given by the formula:
$$E(x_1, x_2, x_3) = x_1^2 + 4x_2^2 + 9x_3^2 - 2x_1 x_2 I_{1,2} - 6x_2 x_3 I_{2,3} - 3x_1 x_3 I_{1,3}$$
where:
- $I_{1,2} = 1$ if testing revealed an interference link between $u_1$ and $u_2$, and $0$ otherwise.
- $I_{2,3} = 1$ if testing revealed an interference link between $u_2$ and $u_3$, and $0$ otherwise.
- $I_{1,3} = 1$ if testing revealed an interference link between $u_1$ and $u_3$, and $0$ otherwise.

Your objective is to isolate a subset of exactly three transceivers that is *critically flawed*. A triplet is considered critically flawed if there exists a choice of real coefficients $x_1, x_2, x_3$ (not all zero) such that their interference energy $E(x_1, x_2, x_3)$ is exactly $0$. 

## Input
The first line contains the transceiver count $n$ and the interference link count $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The following $m$ lines each detail a pair of transceivers $u$ and $v$ that share a mutual interference link.
At least one critically flawed subset of three transceivers is guaranteed to exist.

## Output
Provide the labels of the three transceivers forming the subset, printed on a single line as three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ with $a < b < c$.
Among all valid triples $(a, b, c)$ with $a < b < c$, choose the one with the smallest $a$; if there is a tie, choose the one with the smallest $b$; if there is still a tie, choose the one with the smallest $c$.

## Example 1

```
1 3

```
### Output
```

```

## Example 2

### Output


<RATIONALE>
The explicit matrix determinant condition was replaced with a continuous energy minimization problem defined by a quadratic form. This quadratic form is positive definite (meaning its minimum is strictly greater than 0 for non-zero inputs) for any triplet that is not a triangle. It becomes positive semi-definite with a non-trivial null space (allowing exactly 0 energy) if and only if all three interference links are present (a triangle). This forces the model to mathematically analyze the positive definiteness of the form to deduce the triangle equivalence, defeating models that rely on surface-level pattern matching of matrix entries.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that the quadratic form has a non-trivial zero is mathematically equivalent to the triplet being a triangle, which is exactly the same structural condition as the original matrix determinant, ensuring all valid subsets remain completely identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still essentially requires finding a triangle (a 3-chromatic certificate) in a graph, which remains INFEASIBLE to solve in $O(n^3)$ time and necessitates the same $O(nm/64)$ bitset optimization.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "real coefficients" as requiring floating-point simulations or gradient descent, which would lead to TLE rather than an algorithmic deduction, though the phrase "exactly 0" strongly hints at an algebraic property.
</RISK>
