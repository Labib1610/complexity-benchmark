# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory management has a log of incompatibility reports. They want to find a specific pattern in the log that proves their current two-shift system is doomed to fail. Your job is not to schedule the workers, but to search the log to certify this impossibility.

To certify a "deadlock", imagine a testing system where every worker initially has a 'cleared' status. When a report is tested, the status of the two workers named in it is flipped ('cleared' becomes 'flagged', and 'flagged' becomes 'cleared'). 

A deadlock is formed by choosing exactly three distinct reports from the log such that, if you test those three reports, every single worker ends up with a 'cleared' status. 

Such a configuration provides an undeniable certificate that two shifts cannot suffice. Finding this might seem daunting given the sheer number of possible three-report combinations to check.

It is guaranteed that the log contains at least one such deadlock. You must output the IDs of the workers whose status was flipped during the testing of the deadlock (meaning they were flipped to 'flagged' and then eventually back to 'cleared').

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a report naming two incompatible workers.
It is guaranteed that at least one deadlock exists in the given reports.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the three workers who were flipped during the deadlock. Output the lexicographically smallest such group of workers (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```

Three pairs of elements symmetric-differencing to the empty set is algebraically identical to exactly three elements appearing twice each, preserving the exact set of valid report combinations.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
