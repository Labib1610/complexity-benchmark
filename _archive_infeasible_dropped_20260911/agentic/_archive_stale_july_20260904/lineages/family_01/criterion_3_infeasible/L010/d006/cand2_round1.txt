<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory management has a log of incompatibility reports. Each report identifies a set of two workers who cannot share a shift. To prove that their current two-shift system is doomed to fail, management wants to find a specific pattern in the log. Your job is not to schedule the workers, but to search the log to certify this impossibility.

Your task is to identify a "deadlock". A deadlock is formed by a subset of exactly three distinct reports $R_1, R_2,$ and $R_3$ from the log such that $(R_1 \Delta R_2) \Delta R_3 = \emptyset$, where $\Delta$ denotes the symmetric difference of two sets (i.e., $A \Delta B = (A \setminus B) \cup (B \setminus A)$). Finding this configuration provides an undeniable certificate that two shifts cannot suffice.

It is guaranteed that the log contains at least one such deadlock. You must output the unique IDs of the workers who appear in the chosen reports of the deadlock.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a report naming two incompatible workers. All reports in the log represent distinct pairs.
It is guaranteed that at least one deadlock exists in the given reports.

## Output
Print the sequence of unique worker IDs involved in the deadlock's reports, sorted in ascending order, on one line. If there are multiple valid deadlocks, output the one that produces the lexicographically smallest sorted sequence of worker IDs.

## Example 1
### Input
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
### Output
1 2 3

## Example 2
### Input
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
### Output
1 2 4
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Removed the explicit parity constraint and the parenthetical note that a deadlock always yields exactly three workers. Replaced it with a set-theoretic symmetric difference equation that is mathematically equivalent to a triangle in a graph, forcing the model to deduce that the symmetric difference of three 2-element sets is empty if and only if they form a 3-cycle.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The symmetric difference of three edges is empty if and only if every vertex has an even degree within the subset; for three edges, this strictly requires a triangle, so the valid subsets and resulting 3-worker sequences are identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle (a 3-cycle) in an undirected graph, keeping the time complexity and algorithm exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the tie-breaker "lexicographically smallest sorted sequence" if they do not realize the sequence will always have exactly three elements, although standard sequence comparison rules naturally handle this.
</RISK>
