# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory still has only two shifts, 1 and 2, but the roster is known to be hopeless. Your job is not to schedule — it is to search the $2^n$ possible worker combinations to certify the impossibility. A subset of workers where any assignment attempt fails provides an undeniable certificate that two shifts cannot suffice. Finding such a subset can appear daunting given the sheer number of possible combinations to check.

It is guaranteed that the conflict data contains at least one subset of exactly three workers where assigning any single one of them to shift 1 leaves the other two in shift 2 as a documented conflicting pair. Output one such set.

## Input
The first line contains two integers n and m (3 ≤ n ≤ 2000, 3 ≤ m ≤ min(n·(n−1)/2, 2·10⁵)).
Each of the next m lines contains two integers u v — a conflicting pair.
It is guaranteed that at least one such trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers a b c on one line with a < b < c such that no matter which of the three workers is set aside, the other two form a pair present in the conflict data. Output the lexicographically smallest such trio (smallest a; ties by smallest b; then smallest c).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Removed the explicit enumeration of the three required edges ("all three pairs a-b, a-c, b-c") that served as a direct pattern-matching affordance for a graph triangle. Replaced it with a logical condition based on setting aside one member at a time, forcing the model to deduce that this implies a fully connected triplet before it can confidently apply the bitset intersection shortcut.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The logical condition (setting aside any one of three leaves a valid pair) is mathematically identical to requiring all three pairs to exist, so the valid trios and their lexicographical sorting remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The core task remains finding a 3-clique (triangle) in a graph to prove it is not 2-colorable, preserving the intended bitset algorithm and complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "set aside" as removing the worker from the entire dataset rather than just analyzing the trio, though "the other two form a pair" strongly contextualizes it to the three selected variables.
</RISK>
