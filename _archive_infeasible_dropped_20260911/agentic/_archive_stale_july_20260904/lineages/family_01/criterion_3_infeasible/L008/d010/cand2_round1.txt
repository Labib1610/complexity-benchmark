<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the algebraic degree/edge identity with a matching condition: finding a perfect matching between the 3 workers and 3 internal edges. Since a group of 3 has at most 3 internal edges, requiring 3 distinct internal edges for the matching forces the subgraph to be a triangle. <STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

Management insists on dividing the workforce into exactly two non-overlapping shifts. However, the HR department knows this plan is doomed. You must provide undeniable proof of this failure.

HR has a collection of incident files. Each file lists two specific workers who cannot be placed on the same shift. 

You can prove the plan is impossible by finding a specific combination of records. To do this, you must identify a core group of exactly three distinct workers that satisfies a strict assignment condition.

Specifically, it must be possible to choose exactly three distinct incident files and assign each of the three workers in your core group to a different one of these chosen files, such that:
- A worker is always assigned to an incident file that lists them.
- All three chosen incident files list *only* workers from your core group (i.e., both workers listed in each of the three files must belong to the chosen group of three).

You must find a group of exactly three distinct workers that meets this condition.

## Input
The first line provides the workforce size $n$ and the total incident count $m$. The workforce size $n$ is bounded below by 3 and bounded above by the facility capacity of 2000. The incident count $m$ is at least 3, and is capped by either the theoretical maximum of $n(n-1)/2$ distinct combinations, or the archival capacity of $2 \cdot 10^5$, whichever is smaller. Workers are identified by integers from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), identifying the two workers involved in a single incident file. All $m$ incident files represent unique pairs of workers (no two files list the exact same pair).

It is guaranteed that at least one valid group of three workers satisfying the condition exists.

## Output
Output three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ representing the IDs of the three workers in the group. They must be printed such that $a < b < c$.

To ensure a unique valid response when multiple such groups of workers exist, you must select the group that minimizes the value of $a$. If there is a tie, minimize the value of $b$ among the tied triplets, and if a tie still persists, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I replaced the algebraic incidence identity (T = E + 6) with a structural matching requirement. The new condition asks to bijectively assign each of the three workers to three distinct incident files such that all chosen files exclusively involve the chosen workers. This forces models to reason that choosing 3 distinct internal files from a group of 3 workers is only possible if all 3 potential internal pairs exist, bypassing any shortcut based on algebraic degree formulas.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The assignment condition is mathematically equivalent to requiring the 3 workers to have exactly 3 internal incident files between them, which precisely defines a triangle (the exact same underlying condition as the original T = E + 6).
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle (a 3-clique) in a graph, which is efficiently solvable in O(N * M / 64) time using bitsets, keeping the intended algorithm and complexity class identical.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
