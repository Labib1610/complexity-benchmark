<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

Management insists on dividing the workforce into exactly two non-overlapping shifts. However, the HR department knows this plan is doomed. You must provide undeniable proof of this failure.

HR has a collection of incident files, where each file lists two specific workers who cannot be placed on the same shift. 

Management's plan can be proven impossible if you can find a 'deadlock' in the records. A deadlock is defined by a specific combination of three distinct workers. To verify if a chosen trio of workers constitutes a deadlock, HR applies the following rule:
- Count how many incident files involve at least one worker who is NOT part of the chosen trio.
- If this count is exactly $m - 3$ (where $m$ is the total number of incident files), the trio is a deadlock.

It is guaranteed that at least one such deadlock exists. Your task is to identify the three distinct workers involved in such a deadlock and report them to HR.

## Input
The first line provides the workforce size $n$ and the total incident count $m$. The workforce size $n$ is bounded below by 3 and bounded above by the facility capacity of 2000. The incident count $m$ is at least 3, and is capped by either the theoretical maximum of $n(n-1)/2$ distinct combinations, or the archival capacity of $2 \cdot 10^5$, whichever is smaller. Workers are identified by integers from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), identifying the two workers involved in a single incident. No two incident files list the exact same pair of workers.

It is guaranteed that at least one valid deadlock exists.

## Output
Output three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ representing the IDs of the three workers involved. They must be printed such that $a < b < c$.

To ensure a unique valid response when multiple such deadlocks exist, you must select the one that minimizes the value of $a$. If there is a tie, minimize the value of $b$ among the tied triplets, and if a tie still persists, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

</RATIONALE>
