# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A factory employs a workforce. A minimum of three workers are needed to keep the plant running, but fire safety regulations strictly cap the maximum number of workers at 2000. Management wants to assign everyone to either schedule 1 or schedule 2.

This roster is doomed to fail. You are given a filing of grievances. Each grievance states that a specific pair of workers refuse to share a schedule. The filing contains at least three grievances. Grievances are strictly unique (no pair of workers has more than one grievance), and the total number of grievances never exceeds 200,000.

You must prove the schedules are unworkable by finding a trio of workers who all refuse to work with each other (i.e., all three possible pairs among the trio have a filed grievance). It is guaranteed that at least one such trio exists.

## Input
The first line contains two integers: the total number of workers and the number of filed grievances. Workers are numbered from 1 to the number of workers.
Each subsequent line contains two integers u and v, indicating a grievance between worker u and worker v.

## Output
Print the three worker IDs of the trio on a single line, sorted from lowest to highest.
If more than one such trio exists, you must output the trio that has the smallest lowest ID. If there are ties, resolve them by choosing the trio with the smallest middle ID, and finally the smallest highest ID.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
