# Problem C — Personnel Incompatibilities

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory management is attempting to divide the staff into exactly two operational shifts. However, the personnel department has determined this to be impossible. You must provide concrete evidence of this impossibility. You are given a list of grievance records, each indicating that two specific employees refuse to work on the same shift. The personnel department guarantees that there is at least one group of exactly three employees where every possible pair formed from these three has a filed grievance against each other. Any such trio proves that two shifts are insufficient. Your task is to identify and report the lexicographically smallest such trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers: the number of employees and the number of grievance records. The facility's minimum operational requirement is three employees, while its fire safety certificate limits the total staff to two thousand. The grievance database contains at least three records. Its legacy storage architecture limits the total number of records to two hundred thousand, and naturally, no more records can exist than the number of uniquely possible pairs of employees.

Each of the subsequent lines contains two integers representing the IDs of two employees who have a grievance record against each other. Employees are identified by integers from 1 up to the total number of employees.

It is guaranteed that at least one mutually incompatible trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers `a b c` on one line with `a < b < c` such that all three pairs `a–b`, `a–c`, `b–c` are present in the grievance records. Output the lexicographically smallest such group (smallest `a`; ties by smallest `b`; then smallest `c`).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
