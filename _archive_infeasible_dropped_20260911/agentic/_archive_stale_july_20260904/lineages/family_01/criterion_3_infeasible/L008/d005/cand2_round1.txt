# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

Management insists on dividing the workforce into exactly two non-overlapping shifts. However, the HR department knows this plan is doomed. You must provide undeniable proof of this failure.

HR has a collection of incident files. Each file logs a conflict between two specific workers, meaning they cannot be placed on the same shift. 

Management's plan can be proven impossible if you can find a 'deadlock' in the records. A deadlock is defined as a set of exactly three incident files such that the total number of unique workers mentioned across all three files is exactly three. 

It is guaranteed that at least one such deadlock exists. Your task is to identify the three distinct workers involved in such a deadlock and report them to HR.

## Input
The first line provides the workforce size $n$ and the total incident count $m$. The facility employs at least a trio of staff and can accommodate up to two thousand personnel. The archives hold a minimum of three logs. The total number of logs will never exceed two hundred thousand, nor will it exceed the maximum possible number of unique pairs among the staff. Workers are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), identifying the two workers involved in a single incident. No two incident files list the exact same pair of workers.

It is guaranteed that at least one valid deadlock exists.

## Output
Output three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ representing the IDs of the three workers involved. They must be printed such that $a < b < c$.

If multiple such worker triplets exist, you must report the one where the smallest worker ID is as small as possible. In case of ties, minimize the middle worker ID, and finally the largest worker ID.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I changed the definition of a deadlock from "every worker mentioned appears in exactly two files" (a degree-based vertex definition) to "the total number of unique workers mentioned across all three files is exactly three" (a union-based edge definition). This forces the model to mathematically deduce that three distinct edges spanning exactly three vertices is equivalent to a triangle, punishing models that naively implement an $O(M^3)$ edge-subset iteration. I also implemented S07 by expressing numeric bounds indirectly as facility and archival capacities.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Since each file contains exactly two distinct workers and no two files share the exact same pair, the only way three distinct files can collectively contain exactly three unique workers is if they form a triangle between those three workers, preserving the exact same condition and answers.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying mathematical task remains exactly equivalent to finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, keeping it solvable in the same time complexity using bitset adjacency intersections.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
A reader might misinterpret "across all three files" as applying to the entire dataset rather than the three selected files, though "across all three files" strongly anchors the count to the chosen subset.
</RISK>
