<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The laboratory protocol requires isolating reactive materials, but the provided registry of mutually volatile pairs indicates that certain combinations cannot be safely tested. 

Your task is to identify a minimal certificate of this instability. A certificate consists of exactly three materials with the following property: if you place any one of these three materials into a primary testing chamber and place the remaining two into a secondary chamber, the two materials in the secondary chamber will always form a mutually volatile pair. This must hold true regardless of which of the three materials is chosen for the primary chamber. It is guaranteed that the provided registry contains at least one such certificate.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a volatile pair.
It is guaranteed that a certificate of three materials exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the materials of a minimal certificate. Output the lexicographically smallest such certificate (smallest $a$; ties by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I replaced the two-unit partition impossibility condition (which models easily recognized as the pigeonhole principle forcing a triangle) with an operational isolation test. This forces the model to simulate the isolation of each of the three materials to deduce that every remaining pair must be volatile, breaking the direct pattern match to 2-coloring/bipartite impossibility.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition requires that for any of the three materials chosen, the remaining two form a volatile pair, meaning all three possible pairs in the trio must be volatile, which exactly preserves the requirement to find a triangle.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying mathematical task is still finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, which maintains the same time and space complexity requirements.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
None. The logic of "choose 1, the other 2 are a pair; repeat for all 3" is unambiguous and maps perfectly to the input format's definition of a volatile pair.
</RISK>
