<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The laboratory protocol requires isolating reactive materials to prevent hazards. Certain pairs of materials are documented as reactive.

Your task is to identify a core instability triplet. A core instability triplet consists of exactly three distinct materials with the following property: if you evaluate all three pairs that can be formed from these three materials, every single pair must be documented as reactive.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a reactive pair.
It is guaranteed that at least one core instability triplet of three materials exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the materials of a core instability triplet. Output the lexicographically smallest such triplet (smallest $a$; ties by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The seed problem phrased the condition as the impossibility of partitioning three materials into two non-reactive containers, which models recognize as a 2-coloring impossibility mapping to a triangle. I changed this to a direct pairwise evaluation of the three materials, removing the coloring semantics while preserving the exact same condition.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The requirement that all three pairs among three materials are reactive is logically identical to the impossibility of partitioning them into two containers without a reactive pair, so all valid triplets and the lexicographically smallest one remain unchanged.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The problem still requires finding a 3-clique (triangle) in a graph, preserving the INFEASIBLE complexity class.
