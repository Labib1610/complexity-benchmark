<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Grievance Audit

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory's human resources department is auditing past worker schedules to understand why a complete operational halt occurred. You are provided with a historical archive of filed grievances. Each grievance establishes that a specific pair of employees cannot work together peacefully.

Your goal is to process these archives to isolate a trilateral impasse. A trilateral impasse is a group of exactly three distinct employees such that no two employees within the group have a peaceful relationship. In other words, out of the three possible pairs that can be formed from these three employees, every single pair must be documented in the grievance archive.

We know that during this time, it was strictly impossible to divide the employees into two shifts without internal grievances. By the nature of this scheduling failure, the archive is guaranteed to hold at least one valid trilateral impasse. You must find such a group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), which are the total number of employees and the total number of filed grievances, respectively.
The next $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that there is a grievance on record between employee $u$ and employee $v$.
It is guaranteed that there exists at least one trilateral impasse in the dataset.

## Output
Print a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, arranged in strictly increasing order ($a < b < c$). These three integers must be the identifiers of the employees who make up a trilateral impasse. If the archive contains multiple valid impasses, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and if still tied, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I removed the direct "mutual grievance with one another" phrasing, replacing it with a negative definition ("no peaceful relationship") followed by an explicit combinatorial breakdown ("out of the three possible pairs... every single pair must be documented"). This forces models to synthesize the triangle requirement from the pairwise rules rather than relying on surface-level clique matching, without adding any artificial constraints.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The requirement that all three pairs among three distinct employees exist in the grievance archive is mathematically identical to finding a 3-clique, so the valid sets and their lexicographical ordering remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still asks to find a triangle in a graph with the exact same bounds and tie-breaking rules, preserving the O(n*m/64) bitset time complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
None. The definition explicitly states that all three possible pairs among the three chosen employees must exist in the input, which maps unambiguously to a triangle in a simple graph.
</RISK>
