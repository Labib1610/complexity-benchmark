# Problem C — System Instability

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a distributed monitoring network consisting of $n$ sensors. The network covers $m$ distinct spatial zones. Each of these zones is monitored by exactly two sensors. 

Two sensors are considered to be *interfering* if there is at least one zone that they both monitor. 

The network is designed to operate by assigning each sensor to one of two available frequency bands. A safety rule dictates that if two sensors are interfering, they must not be assigned to the same frequency band. 

However, the current configuration is fundamentally flawed. To demonstrate this flaw, you must identify a specific combination: a group of exactly three distinct sensors such that every pair of sensors within this group is interfering. Because all three possible pairs among these three sensors are interfering, it is impossible to validly assign the three sensors to just two frequency bands.

It is guaranteed that the provided configuration contains at least one such group of three sensors. Your task is to find the lexicographically smallest valid group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of sensors and the number of monitored zones, respectively. Sensors are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that the corresponding zone is monitored by sensor $u$ and sensor $v$. The order of the two sensors on a line does not matter. It is guaranteed that no pair of sensors is listed more than once (regardless of order), and that at least one such group of three sensors exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three sensors that form the flaw. 

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid groups of three sensors, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; if there are ties, minimize $b$ among those ties; and if there are still ties, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```
