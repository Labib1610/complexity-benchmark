<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Processor Allocation Audit

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are auditing a task scheduler that distributes $n$ processes across two identical processors. The scheduler receives a list of $m$ mutual exclusion constraints. Each constraint between process $u$ and process $v$ acts as a strict placement rule: if $u$ is assigned to one processor, $v$ must be assigned to the other processor.

The current list of constraints contains a logical flaw. To demonstrate this flaw, you must extract a minimal paradoxical subset consisting of exactly three processes. 

Specifically, you need to find a group of exactly three distinct processes that form a closed contradiction. That is, if you restrict the system to consider *only* the placement rules that exist between these three processes, assigning any one of them to a processor deterministically forces at least one of the three processes to be assigned to both processors simultaneously.

It is guaranteed that the provided constraints contain at least one such group of three processes. The provided logs can be highly skewed: in some configurations, the processes with the smallest identifiers will have a dense web of constraints that never form a paradoxical group of three, while the only valid subset consists entirely of the largest identifiers. Your task is to find and report the correct subset.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of processes and the number of constraints. Processes are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$), indicating a constraint between process $u$ and process $v$. It is guaranteed that at least one paradoxical group of three processes exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three processes that form the paradoxical subset. 

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid groups of three processes, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; if there are ties, minimize $b$ among those ties; and if there are still ties, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```
