<STATEMENT>
# Factory Synchronization

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A sprawling automated factory contains **n** workstations (numbered 1 to **n**). You are configuring the factory's control network and must ensure that **t** critical assembly units can all exchange synchronization signals.

There are **m** potential data cable routes available for activation. Each route connects two specific workstations and requires a one-time activation cost to power up. Once a route is activated, signals can pass back and forth between its two endpoints.

You may route signals through non-critical workstations, using them as intermediate relays. For example, if you activate a route between Critical Unit A and Standard Unit X, and another between Standard Unit X and Critical Unit B, the two critical units A and B are successfully synchronized via X.

Find the minimum total activation cost you must spend to activate a subset of cable routes such that a signal originating at any critical assembly unit can reach all other critical assembly units (either directly or through a sequence of activated routes).

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The factory floor accommodates a maximum of 100 workstations, with at least 1 always present. The routing hardware supports up to 10 critical units, requiring at least 1. The number of proposed routes **m** ranges from a minimum of 0 up to the theoretical maximum of **n**·(**n**−1)/2.
The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the IDs of the critical assembly units.
Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**. These indicate a proposed data cable route between workstation **u** and workstation **v** (**u** ≠ **v**, both between 1 and **n** inclusive) with an activation cost of **w** (requiring at least 1 and at most 1000000 power credits).
The proposed routes are sufficient to ensure all critical units can be synchronized.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total activation cost required to ensure all critical assembly units are mutually synchronized.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, using workstation 4 as an intermediate relay (activating routes 1-4, 2-4, and 3-4 at a total cost of 1+1+1 = 3) is cheaper than activating routes to directly connect the three critical units (which would cost 4). In Example 3, there is only one critical unit, so no routes need to be activated.
