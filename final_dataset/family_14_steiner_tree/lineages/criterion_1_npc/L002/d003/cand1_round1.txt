<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Monetary Stabilization

Time limit: 5 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description
A global financial institution is stabilizing a network of regional currencies. Due to market fluctuations, the institution can establish fixed bilateral pegs. Each peg allows direct, two-way value equivalence between a specific pair of currencies and requires a one-time capital reserve allocation to establish.

The institution's board has designated a specific subset of the currencies as anchor currencies. Your objective is to select a subset of the available pegs such that every anchor currency is reachable from any other anchor currency through the selected pegs. The goal is to minimize the sum of the capital reserve allocations required for the selected pegs.

## Input
The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$: the total count of regional currencies, the count of proposed bilateral pegs, and the count of anchor currencies, respectively.
- $n$ is at least 1 and bounded by the maximum capacity of the institution's primary registry, which holds exactly 100 distinct currency slots.
- $t$ is at least 1, and no more than the exact number of seats on the board's oversight committee (10 seats).
- $m$ is non-negative and will not exceed the theoretical maximum number of unique pairs among the $n$ regional currencies.

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the IDs of the anchor currencies.

Each of the next $m$ lines describes a proposed bilateral peg with three integers: the two distinct currency IDs it connects (numbered starting from 1 up to $n$), and the required capital reserve allocation. The reserve allocation is a positive integer capped at the equivalent of one full reserve unit, which is defined as exactly 1,000,000 micro-units.

The available pegs are sufficient to ensure all anchor currencies can be united.

## Output
Print a single integer: the minimum total capital reserve allocation required to ensure all anchor currencies are united within a single network.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note
In Example 1, routing the equivalence through currency 4 (which requires a reserve of 1+1+1 = 3) is more cost-effective than establishing the pegs that directly link the three anchor currencies (which would require a reserve of 4). 
In Example 3, there is only one anchor currency, so no pegs are needed.

The explicit affordance explaining that non-anchor currencies can be used as intermediate steps has been removed. The problem now simply asks for a subset of pegs connecting all anchor currencies, forcing the model to independently deduce that any vertex in the graph is a valid intermediate node (the defining characteristic of a Steiner Tree). Additionally, all numeric bounds have been rewritten as scenario-based capacities (e.g., registry slots, committee seats) rather than raw inequalities.
</RATIONALE>
