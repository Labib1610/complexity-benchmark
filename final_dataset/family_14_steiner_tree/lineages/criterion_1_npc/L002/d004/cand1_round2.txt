<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Axiomatic Foundations

Time limit: 5 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description
You are formalizing a mathematical theory containing $n$ propositions. 

There are $m$ bidirectional lemmas available to be proven. Proving a lemma between two propositions requires a specific amount of research time. Once a lemma is proven, the two propositions are established as logically equivalent. This equivalence is transitive: if proposition X is equivalent to proposition Y, and proposition Y is equivalent to proposition Z, then proposition X and proposition Z are also logically equivalent.

A specific subset of $t$ propositions have been designated as foundational conjectures. Your objective is to select a set of lemmas to prove such that all $t$ foundational conjectures are mutually logically equivalent. You are permitted to prove lemmas that involve non-foundational propositions; if utilizing them as transitive logical steps reduces the overall research time, it is a valid strategy.

Write a program to calculate the minimum total research time necessary to ensure all $t$ foundational conjectures are mutually logically equivalent.

## Input
The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$.
The number of propositions $n$ is limited to a maximum of 100, with a minimum of 1 proposition. The number of available lemmas $m$ ranges from a minimum of 0 up to a complete set of $n(n-1)/2$. The number of foundational conjectures $t$ is constrained to at most 10, and there is always at least 1.

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the IDs of the foundational conjectures.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, representing an available lemma between proposition $u$ and proposition $v$ that requires a research time of $w$. Each lemma connects two distinct propositions (identified by integers from 1 to $n$, where $u \neq v$). The research time $w$ is at least 1 and is capped at a limit of 1000000.

It is guaranteed that the available lemmas are sufficient to ensure all foundational conjectures can share logical equivalence.

## Output
Print a single integer: the minimum total research time to ensure all foundational conjectures are mutually logically equivalent.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note
In Example 1, establishing the equivalence through the non-foundational proposition 4 (which requires a time of 1+1+1 = 3) is more efficient than proving the lemmas that directly involve only the three foundational conjectures (which would require a time of 4). 
In Example 3, there is only one foundational conjecture, so no lemmas need to be proven.
