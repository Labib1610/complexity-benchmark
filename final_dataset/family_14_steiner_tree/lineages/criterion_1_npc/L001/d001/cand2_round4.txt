# Problem A — Activating the Communication Grid

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A research complex consists of **n** sites (numbered 1 to **n**). Certain pairs of sites can be connected by activating a communication conduit at a specific expense. Out of all the sites, **t** are designated as **priority** sites. Your objective is to ensure that all priority sites are ultimately connected to one another through the conduits you choose to activate. This connection can be direct, or it can route through other sites. The remaining sites are standard relays: you may route your connections through any of them if it reduces the total expense, or you may leave them disconnected.

Determine a configuration of activated conduits that connects all priority sites together while keeping the total expense of the activated conduits as low as possible. Output this minimum total expense.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**.
- The total number of sites **n** is at least 1 and is physically restricted to a maximum of 100 by the complex's grid layout.
- The number of potential conduits **m** can be any count from an absolute minimum of 0 up to the theoretical limit of n·(n−1)/2. 
- The number of priority sites **t** is at least 1, and is limited to a maximum of 10 by the available ports on the central transceiver module.

The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the identifiers of the priority sites.

Each of the following **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**, describing a conduit that can be activated between distinct sites **u** and **v** (both ranging from 1 to **n** inclusive) for an expense of **w**. The expense **w** is at least 1 and cannot exceed the maximum hardware grant of 1000000.

It is guaranteed that the available conduits are sufficient to connect all priority sites.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total expense required to connect all priority sites.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, routing the connections through the standard relay site 4 (incurring expenses 1+1+1 = 3) is more cost-effective than connecting the three priority sites directly to each other (which would incur an expense of 4). In Example 3, there is only one priority site, so no conduits need to be activated and the expense is 0.
