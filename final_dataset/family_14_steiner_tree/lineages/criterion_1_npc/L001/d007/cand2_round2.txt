# Problem A — Harmonic Synchronization

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are configuring a network of **n** acoustic resonators, numbered 1 through **n**. Initially, each resonator vibrates at its own distinct baseline frequency.

You are provided with a list of **m** available phase-lock mechanisms. A phase-lock mechanism between resonator $u$ and resonator $v$ forces them to vibrate at the exact same frequency, consuming $w$ milliwatts of continuous power. Phase-locking is transitive: if $u$ and $v$ are phase-locked, and $v$ and $z$ are phase-locked, then $u$ and $z$ will naturally resonate at the same frequency.

You are given a query containing a subset of **t** critical resonators: $c_1, c_2, \dots, c_t$. 
Your objective is to activate a subset of the available phase-lock mechanisms such that all **t** critical resonators are forced to share the exact same structural frequency. 

You may activate phase-lock mechanisms involving non-critical resonators; these can serve as harmonic bridges between critical resonators if it results in lower total power consumption. 

Determine the minimum total power consumption required to ensure that the mutual frequency synchronization of all **t** critical resonators is achieved. (If $t=1$, no activation is needed and the minimum power is 0).

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ t ≤ 10, 1 ≤ n ≤ 100, 0 ≤ m ≤ n·(n−1)/2), representing the total number of resonators, the number of available phase-lock mechanisms, and the number of critical resonators.
The second line contains **t** distinct integers $c_1, c_2, \dots, c_t$: the indices of the critical resonators.
Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1000000), indicating that a phase-lock mechanism can be activated between resonator u and resonator v at a cost of w milliwatts.
It is guaranteed that the available mechanisms are sufficient to synchronize all critical resonators.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total power consumption to activate a set of mechanisms that satisfies the requirement.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, the critical resonators are 1, 2, and 3. You can activate mechanisms between (1,4), (2,4), and (3,4) for a total cost of 1+1+1 = 3 milliwatts. Because resonator 1 is phase-locked to 4, and 2 is phase-locked to 4, their synchronization is achieved. Similarly, 3 is phase-locked to 4, so all three critical resonators share the same frequency. Alternatively, activating mechanisms for (1,2) and (2,3) directly would cost 2+2 = 4 milliwatts. The minimum cost is 3. 

In Example 3, there is only one critical resonator ($t=1$), so no activation is needed and the minimum cost is 0.
