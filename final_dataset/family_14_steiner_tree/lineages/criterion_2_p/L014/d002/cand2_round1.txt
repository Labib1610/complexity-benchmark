<STATEMENT>
# Activation of Critical Chambers

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An underground facility houses **n** chambers (numbered sequentially starting from 1). The facility must contain at least one chamber, and its size is capped by the life-support system's maximum capacity of two hundred thousand individual units.

To ensure full accessibility while minimizing structural weakness, the facility is connected by **m** corridors such that any chamber can be reached from any other, but no closed loops exist. This architectural choice strictly fixes the number of corridors based on the number of chambers. 

Among these, a specific subset of **t** chambers (which can be anything from a single chamber up to all **n** of them) is designated as **critical**. Your task is to select a subset of the available corridors to activate such that all **t** critical chambers end up connected to one another, either directly or through intermediate non-critical chambers.

Each corridor requires a specific activation energy **w**. The energy required for any single corridor is always a positive integer of at least one joule, and never exceeds the corridor generator's physical limit of one million joules.

Out of all possible combinations of the corridors, you must find a set of minimum total activation energy that achieves this mutual connectivity for all the critical chambers.

## Input

The first line provides three integers: **n**, **m**, and **t**.
The second line provides **t** distinct integers: the identifiers of the critical chambers.
Each of the following **m** lines provides three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**, representing a corridor connecting chamber **u** and chamber **v** requiring **w** joules to activate. The corridors follow the structural guarantees described above, and no corridor connects a chamber to itself.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total activation energy required to interconnect all critical chambers.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the critical chambers 1 and 4 are combined into a single network by activating the corridors that link 1 to 2, 2 to 3, and 3 to 4, for a total energy of 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. In Example 2, there is only one critical chamber, so no corridors need to be activated.
