<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders. 

To maintain market equilibrium, the antitrust regulatory board has issued a certificate that enforces strict structural limits on the proposed agreements. The board mandates that the overall count of proposed agreements, $m$, must be exactly $n - 1$. Furthermore, the certificate guarantees that the agreements connect all corporations into a single minimally comprehensive market structure.

The board evaluates each of the $m$ proposed agreements individually to determine if it is "critical" to the market's operation. An agreement (let's say it connects corporation $u$ and corporation $v$) is considered **non-critical** if it is possible to assign a regulatory tier (Tier A or Tier B) to every corporation in the market such that all three of the following conditions are met simultaneously:
1. Every key patent holder is assigned to Tier A.
2. For all proposed agreements *other than the one currently being evaluated*, the two corporations involved are assigned to the same tier.
3. Corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ are assigned to different tiers.

An agreement is deemed **critical** if it is impossible to find any tier assignment that satisfies all three conditions.

Your goal is to find the total legal fee of all critical agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the total legal fee of all critical agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. 
Let's evaluate the agreement between 1 and 2. If we assign Tier B to corporation 1 and Tier A to corporations 2, 3, and 4, we satisfy condition 2 (agreements 2-3 and 3-4 connect corporations in the same tier) and condition 3 (1 and 2 are in different tiers). However, condition 1 is violated because key patent holder 1 is in Tier B. Any other assignment also fails. Thus, the agreement between 1 and 2 is critical.
By similar logic, the agreements between 2 and 3, and between 3 and 4 are also critical. The total legal fee of all critical agreements is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder (corporation 3). 
For the agreement between 1 and 2, we can assign Tier B to corporation 1 and Tier A to corporations 2, 3, 4, and 5. This satisfies all conditions: the only key patent holder (3) is in Tier A, all other agreements connect corporations within the same tier, and corporations 1 and 2 are in different tiers. Thus, this agreement is non-critical. All other agreements are similarly non-critical. The total legal fee is 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3. 
Evaluating any agreement and assigning different tiers to its endpoints would force at least one key patent holder into Tier B, violating condition 1. Therefore, both agreements are critical. The total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit Steiner tree formulation ("unique sequence of agreements linking those two corporations") was removed. Instead, the condition for an edge to be required is framed as a boolean 2-coloring problem evaluated independently for each edge. To succeed, the model must mathematically deduce that the coloring conditions are satisfiable if and only if removing the edge leaves one of the resulting components with zero key patent holders, thus logically reducing back to the Steiner tree DFS. A naive simulation of the coloring rules will result in an O(N^2) Time Limit Exceeded.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
An edge satisfies the non-critical coloring conditions if and only if it does not lie on any path between two key patent holders; therefore, the set of critical edges is exactly the edges of the minimal subtree spanning all key patent holders, leaving the sum of their weights unchanged.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying mathematical structure is identical, meaning the problem can still be solved in O(N) time using a single post-order DFS to count key patent holders in each subtree.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
A model might misinterpret "For all proposed agreements other than the one currently being evaluated" as referring to a global state rather than a per-edge independent evaluation, though the phrasing "evaluates each of the m proposed agreements individually" strongly mitigates this.
</RISK>
