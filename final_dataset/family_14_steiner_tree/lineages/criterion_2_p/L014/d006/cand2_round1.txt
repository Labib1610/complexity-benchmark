<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders. 

To maintain market equilibrium, the antitrust regulatory board has issued a certificate that enforces strict structural limits on the proposed agreements. The board mandates that the overall count of proposed agreements, $m$, must be exactly $n - 1$. Furthermore, the certificate guarantees that for any two corporations in the market, there is exactly one valid sequence of proposed agreements that links them (a sequence where consecutive agreements share a corporation, and no corporation is visited more than once).

Your goal is to choose a subset of the proposed agreements to finalize. A chosen subset is considered valid if and only if, for every possible pair of key patent holders, the unique sequence of agreements linking those two corporations is entirely included in the finalized subset.

You must find the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. There is only one pair of key patent holders: (1, 4). The unique sequence of agreements linking 1 and 4 consists of the agreement between 1 and 2, the agreement between 2 and 3, and the agreement between 3 and 4. All three of these agreements must be included in the finalized subset. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder, so there are no pairs of key patent holders. The condition is vacuously satisfied by choosing an empty subset of agreements, resulting in a minimum total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3. The pairs are (1, 2), (1, 3), and (2, 3).
The unique sequence for (1, 2) is the agreement between 1 and 2.
The unique sequence for (2, 3) is the agreement between 2 and 3.
The unique sequence for (1, 3) consists of both of those agreements.
To satisfy the condition for all pairs, the finalized subset must include both agreements. The total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Replaced the global connectivity objective ("reach a market state where all key patent holders belong to the same conglomerate") with a pairwise path inclusion requirement. By removing the explicit "induced-edge" structural guarantee and replacing it with a "unique sequence" guarantee, models can no longer trivially pattern-match the problem to a global tree-pruning template. This forces them to genuinely reason that the union of all pairwise paths in a tree is mathematically equivalent to the minimal spanning subtree, otherwise they will likely attempt an O(T^2) all-pairs path-finding approach and fail due to Time Limit Exceeded.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The minimal valid subset of agreements is exactly the union of the unique paths between all pairs of key patent holders, which is mathematically identical to the minimal connected subgraph (Steiner tree) spanning those holders.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly the minimal Steiner tree on a tree, which is solvable in O(N) time and remains in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "entirely included" as meaning they have to sum the costs of the paths separately (counting overlapping edges multiple times), but the phrasing "subset of finalized agreements" and the worked Example 3 explicitly clarify that each agreement in the subset is only paid for once.
</RISK>
