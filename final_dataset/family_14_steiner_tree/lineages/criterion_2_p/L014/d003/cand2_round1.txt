<STATEMENT>
# Connecting the Critical Facilities

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A large industrial complex consists of $n$ facilities, numbered 1 to $n$. A specific subset of $t$ facilities are designated as critical operations centers. There are $m$ potential communication links that can be constructed between certain pairs of facilities, each requiring a specific construction cost.

Your objective is to select a subset of these links to build so that all $t$ critical facilities can communicate with one another, either directly or by routing through other intermediate facilities. You must achieve this requirement at the minimum possible total construction cost.

The complex's blueprint guarantees that for any pair of facilities, there is at most one sequence of proposed links connecting them without visiting any facility more than once. Furthermore, the total number of proposed links $m$ is exactly one less than the total number of facilities $n$.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. The total number of facilities $n$ is a positive integer bounded by the region's maximum zoning limit of 200,000. The number of critical operations centers $t$ is at least 1 and at most $n$.
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the critical facilities.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$. The identifiers $u$ and $v$ are distinct valid facilities. The cost $w$ is a positive integer up to a maximum budget allowance of 1,000,000 per link.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total cost to ensure all critical facilities are interconnected.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the critical facilities 1 and 4 are integrated by building the links that join 1 to 2, 2 to 3, and 3 to 4, for a total cost of 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. In Example 2, there is only one critical facility, so no links need to be constructed.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit guarantee of global connectivity ("single unified network") was replaced with a local constraint ("at most one sequence... without visiting any facility more than once"). Combined with the condition that $m = n - 1$, this forces the model to mathematically deduce that the graph is a single connected tree rather than relying on surface-level keywords. Additionally, numeric bounds were rewritten as physical scenario limits (zoning limits, budget allowances) following strategy S07.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The underlying graph, edge costs, and the objective to find the minimum cost to interconnect the critical subset are identical, ensuring the minimum cost remains exactly the same for all inputs.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
A graph with $n$ vertices, $n-1$ edges, and at most one simple path between any pair of vertices is mathematically guaranteed to be a single connected tree, meaning the problem remains finding a Steiner tree on a tree, which is solvable in $O(n)$ time.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the lack of an explicit global connectivity guarantee as allowing disconnected components if they fail to deduce that a forest with $n-1$ edges must be fully connected, potentially leading them to apply incorrect general-graph algorithms.
</RISK>
