<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders. 

We classify a proposed agreement as *vital* if its cancellation means there exists at least one pair of key patent holders that can be linked by some sequence of the full set of $m$ agreements, but can no longer be linked by any sequence of the remaining $m - 1$ agreements. (A sequence of agreements linking two corporations is valid if consecutive agreements in the sequence share a corporation).

Your goal is to evaluate the cost of these specific agreements. You must calculate the sum of the legal fees of all *vital* agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$, $m = n - 1$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the total legal fee of all vital agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. 
Canceling the agreement between 1 and 2 means that key patent holders 1 and 4, which can initially be linked, can no longer be linked by any sequence of the remaining agreements. Thus, this agreement is vital.
Similarly, canceling the agreement between 2 and 3, or the agreement between 3 and 4, also breaks the only sequence linking 1 and 4. All three agreements are vital. The total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder (corporation 3). Since there is no pair of distinct key patent holders, no agreement's cancellation can break the linkage between a pair. No agreements are vital, resulting in a total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3.
Canceling the agreement between 1 and 2 means that key patent holders 1 and 2 (which can initially be linked) can no longer be linked. Thus, this agreement is vital.
Canceling the agreement between 2 and 3 means that key patent holders 2 and 3 (which can initially be linked) can no longer be linked. Thus, this agreement is also vital.
Both agreements are vital. The total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.

I also replaced the partition-based definition of criticality with a pairwise reachability dependency. This neutralizes the shortcut affordance: models can no longer blindly rely on the text's "fully integrated" and "minimally sufficient" guarantees to assume a tree, forcing them to either deduce it from the input bounds or implement a more complex general bridge-finding algorithm.
