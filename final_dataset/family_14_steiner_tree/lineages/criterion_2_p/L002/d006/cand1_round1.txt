# Hyperplane Intersection Minimization

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are analyzing a system of variables represented as a vector $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

You are given a pool of $m$ candidate linear constraints. Each constraint is a hyperplane defined by the equation $x_u - x_v = 0$, and activating this constraint incurs a structural cost of $w$. 
The pool of candidate constraints possesses a specific global property: if all $m$ constraints were activated simultaneously, their intersection would be exactly the 1-dimensional subspace where all coordinates are equal (i.e., the subspace spanned by the all-ones vector $\mathbf{1}$). Furthermore, the number of candidate constraints $m$ is exactly the theoretical minimum number of such hyperplanes required to achieve this 1-dimensional intersection.

You are given a target set of $t$ specific coordinate indices.
Your objective is to activate a subset of the candidate constraints such that, for every vector $\mathbf{x}$ that lies within the intersection of the activated constraints, all $t$ target coordinates are strictly forced to equal each other. You may activate constraints involving non-target coordinates if their algebraic implications help restrict the target coordinates.

Determine the minimum possible total structural cost of an activated subset of constraints that satisfies this requirement. The total cost is the sum of the costs $w$ of the activated constraints.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. The dimension $n$ is at most 200,000, $t$ is between 1 and $n$ inclusive, and $m$ is exactly the theoretical minimum number of hyperplanes required to restrict $\mathbb{R}^n$ to a 1-dimensional subspace.
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the target coordinate indices.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), defining a candidate constraint $x_u - x_v = 0$ that costs $w$ to activate.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total structural cost of a subset of constraints whose algebraic intersection forces the target coordinates to be equal.

## Examples

**Input**

```

**Output**
```
8
```

```

```
9
```

The critical tree structure guarantee ($m = n - 1$) is now hidden behind the algebraic fact that reducing $\mathbb{R}^n$ to a 1-dimensional subspace requires a theoretical minimum of $n - 1$ hyperplanes. This forces the model to recognize the isomorphism between the null space of an incidence matrix and graph connectivity to realize there is a unique minimal valid subset.
</RATIONALE>
