# Emergency Pressurization

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An orbital station is composed of modules, identified by integers starting from 1. These modules are physically linked by a network of structural corridors. The architecture of the station guarantees that the entire facility is fully connected. Because the total number of corridors is exactly one less than the number of modules, there are no redundant alternative routes between any two modules.

Due to a reactor failure, the station must operate on emergency power. There are critical modules that house essential life-support systems. The commander must define a safe zone by choosing a collection of corridors to keep pressurized. The safe zone must be functionally contiguous with respect to the critical modules: it must be possible to navigate between any two critical modules exclusively by traversing the chosen pressurized corridors. Non-critical modules may be included in this safe zone to serve as transit nodes.

Pressurizing a corridor requires a specific continuous power draw. Corridors excluded from the collection are depressurized and impassable. Calculate the minimum total power draw required to maintain a collection of pressurized corridors that successfully integrates all critical modules.

## Input

The first line contains three integers: the total number of modules **n**, the number of corridors **m**, and the number of critical modules **t**. The station's structural registry is capped at a capacity of 200000 modules, and a minimum of 1 module is required to form a station. The emergency protocol identifies at least 1 critical module, up to a maximum of all **n** modules. The construction log dictates that **m** is exactly **n** − 1.

The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the identifiers of the critical modules.

Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**, representing a corridor linking module **u** and module **v** (where **u** and **v** are distinct module identifiers from 1 to **n**). The reactor specifications guarantee that the power draw **w** required to pressurize the corridor is at least 1 unit and at most a maximum capacity of 1000000 units. These corridors collectively form the connected structure described above.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total power draw required to keep all critical modules functionally interconnected.

I removed the explicit wording "utilizing the absolute minimum number of corridors necessary" which acted as a strong conceptual hint for a tree. Instead, I established the tree property mechanically by stating the graph is fully connected with exactly one fewer edge than vertices, meaning there are no redundant routes. I also applied strategy S07, expressing all numerical bounds through physical and protocol constraints within the scenario rather than as bare inequalities.
</RATIONALE>
