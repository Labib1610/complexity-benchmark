<STATEMENT>
# Habitat Network Analysis

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A wildlife conservation project tracks **n** ecological zones, indexed from 1 to **n**. You are provided with a list of **t** zones that are designated as prime habitats. There are also **m** proposed wildlife corridors, each connecting a pair of zones and requiring a specific maintenance fee.

The proposed corridors are arranged such that an animal can migrate between any two zones via exactly one unique sequence of corridors.

A corridor is defined as **essential** if there exists at least one pair of distinct prime habitats, say **A** and **B**, such that the unique sequence of corridors connecting **A** and **B** includes this corridor. 

Your task is to determine the sum of the maintenance fees of all essential corridors. Each essential corridor's fee is included exactly once in the final sum, regardless of how many different pairs of habitats rely on it.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The total number of zones **n** is at least 1 and at most 200000. The number of proposed corridors **m** is exactly **n** - 1. The number of prime habitats **t** is at least 1 and at most **n**.

The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the indices of the prime habitats.

The following **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**. These specify a proposed corridor between zone **u** and zone **v** (with **u** and **v** being distinct integers from 1 to **n**) having a maintenance fee of **w** (an integer from 1 to 1000000).

## Output

Print a single integer representing the sum of the maintenance fees of all essential corridors.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the distinct prime habitats are 1 and 4. The unique sequence of corridors connecting them goes through zones 2 and 3, so all three corridors are essential. Their fees (5 + 1 + 2 = 8) are summed. 

In Example 2, there is only one prime habitat. Since no pair of distinct prime habitats can be formed, zero corridors are essential, resulting in a sum of 0.

In Example 3, the prime habitats are 1, 2, and 3. The pairs of distinct habitats are (1,2), (2,3), and (1,3). The corridor between 1 and 2 is essential for pair (1,2) and pair (1,3). The corridor between 2 and 3 is essential for pair (2,3) and pair (1,3). Both corridors are essential, and each is counted exactly once: 4 + 5 = 9.

<RATIONALE>
Replaced the global "connect all required nodes" objective with a local, pairwise property ("an edge is essential if it lies on the path between any pair of target nodes"). This mathematically defines the exact same set of edges (the minimal spanning subtree) but baits models into writing an $O(t^2)$ all-pairs simulation that will Time Limit Exceed, unless they genuinely reason about the tree's structural properties to recover the $O(n)$ DFS or leaf-peeling approach.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
In a tree, the union of all simple paths between pairs of vertices in a subset $S$ is exactly the minimal subtree spanning $S$, so the set of "essential" edges and their summed weight is identical to the original problem's minimum connection cost.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying graph is still a tree and the problem still asks for the weight of the Steiner tree on a subset of vertices, which remains solvable in $O(n)$ time using a single DFS.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>

</RISK>
