<STATEMENT>
# Centralized Diagnostic Routing

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An industrial manufacturing complex features $n$ distinct workstations, numbered $1$ to $n$. These are linked by $m$ bidirectional conveyor belts. The facility's infrastructure ensures that all workstations are interlinked in a single contiguous network. To minimize maintenance costs, the total number of belts $m$ is strictly fixed at exactly $n - 1$, meaning there is exactly one unique sequence of belts between any two workstations.

A specific subset of $t$ workstations are designated as quality control stations. A safety inspector must conduct a full diagnostic tour to visit all $t$ of these quality control stations. 

The inspector may choose to start their tour at any workstation in the complex (it does not need to be one of the quality control stations). However, the conveyor belts are initially locked. Before the tour begins, you must select a set of belts to unlock. Unlocking the belt between workstation $u$ and workstation $v$ incurs a one-time energy overhead of $w$. Once a belt is unlocked, the inspector can travel across it in either direction as many times as needed during the tour without any additional energy cost. Belts that are not unlocked cannot be crossed.

Your task is to determine the minimum possible total energy overhead required to unlock a set of belts such that the inspector can successfully visit all $t$ quality control stations from their chosen starting location.

## Input

The first line provides three integers: $n$, $m$, and $t$. The facility's blueprint supports a maximum of two hundred thousand workstations ($1 \le n \le 200000$). The number of belts $m$ is always $n - 1$. The number of quality control stations $t$ can range from a single station up to the entire facility ($1 \le t \le n$).

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers representing the IDs of the quality control stations.

Following this, there are $m$ lines, each detailing a single conveyor belt with three integers: $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$). The energy overhead $w$ for unlocking any single belt is at least one and does not exceed one million units ($1 \le w \le 1000000$). 

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total energy overhead required.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the quality control stations are 1 and 4. The inspector could choose to start at workstation 2. To visit station 1, they need the belt between 1 and 2 unlocked (overhead 5). To visit station 4, they need the belts between 2 and 3, and 3 and 4 unlocked (overheads 1 and 2). The inspector can now walk from 2 to 1, back to 2, then to 3 and 4. The total overhead is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. Alternatively, starting at workstation 3 requires exactly the same belts to be unlocked. The minimum possible total overhead is 8.
In Example 2, there is only one quality control station (station 3). The inspector can simply start at workstation 3. Since all required stations are already visited, no belts need to be unlocked. The minimum possible total overhead is 0.
