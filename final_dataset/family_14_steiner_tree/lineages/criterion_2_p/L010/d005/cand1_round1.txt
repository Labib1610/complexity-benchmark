<STATEMENT>
# Thermodynamic Balancing

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are designing a thermodynamic system consisting of $n$ isolated chambers. Each chamber can hold an arbitrary continuous amount of energy.

There are $m$ optional thermal conduits available in the blueprint. A potential conduit connects chamber $u$ and chamber $v$. If you pay a fixed construction cost of $w$ to build a conduit, energy can freely flow back and forth between its two endpoints in any amount. The total number of potential conduits $m$ is exactly $n - 1$. The blueprint guarantees that if all $m$ conduits were built, energy could flow between any two chambers in the entire system.

A specific subset of $t$ chambers are designated as "active chambers".

Your task is to build a subset of the conduits such that the active chambers can freely exchange energy with each other. Specifically, using *only* the built conduits, the system must be able to achieve *any* mathematically valid redistribution of energy among the $t$ active chambers (i.e., any final state where the sum of the active chambers' energies equals their initial sum). During this process, energy may temporarily flow through non-active chambers, but the final energy of every non-active chamber must be exactly equal to its initial energy.

Determine the minimum possible total construction cost of the conduits you must build to satisfy this requirement.

## Input

The first line provides three integers: $n$, $m$, and $t$. The system supports a maximum of two hundred thousand chambers ($1 \le n \le 200000$). The number of potential conduits $m$ is always $n - 1$. The number of active chambers $t$ can range from a single chamber up to the entire system ($1 \le t \le n$).

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers representing the IDs of the active chambers.

Following this, there are $m$ lines, each detailing a single potential conduit with three integers: $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$). Building the conduit between chamber $u$ and chamber $v$ incurs a construction cost $w$. The cost $w$ is at least one and does not exceed one million ($1 \le w \le 1000000$).

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total construction cost required.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, chambers 1 and 4 are active. To allow arbitrary energy redistribution between them, they must be able to exchange energy. This requires building the conduits between 1 and 2 (cost 5), 2 and 3 (cost 1), and 3 and 4 (cost 2). The total construction cost is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8.
In Example 2, there is only one active chamber. Any valid redistribution is exactly its initial state, requiring no energy exchange. Thus, zero conduits need to be built.
In Example 3, all three chambers are active. To allow arbitrary redistribution among all three, they must all be able to exchange energy with each other, requiring both available conduits to be built for a total cost of 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The problem was rewritten to completely remove graph connectivity terminology (networks, hubs, sequences, paths). Instead, it uses a thermodynamic framing where the goal is to allow zero-sum energy redistributions among a subset of active chambers, while preserving the net energy of non-active chambers. Models must now logically deduce that satisfying this conservation constraint across all possible redistributions is mathematically equivalent to ensuring that all active chambers belong to the same connected component. This defeats models that rely on surface-level keyword matching to trigger standard tree-pruning algorithms.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Achieving any arbitrary zero-sum energy redistribution among a subset of nodes, with zero net-change on all intermediate nodes, is possible if and only if all nodes in the subset belong to the same connected component; thus, the optimal set of conduits to build is exactly the minimum Steiner tree, preserving all answers.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying mathematical structure remains finding the minimal tree spanning a subset of terminals in a graph that is already a tree, which is solvable in O(n) time and remains in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "energy can freely flow ... in any amount" as implying a capacity constraint or flow network problem, though the explicit phrase "in any amount" and the lack of capacity bounds in the input should clarify that this is a purely structural connectivity requirement.
</RISK>
