# Phase 2: High-Priority Routing

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A cybersecurity firm is upgrading a facility's hardware. This project is divided into three distinct phases:

**Phase 1: Facility Survey.** Engineers map out the available bidirectional conduit segments between $n$ equipment rooms (numbered 1 to $n$). The engineers verify that the mapped segments form a fully integrated infrastructure with absolutely no redundant loops, meaning there is exactly one unique routing sequence between any two rooms. 

**Phase 2: Cable Planning.** You are responsible for this phase. The client has designated $t$ specific active rooms as high-priority. These high-priority rooms must all be able to exchange data with one another, either directly or by passing data through a sequence of intermediate cabled rooms. You are given the cost of installing fiber optic cable in each mapped conduit segment. You must choose a subset of the available segments to cable such that the high-priority rooms are all interconnected. Calculate the minimum possible total installation cost to achieve this connectivity.

**Phase 3: Procurement.** The finance department takes your calculated minimum cost, adds a 20% markup for price fluctuations, and approves the final budget.

Your task is to write a program that executes Phase 2. Given the network topology from Phase 1 and the list of high-priority rooms, output the exact minimum cost before any Phase 3 markups are applied.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. The facility's maximum capacity limits the total rooms $n$ to at most 200000 (with a minimum of 1). The absolute lack of redundant loops in the fully integrated infrastructure physically constrains the total number of segments $m$ to be exactly one less than $n$. The count of high-priority rooms $t$ is between 1 and $n$ inclusive.
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers representing the identifiers of the high-priority rooms.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$. These represent a conduit segment joining two distinct rooms $u$ and $v$ (both valid room identifiers from 1 to $n$) at an installation cost of $w$. The physical properties of the specialized cable dictate an installation cost $w$ of at least 1, capped at a maximum of 1000000.
The segments provided in the input are guaranteed to satisfy the strict connectivity verified in Phase 1.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total cost calculated in Phase 2.
