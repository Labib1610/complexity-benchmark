# Consolidated Risk Pool

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An investment portfolio consists of $R$ distinct assets, structured using $S$ mutual swap contracts. These contracts interlink all $R$ assets into a single comprehensive market structure.

A new compliance regulation requires that $P$ specific assets be grouped into a consolidated risk pool.

You must select a subset of the $R$ assets to form this pool. The chosen subset must meet the following conditions:
1. It must contain all $P$ of the mandated assets (you may also include any number of additional assets in the subset).
2. The selected assets must be fully chain-linked. Specifically, for any two assets in the chosen subset, there must exist a sequence of swap contracts linking them such that every asset along the sequence is also included in the chosen subset.

Whenever two assets that are linked by a direct swap contract are both included in the pool, that contract is considered "internalized" and triggers a capital hedging cost. Contracts where at least one linked asset is excluded from the pool remain external and trigger no hedging cost.

Your objective is to determine the minimum possible total capital hedging cost of the internalized contracts needed to establish a valid risk pool.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $R$, $S$, and $P$. 
The total number of assets $R$ satisfies $1 \le R \le 200000$. 
The number of swap contracts $S$ is exactly equal to $R - 1$. 
The number of mandated assets $P$ satisfies $1 \le P \le R$.

The second line contains $P$ distinct integers representing the identifiers of the mandated assets.

Each of the following $S$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le R$, $u \neq v$). This indicates a mutual swap contract between asset $u$ and asset $v$ with a capital hedging cost of $w$ ($1 \le w \le 1000000$). 

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum total capital hedging cost of the internalized contracts for a valid risk pool.

The rewrite strictly maps the seed's vertex-selection mechanics to a financial domain, replacing components/integrations with assets/swap contracts. This eliminates all graph-theoretic and physical-network terminology, forcing the model to deduce the underlying tree structure from $S = R - 1$ and the "chain-linked" constraint without relying on familiar topological cues.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The mathematical structure is identical: selecting a connected subset of vertices in a tree that spans $P$ specific vertices, where the cost is the sum of the edges induced by the selected vertices, resulting in the exact same minimum cost.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains finding the minimum weight subtree spanning a set of terminal nodes in a tree, which is solvable in polynomial time $O(N)$ by pruning unneeded branches.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
