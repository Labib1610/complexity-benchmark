# Problem B — Heritage Zoning District

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are an urban planner evaluating a region composed of **n** land parcels, numbered 1 to **n**. The city records detail **m** surveyed borders. Each surveyed border separates two distinct parcels and has an associated annual maintenance cost of **w**.

The **m** surveyed borders are arranged such that the entire region of **n** parcels is *fully contiguous*. A set of parcels is defined as contiguous if, for every proper, non-empty subset $A$ of the set, there is at least one surveyed border that separates a parcel in $A$ from a parcel in the set that is not in $A$. The entire region of **n** parcels satisfies this property.

A specific set of **t** parcels are designated as **heritage sites**. Your objective is to define a **special zoning district**, which is a subset of parcels, satisfying the following conditions:
1. The zoning district must include all **t** heritage sites.
2. The zoning district itself must be *contiguous*, according to the exact same definition above. (A district consisting of a single parcel has no proper, non-empty subsets and is inherently contiguous).

By local law, when a special zoning district is formed, the district administration must pay the maintenance cost for EXACTLY those surveyed borders that lie ENTIRELY WITHIN the district—that is, borders where BOTH of the parcels it separates are included in the zoning district. Borders on the perimeter (where one parcel is in the district and one is outside) or entirely outside the district are maintained by the state, at no cost to the district administration.

The total internal maintenance cost of your zoning district is the sum of the maintenance costs of all surveyed borders that lie entirely within it. Determine the minimum total internal maintenance cost required to form a valid zoning district.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ t ≤ n, 1 ≤ n ≤ 200000, m = n − 1).
The second line provides **t** distinct integers representing the labels of the heritage sites.
Each of the subsequent **m** lines provides three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1000000), indicating a surveyed border separating parcel **u** and parcel **v** with a maintenance cost of **w**.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum total internal maintenance cost required to form a valid zoning district.

The problem was reframed from an abstract network of "modules and synergies" into a spatial map of "parcels and borders". By describing edges as borders that "separate" parcels, the text strongly evokes planar graphs and minimum-cut/boundary problems. The cost rule forces the user to pay for the *internal* borders rather than the perimeter, directly clashing with standard geometric cut heuristics and forcing the model to genuinely deduce that minimizing induced edges in a contiguous $m=n-1$ structure simply requires finding the minimal connecting subtree.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The subset of parcels maps identically to the subset of modules, the contiguity definition mathematically perfectly mirrors the cohesiveness definition, and paying for borders where both adjacent parcels are included is exactly paying for the induced edges, ensuring all costs and optimal selections remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The input graph remains a tree ($m = n - 1$ and fully connected), meaning the minimal contiguous subset containing the target vertices is still the unique minimal spanning subtree, keeping the $O(n)$ time complexity intact.
