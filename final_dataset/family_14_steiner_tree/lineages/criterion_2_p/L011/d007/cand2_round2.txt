# Problem B — Energy-Cohesive Grid Activation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are configuring an active power grid from a system of **n** available components, numbered 1 to **n**. The system currently features **m** bilateral interactions. Each interaction links two distinct components and carries a positive energy cost.

The system is structured such that the entire set of **n** components is fully *energy-cohesive*. A subset of components $F$ is defined as *energy-cohesive* if every non-empty proper subset of $F$ shares at least one interaction with the remainder of $F$. (A subset consisting of a single component has no non-empty proper subsets and is inherently energy-cohesive).

You must select a subset of components to form your active grid, satisfying the following operational criteria:
1. The active grid must include a specific list of **t** *critical components*.
2. The active grid itself must be *energy-cohesive*.

When you select your active grid, any interaction that has BOTH of its linked components included in your active grid becomes energized, and you must account for its energy cost. Interactions where one or both linked components are excluded from your active grid remain dormant and cost nothing.

Your objective is to determine the minimum total energy cost of the energized interactions required to form a valid active grid.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The grid size **n** ranges from a minimal setup of 1 component up to a maximum infrastructure limit of 200,000 components. The count of critical components **t** is at least 1 and at most **n**. The number of established interactions **m** is exactly one less than **n**.
The second line provides **t** distinct integers representing the labels of the critical components.
Each of the subsequent **m** lines provides three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**. These specify an interaction between component **u** and component **v** (which are always distinct valid component IDs from 1 to **n**) with an energy cost **w**. The energy cost is at least 1 unit and capped at a maximum of 1,000,000 units.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum total energy cost of the energized interactions.
