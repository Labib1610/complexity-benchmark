<STATEMENT>
# Problem B — Energy-Cohesive Subsystems

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are analyzing a system of $n$ components. There are $m$ potential bidirectional interactions between specific pairs of components. Each interaction has a positive energy coefficient $w$.

For any subset of components $S$, we define its *internal energy* $E(S)$ as the sum of the energy coefficients of all interactions that have BOTH of their endpoints strictly within $S$. (If an interaction has one or both endpoints outside of $S$, it does not contribute to $E(S)$).

A subset of components $S$ is defined as *energy-cohesive* if it cannot be decomposed into two independent parts without a loss of internal energy. Formally, $S$ is energy-cohesive if, for every proper, non-empty subset $A \subset S$, the following strict inequality holds:
$E(S) > E(A) + E(S \setminus A)$
(A subset consisting of a single component has no proper, non-empty subsets and is inherently energy-cohesive).

The global system of all $n$ components is known to be energy-cohesive. 

You must select a subset of components to form a stabilized subsystem. Your selection must satisfy two criteria:
1. It must include a specific list of $t$ mandatory components.
2. The selected subset itself must be *energy-cohesive*.

Your objective is to find a valid subset $S$ that minimizes the internal energy $E(S)$.

## Input

The first line provides three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. 
The system architecture supports a maximum capacity of 200,000 components for $n$. The interaction count $m$ is architecturally constrained to be exactly one less than the component count. The required mandatory components $t$ can range from a single component up to the full capacity $n$.
The second line provides $t$ distinct integers representing the labels of the mandatory components.
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines provides three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, indicating an interaction between component $u$ and component $v$ with an energy coefficient of $w$. Components are assigned unique integer identifiers from 1 up to $n$, no interaction connects a component to itself, and each interaction's energy coefficient $w$ is a positive integer capped by a hardware limit of 1,000,000.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum possible internal energy $E(S)$ of a valid subsystem $S$.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

<RATIONALE>
I replaced the spatial graph connectivity requirement with an algebraically equivalent strict inequality on the objective function ($E(S) > E(A) + E(S \setminus A)$). This masks the "connected subgraph" concept as a submodular pseudo-boolean optimization constraint, forcing models to deduce the structural meaning rather than relying on surface-level graph terminology to trigger tree algorithms. Additionally, S07 was applied to express numeric bounds indirectly as system capacities.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because all energy coefficients are strictly positive, the inequality $E(S) > E(A) + E(S \setminus A)$ holds if and only if there is at least one interaction crossing the partition between $A$ and $S \setminus A$; this is the exact mathematical definition of a connected subgraph, meaning the valid subsets and their costs are identical to the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The input remains a connected graph with $n-1$ edges (a tree), so the minimal valid connected subset containing the mandatory nodes is unique and can still be computed in $O(n)$ time via a simple tree traversal.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might fail to parse "proper, non-empty subset $A \subset S$" if they lack basic set theory knowledge, though this is highly unlikely for competent models, making the risk of genuine ambiguity negligible.
</RISK>
