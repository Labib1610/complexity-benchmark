# Subspace Projection of Sparse Interaction Matrices

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are analyzing a real symmetric $N \times N$ matrix $H$ which describes the pairwise interaction costs within a system of $N$ components. All diagonal entries of $H$ are zero. The matrix is exceedingly sparse: there are exactly $K$ pairs of distinct indices $\{u, v\}$ such that $H_{u,v} = H_{v,u} > 0$. For all other pairs, $H_{u,v} = 0$.

A subset of indices $S$ is called *non-separable* if there does not exist a non-empty proper subset $X \subset S$ such that for all $x \in X$ and $y \in S \setminus X$, $H_{x,y} = 0$. The initial set of all $N$ indices $\{1, 2, \dots, N\}$ is guaranteed to be non-separable. Furthermore, the number of non-zero symmetric pairs $K$ is exactly $N - 1$.

You may perform a sequence of subspace projections to simplify the system. In a single projection step, you choose one index $k$ from the current active set $S$ and permanently discard it, which conceptually restricts the system to a new principal submatrix on $S \setminus \{k\}$. However, your sequence of projections must strictly adhere to two rules:
1. You may never discard any of the $T$ *invariant* indices, as they define the essential basis of the final subspace.
2. After every individual projection step, the newly formed subset of remaining indices must still be *non-separable*. If discarding an index would cause the remaining active indices to become separable (i.e., they could be partitioned into two non-empty subsets with purely zero cross-terms), that index cannot be discarded at that time.

You can halt the projection process at any point, including making no projections at all. Your objective is to determine the minimum possible value of half the sum of all elements in the final principal submatrix.

## Input

The first line provides three integers $N$, $K$, and $T$. The matrix dimension $N$ is at least $1$ and at most the number of pixels in a $400 \times 500$ image. $K$ is exactly $N - 1$. The number of invariant indices $T$ ranges from $1$ up to $N$ inclusive.
The second line provides $T$ distinct integers representing the indices that are invariant.
Each of the subsequent $K$ lines provides three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, indicating that $H_{u,v} = H_{v,u} = w$. The indices $u$ and $v$ are distinct and between $1$ and $N$. The interaction cost $w$ is a positive integer not exceeding the square of $1000$.

## Output

Output a single integer representing half the sum of all elements in the final principal submatrix after a valid sequence of projections.
