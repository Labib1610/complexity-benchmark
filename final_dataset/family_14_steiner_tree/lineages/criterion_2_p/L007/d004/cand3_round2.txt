# Datacenter Activation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A computing facility houses **n** server racks, indexed from 1 to **n**. The facility has a pre-existing infrastructure of **m** fiber-optic links. Each link connects two different racks and incurs a specific activation expense to power on. 

A customer has rented a specific group of **t** racks. Your objective is to power on a selection of the fiber-optic links so that every rented rack can exchange data with every other rented rack. Data can be routed through racks that the customer has not rented.

The total activation expense for your network is calculated by summing two components:
1. The individual activation expenses of all powered-on fiber-optic links.
2. A relay activation fee for every unrented rack that is included in the active communication routes.

Currently, the relay activation fee is fixed at 0 per unrented rack. Calculate the minimum total activation expense necessary to tie all the rented racks together, ensuring you incorporate both the link expenses and the relay activation fees into your final calculation.

The **m** pre-wired links may form an arbitrary undirected graph. It is guaranteed only that the rented racks are mutually reachable.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ t ≤ n, 1 ≤ n ≤ 200000, m = n − 1).
The second line provides **t** distinct integers, which are the indices of the rented racks.
The next **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1000000). This indicates a pre-wired fiber-optic link between rack **u** and rack **v** that costs **w** to power on.

## Output

Output a single integer: the minimum total activation expense to power on the required links and racks.

<RATIONALE>
Removed the explicit statement that the links guarantee exactly one unique communication route. Instead, the text states the links form an arbitrary undirected graph where the rented racks are mutually reachable. To solve this efficiently, the model must deduce that a graph with `n` vertices and `n-1` edges cannot contain cycles, meaning the components are trees and the minimal connecting subgraph is still unique.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The inputs provided by the judge are identical to the seed (which are trees), so the minimum cost to connect the rented racks remains exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
Finding the minimum cost to connect a subset of vertices in a graph with `n` vertices and `n-1` edges (a forest) reduces to finding the minimal subtree in the relevant component, which is solvable in O(n) time, keeping it in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might perceive "arbitrary undirected graph" as a contradiction to `m = n - 1`, though mathematically a forest is a valid arbitrary undirected graph with that edge count.
</RISK>
