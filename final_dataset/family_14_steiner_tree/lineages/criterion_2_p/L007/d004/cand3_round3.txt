<STATEMENT>
# Variable Unification

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are given a system of **n** independent variables, indexed from 1 to **n**. The number of variables is at most the number of centimeters in two kilometers.
You are also provided with a set of **m** potential equality constraints. Each constraint, if enforced, algebraically binds two variables **u** and **v** such that **u** = **v**. There are exactly **n** − 1 such potential constraints. The structure of these constraints guarantees that if all of them were enforced, every variable would be forced to equal every other variable, and there would be exactly one non-redundant chain of deductions proving **u** = **v** for any pair of variables.

Enforcing each constraint incurs a specific computational cost.
A specific subset of **t** variables has been flagged as critical. Your objective is to select a subset of the potential constraints to enforce such that all **t** critical variables are mathematically guaranteed to be equal to one another, based on the transitive property of equality. The equality deductions may utilize non-critical variables as intermediate steps.

Calculate the minimum total computational cost required to enforce a valid subset of constraints that unifies all the critical variables. The total cost is the sum of the costs of the enforced constraints.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The value of **n** is at least 1 and at most the number of centimeters in two kilometers. **m** is exactly **n** − 1, and **t** is between 1 and **n** inclusive.
The second line provides **t** distinct integers, which are the indices of the critical variables.
The next **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**. This indicates a potential constraint **u** = **v** that costs **w** to enforce. **u** and **v** are distinct valid variable indices. The cost **w** is at least 1 and at most the number of grams in a metric tonne. It is guaranteed that exactly one valid chain of deductions exists between any two variables when all **m** constraints are enforced.

## Output

Output a single integer: the minimum total computational cost to enforce the chosen constraints such that all critical variables are equal.

```
8

```

<RATIONALE>
The problem was completely reframed from a physical networking scenario to an abstract system of algebraic equality constraints. This forces the model to deduce that variables and equality constraints map exactly to vertices and edges in a graph, and that algebraic transitivity maps to connected components. The explicit bounds were also replaced with physical constants (e.g., centimeters in two kilometers) requiring indirect derivation.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The mathematical structure is identical: finding the minimum weight subset of edges (constraints) in a tree (m=n-1 with unique paths) to connect (make equal) a subset of vertices (variables) yields the exact same total cost for every input.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains finding the minimal subtree connecting a set of terminal nodes in a given tree structure, which is solvable in O(n) time, preserving the P complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "non-redundant chain of deductions" as implying a directed logical inference graph rather than bidirectional undirected equality, though "u = v" strongly implies symmetry.
</RISK>
