# Permutation Matrix Synthesis

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are configuring a transformation engine for an $n \times n$ state matrix, which is always initialized to the identity matrix $I$.

The engine can be equipped with a subset of bidirectional row-swap modules. You have a catalog of $m$ available modules. Each module targets two specific rows $u$ and $v$; when activated, it swaps the entire contents of row $u$ with row $v$. Each module has a one-time licensing fee $w$.

The catalog is minimally complete: the number of available modules is exactly one less than the matrix dimension, yet if you licensed all of them, there exists a sequence of modules to transform $I$ into any possible permutation matrix.

A client requires the ability to perform isolated exchanges for a specific subset of $t$ row indices, denoted as $T$. Specifically, for each individual pair of distinct indices $a, b \in T$, there must exist some sequence of your licensed modules (which may use any licensed module multiple times) that transforms $I$ into a matrix where row $a$ and row $b$ are exchanged, while every other row $k$ (for all $k \notin \{a, b\}$) remains exactly at its original position $k$. During the intermediate steps of a sequence, rows outside $T$ may be temporarily moved, but they must be restored to their original positions by the end of the sequence.

Your objective is to determine the minimum total licensing fee for a subset of modules that satisfies this requirement. Modules that are not strictly necessary to synthesize all the required isolated exchanges can be left unlicensed.

## Input

The first line provides three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. (As dictated by the engine's hardware limits, the matrix dimension $n$ does not exceed two hundred thousand. The target subset size $t$ is at least one and at most $n$. The catalog's minimal completeness ensures $m$ is exactly one less than $n$.)
The second line provides $t$ distinct integers, representing the target row indices in $T$.
The next $m$ lines each contain three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$. (The values $u$ and $v$ are distinct valid row indices from $1$ to $n$. The licensing fee $w$ is a positive integer capped at one million credits.)

## Output

Output a single integer: the minimum total licensing fee of the selected subset of modules.

<RATIONALE>
I recast the problem from group theory (symmetric groups, subgroups, transpositions) into linear algebra (permutation matrices, elementary row operations). The bounds were rewritten into prose as physical or system constraints (S07). This forces the model to realize that synthesizing isolated row exchanges from a minimal complete set of operations is equivalent to finding the minimal spanning subtree for the target components.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The requirement to synthesize an isolated exchange between rows $a$ and $b$ is mathematically identical to generating the transposition $(a, b)$; since the available operations form a minimal complete set (a tree), this still requires the selected operations to connect all target indices in a single component, preserving the optimal subset and its cost.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying mathematical task remains finding the minimum-weight Steiner tree on a given tree where the terminals are the target indices, which is solvable in linear time O(n).
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "sequence of modules" as implying the cost is the sum of costs for each step in the sequence, rather than a one-time licensing fee for the subset, though "one-time licensing fee" and "fee for a subset" strongly guard against this.
</RISK>
