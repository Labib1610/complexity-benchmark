# Problem B — Relative Calibration

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A facility contains **n** sensors, labeled from 1 to **n**. Because their absolute baseline readings are unobservable, their calibration must rely entirely on pairwise comparisons. You are given a roster of **m** potential testing operations. A single operation evaluates a specific pair of sensors and yields the precise numerical offset between them. 

The roster of operations forms a complete, minimal basis for the facility: executing all of them would yield a full-rank system of equations sufficient to calculate the relative offset between any two sensors. Because the roster is linearly independent, no operation's result can be derived from the others. Thus, there are exactly **n** − 1 operations, meaning the relative offset between any two sensors is resolved by exactly one algebraic combination of tests.

Your objective focuses on a designated priority group of **t** sensors. You need to choose a subset of the testing operations so that the relative offset between every pair of these **t** priority sensors can be mathematically deduced. You are allowed to include tests involving non-priority sensors if their variables are required to bridge the equations between the priority ones.

Each testing operation carries a specific monetary cost. Determine the minimum total cost required to fund a valid subset of operations that fully resolves the offsets among all priority sensors.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The facility's architectural limit supports up to 200000 sensors, meaning **n** is between 1 and 200000. The priority headcount **t** is at least 1 and cannot exceed **n**. The operation count **m** is exactly **n** − 1.
The second line lists **t** distinct integers representing the labels of the priority sensors.
Following this are **m** lines, each containing three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**. These indicate an operation evaluating sensors **u** and **v** (where **u** and **v** are distinct identifiers between 1 and **n**) at a cost of **w**. The monetary cost **w** is at least 1 and cannot exceed the maximum grant limit of 1000000.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum possible total cost to execute the necessary operations to deduce the offsets among all priority sensors.

<RATIONALE>
The previous statement explicitly described the operations as providing a "unique sequence of tests linking any given pair", which directly handed the models the tree structure and unique path properties. I replaced this graph-theoretic framing with a linear algebra framing ("full-rank system of equations", "linearly independent basis", "algebraic combination"). This forces models to recognize the equivalence between the algebraic independence of bipartite comparisons and acyclic graph connectivity, preventing simple pattern-matching on graph algorithms.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The requirement to mathematically deduce the offsets among priority variables from a linearly independent basis of pairwise differences is mathematically identical to finding a Steiner tree on the given graph, preserving all costs and selections exactly.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains finding the minimum weight subgraph connecting a subset of vertices in a tree, which is solvable in O(n) time by rooting the tree and counting required vertices in subtrees, keeping it in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might interpret "algebraic combination" as allowing fractional edges or continuous variables, though the discrete nature of funding specific operations (each with a fixed cost) clarifies that this is a combinatorial choice.
</RISK>
