# Problem B — Priority Infrastructure

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A university is upgrading its communication infrastructure across $n$ facilities (numbered 1 through $n$). The campus planners have outlined $m$ potential fiber optic segments, each connecting a pair of facilities and requiring a specific budget to activate. The total number of proposed segments is strictly one fewer than the number of facilities; however, the layout ensures that if every segment were activated, a communication path would exist between any pair of facilities.

The board of directors has designated $t$ specific facilities as critical. Your objective is to choose which of the potential fiber segments to activate so that data can flow between all critical facilities (either directly or by passing through other facilities, which do not need to be critical themselves). You must determine the absolute minimum total activation budget required to achieve this integration.

## Input

The first line of the manifest contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. The total facility count $n$ is constrained by the city's zoning limit of two hundred thousand structures, with a minimum of one. To minimize initial planning overhead, the proposed segment count $m$ is exactly one less than $n$. The critical facility count $t$ ranges from one up to the entire campus size $n$.

The second line lists $t$ unique integers: the identifiers of the critical facilities.

Following this are $m$ lines detailing the proposed segments. Each line provides three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, indicating a segment between distinct facilities $u$ and $v$ requiring an activation budget $w$. This individual segment budget $w$ is measured in credits, ranging from a base cost of one up to a maximum grant limit of one million credits. The proposed segments collectively provide a path between any two facilities on campus.

## Output

Print a single integer representing the minimum total activation budget required to ensure all critical facilities can communicate with one another.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

```

```

**Output**
```
9
```

<RATIONALE>
Replaced the direct mathematical inequality bounds (e.g., 1 <= n <= 200000, m = n - 1) with descriptive prose tied to the scenario's physical and administrative limits (Strategy S07). This forces the model to parse the bounds from natural language rather than relying on standard inequality patterns to quickly recognize the $m=n-1$ tree structure property.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The underlying topology, connectivity requirement, and cost minimization objectives remain exactly the same, ensuring the identical set of optimal segments is chosen.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding the minimum subgraph connecting a subset of vertices in a connected graph with $n-1$ edges, which is inherently solvable in $O(n)$ time.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "maximum grant limit of one million credits" as a constraint on the total budget rather than an individual segment, though the phrasing "individual segment budget $w$" is designed to prevent this.
</RISK>
