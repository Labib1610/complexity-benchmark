# Coherent Scalar Routing

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are configuring a power distribution grid comprising $n$ distinct substations, indexed 1 through $n$. Among these, a specific subset of $t$ substations is designated as the *critical load*, $T$.

You are provided with a catalog of $m$ potential transmission lines. Each potential line connects a pair of substations $u$ and $v$ and requires a continuous stabilization energy $w$ to maintain.

Your objective is to activate a subset of these lines such that all $t$ substations in the critical load are mutually integrated. In this grid, a set of substations is mutually integrated if there is a sequence of activated lines connecting any pair of substations within the set.

The catalog of potential lines is subject to strict structural constraints:
1. It is mathematically guaranteed that any scalar field $\phi: \{1, \dots, n\} \to \mathbb{R}$ assigned to the substations, which satisfies the zero-gradient condition $\phi(u) = \phi(v)$ for every potential line in the catalog, must be a globally constant field (i.e., $\phi(1) = \phi(2) = \dots = \phi(n)$).
2. The number of potential lines $m$ provided in the catalog is exactly the minimum number of lines theoretically required to satisfy the above scalar field property for $n$ substations.

Determine the minimum total stabilization energy (the sum of $w$ for the activated lines) required to ensure that all $t$ substations in the critical load are mutually integrated.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$). The value of $m$ is dictated by the structural constraints described above.
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers representing the indices of the substations in the critical load $T$.
Following this are $m$ lines, each containing three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), describing an available transmission line between substations $u$ and $v$ with a stabilization energy of $w$.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum possible total energy to activate a valid subset of lines that mutually integrates all substations in $T$.

<RATIONALE>
Removed all explicit references to simplicial homology, 1-dimensional complexes, Betti numbers, and explicit edge counts. The guarantee of a connected tree is now encoded entirely through a scalar field zero-gradient condition and a minimality constraint, forcing models to logically deduce that the graph is a tree before they can safely apply the linear-time unique-path subtree algorithm.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The structural constraints mathematically guarantee that the input graph is a tree, meaning there is exactly one minimal connected subgraph containing the critical load, ensuring the optimal energy sum is identical to the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The input instances remain exactly the same trees as in the seed, so the problem is still solvable in linear time by pruning or rooting the tree.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the scalar field property as implying the activated lines themselves must satisfy some continuous energy equation, though the objective clearly states it is just the sum of $w$ for the chosen subset.
</RISK>
