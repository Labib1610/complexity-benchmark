<STATEMENT>
# Benchmark Price Regulation

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are analyzing an economic market containing $n$ distinct commodities, indexed $1$ through $n$. A subset of $t$ commodities are designated as **Benchmark Commodities**.

You are evaluating a set of $m$ proposed price regulations. Each proposed regulation $d$ links two distinct commodities $u$ and $v$. If enacted, the regulation enforces that the price difference between commodity $u$ and commodity $v$ is fixed to a specific constant. Enacting this regulation incurs an enforcement cost of $w$.

The complete set of $m$ proposed regulations is globally restrictive and minimally so: if all $m$ regulations were enacted, the relative price difference between *any* two commodities in the market would be completely determined. Furthermore, the number of proposed regulations is exactly $m = n - 1$. This implies that the proposed regulations form a connected acyclic structure; between any two commodities, there is exactly one sequence of regulations that determines their relative price.

You must select a subset of these proposed regulations to enact. Your selection must satisfy the following condition: for any pair of Benchmark Commodities $a$ and $b$, their relative price difference must be completely determined solely by the regulations you have chosen to enact. (If $t=1$, this condition imposes no restrictions, as there are no pairs of Benchmark Commodities).

Your objective is to determine the minimum total enforcement cost required to enact a valid subset of regulations that meets this condition.

## Input

The first line contains three integers: $n$, $m$, and $t$.
- $n$ is the number of commodities, satisfying $1 \le n \le 200000$.
- $m$ is the number of proposed regulations, where exactly $m = n - 1$.
- $t$ is the number of Benchmark Commodities, satisfying $1 \le t \le n$.

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the indices of the Benchmark Commodities (from $1$ to $n$).

Each of the next $m$ lines details a proposed regulation with three integers: $u$, $v$, and $w$.
- $u$ and $v$ are the distinct commodity indices ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$) linked by the regulation.
- $w$ is the enforcement cost to enact this regulation. This cost is a positive integer satisfying $1 \le w \le 1000000$.

These $m$ lines form the exact structure described above, guaranteeing the full collection completely determines all relative prices.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total enforcement cost required to ensure the relative prices between all Benchmark Commodities are determined by the enacted subset.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the Benchmark Commodities are 1 and 4. The relative price between 1 and 4 is determined only if all three regulations are enacted, since the sequence links 1 to 2, 2 to 3, and 3 to 4. The minimum cost is therefore 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one Benchmark Commodity. Since no pairs of Benchmark Commodities exist, no regulations need to be enacted, resulting in a minimum cost of 0.
