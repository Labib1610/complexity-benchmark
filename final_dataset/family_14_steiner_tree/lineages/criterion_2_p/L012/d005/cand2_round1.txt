# Meteorological Synchronization

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A regional climate control system monitors $n$ atmospheric stations. Exactly $t$ of these are designated as **Primary Stations**.

The system has identified $m$ atmospheric corridors. Each corridor connects two specific stations. By expending a specific amount of energy, a corridor can be stabilized, which permanently synchronizes the barometric pressure between its two endpoints. Because pressure equilibrium is transitive, a chain of stabilized corridors ensures synchronization between its endpoints, even through intermediate stations.

You must select a subset of these corridors to stabilize. In the resulting network of stabilized corridors, all $t$ Primary Stations must be synchronized with each other. Corridors that are not stabilized do not contribute to synchronization.

Your objective is to determine the minimum total energy expenditure required to stabilize a valid subset of corridors that ensures all Primary Stations are synchronized.

The total number of available corridors $m$ is exactly $n - 1$. The geographical layout of the corridors guarantees that a valid stabilizing subset always exists, even in the extreme case where every single station in the network is designated as a Primary Station.

## Input

The first line contains three integers: $n$, $m$, and $t$.
- $n$ is the total number of stations. The system contains at least 1 station. The maximum capacity of the network is exactly the cube of 60, minus 16000.
- $m$ is the number of available corridors.
- $t$ is the number of Primary Stations, which is at least 1 and at most $n$.

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the Primary Stations (from 1 to $n$).

Each of the next $m$ lines details an available corridor with three integers: $u$, $v$, and $w$.
- $u$ and $v$ are distinct station identifiers (from 1 to $n$) linked by the corridor.
- $w$ is the energy required to stabilize this corridor. This cost is a positive integer that does not exceed exactly the sixth power of ten.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total energy expenditure required to synchronize all Primary Stations using only the stabilized corridors.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the Primary Stations 1 and 4 can only be synchronized by chaining the equilibrium through stations 2 and 3, so all three corridors (5 + 1 + 2 = 8) must be stabilized. In Example 2, there is only one Primary Station. With only one required station, no corridors need to be stabilized to synchronize it with itself, so the minimum energy expenditure is 0.

<RATIONALE>
The explicit guarantees of "no alternative deduction chains" and "covers the entire system" have been removed. Instead, the tree property is guaranteed implicitly by stating that $m = n - 1$ and that a solution would exist even if all nodes were targets. This forces the model to deduce the structural acyclic connectivity itself; models that rely on surface keywords to trigger tree-specific algorithms will likely fall back to general Steiner tree approaches and fail due to time limits. The bounds are also stated indirectly using arithmetic descriptions.
</RATIONALE>
