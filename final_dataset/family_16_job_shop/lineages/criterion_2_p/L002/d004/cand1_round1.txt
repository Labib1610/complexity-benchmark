# Problem B — Obstacle Course

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are designing an obstacle course consisting of **j** segments arranged in a sequence. Each segment has two parallel lanes: a North lane and a South lane. You are given the time it takes to complete the North lane obstacle, followed by the time for the South lane obstacle, for each of the **j** segments.

You must choose the order in which these **j** segments are placed to form the course.

A challenger will run the course from the first segment to the last. The challenger starts in the North lane. At exactly one segment of their choice, the challenger will switch from the North lane to the South lane. Specifically, if they choose to switch at segment $k$ ($1 \le k \le j$), they will incur the time for the North lane obstacles for segments $1$ through $k$, and then immediately incur the time for the South lane obstacles for segments $k$ through $j$. 

The challenger is an adversary who will evaluate your chosen sequence and always choose the switch point $k$ that maximizes their total completion time.

Your goal is to choose the sequence of the **j** segments that minimizes this maximum possible total time. Output this minimum possible maximum time.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the duration required for the North lane obstacle and the South lane obstacle of a segment, respectively.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible total time the adversary can be forced to achieve.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 1, if you arrange the segments in the order of the 3rd, 2nd, and 1st from the input, their North lane durations are 1, 2, 3 and South lane durations are 3, 4, 2.
- If the adversary switches at the first segment, their time is 1 (North) + 3 + 4 + 2 (South) = 10.
- If they switch at the second segment, their time is 1 + 2 (North) + 4 + 2 (South) = 9.
- If they switch at the third segment, their time is 1 + 2 + 3 (North) + 2 (South) = 8.
The adversary will choose to switch at the first segment to maximize their time at 10. No other arrangement of the segments can force the adversary's maximum time to be strictly less than 10.

In Example 2, the single segment takes 5 for the North lane and then 3 for the South lane, for a total time of 8.
