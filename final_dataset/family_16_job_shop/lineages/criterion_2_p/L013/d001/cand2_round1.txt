# Problem B — Mountain Railway

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A transport company has **j** cargo trains waiting in a valley that must be routed to a mountain summit. The journey consists of two sequential single-track segments: the Lower Track (from the valley to a midway plateau) and the Upper Track (from the midway plateau to the summit). 

For each train, you are given its travel time on the Lower Track and its travel time on the Upper Track. The network operates under strict safety protocols:

- The Lower Track can only support one train at a time.
- The Upper Track can only support one train at a time.
- A train can only enter the Upper Track after it has fully completed its journey on the Lower Track.
- The midway plateau is large enough for trains to wait if the Upper Track is currently occupied by another train.
- You must determine a single dispatch sequence for the trains. They will enter the Lower Track in this sequence, and they must also enter the Upper Track in this exact same sequence. 
- The two tracks can be active simultaneously (for instance, one train can climb the Lower Track while an earlier train climbs the Upper Track).

To ensure the summit station can finalize its operations for the day as early as possible, choose the sequence that minimizes the total time required for all trains to reach the summit. Report the earliest possible time at which the final train arrives at the summit.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **L** and **U** (1 ≤ L, U ≤ 1000000): the travel times of a train on the Lower Track and the Upper Track, respectively.

## Output

Print a single integer: the earliest possible arrival time of the last train at the summit.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, the single train takes 5 on the Lower Track and then 3 on the Upper Track, arriving at the summit at time 8.

The problem was completely rewritten to move away from the standard "jobs on machines" formulation of the flow-shop scheduling problem. By framing it as trains traveling on sequential single-track segments with a midway waiting plateau, the mechanics of the two-machine flow-shop are perfectly preserved while stripping away the vocabulary (jobs, machines, processing time) that models use to trigger memorized templates for Johnson's rule. Models must now structurally map the track constraints to the flow-shop algorithm.
