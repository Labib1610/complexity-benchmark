# Problem B — Canyon Descent

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are mapping a route through a sequence of **j** consecutive canyon zones. You must decide the strict order in which to arrange these **j** zones.

Each zone has a high-ridge trail and a riverbed trail. For each zone, you are given the time it takes to traverse its high-ridge trail, **t1**, and the time it takes to traverse its riverbed trail, **t2**.

Your journey begins on the high-ridge trail of the first zone in your sequence. You must eventually drop down to the riverbed trail and finish your journey at the end of the riverbed trail of the last zone. You are required to drop down exactly once. 

Specifically, if you drop down in the **k**-th zone of your sequence (where 1 ≤ **k** ≤ **j**), your total travel time is calculated as exactly the sum of:
- The high-ridge trail times for all zones from the 1st up to the **k**-th zone in your sequence, inclusive.
- The riverbed trail times for all zones from the **k**-th up to the **j**-th zone in your sequence, inclusive.
(Note that for the **k**-th zone, both its high-ridge time and its riverbed time are included in your total).

A rival explorer wants to maximize your travel time. After you choose the sequence of the **j** zones, the rival will choose the drop-down zone **k** that makes your total travel time as large as possible.

Your task is to determine the optimal sequence of the **j** zones that minimizes the maximum possible travel time the rival can force upon you.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains exactly 2 integers **t1** and **t2** (1 ≤ t1, t2 ≤ 1000000): the high-ridge trail time and the riverbed trail time of a zone.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible travel time you can guarantee, assuming the rival chooses the worst-case drop-down zone for your sequence.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, there is only one zone ($j=1$). The only possible drop-down zone the rival can choose is $k=1$. The total travel time is the sum of the high-ridge trail times up to zone 1 (which is 5) plus the riverbed trail times from zone 1 onwards (which is 3), resulting in a guaranteed time of 5 + 3 = 8.
