<STATEMENT>
# Problem B — Canal Convoy

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A fleet of **j** vessels is arranged end-to-end to form a temporary pontoon bridge down a canal. You must determine the exact left-to-right sequence of the fleet.

Each vessel features two parallel walkways: an upper deck and a lower deck. For each vessel, you are given the duration required to walk across its upper deck, **t1**, and the duration required to walk across its lower deck, **t2**. 

An inspector will cross the pontoon bridge from left to right. The crossing adheres strictly to the following rules:
- The inspector starts by traversing the upper deck of the first (leftmost) vessel in your sequence.
- After traversing the upper deck of any vessel, the inspector must either step forward to traverse the upper deck of the next vessel (if there is one), or descend to traverse the lower deck of the current vessel.
- After traversing the lower deck of any vessel, the inspector must step forward to traverse the lower deck of the next vessel (if there is one).
- The crossing concludes immediately after the inspector has traversed the lower deck of the final (rightmost) vessel in your sequence.
- The total duration of a route is the sum of the walking durations of every deck the inspector traverses. (Descending between decks takes 0 time).

Consequently, the inspector will traverse the upper decks of some number of initial vessels, descend at exactly one vessel, and then traverse the lower decks of all remaining vessels to the end.

The inspector is adversarial: after you choose the sequence of the vessels, the inspector will evaluate all valid routes and take the path that **maximizes** their total walking duration.

Your task is to arrange the fleet into a sequence that **minimizes** this maximum possible duration.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains exactly 2 integers **t1** and **t2** (1 ≤ t1, t2 ≤ 1000000): the upper deck duration and the lower deck duration of a vessel.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible duration the inspector can be forced to take, assuming they always choose the longest route for your given sequence.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, there is only one vessel. The inspector starts by traversing its upper deck (duration 5), must descend because there is no next vessel, and then traverses its lower deck (duration 3). The total duration is 5 + 3 = 8.

<RATIONALE>
The problem has been mathematically transformed from a 2-machine flow shop makespan minimization into minimizing the maximum path sum on a 2xN grid where columns are permutable. This perfectly preserves the underlying mathematical structure while completely removing all scheduling semantics (machines, locks, back-to-back processing). To succeed, models must now genuinely recognize the algebraic isomorphism between the grid's minimax path sum and the flow-shop makespan to realize Johnson's rule still applies.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The maximum path sum for any given sequence of columns in a 2xN grid is algebraically identical to the makespan of the same sequence of jobs in a 2-machine flow shop, meaning the optimal permutation and minimal maximum value remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly the same mathematical optimization problem over permutations, leaving it perfectly solvable in O(N log N) using Johnson's rule.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
