# Problem B — Canal Convoy

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A fleet of vessels must travel down a narrow canal to a dock. Each vessel requires a specific duration to navigate the channel, and a subsequent duration to secure its mooring at the destination dock.

Because the canal is strictly one-lane and overtaking is physically impossible, the vessels must be dispatched one at a time. Your task is to choose the optimal dispatch sequence for the fleet.

Once the sequence is chosen, the timeline of events is strictly determined by the physical constraints:
- The first dispatched vessel begins navigating the channel at time 0.
- The channel is governed by a strict follow-the-leader rule: each subsequent vessel begins navigating the channel at the exact moment the preceding vessel in the dispatch sequence completes its navigation.
- The dock requires a dedicated harbor master, meaning only one vessel can actively secure its mooring at a time. A vessel begins securing its mooring as early as possible—specifically, at the exact moment it has finished navigating the channel, provided the preceding vessel in the dispatch sequence has completely finished securing its mooring. (The first dispatched vessel begins securing its mooring the exact moment its navigation ends).

You are given the navigation duration and mooring duration for each vessel. Determine the dispatch sequence that minimizes the time at which the final vessel completes securing its mooring. Output this minimum possible completion time.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j**, the number of vessels. The fleet size **j** can be as small as a single solitary ship, and cannot exceed the regional port's maximum capacity of two hundred thousand vessels.
Each of the next **j** lines contains exactly 2 integers **t1** and **t2**: the channel navigation duration and the mooring duration of a vessel. Every maneuver takes at least a single unit of time, and due to fuel constraints, no duration will ever exceed one million units of time.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible time at which the last vessel completes securing its mooring.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, the single vessel takes 5 to navigate the channel, finishing at time 5. It immediately begins securing its mooring, taking 3, and finishes at time 8.
