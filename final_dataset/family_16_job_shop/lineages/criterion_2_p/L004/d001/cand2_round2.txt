<STATEMENT>
# Problem B — Void Channeling

Time limit: 1 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A coven of warlocks is preparing $j$ magical rituals. Each ritual requires two consecutive phases to be executed: inscribing a runic circle, and subsequently channeling void energy. 

The coven possesses one dedicated Inscriber and one dedicated Channeler. Before beginning, they must establish a single sequence in which the Inscriber will process the rituals. 

The mechanics of the ritual execution are as follows:
- The Inscriber works through the rituals back-to-back in the chosen sequence, completely inscribing one circle at a time.
- The Channeler handles the void energy phases one at a time. Unlike the Inscriber, the Channeler does not follow a pre-assigned sequence. Instead, they simply watch for completed circles.
- Whenever the Channeler is not actively channeling, they will immediately begin channeling void energy for a ritual whose circle has been completely inscribed. If multiple inscribed circles are waiting, the Channeler always selects the one whose inscription was finished the earliest.
- Once either member begins their respective phase for a ritual, they work continuously until that phase is completed.

For each ritual, you are provided the duration it takes to inscribe its circle, and the duration it takes to channel its void energy.

You must determine the optimal sequence for the Inscriber to follow. Your objective is to find the sequence that minimizes the total time elapsed from the moment the Inscriber begins the first circle until the moment the Channeler completes the final channeling phase, and report this minimum possible total time.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer $j$ ($1 \le j \le 200000$).
Each of the next $j$ lines contains two integers $p_1$ and $p_2$ ($1 \le p_1, p_2 \le 1000000$): the time required to inscribe the circle and the time required to channel the void energy for that ritual, respectively.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible time at which all rituals can be completely finished.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 1, if the Inscriber processes the rituals in the order 3, then 2, then 1:
- **Ritual 3**: The Inscriber works from time 0 to 1. At time 1, its circle is complete. The Channeler is idle and immediately works on Ritual 3 from time 1 to 4.
- **Ritual 2**: The Inscriber works from time 1 to 3. At time 3, its circle is complete. The Channeler is still busy, so Ritual 2 waits. At time 4, the Channeler finishes Ritual 3 and begins Ritual 2, working from time 4 to 8.
- **Ritual 1**: The Inscriber works from time 3 to 6. At time 6, its circle is complete. The Channeler is busy until time 8. At time 8, the Channeler begins Ritual 1, working from time 8 to 10.

The final completion time is 10. No other sequence results in a smaller total time.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The models relied on the explicit phrase "both members will adhere strictly to this sequence" to pattern-match the problem as a permutation flow-shop solvable by Johnson's rule. I removed this affordance by changing the Channeler's behavior to a dynamic FIFO queue ("always selects the one whose inscription was finished the earliest"). To apply Johnson's rule now, a model must deduce that because the Inscriber processes rituals sequentially and $p_1 \ge 1$, the completion times are strictly increasing, meaning the FIFO queue naturally and unavoidably perfectly mirrors the Inscriber's chosen sequence.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because $p_1 \ge 1$ for all rituals, no two inscriptions can finish simultaneously; the strictly increasing completion times guarantee that the Channeler's FIFO queue processes the rituals in the exact same order the Inscriber started them, making the makespan mathematically identical to the seed for every input.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

</PRESERVED_CLASS>
