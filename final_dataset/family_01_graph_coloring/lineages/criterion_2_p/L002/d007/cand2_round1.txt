# Tiered Settlement Obligations

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A financial clearinghouse is migrating $n$ institutional accounts to a new regulatory framework. Under this framework, each account $i$ must be assigned a compliance tier $t_i$, strictly designated as either `1` or `2`.

The clearinghouse has identified $m$ reciprocal obligations between specific pairs of accounts. To satisfy risk-margin requirements, any two accounts $u$ and $v$ involved in a reciprocal obligation must be assigned tiers $t_u$ and $t_v$ such that they can form a valid joint supervisory committee. 

The rule for the committee is evaluated by constructing a specific $3 \times 3$ matrix $M$ for the obligation:
$$
M = \begin{pmatrix} 
t_u & 1 & 1 \\ 
1 & t_v & 1 \\ 
1 & 1 & \frac{t_u + t_v}{3} 
\end{pmatrix}
$$
The committee is considered valid if and only if the determinant of this matrix $M$ is exactly zero.

If there is no way to assign tiers to all $n$ accounts such that every reciprocal obligation satisfies this committee rule, the migration must be halted. When multiple valid tier assignments are possible, the compliance protocol mandates selecting the assignment that strictly minimizes the polynomial evaluation $P(2) = \sum_{i=1}^n t_i \cdot 2^{n-i}$.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($1 \le n \le 2\cdot 10^5$, $0 \le m \le 2\cdot 10^5$), representing the number of accounts and the number of reciprocal obligations.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$) — a pair of accounts that share a reciprocal obligation.

## Output
If no valid tier assignment can be found, print `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $t_1 \dots t_n$ (each either `1` or `2`), denoting the compliance tier for accounts 1 through $n$. This sequence must be the one that minimizes the evaluation polynomial.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 4
1 2
2 3
3 4
1 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 1 2
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
3 3
1 2
1 3
2 3
```
Output:
```
NO
```

The direct statement about arranging primary and secondary audits such that no two are adjacent was replaced with a matrix determinant condition. The determinant of the provided matrix evaluates to zero if and only if $t_u \neq t_v$ for $t_u, t_v \in \{1, 2\}$. Furthermore, the explicit "lexicographically smallest" tie-breaker was replaced with minimizing a base-2 polynomial, which is mathematically identical but forces models to recognize the equivalence to lexicographical order. This breaks the surface-level pattern matching to 2-coloring and BFS.
</RATIONALE>
