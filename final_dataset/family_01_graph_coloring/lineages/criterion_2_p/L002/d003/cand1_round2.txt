<STATEMENT>
# Module 2: Clearance Allocation

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The corporate review pipeline operates in three consecutive stages. Initially, an analytics engine processes historical incident logs to identify critical interactions between personnel that demand rigorous monitoring.

You are responsible for the second stage: the security clearance allocator. Given the list of critical interactions, your allocator must distribute a clearance designation of either `1` or `2` to each of the $n$ employees. For operational stability, any pair of employees $u$ and $v$ involved in a flagged interaction must satisfy a strict checksum requirement. Specifically, the greatest common divisor of the clearance designation assigned to $u$ and the clearance designation assigned to $v$, added to their least common multiple, must sum to exactly 3.

Should no such distribution of clearance designations exist, the allocator must trigger a workflow abort. If multiple valid distributions can be formed, the protocol mandates selecting the sequence of designations (ordered from employee 1 to $n$) that is lexicographically smallest.

The resulting designations are then passed to the final stage, the compensation system, which adjusts shift payments accordingly.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($1 \le n \le 2\cdot 10^5$, $0 \le m \le 2\cdot 10^5$), representing the number of personnel and the number of flagged interactions.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$) — a pair of employees whose clearance designations must satisfy the checksum requirement.

## Output
If no valid clearance distribution can be found, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $s_1 \dots s_n$ (each either `1` or `2`), denoting the clearance designation for employees 1 through $n$. This sequence must be the lexicographically smallest valid distribution.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 4
1 2
2 3
3 4
1 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 1 2
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
3 3
1 2
1 3
2 3
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Changed the checksum condition from a modulo arithmetic expression to a sum of the greatest common divisor and least common multiple. This mathematically preserves the requirement that the two designations (which must be 1 and 2) are distinct, but forces the model to logically deduce this property rather than pattern-matching the modulo formulation directly to a bipartite graph opposite-color condition.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The GCD and LCM of 1 and 2 sum to 3, while the sum for identical designations (1 and 1, or 2 and 2) evaluates to 2 and 4 respectively, meaning the valid assignments remain exactly those where interacting employees have different designations.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying structure remains exactly equivalent to finding the lexicographically smallest 2-coloring of a graph, which is solvable in O(n+m) time using Breadth-First Search.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the GCD and LCM operations as applying to the employee indices rather than their assigned clearance designations, though the text explicitly states "the clearance designation assigned to u".
</RISK>
