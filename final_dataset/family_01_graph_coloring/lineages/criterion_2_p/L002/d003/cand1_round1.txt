# Module 2: Clearance Allocation

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The corporate review pipeline operates in three consecutive stages. Initially, an analytics engine processes historical incident logs to identify critical interactions between personnel that demand rigorous monitoring.

You are responsible for the second stage: the security clearance allocator. Given the list of critical interactions, your allocator must distribute a strictly positive integer clearance designation (i.e., an integer greater than zero) to each of the $n$ employees. For operational stability, any pair of employees $u$ and $v$ involved in a flagged interaction must satisfy a strict checksum requirement. Specifically, the greatest common divisor of $u$'s designation and $v$'s designation, added to the least common multiple of their designations, must sum to exactly 3.

Should no such distribution of clearance designations exist, the allocator must trigger a workflow abort. If multiple valid distributions can be formed, the protocol mandates selecting the sequence of designations (ordered from employee 1 to $n$) that is lexicographically smallest.

The resulting designations are then passed to the final stage, the compensation system, which adjusts shift payments accordingly.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The headcount $n$ ranges from a single employee up to the facility's maximum capacity of two hundred thousand. The analytics engine's backlog of flagged interactions $m$ starts from empty (zero) and can reach the same maximum limit of two hundred thousand.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ (valid employee identifiers from $1$ to $n$) — a pair of employees whose clearance designations must satisfy the checksum requirement.

## Output
If no valid clearance distribution can be found, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $s_1 \dots s_n$, denoting the clearance designation for employees 1 through $n$. This sequence must be the lexicographically smallest valid distribution.

## Example 1
Input:
4 4
1 2
2 3
3 4
1 4
Output:
YES
1 2 1 2

## Example 2
Input:
3 3
1 2
1 3
2 3
Output:
NO

<RATIONALE>
I replaced the explicit binary `{1, 2}` domain with the set of all strictly positive integers, and changed the modulo checksum to a GCD and LCM sum constraint. This forces the model to mathematically deduce that the only valid designations are 1 and 2, preventing simple pattern-matching on the explicit binary domain while keeping the underlying graph structure identical. I also applied the S07 strategy by removing bare inequalities and expressing the numeric bounds indirectly through scenario capacities.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The only strictly positive integer pairs that satisfy $\gcd(u,v) + \operatorname{lcm}(u,v) = 3$ are $\{1, 2\}$, meaning the valid assignments and lexicographical tie-breaking remain exactly identical to the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
