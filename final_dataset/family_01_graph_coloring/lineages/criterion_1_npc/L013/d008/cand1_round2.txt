<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
The simple inequality e_u != e_v is hidden behind a quadratic equation whose discriminant simplifies to (u-v)^2 (e_u - e_v)^2. By incorporating the vertex indices u and v into the coefficients, the constraint appears to be edge-specific rather than a uniform coloring constraint, preventing models from matching it to 3-coloring.
</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Quantum Resonance Synthesis

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are configuring a network of $n$ quantum resonance emitters. Each emitter $i$ (where $1 \le i \le n$) must be assigned an excitation state $e_i$, chosen from the discrete energy levels $\{1, 2, 3\}$.

The network's stability is constrained by $m$ proximity conditions. Each condition specifies two distinct emitters, identified by their indices $u$ and $v$, which share a quantum entanglement channel. To avoid destructive interference, their assigned states must satisfy a specific non-harmonic requirement.

Specifically, for each condition specifying emitters $u$ and $v$, define two channel coefficients based on their assigned states $e_u$ and $e_v$:
- $C_1 = (u+v)(e_u+e_v)$
- $C_2 = uv(e_u+e_v)^2 + (u-v)^2 e_u e_v$

For the entanglement channel to remain stable, the quadratic equation $t^2 - C_1 t + C_2 = 0$ must have two distinct real solutions for $t$. You must ensure this requirement holds for all $m$ conditions.

Your task is to determine whether there exists a global assignment of excitation states for all $n$ emitters such that all $m$ proximity conditions are satisfied simultaneously. If such an assignment exists, you must provide one. Providing any single valid assignment is sufficient, even if multiple exist.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — the total number of emitters and the number of proximity conditions ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n(n-1)/2$).
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating a condition that the emitters $u$ and $v$ must satisfy the non-harmonic requirement.

## Output
If it is impossible to assign excitation states to all emitters such that every condition is satisfied, print a single line containing `NO`.
If it is possible, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n$ separated by spaces, where each $e_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ indicates the excitation state assigned to emitter $i$. You only need to supply one valid assignment, even if multiple are possible.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Replaced the symmetric modular arithmetic constraint (which models instantly recognized as requiring distinct states) with a quadratic equation constraint based on edge-specific coefficients $C_1$ and $C_2$. The discriminant simplifies to $(u-v)^2(e_u-e_v)^2 > 0$, exactly preserving the $e_u \neq e_v$ requirement while forcing models to perform algebraic deduction rather than pattern-matching. Banned complexity vocabulary (specifically "polynomial") was removed to pass the safety gate.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that the quadratic equation has two distinct real roots is mathematically equivalent to its discriminant being strictly positive; since the discriminant equals $(u-v)^2(e_u-e_v)^2$ and $u \neq v$, this holds if and only if $e_u \neq e_v$, matching the original constraint.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly Graph 3-Coloring, which is NP-complete, as the constraint between any pair of connected vertices simplifies strictly to them having distinct assignments from a set of size 3.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might struggle with the algebraic expansion of the discriminant and incorrectly conclude the problem requires floating-point arithmetic or complex numbers, but the integers-only nature of $C_1$ and $C_2$ and the standard quadratic formula make it unambiguous.
</RISK>
