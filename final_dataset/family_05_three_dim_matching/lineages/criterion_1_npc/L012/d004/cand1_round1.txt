<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Resonance Calibration

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A geological scanner operates in a subterranean grid. Every location in the grid is defined by a depth **x**, a latitude **y**, and a longitude **z**.

The scanner has identified **t** resonance pockets at specific `(x, y, z)` locations. The coordinates for these pockets are integers between 1 and **k**, inclusive.

Each resonance pocket emits a signature across three distinct frequency bands based on its location. Specifically, a pocket at coordinates `(x, y, z)` emits:
- A power of $2^x$ in the first frequency band.
- A power of $2^y$ in the second frequency band.
- A power of $2^z$ in the third frequency band.

The scanner's memory bank has exactly **k** slots for resonance pockets. You must load exactly **k** distinct pockets from the identified list into the scanner. When the pockets are loaded, their emissions sum together in each frequency band.

Once loaded, the scanner runs a diagnostic check on the total combined power in each band. For the calibration to be successful, the chosen subset of **k** pockets must satisfy all three of the following conditions simultaneously:
- The total combined power in the first frequency band must be exactly $2^{k+1} - 2$.
- The total combined power in the second frequency band must be exactly $2^{k+1} - 2$.
- The total combined power in the third frequency band must be exactly $2^{k+1} - 2$.

Determine whether a successful calibration is possible, and if so, report one valid selection of resonance pockets.

## Input

The first line contains two integers **k** and **t** (1 ≤ k ≤ 22, 0 ≤ t ≤ k·k·k): the number of slots in the scanner and the number of identified resonance pockets.
Each of the next **t** lines contains three integers **x**, **y**, **z** (1 ≤ x, y, z ≤ k), the coordinates of a resonance pocket. No location is listed more than once.

## Output

If a valid calibration configuration of exactly **k** pockets exists, print `YES` on the first line. Then, print **k** lines, each containing the three coordinates `x y z` of one selected pocket. Any valid selection is accepted, and the pockets may be printed in any order.

If no valid selection exists, print `NO`.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
2 3
1 1 1
1 2 2
2 2 2
```

**Output**
```
YES
1 1 1
2 2 2
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
2 2
1 1 1
2 1 2
```

**Output**
```
NO
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
1 1
1 1 1
```

**Output**
```
YES
1 1 1
```

## Note

In Example 2, both identified resonance pockets have a latitude (**y**) of 1. If both are loaded to fill the 2 slots, the total combined power in the second frequency band will be $2^1 + 2^1 = 4$. However, the scanner requires the combined power in the second band to be exactly $2^{2+1} - 2 = 6$. Thus, a successful calibration is impossible.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Replaced the explicit coordinate-coverage requirements with an algebraic equivalent: the sum of $2^{coord}$ over the $k$ pockets must equal $2^{k+1}-2$. This forces models to logically deduce that exact coordinate coverage is required (since exactly $k$ powers of 2 can sum to this value if and only if they are all distinct) rather than pattern-matching directly to Exact Cover or 3-Dimensional Matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The algebraic condition mathematically guarantees that the $k$ chosen coordinates are a permutation of $1 \dots k$, which is exactly equivalent to the original requirement that every coordinate value is covered by exactly one pocket, ensuring all valid and invalid configurations remain identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying combinatorial constraints are completely unchanged, so the problem remains exactly 3-Dimensional Matching (an NP-complete problem) disguised under an algebraic condition.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models or humans might fail to deduce the mathematical equivalence and write a backtracking search that does not prune early based on used coordinates, leading to a Time Limit Exceeded. However, the logical equivalence is mathematically unambiguous.
</RISK>
