<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Synthetic Protein Signatures

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are standardizing a synthetic protein chain consisting of $n$ distinct domains. The design is subject to a strict mass limit of 400 kilodaltons (kDa). Every domain has a mass of exactly 10 kDa, and a valid chain must contain at least one domain. Each domain must be modified with exactly one of three available isotopes, which are identified by the integers 1, 2, and 3.

During the folding process, certain pairs of domains come into direct physical contact. You are given a list of $m$ such contact pairs. The number of contact pairs $m$ can range from zero up to the theoretical maximum where every distinct pair of domains touches. If two domains in a contact pair are modified with the identical isotope, they will bond incorrectly and cause the protein to misfold. Therefore, for every given contact pair, the two domains must be assigned strictly different isotopes.

Please note that the evaluation system includes specific maximum-size contact configurations, such as sparse valid structures and minimally invalid instances, structured to maximize the number of branches explored by chronological fixed-order search procedures.

Determine if there is a valid isotope assignment for all $n$ domains that prevents misfolding.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — the number of domains and the number of contact pairs.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a contact pair. Domains are numbered from 1 to $n$.

## Output
If no valid assignment exists, print a single line `NO`.
Otherwise print `YES` on the first line. On the second line print $n$ integers $t_1, t_2, \dots, t_n$ (each in $\{1, 2, 3\}$), where $t_i$ is the isotope of domain $i$. Among all valid assignments output the lexicographically smallest sequence (compare $t_1$ first, then $t_2$, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit inequalities for $n$ and $m$ were removed and replaced with physical mass limits (400 kDa max limit, 10 kDa per domain) to force the reader to derive the bounds. Furthermore, a note was added warning that the test cases are structured to maximize the branches explored by chronological fixed-order searches. This baits models into optimizing their variable ordering (e.g. using Minimum Remaining Values), which inherently breaks the lexicographically-smallest output requirement unless carefully accounted for.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The problem still asks for the lexicographically smallest valid 3-coloring of a graph, meaning the correct output for any given input remains exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem requires assigning one of 3 labels to vertices such that no adjacent vertices share a label, which is exactly the NP-complete 3-COLORING problem.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "maximum-size contact configurations" as implying $m$ is always at its maximum, though the input section clarifies that $m$ is provided explicitly.
</RISK>
