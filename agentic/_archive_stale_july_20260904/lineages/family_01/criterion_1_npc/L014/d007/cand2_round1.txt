<METHOD_NAME>Matrix Determinant Gadget</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the dot product of 3D vectors with the determinant of the sum of two 3x3 matrices. The matrices are chosen such that det(X+X)=16 and det(X+Y)=0 for X!=Y. By shifting the target sum, the exact same mathematical constraint (proper 3-coloring) is enforced, but models relying on recognizing the specific vector values from Karp's reductions will fail to pattern-match it and will likely write an unpruned exponential search.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Independent Audits

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing the annual compliance audit for a corporation with $n$ departments. The company requires each department to be audited by exactly one of three external agencies, labeled 1, 2, and 3.

Because departments collaborate, there are $m$ joint ventures between specific pairs of departments. The audit must be structured to meet a strict regulatory metric across these collaborations.

Each auditing agency is assigned a 3-by-3 operational matrix:

Agency 1 operates with:
$X_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

Agency 2 operates with:
$X_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

Agency 3 operates with:
$X_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

For every joint venture between two departments $u$ and $v$ (assigned to agencies $f_u$ and $f_v$ respectively), an "interaction value" is computed. This value is defined as the determinant of the sum of their assigned agencies' matrices, and then subtracting 7 from that determinant. Mathematically, the interaction value is $\det(X_{f_u} + X_{f_v}) - 7$.

An assignment of agencies to all $n$ departments is legally compliant if and only if the total sum of the interaction values over all $m$ joint ventures equals exactly $-7m$.

The corporate network can be highly interconnected, and the list of joint ventures is provided in no particular order. You must determine whether a compliant assignment exists.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$ — the number of departments and joint ventures, respectively ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a joint venture between department $u$ and department $v$. Departments are indexed from 1 to $n$.

## Output
If there is no way to assign agencies to satisfy the compliance metric, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ (where $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is the agency for department $i$). If multiple valid assignments exist, you must provide the one that is lexicographically smallest (comparing the agency of department 1 first, then department 2, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The dot product of the 3D vectors in the seed problem was a recognizable reduction gadget that models used to immediately map the problem to 3-coloring. This rewrite replaces those vectors with 3x3 matrices where the determinant of their sums yields 16 for identical agencies and 0 for different agencies. By subtracting 7, the interaction value becomes 9 for identical and -7 for different agencies. To hit a total sum of exactly -7m, every joint venture must contribute -7, perfectly preserving the 3-coloring constraint while forcing the model to algebraically evaluate the matrices to discover the necessary pruning condition.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The interaction value is exactly -7 if two departments share different agencies and 9 if they share the same agency; achieving a total sum of -7m over m edges strictly requires every edge to connect different agencies, which is identical to the seed's proper 3-coloring requirement.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem requires assigning one of 3 states to each node such that no two adjacent nodes share the same state, which is exactly the NP-complete 3-COLORING problem.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
