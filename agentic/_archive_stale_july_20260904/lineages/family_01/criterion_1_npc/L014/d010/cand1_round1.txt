<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Independent Audits

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing the annual compliance audit for a corporation with $n$ departments (indexed 1 to $n$). The company requires each department to be audited by exactly one of three external agencies, labeled 1, 2, and 3. Let $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ denote the agency assigned to department $i$.

Because departments collaborate, there are $m$ joint ventures between specific pairs of departments. The audit must be structured to meet a strict regulatory metric across these collaborations.

First, for each department $i$, we define its *network weight* $C_i$ as the sum of $(i + v)$ for all departments $v$ that share a joint venture with department $i$. (If a department has no joint ventures, its network weight is 0). 
The *baseline risk* of department $i$ is then calculated as:
$C_i \times (5 f_i^2 - f_i^4)$

Second, for every joint venture between two departments $u$ and $v$, the *collaborative load* is calculated based on their assigned agencies:
$L(u, v) = (u + v) \times f_u f_v (4 f_u^2 - 6 f_u f_v + 4 f_v^2 - 10)$
*(Note: This formula is symmetric, so the order of $u$ and $v$ does not matter.)*

The *total system metric* is the sum of the baseline risks across all $n$ departments, plus the sum of the collaborative loads across all $m$ joint ventures.

Let $K$ be the sum of $(u+v)$ over all $m$ joint ventures. An assignment of agencies to all $n$ departments is legally compliant if and only if the total system metric is exactly equal to $4K$.

The corporate network can be highly interconnected, and the list of joint ventures is provided in no particular order. You must determine whether a compliant assignment exists.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$ — the number of departments and joint ventures, respectively ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a joint venture between department $u$ and department $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$).

## Output
If there is no way to assign agencies to satisfy the compliance metric, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ (where $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is the agency for department $i$). If multiple valid assignments exist, you must provide the one that is lexicographically smallest (comparing the agency of department 1 first, then department 2, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

The previous versions were defeated because the equivalence to proper 3-coloring could be deduced by evaluating the isolated terms for an edge and seeing they formed a simple 3x3 table of extremum values. 
This rewrite uses a "Topology-Entangled Polynomial". The 3-coloring condition $(f_u - f_v)^2(5 - (f_u - f_v)^2)$ is expanded into a degree-4 polynomial, which is then mathematically split into vertex-centric terms and edge-centric terms. These terms are further entangled with the graph's topology by multiplying them by $(u+v)$ and shifting the vertex terms into a global degree-based sum ($C_i$). To recognize the problem as 3-coloring and prune the DFS, the model must algebraically reverse-engineer this global sum and factor the polynomial, preventing any simple pattern-matching or isolated-term evaluation.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The global sum algebraically simplifies to $\sum_{(u,v)} (u+v) (f_u - f_v)^2 (5 - (f_u - f_v)^2)$, which evaluates to $4(u+v)$ if $f_u \neq f_v$ and $0$ if $f_u = f_v$; since the target is exactly $4 \sum (u+v)$, the condition is satisfied if and only if $f_u \neq f_v$ for all edges, preserving the exact proper 3-coloring requirement.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
