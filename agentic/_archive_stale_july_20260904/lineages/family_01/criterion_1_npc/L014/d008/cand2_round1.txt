# Problem A — Independent Audits

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing the annual compliance audit for a corporation with $n$ departments. The company requires each department to be audited by exactly one of three external agencies, labeled 1, 2, and 3. 

The corporation assigns each department its own dedicated manager. The board of directors consists of 8 senior partners, each overseeing exactly 5 potential department managers. Thus, the number of departments $n$ is at least 1 and cannot exceed the total managerial capacity.

Because departments collaborate, there are $m$ joint ventures between specific pairs of departments. Joint ventures can exist between any two distinct departments. The number of such ventures $m$ ranges from none at all up to a complete network where every unique pair of departments shares exactly one venture.

The audit must be structured to meet a strict regulatory metric across these collaborations. For every joint venture between two departments $x$ and $y$ (which are assigned agencies $f_x$ and $f_y$, respectively), we define their agency shift values as $a = f_x - 1$ and $b = f_y - 1$. 

The interaction factor $W$ for this joint venture is computed using the following expression:
$$ W = -18(a^2 + b^2) - 3ab(1 + a + b) + 54(a+b) - 3a^2 b^2 - 24 $$

An assignment of agencies to all $n$ departments is legally compliant if and only if the overall product of the interaction factors across all $m$ joint ventures equals exactly $12^m$ (if $m=0$, the overall product is defined as 1).

The corporate network can be highly interconnected, and the list of joint ventures is provided in no particular order. You must determine whether a compliant assignment exists.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$ — the number of departments and joint ventures, respectively.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $x$ and $y$, representing a joint venture between department $x$ and department $y$. Departments are indexed from 1 to $n$.

## Output
If there is no way to assign agencies to satisfy the compliance metric, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ (where $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is the agency for department $i$). If multiple valid assignments exist, you must provide the one that is lexicographically smallest (comparing the agency of department 1 first, then department 2, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I changed the compliance metric from a sum of edge interactions (where the target was the global minimum) to a product of edge interactions. The polynomial is engineered to evaluate to 12 for valid edges (different agencies) and to -24, 36, or -60 for invalid edges (same agencies). The target product is exactly $12^m$. Because the invalid values introduce prime factors of 2, 3, and 5, no combination of invalid edges can ever multiply out to exactly $12^m$. This forces models to genuinely analyze the polynomial's values over the discrete domain rather than falsely assuming the target is an absolute minimum or maximum of the continuous function. I also applied S07 to hide the bounds of $n$ and $m$ in the corporate structure description.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The new polynomial evaluates to 12 if and only if the endpoints have different agencies, and since the invalid values (-24, 36, -60) contain prime factors that cannot cancel out to reach $12^m$, the condition is still strictly equivalent to proper 3-coloring, leaving all valid assignments identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly equivalent to finding a lexicographically smallest 3-coloring of a graph, which is NP-complete.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Some readers might be confused by the mathematical formulation of a product of terms for $m=0$, but the explicit clarification "(if $m=0$, the overall product is defined as 1)" resolves this cleanly; otherwise, none.
</RISK>
