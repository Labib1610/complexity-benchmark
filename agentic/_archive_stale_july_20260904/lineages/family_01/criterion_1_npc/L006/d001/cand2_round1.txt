# Problem A — Hopper Allocation

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An automated dispensing facility processes $n$ distinct pharmaceutical ingredients. The facility stores ingredients in a rack consisting of exactly 4 rows with 10 slots each. A batch contains at least one ingredient and never exceeds the rack's total slot capacity. The system must distribute the ingredients among three isolated holding hoppers, designated mechanically as hopper 1, hopper 2, and hopper 3.

Due to cross-contamination risks, specific pairs of ingredients will degrade if they are loaded into the same holding hopper. The number of such risks, $m$, ranges from zero up to the theoretical maximum where every ingredient poses a risk to every other ingredient in the batch. The certification batches are adversarially selected from arbitrary risk configurations up to the maximum capacity. This includes configurations structured to ensure that straightforward sequential hopper assignment explores almost the entire space of possible combinations before determining feasibility or identifying the required assignment.

You must assign every ingredient to exactly one of the three hoppers such that no two ingredients that pose a mutual cross-contamination risk share a hopper. To standardize loading procedures across multiple facilities, the system enforces a strict priority rule for valid assignments. The configuration must be chosen such that the sequence of hopper numbers assigned to ingredients $1$ through $n$ is lexicographically minimized. This means the system prioritizes placing ingredient 1 into the lowest-numbered hopper possible, then ingredient 2 into the lowest-numbered hopper possible, and so forth, while still ensuring a completely safe arrangement for all ingredients.

If no safe configuration exists for the given ingredients and contamination risks, the system must reject the batch.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — the number of ingredients and the number of cross-contamination risks. The bounds for these values correspond to the physical limits of the storage rack (at least 1, up to the total slots) and the maximum possible pairs described above.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$) — a pair of ingredients that pose a mutual cross-contamination risk. Each pair is listed at most once.

## Output
If no safe hopper assignment exists, print a single line containing `NO`.
Otherwise, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $h_1, h_2, \dots, h_n$ (each in $\{1, 2, 3\}$), where $h_i$ is the hopper assigned to ingredient $i$. This sequence must be the lexicographically smallest valid configuration.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
