# Problem A — Hopper Allocation

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

An automated dispensing system processes $n$ distinct pharmaceutical ingredients. The system distributes these ingredients among three isolated holding hoppers, designated mechanically as hopper 1, hopper 2, and hopper 3.

Due to cross-contamination risks, specific pairs of ingredients will degrade if they are loaded into the same holding hopper. You must assign every ingredient to exactly one of the three hoppers such that no two ingredients that pose a mutual cross-contamination risk share a hopper.

To standardize loading procedures across multiple facilities, the system enforces a strict priority rule for valid assignments. The chosen configuration must be the one where the sequence of hopper numbers assigned to ingredients $1$ through $n$ is strictly lexicographically smallest compared to any other valid configuration. This means that when comparing two valid configurations from ingredient 1 to $n$, the preferred configuration has a smaller hopper number at the first ingredient index where they differ.

The automated system undergoes rigorous certification against complex risk batches. These batches are deliberately selected to include extreme conflict patterns at the maximum allowed size, specifically designed to force straightforward, step-by-step hopper enumeration to explore nearly its full combination space before determining feasibility or finding the priority configuration. If no safe configuration exists for the given ingredients and contamination risks, the system must reject the batch.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — the number of ingredients and the number of cross-contamination risks. A standard holding rack dictates the physical limits, meaning $n$ is at least 1 and at most 40. The number of risks $m$ is at least 0 and at most $n(n-1)/2$.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of ingredients that pose a cross-contamination risk.

## Output
If no safe hopper assignment exists, print a single line containing `NO`.
Otherwise, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $h_1, h_2, \dots, h_n$ (each in $\{1, 2, 3\}$), where $h_i$ is the hopper assigned to ingredient $i$. This sequence must be the lexicographically smallest valid configuration.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
