# Problem A — Scheduling Pipeline: Phase 2

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The automated workforce management system at a manufacturing plant executes its daily routine in three distinct steps. 
During the initial step, the personnel module processes the roster of $n$ staff members and flags specific pairs of individuals who cannot work together due to interpersonal friction.

Your responsibility is to build the second step: the roster generation engine. The plant operates continuously across three blocks of time, designated as block 1, block 2, and block 3. Every staff member must be allocated to exactly one of these three time blocks. Your allocation must guarantee that no two individuals flagged with interpersonal friction are placed in the same time block.

During the final step, the payroll system processes your allocation to finalize the day's schedule. To guarantee deterministic behavior from the payroll system, whenever there is more than one valid allocation, you are required to output the lexicographically smallest sequence of block allocations (evaluating the block of staff member 1 first, then staff member 2, and so forth). If there is no way to satisfy the friction constraints, your engine must output an impossibility signal to notify the plant supervisors.

Please note: The test data may contain arbitrary adversarial configurations, including worst-case satisfiable and unsatisfiable instances intended to defeat exhaustive assignment enumeration; solutions must meet the time limit on those instances.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ — the total staff count and the number of friction pairs flagged during the initial step ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n(n-1)/2$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a pair of staff members who cannot share a time block.

## Output
If a valid allocation cannot be formed, output NO on a single line.
If a valid allocation exists, output YES on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $s_1 s_2 \dots s_n$ (each belonging to $\{1, 2, 3\}$), where $s_i$ denotes the time block assigned to staff member $i$. If multiple valid allocations exist, you must output the lexicographically smallest sequence.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

The problem description was rewritten to replace standard terminology with a narrative about allocating staff to time blocks. Crucially, a warning was added to the description explicitly stating that test cases contain adversarial configurations intended to defeat exhaustive assignment enumeration. This removes the affordance where models previously relied on the small bound $n \le 40$ to justify naive backtracking, forcing them to consider worst-case efficiency. The explicit numeric bounds were restored to satisfy the automated validation.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The requirement to assign each entity to one of three groups such that no two conflicting entities share a group, with a lexicographically smallest tie-breaker, is mathematically identical to the seed problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The problem remains exactly equivalent to finding the lexicographically smallest 3-coloring of a graph, which is NP-complete.
