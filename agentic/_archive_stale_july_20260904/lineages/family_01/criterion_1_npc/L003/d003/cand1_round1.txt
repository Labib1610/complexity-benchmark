<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Shift Distribution

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility employs a team of staff members. To maintain continuous production, the facility schedules its operations across three daily shifts, designated as 1, 2, and 3. Each staff member must be allocated to exactly one of these three shifts.

Due to interpersonal issues, certain pairs of staff members cannot work together. You are given a historical log of disputes between distinct individuals. If two staff members have a logged dispute, they cannot be allocated to the same shift. 

The facility operates a single production hall containing a grid of workstations arranged in exactly five rows of eight. Every staff member is assigned their own workstation, so the workforce size $n$ is at least one and will never exceed the total number of workstations in this grid. 

Your task is to determine whether a valid shift allocation exists for all $n$ staff members. If a valid allocation is possible, you must find the one that minimizes the value of $\sum_{i=1}^n s_i \cdot 4^{n-i}$, where $s_i$ represents the shift designated to staff member $i$. 

Be aware that the dispute logs may be adversarially selected. The test cases include full-capacity workforces with dispute networks specifically constructed to force extensive search for fixed-order evaluation algorithms.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$. The workforce size $n$ and the number of dispute records $m$ obey the natural limits described above, with $m$ ranging from zero up to the maximum possible number of distinct staff member pairs.
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of distinct staff members who cannot be assigned to the same shift.

## Output
If it is impossible to allocate the shifts without violating the dispute constraints, print a single line containing `NO`.
If a valid allocation can be found, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ ($s_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), where $s_i$ is the shift assigned to staff member $i$. If there are multiple valid allocations, print the sequence $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ that minimizes the value of $\sum_{i=1}^n s_i \cdot 4^{n-i}$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
