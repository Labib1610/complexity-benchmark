The problem asks us to find the lexicographically smallest triplet of distinct indices $(a, b, c)$ such that $1 \le a < b < c \le n$ and there are incompatibilities between $(a, b)$, $(a, c)$, and $(b, c)$. The incompatibilities are given as edges in a graph where vertices represent staff members. We are looking for the lexicographically first triangle (cycle of length 3) in the graph, where "lexicographically first" refers to the triplet $(a, b, c)$ sorted by vertex indices. Specifically, we want to minimize $a$, then $b$, then $c$.

Input constraints:
$n \le 2000$
$m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$
It is guaranteed that at least one triangle exists.

Since we want to minimize $a$ first, then $b$, then $c$, we should iterate through possible values of $a$ starting from 1 up to $n$. For a fixed $a$, we want to minimize $b$, then $c$.
The condition is that $a, b, c$ form a triangle. This means $a$ is connected to $b$, $a$ is connected to $c$, and $b$ is connected to $c$. Also $a < b < c$.

Algorithm idea:
Iterate $a$ from 1 to $n$.
  Iterate $b$ from $a+1$ to $n$.
    If $a$ and $b$ are connected:
      Iterate $c$ from $b+1$ to $n$.
        If $a$ and $c$ are connected AND $b$ and $c$ are connected:
          We found the answer $(a, b, c)$. Since we iterate $a, b, c$ in increasing order, the first one we find will be the lexicographically smallest. Return it.
