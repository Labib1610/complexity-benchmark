The problem asks us to find the lexicographically smallest triple of nodes $(a, b, c)$ such that $1 \le a < b < c \le n$ and all pairs $(a, b)$, $(a, c)$, and $(b, c)$ interfere with each other. We are given $n$ nodes and $m$ pairs of interfering nodes. We are guaranteed that at least one such triple exists.

Constraints:
$n \le 2000$
$m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$
Time limit: 2 seconds

Let's denote the adjacency matrix as $G$, where $G[u][v] = 1$ if $u$ and $v$ interfere, and 0 otherwise.
We need to find $a < b < c$ such that $G[a][b] = 1$, $G[a][c] = 1$, and $G[b][c] = 1$.
Among all such triples, we want the one that is lexicographically smallest.
This means we iterate $a$ from $1$ to $n-2$. For each $a$, we iterate $b$ from $a+1$ to $n-1$. For each pair $(a, b)$, if they interfere, we look for the smallest $c > b$ such that $c$ interferes with both $a$ and $b$. The first such triple we find will be the lexicographically smallest because we are iterating $a$ and $b$ in increasing order, and for a fixed pair $(a, b)$, we want the smallest $c$.
