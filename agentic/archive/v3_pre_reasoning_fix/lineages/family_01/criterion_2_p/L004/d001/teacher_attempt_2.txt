# Problem B — Day and Night

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The manufacturing plant operates on two distinct shifts, designated as 1 and 2. The workforce consists of $n$ employees. Due to past conflicts, management has compiled $m$ incident reports, each involving a specific pair of employees. To ensure a peaceful environment, any two employees involved in the same incident report must be scheduled for different shifts.

When multiple valid schedules exist, management finalizes the roster through a sequential review from employee $1$ up to employee $n$. For each employee $i$ in turn, they are placed in shift 1 if there is at least one valid plant-wide schedule that maintains the previously finalized shifts of employees $1$ through $i-1$ while putting employee $i$ in shift 1. If no such schedule is possible, employee $i$ is placed in shift 2.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($1 \le n \le 2\cdot 10^5$, $0 \le m \le 2\cdot 10^5$).
Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of employees who share an incident report.

## Output
If it is impossible to schedule the employees without violating the conflict rules, print a single line NO.
Otherwise, print YES on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $s_1 \dots s_n$ (each either 1 or 2) representing the finalized shift assignments for employees $1$ through $n$ respectively.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 4
1 2
2 3
3 4
1 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 1 2
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
3 3
1 2
1 3
2 3
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Replaced the explicit "lexicographically smallest" tie-breaker with its operational definition: a sequential decision process that fixes shifts one by one. This prevents models from pattern-matching the standard "lexicographically smallest 2-coloring" algorithm, forcing them to deduce that this sequential process is equivalent to assigning shift 1 to the lowest-indexed node of each independent connected component to avoid an $O(n^2)$ simulation.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The sequential prefix-fixing process is mathematically identical to finding the lexicographically smallest sequence, ensuring all valid outputs remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem is still 2-coloring (bipartiteness testing), solvable in $O(n+m)$ time.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "maintains the previously finalized shifts" as a suggestion to actually simulate the process sequentially, leading to Time Limit Exceeded, though this is the intended trap for models that fail to reason about the underlying structure.
</RISK>
