The problem asks us to find the lexicographically smallest triple of distinct nodes $(a, b, c)$ such that $a < b < c$ and there are edges between $(a, b)$, $(b, c)$, and $(a, c)$. This is essentially finding the lexicographically smallest triangle in a graph. The input size is $n \le 2000$ and $m \le 200,000$. The time limit is 2 seconds.

Since we need the lexicographically smallest triple, we should iterate through possible values of $a$, then $b$, then $c$, and check if they form a triangle. The condition $a < b < c$ implies we can iterate $a$ from $1$ to $n$, $b$ from $a+1$ to $n$, and $c$ from $b+1$ to $n$. However, a naive check takes $O(n^3)$ or $O(m \cdot n)$ depending on implementation, which might be too slow if $n=2000$ and $m$ is large, although $O(n^3)$ is definitely out. $O(m \sqrt{m})$ or $O(n^2)$ might be acceptable.
