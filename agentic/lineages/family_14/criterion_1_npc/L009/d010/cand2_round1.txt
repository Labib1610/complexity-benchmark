# Problem A — Quotient Space Configuration

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are configuring a sensory processing system modeled as an $n$-dimensional vector space $E$ over the finite field GF(2). Let $B = \{b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_n\}$ be the standard basis of $E$, where each basis vector represents an independent sensory channel. Within this system, a specific subset of $t$ basis vectors are designated as critical feedback loops.

You are provided with a catalog of optional hardware links. Procuring a link between channel $u$ and channel $v$ adds a structural generator of the form $b_u + b_v$ to your configuration, and incurs a specified cost. Since the field is GF(2), addition is its own inverse and $b_u + b_v$ is identical to $b_v + b_u$.

Your objective is to select a subset of these links to procure. Let $W$ be the subspace of $E$ spanned by your procured generators. This subspace defines a quotient vector space $E/W$, along with the canonical projection map $\pi: E \to E/W$ defined by $\pi(v) = v + W$.

To ensure system stability, the procured subspace $W$ must collapse all critical feedback loops into a single point in the quotient space. Specifically, for any two critical basis vectors $b_x$ and $b_y$, their images under the projection must be strictly identical: $\pi(b_x) = \pi(b_y)$.

Calculate the minimum total cost of links you must procure to satisfy this quotient-space property.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The positive dimension **n** is at most the square of ten. The non-negative catalog size **m** is at most the number of unique unordered pairs of distinct channels. The strictly positive number of critical loops **t** is at most the number of standard decimal digits.
The second line provides **t** distinct integers representing the identities of the critical basis vectors.
The subsequent **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**. The indices **u** and **v** represent distinct basis vectors, and the strictly positive procurement cost **w** is capped at exactly one million.
It is guaranteed that the available links are sufficient to construct a subspace $W$ satisfying the quotient-space property.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total cost required to procure a subspace that satisfies the quotient-space property for all critical loops.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, procuring the generators $b_1 + b_4$, $b_2 + b_4$, and $b_3 + b_4$ incurs a total cost of 1+1+1 = 3. The resulting subspace $W$ contains $b_1 + b_2$ (since $(b_1 + b_4) + (b_2 + b_4) = b_1 + b_2$), $b_2 + b_3$, and $b_1 + b_3$. In the quotient space $E/W$, this implies $\pi(b_1) = \pi(b_2) = \pi(b_3)$, fulfilling the property for the critical loops 1, 2, and 3. This is more economical than procuring a subspace without involving channel 4 (which would cost at least 4).

In Example 3, there is only a single critical loop. The requirement that all critical loops map to identical images in the quotient space is vacuously satisfied by an empty subspace $W$, resulting in a cost of 0.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The direct requirement that "$b_x + b_y$ is contained in the row space" was replaced with an equivalent condition on the canonical projection into the quotient space $E/W$. This forces the model to perform the algebraic deduction that $\pi(b_x) = \pi(b_y) \iff b_x - b_y \in W \iff b_x + b_y \in W$ over GF(2), breaking the surface-level pattern match to Steiner tree connectivity. Additionally, numeric bounds were rewritten indirectly (e.g., "square of ten" for 100, "number of standard decimal digits" for 10) per Strategy S07.
</RATIONALE>
