<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Isotope Amalgamation

Time limit: 5 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A laboratory possesses samples of **n** distinct elemental isotopes, initially kept in separate isolation chambers. The isotopes are numbered from 1 to **n**.

The laboratory can perform specific amalgamation procedures. A procedure between isotope **u** and isotope **v** merges their respective chambers into a single combined chamber, mixing their contents. The thermal energy required to execute this specific procedure is **w** Joules. 
When chambers are merged, all isotopes previously contained in either chamber become part of the new combined chamber. 

A new research directive dictates that a specific subset of **t** isotopes—designated as *target isotopes*—must all end up together in the exact same chamber. 
You are permitted to merge chambers containing non-target isotopes with the target ones if doing so achieves the goal using less total thermal energy.

Your task is to select a subset of the available amalgamation procedures to execute such that all target isotopes reside in a single shared chamber, while keeping the total thermal energy expended to the absolute minimum.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**.  
- The total number of isotopes **n** is at least 1 and is limited by the facility's maximum capacity of exactly one hundred isolation chambers.  
- The number of available amalgamation procedures **m** is non-negative and bounded by the maximum possible number of unique pairs of isotopes.  
- The number of target isotopes **t** is at least 1 and cannot exceed the ten available containment slots in the final target chamber.  

The second line lists **t** distinct integers representing the identifiers of the target isotopes.  

Following this, there are **m** lines. Each line provides three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (where **u** and **v** are distinct valid isotope identifiers between 1 and **n**), indicating that the procedure to merge the chamber containing isotope **u** with the chamber containing isotope **v** requires **w** Joules of thermal energy. The energy **w** is at least 1 and is capped by the reactor's maximum output of exactly one million Joules.  

It is guaranteed that the available procedures provide at least one valid way to combine all target isotopes into the same chamber.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum possible total thermal energy required to fulfill the research directive.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, executing the procedures that merge the chamber containing isotope 4 with those containing 1, 2, and 3 requires a total thermal energy of 1 + 1 + 1 = 3. This successfully places the target isotopes (1, 2, and 3) into a single combined chamber. Using isotope 4 as a mixing intermediary is more energy-efficient than forcing merges solely among the target isotopes, which would incur an energy cost of at least 4. 

In Example 3, only one isotope is designated as a target. Since a single isotope is already in its own chamber and requires no merges to be grouped with itself, zero procedures are executed, resulting in an energy cost of 0.
