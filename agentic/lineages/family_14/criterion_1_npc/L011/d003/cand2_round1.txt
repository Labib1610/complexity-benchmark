# Problem A — Pneumatic Infrastructure

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An industrial complex contains **n** buildings (numbered 1 through **n**). The site's land area physically restricts the complex to a maximum of one hundred buildings, with at least one building always present. You have a catalog of **m** potential bidirectional pneumatic transport tubes that can be constructed between specific pairs of buildings. The catalog could be completely empty, or it could encompass up to the theoretical maximum where every distinct pair of buildings has exactly one potential tube. Each potential tube has a known construction cost. The cost of any tube is at least one credit, and the budget approval process limits any single tube's cost to one million credits.

Due to an impending inspection, a roster of **t** specific buildings are flagged as *audit-critical*. The inspection requires that all audit-critical buildings be fully interconnected. The number of such buildings is at least one, but administrative capacity caps this roster at a maximum of ten buildings. This interconnection means a physical canister must be able to travel between any two audit-critical buildings. The path between any two audit-critical buildings can traverse any sequence of constructed tubes, utilizing any other buildings in the complex as relay points.

You may select any combination of the available tubes to construct. Determine the minimum possible total cost required to ensure all audit-critical buildings are interconnected.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The bounds for these values are determined by the physical and administrative limits of the complex described above.
The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the identifiers of the audit-critical buildings.
Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (where **u** and **v** are distinct, valid building numbers), representing a potential tube between building **u** and building **v** with a construction cost of **w**.
It is guaranteed that the available potential tubes are sufficient to interconnect all audit-critical buildings.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total construction cost.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, constructing the tubes that join building 4 to buildings 1, 2, and 3 incurs a total cost of 1 + 1 + 1 = 3. This successfully interconnects the audit-critical buildings (1, 2, and 3) and is more economical than linking them exclusively using direct connections between each other, which would cost at least 4. In Example 3, there is only one audit-critical building, so no tubes need to be constructed.
