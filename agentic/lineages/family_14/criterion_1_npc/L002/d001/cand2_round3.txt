# Problem A — Synchronization Backbone

Time limit: 5 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description
A regional agency is establishing a secure synchronization backbone across its various terrain outposts. 

There are $n$ outposts in the region. Field teams have identified $m$ potential underground conduits where secure cables can be laid, and have determined the exact material expense for each.

Out of the $n$ outposts, exactly $t$ have been selected as synchronization hubs. Your objective is to design a unified backbone that ensures all $t$ synchronization hubs can communicate with one another. This means they must all be connected within the same network. 

To minimize the total material expense, you may route the cables through any combination of the available conduits. You are allowed to use any of the non-hub outposts as passive junctions if doing so results in a cheaper overall network connecting the $t$ hubs.

Determine the minimum possible total expense required to link all synchronization hubs together.

## Input
The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$.
- The outposts are numbered 1 to $n$. The hardware addressing limits the total number of outposts $n$ to a maximum of 100, with at least 1 always present.
- The number of potential conduits $m$ can be as low as 0 and as high as $n(n-1)/2$ (a fully interconnected grid).
- Each synchronization hub requires a dedicated supervisor. With exactly 10 supervisors on staff, the number of hubs $t$ is at most 10 (and at least 1).

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the IDs of the outposts selected as synchronization hubs.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, detailing a potential conduit between outpost $u$ and outpost $v$ ($u \neq v$, and both are between 1 and $n$). The material expense $w$ is at least 1 and cannot exceed the single-project funding ceiling of 1000000.

It is guaranteed that the potential conduits are sufficient to connect all synchronization hubs.

## Output
Print a single integer: the minimum total expense to ensure all synchronization hubs are connected.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note
In Example 1, routing the cables through outpost 4 as a passive junction (costing 1+1+1 = 3) is cheaper than linking the three synchronization hubs directly to each other (which would cost 4). 
In Example 3, there is only one synchronization hub, so no cables need to be laid.
