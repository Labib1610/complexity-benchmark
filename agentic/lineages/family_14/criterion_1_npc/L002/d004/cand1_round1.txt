# Problem A — Propositional Equivalence

Time limit: 5 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description
You are mapping out a formal theory containing $n$ distinct propositions. 

There are $m$ potential lemmas that have been proposed. Each lemma, if proved, establishes a bidirectional logical equivalence between two specific propositions (i.e., proposition $u$ holds if and only if proposition $v$ holds). Proving a lemma requires a specific number of research hours. 

By the rules of inference, logical equivalence is transitive: if your proved lemmas establish that proposition A is equivalent to proposition B, and that proposition B is equivalent to proposition C, it is mathematically deduced that proposition A is equivalent to proposition C. 

A specific set of $t$ propositions are identified as the foundational conjectures. Your objective is to select a subset of the potential lemmas to prove such that all $t$ foundational conjectures are deduced to be logically equivalent to each other. You may choose to prove lemmas that involve other propositions outside this foundational set; utilizing them as intermediate logical stepping stones is a valid approach if it reduces the total research time.

Write a program to calculate the minimum sum of research hours required to prove a valid subset of lemmas.

## Input
The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$.
The number of propositions $n$ is between 1 and 100.
The number of potential lemmas $m$ is between 0 and $n(n-1)/2$.
The number of foundational conjectures $t$ is at most 10 (and at least 1).

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the indices of the foundational conjectures.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$. This indicates that a lemma establishing the equivalence of proposition $u$ and proposition $v$ can be proved using $w$ research hours. Propositions are indexed from 1 to $n$. Each lemma relates two distinct propositions ($u \neq v$). The research hours $w$ for any lemma is an integer between 1 and 1000000.

It is guaranteed that the available lemmas are sufficient to establish the equivalence of all foundational conjectures.

## Output
Print a single integer: the minimum sum of research hours required to prove the necessary lemmas so that all foundational conjectures are logically equivalent.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note
In Example 1, proving the lemmas that connect proposition 4 to propositions 1, 2, and 3 requires a sum of 1+1+1 = 3 research hours. This establishes that 1, 2, and 3 are equivalent via proposition 4 as an intermediate step, which is more efficient than proving the lemmas directly between the foundational conjectures (which would require 4 hours). 
In Example 3, there is only one foundational conjecture, so no lemmas need to be proved.
