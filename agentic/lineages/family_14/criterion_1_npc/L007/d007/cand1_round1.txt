# Quotient Group Rank Penalty

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are analyzing the structure of a group defined by a presentation. Initially, you have the free group $F$ on $n$ generators $g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n$.

You may introduce a set of relations to define a quotient group. There are $m$ available relations you can choose to apply. The $i$-th available relation is of the form $g_u g_v^{-1} = e$ (where $e$ is the identity element), and applying it to your presentation incurs a fixed structural penalty of $w$.

When you select a subset of these relations, the resulting quotient group $G$ is the quotient of $F$ by the normal closure of the selected relations. 

You are given a list of $t$ target indices, representing a specific subset of the generators. Let $H$ be the subgroup of $G$ generated by the images of these $t$ target generators in $G$.

Your goal is to select a subset of the available relations such that the subgroup $H$ is a free group of rank exactly 1. 

Determine the minimum total penalty incurred by selecting a subset of relations that achieves this requirement.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ t ≤ 10, 1 ≤ n ≤ 100, 0 ≤ m ≤ n·(n−1)/2).
The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the indices of the target generators.
Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1000000): an available relation $g_u g_v^{-1} = e$ and its structural penalty $w$.
The provided set of relations is sufficient to ensure $H$ has a rank of exactly 1.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total structural penalty to ensure the subgroup $H$ has a rank of exactly 1.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, applying the relations involving generator 4 ($g_1 g_4^{-1} = e$, $g_2 g_4^{-1} = e$, and $g_3 g_4^{-1} = e$ at a penalty of 1+1+1 = 3) means that in the quotient group $G$, the images of $g_1$, $g_2$, $g_3$, and $g_4$ are all the same element. The subgroup $H$ is generated by the images of the target generators $g_1, g_2, g_3$. Since they all map to the same element in $G$, $H$ is generated by a single free generator. A free group generated by a single element has rank 1. This incurs a lower penalty than applying the relations directly between the target generators.

In Example 3, there is only one target generator. The subgroup $H$ is generated by a single free generator, meaning it already has a rank of exactly 1. No relations need to be applied, and the minimum penalty is 0.

<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The quotient of a free group by relations of the form $g_u = g_v$ is a free group on the equivalence classes of the generators; thus, the subgroup generated by the targets has rank 1 if and only if all targets are in the same equivalence class, which is mathematically identical to connecting them in a graph.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem requires finding the minimum-weight subset of edges (relations) to place all target vertices (generators) into the same connected component (equivalence class), which is exactly the NP-complete Minimum Steiner Tree problem.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
