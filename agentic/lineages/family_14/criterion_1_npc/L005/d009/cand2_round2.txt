<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Liquidity Network Synchronization

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are auditing a network of **n** temporary holding accounts. Initially, every account has a balance of exactly 0.

You can establish automated clearing agreements. Each agreement is fixed between exactly two distinct accounts, $u$ and $v$. When executed, a transfer along this agreement moves exactly 1 unit of value from account $u$ to account $v$, or from account $v$ to account $u$.

Once an agreement is established by paying its one-time setup fee, it is permanently available. You may execute transfers on your established agreements in any order, and you may execute transfers on the same agreement multiple times.

A specific subset of **t** accounts are designated as *flagged*. Your task is to establish a subset of available agreements such that the following condition is satisfied: 
For *every* pair of distinct flagged accounts $i$ and $j$, there must exist some sequence of transfers that transitions the network from its initial state (all balances exactly 0) to a target state where exactly account $i$ and account $j$ have a **non-zero** balance, and all other $n-2$ accounts have a balance of exactly **0**. 

The specific sequence of transfers may be different for each pair of flagged accounts, but every sequence may only use agreements from the subset you established.

You are given a list of **m** available agreements, each with a one-time setup fee **w**. Determine the minimum total setup fee of a subset of agreements that satisfies this requirement.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ t ≤ 10, 1 ≤ n ≤ 100, 0 ≤ m ≤ n·(n−1)/2).
The second line lists **t** distinct integers representing the indices of the flagged accounts (1-indexed).
The following **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1,000,000). These represent an available clearing agreement between accounts **u** and **v** that has a one-time setup fee of **w**. (Note that the order of $u$ and $v$ does not matter, as transfers can occur in either direction).
It is guaranteed that it is possible to satisfy the requirement using the available agreements.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum total setup fee of a subset of agreements that allows reaching the required target states for all pairs of flagged accounts.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, the flagged accounts are 1, 2, and 3. We must be able to reach three target states from the initial state:
- Exactly accounts 1 and 2 have a non-zero balance.
- Exactly accounts 2 and 3 have a non-zero balance.
- Exactly accounts 1 and 3 have a non-zero balance.

By establishing the three agreements between (1, 4), (2, 4), and (3, 4) for a total setup fee of 1+1+1 = 3, we can satisfy the requirement. For instance, to reach the state where exactly 1 and 2 have a non-zero balance, we can execute a transfer of 1 unit from account 1 to account 4, and then a transfer of 1 unit from account 4 to account 2. Account 4's balance becomes +1 - 1 = 0 (exactly zero), while account 1 has -1 and account 2 has +1 (both non-zero). This is cheaper than establishing the agreements (1, 2) and (2, 3) directly, which would cost 2+2=4.

In Example 3, there is only one flagged account. Because there are no pairs of distinct flagged accounts, the requirement is vacuously satisfied. No agreements need to be established, resulting in a cost of 0.
