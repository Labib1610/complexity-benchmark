<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Terminal Synchronization

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A facility consists of **n** terminals (numbered 1 through **n**). A specific subset of **t** primary terminals must be able to share signals with each other. 

You can establish bidirectional signal channels between certain pairs of terminals. Each potential channel requires a specific energy overhead to maintain. 

Signal sharing operates across established channels: signals can flow directly between two terminals if a channel is established between them. Furthermore, this flow is transitive: if signals can flow between terminal A and terminal B, and between terminal B and terminal C, then signals can flow between terminal A and terminal C.

Your goal is to select a set of channels to establish such that signals can flow between any two primary terminals. You may establish channels involving non-primary terminals if using them as relays reduces the overall energy required.

Find the minimum total energy overhead required to establish a valid set of channels.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The facility's structural limit restricts the total terminal count **n** to a maximum of 100 (and at least 1). The primary control board has exactly 10 slots, meaning the number of primary terminals **t** cannot exceed 10 (and is at least 1). The channel count **m** ranges from 0 up to the theoretical maximum of **n**·(**n**−1)/2.

The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the identifiers of the primary terminals.

Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**, representing a potential bidirectional signal channel between distinct terminals **u** and **v** (where both are from 1 to **n**) requiring an energy overhead **w** bounded by the micro-reactor's maximum output of 1000000 (and at least 1).

It is guaranteed that the available channels are sufficient to connect all primary terminals.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total energy overhead required to establish channels such that all primary terminals can share signals with each other.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 6 3
1 2 3
1 4 1
2 4 1
3 4 1
1 2 2
2 3 2
1 3 2
```

**Output**
```
3
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2 3
1 2 1
2 3 1
1 4 10
3 4 10
```

**Output**
```
2
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 1
1
1 2 5
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
0
```

## Note

In Example 1, establishing channels through the non-primary terminal 4 (requiring 1+1+1 = 3 energy) is more efficient than linking the three primary terminals directly (which would require 2+2 = 4 energy). In Example 3, there is only one primary terminal, so no channels need to be established (0 energy).
