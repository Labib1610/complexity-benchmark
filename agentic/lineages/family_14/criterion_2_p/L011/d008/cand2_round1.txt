# Problem B — Platform Liquidity Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are tasked with the controlled consolidation of a digital exchange platform. The platform supports **n** distinct assets, labeled 1 to **n**, and operates **m** direct exchange pairs. Each exchange pair links two distinct assets and incurs a known operational cost.

The platform operates under a strict liquidity balance. Each active asset contributes exactly 1 unit of liquidity to the system, while each active exchange pair consumes exactly 1 unit of liquidity. The platform's *net liquidity* is the total contributed liquidity minus the total consumed liquidity. The initial platform is fully integrated (it is impossible to partition the assets into two non-empty sets that share no exchange pairs), and its net liquidity is exactly 1 unit.

You may perform a sequence of asset delistings to reduce operational costs. In a single delisting step, you permanently remove exactly one asset. This safely closes any exchange pairs that linked to the removed asset, stopping their operational costs but also removing their liquidity consumption. However, your sequence of delistings must satisfy two strict safety regulations:
1. You may never delist any of the **t** *core assets*, as they are required for foundational operations.
2. After every individual delisting step, the platform's net liquidity must remain exactly 1 unit. If removing an asset (and its attached pairs) would cause the net liquidity to deviate from 1 unit, that asset cannot be delisted at that time.

You can stop the delisting process at any point, including making no delistings at all. Your objective is to determine the minimum possible total operational cost of the exchange pairs that remain active when you finish.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (where **m** is exactly the number required to yield an initial net liquidity of 1, 1 ≤ t ≤ n, 1 ≤ n ≤ 200000).
The second line provides **t** distinct integers representing the labels of the core assets.
Each of the subsequent **m** lines provides three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1000000), indicating an exchange pair between asset **u** and asset **v** with an operational cost of **w**.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum possible total operational cost of the remaining exchange pairs after a valid sequence of delistings.
