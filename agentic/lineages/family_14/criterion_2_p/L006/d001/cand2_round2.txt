# Problem B — Critical Distribution

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A water management district is overhauling its distribution network across **n** pumping stations (numbered 1 to **n**). There are **m** proposed pipeline segments. Each segment connects a pair of stations and requires a specific budget to activate. The proposed segments guarantee that, if all were activated, water could flow between any two stations in the district.

The district has designated **t** critical stations. Your task is to select a subset of the proposed pipeline segments to activate so that all critical stations are connected to one another (either directly or by routing through other stations, which do not need to be critical). You must find a valid selection that keeps the total activation budget as low as possible, and report this minimum total budget.

To comply with zoning laws, the district size **n** is restricted to a maximum capacity of 200000 stations, with at least 1 station always present. The number of proposed segments **m** is exactly **n** - 1. The number of critical stations **t** is at least 1 and at most **n**. Each segment's activation cost **w** is at least 1 and cannot exceed the maximum grant limit of 1000000.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**. The total number of stations **n** is between 1 and the zoning limit of 200000. The number of proposed segments **m** is exactly **n** - 1. The number of critical stations **t** is between 1 and **n**.

The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the identifiers of the critical stations.

Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**, representing a proposed segment connecting station **u** and station **v** (where **u** and **v** are distinct, and between 1 and **n**) requiring an activation cost **w** (between 1 and the maximum grant limit of 1000000). It is guaranteed that the full set of **m** segments connects all **n** stations.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total activation budget required to ensure all critical stations are connected to one another.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```

```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

By describing the connectivity as water flowing between stations and stating `m = n - 1`, the tree structure is heavily implied but left for the model to deduce, disrupting pure pattern matching on graph terminology.
