# Problem B — Market Arbitrage Equilibrium

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are analyzing a financial network consisting of assets, where a specific subset of assets are designated as "core" assets.

You have a catalog of available bidirectional exchange channels. Each channel can be licensed to enforce strict price parity between two specific assets (i.e., forcing their prices to be equal). Licensing a channel requires paying a specific fee.

Your objective is to select a subset of these channels to license such that, for any two core assets, their prices are mathematically guaranteed to be equal through the direct, transitive, symmetric, and reflexive properties of the licensed channels. You may license channels that connect to non-core assets if they are useful for propagating price parity between core assets.

The total number of available channels is exactly one less than the number of assets. It is mathematically guaranteed that if you were to license all available channels, the resulting system of parity constraints would force all assets in the network into a single universal price. Because the number of channels is exactly one less than the number of assets, this system is minimally sufficient: if all channels were licensed, omitting any single one of them would cause the market to fracture into exactly two independent groups of assets. Consequently, any chain of transitive parity relationships between any two assets relies on a strictly unique set of channels.

Determine the minimum total licensing fee required to select a valid subset of channels that guarantees price parity across the entire subset of core assets.

## Input

The first line provides three integers: the total number of assets ($n$), the number of available channels ($m$), and the number of core assets ($t$). The asset count $n$ is constrained by the exchange registry's minimum operational size of one and its maximum capacity of two hundred thousand. The channel count $m$ is exactly $n - 1$. The core asset count $t$ is at least one, and at most the total asset count $n$. Assets are identified by sequential integers starting from 1 up to $n$.

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers representing the identifiers of the core assets.

This is followed by $m$ lines, each describing an available channel with three integers: the identifiers of the two distinct assets it bridges, and the licensing fee. The licensing fee requires a minimum deposit of one credit and is capped at a maximum regulatory limit of one million credits.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum possible total licensing fee to guarantee price parity for all core assets.
