<METHOD_NAME>Information Theoretic Entropy Framing</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Transforms the graph connectivity problem into an information theory problem about random variables and joint entropy. The tree structure ($m=n-1$ and connected) is obfuscated by describing it as a system where every coupling mechanism strictly reduces joint entropy (meaning no redundant constraints/cycles). This prevents models from immediately triggering graph/tree algorithms based on terms like "synchronizers" and "forces x_1 = x_n".</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem B — Entropic Synchronization

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are analyzing a system of $n$ discrete random variables, $X = [X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n]$. You have a critical subset $T$ containing $t$ indices that require perfect mutual predictability.

You are given a catalog of $m$ available bidirectional coupling mechanisms. Each mechanism can be activated between variables $X_u$ and $X_v$ at an energy cost of $w$. When activated, it restricts the joint distribution of the two variables such that their conditional entropy is zero ($H(X_u | X_v) = H(X_v | X_u) = 0$), meaning $X_u$ and $X_v$ are perfectly identical in any valid state of the system.

Your goal is to select a subset of coupling mechanisms $C$ such that for any two indices $i, j \in T$, the variables $X_i$ and $X_j$ are mathematically guaranteed to be identical ($H(X_i | X_j) = 0$) purely as a logical consequence of the mechanisms in $C$. 

Equivalently, the collection $C$ must satisfy the following structural property: For every possible partition of the $n$ variables into two disjoint non-empty sets $A$ and $B$, if both $A$ and $B$ contain at least one index from $T$, then there must exist at least one activated mechanism in $C$ that is applied between a variable in $A$ and a variable in $B$.

You may activate mechanisms that do not involve any indices from $T$ if they are needed to propagate the zero-conditional-entropy constraints between critical variables.

You are given that $m = n - 1$. Furthermore, it is mathematically guaranteed that the available mechanisms are completely irredundant. Specifically, if you were to activate all $m$ available mechanisms, the joint entropy of the entire system $H(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n)$ would be minimized. Removing even a single mechanism from this full set would strictly increase the joint entropy of the system, indicating that no mechanism's constraint can be derived from any combination of the others.

Determine the minimum total activation energy required to choose a valid collection $C$ that ensures perfect mutual predictability for all variables in the critical subset $T$.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ **t** ≤ **n**, 1 ≤ **n** ≤ 200000, **m** = **n** − 1). The random variables are indexed from 1 to **n**.
The second line lists **t** distinct integers representing the indices of the critical subset $T$.
Following this are **m** lines, each containing three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ **u**, **v** ≤ **n**, **u** ≠ **v**, 1 ≤ **w** ≤ 1000000). These values indicate an available coupling mechanism that enforces $H(X_u | X_v) = 0$ and can be activated at an energy cost of **w**.

## Output

Output a single integer representing the minimum possible total energy to activate a collection of coupling mechanisms that satisfies the required property for $T$.

<RATIONALE>
The models were using the explicit graph-connectivity guarantee ("forces x_1 = x_n") combined with "m = n - 1" to immediately deduce the graph was a single tree and apply an O(N) tree DP. I changed the semantic domain to information theory (random variables and entropy). The tree property is now communicated by stating that removing any mechanism strictly increases joint entropy (meaning no cycles/redundant constraints exist). This forces the model to mathematically prove the structure is a tree before applying the optimal algorithm.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The structural cut condition remains identical, meaning the valid subsets of mechanisms are exactly the valid subsets of synchronizers from the seed problem, preserving the minimum cost.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The guarantee that no mechanism is redundant implies the constraint graph has no cycles, and since m = n - 1, it is mathematically guaranteed to be a single tree, keeping the problem solvable in polynomial time (O(N) tree DP).
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "joint entropy would be minimized" as a continuous optimization problem, though the discrete nature of the costs and mechanisms makes this unlikely.
</RISK>
