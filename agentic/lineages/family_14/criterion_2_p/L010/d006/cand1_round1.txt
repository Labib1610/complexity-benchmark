<STATEMENT>
# Centralized Diagnostic Routing

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An industrial manufacturing complex features $n$ distinct workstations, numbered $1$ to $n$. These are linked by $m$ bidirectional conveyor belts. The facility's infrastructure ensures that all workstations are interlinked in a single contiguous network. To minimize maintenance costs, the total number of belts $m$ is strictly fixed at exactly $n - 1$, meaning there is exactly one unique sequence of belts between any two workstations.

A specific subset of $t$ workstations are designated as quality control stations. The facility is preparing for a system test to verify the communication channels between these critical locations.

During the system test, every possible pair of distinct quality control stations will exchange a diagnostic ping. For any given pair, the ping travels along the unique sequence of belts connecting those two stations. 

A belt is classified as *tested* if it carries at least one ping during this entire process. Each belt has a one-time energy overhead $w$ associated with its operation. Your task is to determine the sum of the energy overheads of all tested belts.

## Input

The first line provides three integers: $n$, $m$, and $t$. The facility's blueprint supports a maximum of two hundred thousand workstations ($1 \le n \le 200000$). The number of belts $m$ is always $n - 1$. The number of quality control stations $t$ can range from a single station up to the entire facility ($1 \le t \le n$).

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers representing the IDs of the quality control stations.

Following this, there are $m$ lines, each detailing a single conveyor belt with three integers: $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$). The energy overhead $w$ for any single belt is at least one and does not exceed one million units ($1 \le w \le 1000000$). 

## Output

Print a single integer: the total energy overhead of all tested belts.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the quality control stations are 1 and 4. The only pair of distinct stations is (1, 4). The diagnostic ping between them travels along the sequence of belts 1-2, 2-3, and 3-4. All three of these belts carry a ping and are therefore tested. Their energy overheads are 5, 1, and 2. The total overhead is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8.

In Example 2, there is only one quality control station (station 3). Since there are no pairs of distinct quality control stations, no diagnostic pings are exchanged. Zero belts are tested, resulting in a total overhead of 0.

In Example 3, the quality control stations are 1, 2, and 3. Distinct pairs exchange pings: (1, 2) uses belt 1-2; (2, 3) uses belt 2-3; and (1, 3) uses belts 1-2 and 2-3. The tested belts are 1-2 and 2-3. Their overheads are 4 and 5, giving a total of 9.
