<METHOD_NAME>Permutation Subgroup Isomorphism</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the vector space basis span with the generation of transpositions in a permutation group. Finding a subset of transpositions (swaps) that can generate all pairwise swaps of a target set is mathematically isomorphic to finding a connected subgraph containing those targets. Since the available transpositions form a tree (guaranteed by $m=n-1$ and generating $S_n$), this reduces to the identical minimal spanning subtree problem. Models relying on keyword matching will fail to recognize the tree structure and instead attempt to simulate permutations or solve it as a state-space search.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>

<STATEMENT>
# Permutation Mechanism

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are designing a mechanical sorting device with **n** storage slots, each holding a distinct item.

You are offered a catalog of **m** swapping mechanisms. Each proposed mechanism connects a specific pair of slots **u** and **v**. When activated, the mechanism exchanges the items currently located in slot **u** and slot **v**. Each mechanism has a one-time installation cost of **w**. You are guaranteed that **m** = **n** − 1, and that if you were to install all **m** mechanisms, you could achieve any possible permutation of the **n** items through some finite sequence of activations.

You are given a list of **t** critical slots. You must select a subset of the proposed mechanisms to install such that your device satisfies the following strict requirement:
For every pair of distinct critical slots **a** and **b**, there must exist some finite sequence of activations of your installed mechanisms whose net result is that the items originally in slot **a** and slot **b** are exchanged, while the items originally in **every other slot** (whether critical or not) end up in their exact original starting slots. 

You may activate the same installed mechanism multiple times in a sequence. It is completely acceptable for items to be temporarily moved into wrong slots during the intermediate steps of the sequence, provided the final state of the device restores all items except those from **a** and **b** to their original starting slots.

Find the minimum total installation cost of a subset of mechanisms that satisfies this requirement. The total cost is the sum of the installation costs **w** of the mechanisms you choose to install.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ **t** ≤ **n**, 1 ≤ **n** ≤ 200000, **m** = **n** − 1). The proposed mechanisms strictly satisfy the permutation guarantee described above.
The second line contains **t** distinct integers: the indices of the critical slots.
Each of the next **m** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ **u**, **v** ≤ **n**, **u** ≠ **v**, 1 ≤ **w** ≤ 1000000), representing a proposed mechanism between slot **u** and slot **v** that requires a cost of **w** to install.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total installation cost of a subset of mechanisms that allows the isolated exchange of any pair of critical slots.

<RATIONALE>
The problem was completely reframed from vector space basis span to generating transpositions in a permutation group. Finding a subset of transpositions (swaps) that can generate the isolated swap of any two target elements is mathematically equivalent to finding a connected subgraph containing those targets. By disguising the tree structure as a mechanical sorting puzzle requiring specific permutation cycles, models that rely on keyword matching will fail to recognize the underlying tree and instead attempt to simulate the swaps or solve it as a state-space search.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
A set of transpositions generates the isolated swap of two elements if and only if those elements are in the same connected component of the transposition graph; thus, the minimal cost to enable all target swaps is exactly the weight of the minimal spanning subtree, identical to the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying graph is still a tree (guaranteed by m = n - 1 and the ability to generate any permutation), and the task remains finding the minimum weight subtree connecting all target vertices, which is solvable in O(N) time.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "sequence of activations" as implying the cost is based on the number of activations rather than the one-time installation cost, though the text explicitly defines total cost as the sum of installation costs of the chosen subset.
</RISK>
