<STATEMENT>
# Ideal Generator Optimization

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are working in the commutative ring $R = \mathbb{R}[X_1, X_2, \dots, X_N]$ over the real numbers.

You are given a catalog of $M$ proposed binomial generators. Each proposed generator is of the form $X_u - X_v$, and requires a computational cost $W$ to include in your active generating set. The catalog of $M$ generators is minimally sufficient to generate the ideal of all elements in $R$ that vanish on the line $X_1 = X_2 = \dots = X_N$. This precise minimal sufficiency implies there are exactly $N-1$ proposed generators with no algebraic redundancies.

You are given a specific list of $T$ target variable indices. Your task is to select a subset of the proposed generators to form an ideal $I$. The ideal $I$ must be large enough such that for any pair of target indices $a$ and $b$, the binomial $X_a - X_b$ belongs to $I$. You may include generators that do not directly involve the target indices if they help satisfy this algebraic condition.

Find the minimum total computational cost of a selected subset of generators that satisfies this requirement. The total cost is the sum of the costs $W$ of the included generators.

The computer algebra system's memory architecture supports up to two hundred thousand variables in the ring. The cost tracking module has a hardcoded physical limit of one million per generator. The target list must contain at least one variable and can span up to the entire ring. The variables are indexed starting from 1.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $N$, $M$, and $T$. The proposed generators strictly satisfy the minimal sufficiency described above.
The second line contains $T$ distinct integers: the target variable indices.
Each of the next $M$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $W$, representing a proposed generator $X_u - X_v$ that requires a cost of $W$ to include. It is guaranteed that $u \neq v$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total computational cost of a subset of generators whose ideal contains $X_a - X_b$ for all target indices $a$ and $b$.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```

```

**Output**
```
8
```

```

```

<RATIONALE>
The problem was completely reframed into the domain of commutative algebra and ideal generation. By defining the available links as binomial generators of an ideal and the connectivity requirement as ideal membership ($X_a - X_b \in I$), the models are forced to recognize that degree-1 polynomial ideal membership is mathematically isomorphic to graph connectivity. This disrupts any surface-level pattern matching that triggers tree-pruning algorithms. Numeric bounds were also embedded into the scenario's physical limits to satisfy S07.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The binomial $X_a - X_b$ belongs to the ideal generated by a subset of $X_u - X_v$ if and only if $a$ and $b$ are connected in the graph formed by the selected subset, making the minimum cost required exactly the minimum weight Steiner tree connecting the targets.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains equivalent to finding the minimum weight Steiner tree on a graph that is guaranteed to be a tree, which is solvable in polynomial time (O(N)) via a simple post-order traversal.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the ideal membership condition as requiring a full Gröbner basis computation or polynomial division, but careful readers will deduce that linear combinations of degree-1 binomials strictly map to connected components.
</RISK>
