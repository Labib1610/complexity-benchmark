<METHOD_NAME>Path-Union Specification</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the edge-removal bottleneck definition (which prompts bridge-finding and subtree counting) with a requirement to include the union of all unique paths between pairs of required nodes. This mathematically defines the exact same set of edges but forces models to recognize that the union of paths in a tree can be found efficiently via subtree traversal, without prompting the traversal directly.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>

<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

The market's proposed agreements form a minimally integrated structure: there are exactly $m = n - 1$ proposed agreements, and any two corporations are linked by exactly one simple route (a sequence of agreements without repeating any corporation) in this full set.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders.

Your goal is to choose a subset of the proposed agreements to finalize. A chosen subset is considered valid if it satisfies the following regulatory condition: for every pair of distinct key patent holders, the unique route of agreements that links them in the full market must be entirely included in the finalized subset. 

You must find the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$, $m = n - 1$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. The unique route of agreements linking corporation 1 to corporation 4 consists of the agreement between 1 and 2, the agreement between 2 and 3, and the agreement between 3 and 4. To form a valid subset, all three of these agreements must be included. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8.

In Example 2, there is only $t=1$ key patent holder (corporation 3). Because there are no pairs of distinct key patent holders, the regulatory condition is vacuously satisfied without including any agreements. The minimum total legal fee is 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3. The unique route between 1 and 2 requires the agreement between 1 and 2. The unique route between 2 and 3 requires the agreement between 2 and 3. The unique route between 1 and 3 requires both agreements. The finalized subset must include both agreements to satisfy the condition for all pairs. The minimum total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The seed problem explicitly defined target edges by stating that their removal would disconnect key patent holders. This definition acts as a massive affordance, directly prompting models to use subtree key counts to check if an edge separates the tree into two components with key holders. By replacing this with a requirement to include the union of all unique paths between pairs of key holders, the exact same set of edges is defined, but models must independently realize that computing this union optimally still reduces to the $O(n)$ subtree-counting DFS, rather than attempting an $O(t^2 \cdot n)$ path-finding approach which will TLE.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The union of unique paths between all pairs of nodes in a subset of a tree is mathematically identical to the minimal spanning subtree for those nodes, which is exactly the set of "critical" edges in the seed problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains finding the Steiner tree on a tree, which is solvable in O(n) time using a single post-order traversal to count required nodes in subtrees.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
