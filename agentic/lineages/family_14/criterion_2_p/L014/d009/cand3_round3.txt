<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders. 

To maintain market equilibrium, the antitrust regulatory board has issued a certificate that enforces strict structural limits on the proposed agreements. The certificate guarantees that the market is fully integrated and minimally sufficient: all $n$ corporations are connected by the $m$ agreements, the total count of proposed agreements $m$ is exactly $n - 1$, and there is exactly one valid sequence of proposed agreements linking any two corporations.

We classify a proposed agreement as *essential* based on its patent imbalance. For any agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$, every corporation in the market is either closer to $u$ or closer to $v$ (measured by the number of agreements in the unique sequence linking them). This naturally partitions the market into two sets: corporations closer to $u$, and corporations closer to $v$. The *patent imbalance* of that agreement is the absolute difference between the number of key patent holders in the first set and the number of key patent holders in the second set. An agreement is considered *essential* if and only if its patent imbalance is strictly less than $t$.

Your goal is to choose a subset of the proposed agreements to finalize. A chosen subset is considered valid if and only if it includes every *essential* agreement. 

You must find the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$, $m = n - 1$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.
These $m$ agreements satisfy the structural limits described above.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4 (so $t = 2$). 
For the agreement between 1 and 2, corporation 1 is closer to 1, while corporations 2, 3, and 4 are closer to 2. The first set contains 1 key patent holder (corporation 1) and the second set contains 1 key patent holder (corporation 4). The patent imbalance is $|1 - 1| = 0$. Since $0 < 2$, this agreement is essential.
Similarly, for the agreement between 2 and 3, or the agreement between 3 and 4, the patent imbalance is also 0. All three agreements are essential. To form a valid subset, all three must be included. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder (corporation 3, so $t = 1$). For any agreement, one of the two distance-based sets will contain corporation 3 (1 key patent holder), while the other set will contain zero key patent holders. The patent imbalance for any agreement is $|1 - 0| = 1$. Since $1$ is not strictly less than $1$, no agreements are essential. The condition is satisfied by choosing an empty subset, resulting in a minimum total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3 (so $t = 3$).
For the agreement between 1 and 2, corporation 1 is closer to 1 (1 key patent holder), while corporations 2 and 3 are closer to 2 (2 key patent holders). The patent imbalance is $|1 - 2| = 1$. Since $1 < 3$, this agreement is essential.
For the agreement between 2 and 3, corporations 1 and 2 are closer to 2 (2 key patent holders) and corporation 3 is closer to 3 (1 key patent holder). The patent imbalance is $|2 - 1| = 1$. Since $1 < 3$, this agreement is also essential.
Both agreements are essential and must be included in any valid subset. The minimum total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The problem was reframed to hide the standard "bridge finding" or "Steiner tree" patterns. The concept of removing an edge to form two components was replaced with a distance-based partition ("closer to u or closer to v"), which evaluates to the exact same two sets in a tree structure. Furthermore, the condition that both resulting sets must contain at least one key node was replaced with the mathematically equivalent arithmetic condition that the absolute difference in their key node counts must be strictly less than $t$. This forces solvers to either implement a naive distance calculation (which will TLE) or genuinely deduce that the distance partition maps to tree components and optimize it using subtree counts.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
In a unique-path network, the set of nodes closer to $u$ than $v$ for an edge $(u, v)$ is exactly the component containing $u$ if the edge were removed. Furthermore, if the total number of required nodes is $t$, the absolute difference between the counts in the two sets is strictly less than $t$ if and only if both sets contain at least one required node, meaning the exact same subset of agreements is deemed essential.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains equivalent to finding the sum of weights of the edges in the minimal subtree connecting a set of required vertices in a tree, which is solvable in O(N) time with a post-order traversal.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
