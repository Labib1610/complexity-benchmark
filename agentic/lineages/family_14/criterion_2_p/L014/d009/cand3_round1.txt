<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement allows two specific corporations to directly exchange assets and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a specific group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders.

To maintain equilibrium, the antitrust regulatory board guarantees that the market is fully integrated: any two corporations can perform indirect exchanges through some valid sequence of proposed agreements (a sequence where consecutive agreements share a corporation). Furthermore, the board enforces that the market structure is minimally sufficient. As a mathematical consequence of these two regulations, the total count of proposed agreements, $m$, is always exactly one fewer than the number of corporations.

For any proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$, we can measure the communication delay from any key patent holder $k$ to $u$ and to $v$. The delay is defined as the minimum number of agreements in a valid sequence linking them. 

We classify a proposed agreement between $u$ and $v$ as *critical* if both of the following conditions are met:
1. There is at least one key patent holder whose communication delay to $u$ is strictly less than their communication delay to $v$.
2. There is at least one key patent holder whose communication delay to $v$ is strictly less than their communication delay to $u$.

Your goal is to choose a subset of the proposed agreements to finalize. A chosen subset is considered valid if and only if it includes every *critical* agreement. 

You must find the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. The market operates under a charter that supports up to exactly two hundred thousand active corporations, meaning $n$ is between one and this maximum capacity. The headcount of key patent holders, $t$, ranges from a single holder up to the entire market of $n$.
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders. Corporations are numbered starting from 1 up to $n$.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ ($u \neq v$). The legal fee $w$ requires a minimum deposit of one credit and is physically limited by the escrow system's maximum transaction size of exactly one million credits.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. 
Consider the agreement between 1 and 2. The delay from key holder 1 to corporation 1 is 0, and to corporation 2 is 1 (strictly less to 1). The delay from key holder 4 to corporation 2 is 2, and to corporation 1 is 3 (strictly less to 2). Since at least one key holder is closer to 1 and at least one is closer to 2, this agreement is critical.
Similarly, for the agreement between 2 and 3, key holder 1 is closer to 2 (delay 1 vs 2) and key holder 4 is closer to 3 (delay 1 vs 2). For the agreement between 3 and 4, key holder 1 is closer to 3 and key holder 4 is closer to 4. All three agreements are critical. To form a valid subset, all three must be included. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8.

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder (corporation 3). For any agreement, corporation 3 will be closer to one endpoint than the other, but there is no *second* key patent holder to be closer to the opposite endpoint. Thus, no agreements are critical. The condition is vacuously satisfied by choosing an empty subset, resulting in a minimum total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3.
Consider the agreement between 1 and 2. Key holder 1 is closer to 1 (delay 0 vs 1). Key holder 2 is closer to 2 (delay 0 vs 1). Thus, this agreement is critical.
Consider the agreement between 2 and 3. Key holder 2 is closer to 2 (delay 0 vs 1). Key holder 3 is closer to 3 (delay 0 vs 1). Thus, this agreement is also critical.
Both agreements are critical and must be included in any valid subset. The minimum total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I redefined criticality based on shortest-path distances ("communication delay") from key nodes to the endpoints of each edge, rather than explicitly describing graph components. This forces models to either perform a deep mathematical deduction (realizing that "closer to u than v" in a tree is exactly the connected component of u when the edge is cut) or implement a literal distance-checking algorithm which will inevitably result in a Time Limit Exceeded due to $O(N^2)$ complexity. Furthermore, I applied S07 by expressing the numeric bounds indirectly through market regulations and system limits.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
In a tree, the set of nodes strictly closer to endpoint $u$ than to $v$ is exactly the set of nodes in the connected component containing $u$ when the edge $(u,v)$ is removed, making this distance-based condition mathematically identical to the original component-based condition, so the set of critical agreements and their minimum total fee remains exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains solvable in $O(N)$ time using the exact same DFS approach to count key nodes in subtrees, preserving its polynomial complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
