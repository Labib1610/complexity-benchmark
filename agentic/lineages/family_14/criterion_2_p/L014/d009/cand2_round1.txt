<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders. 

The market is currently fully integrated, meaning any two corporations can be linked by some valid sequence of proposed agreements (a sequence where consecutive agreements share a corporation).

We classify a proposed agreement as *critical* if it acts as an absolute bottleneck between at least one pair of key patent holders. Specifically, an agreement is critical if there exists some pair of key patent holders such that *every* valid sequence of proposed agreements linking them includes this specific agreement. 

Your goal is to choose a subset of the proposed agreements to finalize. A chosen subset is considered valid if and only if it includes every *critical* agreement. 

You must find the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements. (Note that the finalized subset does not need to keep the entire market fully integrated; it only needs to contain all critical agreements).

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. 
The only way to link corporation 1 and corporation 4 is through the sequence of agreements: (1,2), (2,3), and (3,4). Since all three agreements must be used to link these key patent holders, all three are absolute bottlenecks and thus critical. To form a valid subset, all three must be included. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder (corporation 3). Since a pair of key patent holders is required to define a bottleneck, no agreements can act as a bottleneck between a pair. Thus, no agreements are critical. The condition is vacuously satisfied by choosing an empty subset, resulting in a minimum total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3.
The agreement between 1 and 2 is an absolute bottleneck between key patent holders 1 and 2 (and also between 1 and 3), because every sequence linking them must use it.
The agreement between 2 and 3 is an absolute bottleneck between key patent holders 2 and 3 (and also between 1 and 3).
Both agreements are critical and must be included in any valid subset. The minimum total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.

This forces the models to either recognize the hidden tree structure from the examples or attempt to implement a general bridge-finding algorithm, breaking their reliance on simple subtree counting.
