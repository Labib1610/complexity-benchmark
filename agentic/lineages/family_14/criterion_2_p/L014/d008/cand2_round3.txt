<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders. 

To maintain market equilibrium, the antitrust regulatory board has issued a certificate that enforces strict structural limits on the proposed agreements. The board mandates that the proposed agreements are sufficient to link all corporations together into a single unified market (meaning any corporation can be linked to any other through a sequence of proposed agreements). Furthermore, the certificate guarantees that the total count of proposed agreements, $m$, is exactly the minimum possible number required to achieve this full market connectivity for the $n$ corporations.

We classify a proposed agreement as *critical* if its removal from the set of all $m$ proposed agreements would divide the market's corporations into two isolated segments, such that each segment contains at least one key patent holder. (Two corporations are considered to be in the same segment if they can still be linked by a sequence of the remaining proposed agreements).

Your goal is to choose a subset of the proposed agreements to finalize. A chosen subset is considered valid if and only if it includes every *critical* agreement. 

You must find the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. 
Removing the agreement between 1 and 2 separates the market into a segment containing corporation 1 and another containing 2, 3, and 4. Since corporation 1 is in the first segment and corporation 4 is in the second, both segments contain at least one key patent holder. Thus, this agreement is critical.
Similarly, removing the agreement between 2 and 3, or the agreement between 3 and 4, also separates the key patent holders 1 and 4 into different segments. All three agreements are critical. To form a valid subset, all three must be included. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder (corporation 3). Removing any agreement will leave corporation 3 in one segment, while the other segment will contain zero key patent holders. Thus, no agreements are critical. The condition is vacuously satisfied by choosing an empty subset, resulting in a minimum total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3.
Removing the agreement between 1 and 2 leaves corporation 1 in one segment and corporations 2 and 3 in the other. Both segments contain key patent holders, so this agreement is critical.
Removing the agreement between 2 and 3 leaves corporations 1 and 2 in one segment and corporation 3 in the other. Both segments contain key patent holders, so this agreement is also critical.
Both agreements are critical and must be included in any valid subset. The minimum total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I removed the explicit statements that $m = n - 1$ and that there is exactly one sequence linking any two corporations, substituting them with the guarantee that the agreements achieve full market connectivity using the absolute minimum possible number of agreements. This mathematically preserves the tree topology exactly (a connected graph with minimal edges must be a tree with $n-1$ edges), but forces the solver to deduce this structure rather than triggering on explicit keywords. This addresses the shortcut where models instantly initialize tree arrays without analyzing the graph properties.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The graph is still guaranteed to be a tree, the definition of a critical agreement is unchanged, and all positive edge weights mean the optimal subset is still exactly the set of critical agreements, keeping all answers identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still reduces to finding the unique minimal subtree spanning a set of required vertices, resolvable via simple linear-time tree traversals, preserving its polynomial complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
