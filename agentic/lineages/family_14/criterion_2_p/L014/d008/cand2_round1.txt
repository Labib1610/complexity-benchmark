<METHOD_NAME>Minimally Integrated Market (Bridge-Based Tree Definition)</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the explicit tree properties ("m = n - 1" and "exactly one valid sequence") with a structural definition of a tree as a connected graph where every edge is a bridge ("minimally integrated market"). This removes the shortcut that allows models to immediately instantiate a rooted tree data structure without first deducing the graph's topology.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>
<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of corporations are identified as key patent holders.

To maintain market equilibrium, the antitrust regulatory board has issued a certificate that enforces strict structural limits on the proposed agreements. The board has certified the proposed agreements as a *minimally integrated market*: it is possible for any corporation to interact with any other through a chain of agreements, but the cancellation of any single proposed agreement would destroy this full integration, leaving at least some corporations unable to interact.

An agreement is deemed *essential* if its cancellation from the entire set of proposed agreements would fracture the market into two isolated sectors, such that each sector retains at least one key patent holder. (Corporations reside in the same sector if they can still interact via a chain of the remaining proposed agreements).

Your objective is to authorize a subset of the proposed agreements. An authorized subset is considered compliant if and only if it includes every *essential* agreement. You must determine the minimum total legal fee of a compliant subset of authorized agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$. The value $n$ represents the total number of corporations (between one and the board's maximum caseload of two hundred thousand). The value $m$ is the total count of proposed agreements. The value $t$ is the count of key patent holders (from one up to $n$).

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ (where $u$ and $v$ are distinct valid identifiers from $1$ to $n$) with a legal fee of $w$ (between one and the statutory limit of one million).

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a compliant subset of authorized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4.
Cancelling the agreement between 1 and 2 separates the market into a sector containing corporation 1 and another containing 2, 3, and 4. Since corporation 1 is in the first sector and corporation 4 is in the second, both sectors contain at least one key patent holder. Thus, this agreement is essential.
Similarly, cancelling the agreement between 2 and 3, or the agreement between 3 and 4, also separates the key patent holders 1 and 4 into different sectors. All three agreements are essential. To form a compliant subset, all three must be authorized. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8.

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder (corporation 3). Cancelling any agreement will leave corporation 3 in one sector, while the other sector will contain zero key patent holders. Thus, no agreements are essential. The condition is vacuously satisfied by authorizing an empty subset, resulting in a minimum total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3.
Cancelling the agreement between 1 and 2 leaves corporation 1 in one sector and corporations 2 and 3 in the other. Both sectors contain key patent holders, so this agreement is essential.
Cancelling the agreement between 2 and 3 leaves corporations 1 and 2 in one sector and corporation 3 in the other. Both sectors contain key patent holders, so this agreement is also essential.
Both agreements are essential and must be authorized in any compliant subset. The minimum total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit tree guarantees ("m = n - 1" and "exactly one valid sequence") were removed, as they acted as direct triggers for models to instantiate rooted tree traversal structures. Instead, the graph is defined as a "minimally integrated market" (a connected graph where every edge is a bridge), which mathematically guarantees it is a tree but requires the solver to deduce this topology. Numeric bounds were also translated into scenario-based regulatory limits (S07) to further obfuscate standard competitive programming templates.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition for an agreement to be "essential" is mathematically identical to the original "critical" condition, and since all agreement fees are strictly positive, the minimum total fee of a subset containing all essential agreements remains the sum of the fees of those essential agreements.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires identifying the edges that lie on the paths between the required nodes in a tree, which can be solved in O(n) using standard tree traversals, preserving the polynomial time complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
