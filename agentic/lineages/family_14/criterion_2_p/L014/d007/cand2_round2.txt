<STATEMENT>
# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

In a regulated market, there are $n$ independent corporations (numbered 1 through $n$) and a set of $m$ proposed bilateral merger agreements. Each agreement involves two specific corporations and requires a defined legal fee to finalize.

Within this market, a particular group of $t$ corporations are identified as key patent holders.

The antitrust regulatory board has issued a certificate that enforces strict structural limits on the proposed agreements. The set of all $m$ proposed agreements is minimally connected: they link all $n$ corporations, and removing any single agreement would partition the market into exactly two isolated groups. Consequently, the overall count of proposed agreements, $m$, is exactly $n - 1$, and for any two corporations in the market, there is exactly one valid sequence of proposed agreements that links them (a sequence where consecutive agreements share a corporation, and no corporation is visited more than once).

Your goal is to choose a subset of the proposed agreements to finalize. A chosen subset is considered valid if and only if all key patent holders are connected to one another through the finalized agreements (i.e., for any two key patent holders, there exists a sequence of finalized agreements linking them).

You must find the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$, $m = n - 1$).
The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the key patent holders.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), representing a proposed agreement between corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ with a legal fee of $w$.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total legal fee of a valid subset of finalized agreements.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the key patent holders are 1 and 4. To ensure 1 and 4 are connected to each other, the finalized subset must include the agreement between 1 and 2, the agreement between 2 and 3, and the agreement between 3 and 4. The minimum total legal fee is 5 + 1 + 2 = 8.

In Example 2, there is only one key patent holder. The condition that all key patent holders are connected to one another is vacuously satisfied by choosing an empty subset of agreements, resulting in a minimum total legal fee of 0.

In Example 3, the key patent holders are 1, 2, and 3. To ensure all key patent holders are connected to one another, the finalized subset must include both the agreement between 1 and 2 and the agreement between 2 and 3. The total legal fee is 4 + 5 = 9.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I restored the exact numeric bounds in canonical form in the Input section to satisfy the hard constraints and gate feedback. To defeat the surviving models, I changed the validity condition from explicitly requiring the "unique sequence of agreements between every pair" to requiring that all key patent holders be "connected to one another". This forces models to deduce that connectivity on a minimally connected graph requires the union of unique paths, preventing them from relying on surface pattern matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because the underlying structure guarantees exactly one path between any two nodes, any subset of edges that connects a given set of nodes must contain the union of the unique paths between them; therefore, the minimum cost subset is identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains finding the minimum weight subtree connecting a subset of vertices in a tree, which is solvable in O(n) time using a post-order traversal or depth-first search.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
The phrase "connected to one another" could be misread as requiring direct connections (a clique), but the parenthetical definition "(i.e., for any two key patent holders, there exists a sequence of finalized agreements linking them)" eliminates this ambiguity.
</RISK>
