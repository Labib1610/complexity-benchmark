# Omnibus Bill Reconciliation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A massive omnibus bill contains $R$ distinct clauses, numbered 1 to $R$. Over the course of negotiations, $S$ amendments have been proposed. Each amendment bridges exactly two clauses.
By parliamentary rules, the $S$ amendments (where $S = R - 1$) are drafted such that for any two clauses, there is exactly one series of amendments that can be traced sequentially to bridge them.

A coalition of lawmakers has designated $P$ specific clauses as "non-negotiable". To secure their votes, you must ensure that all $P$ non-negotiable clauses are fully reconciled with one another.
Two clauses are considered "reconciled" if the single series of amendments bridging them consists entirely of adopted amendments.
Each amendment requires a certain amount of political capital to adopt.
Your task is to select a subset of the amendments to adopt such that every possible pair of the $P$ non-negotiable clauses is reconciled, while spending the minimum possible total political capital.

## Input

The first line contains three integers $R$, $S$, and $P$ ($1 \le P \le R \le 200000$). The number of amendments $S$ is always exactly $R - 1$.
The second line contains $P$ distinct integers: the identifiers of the non-negotiable clauses.
Each of the next $S$ lines contains three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le R$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$): an amendment bridging clause $u$ and clause $v$, requiring $w$ political capital to adopt. 
The amendments provided in the input are guaranteed to bridge all $R$ clauses such that for every pair of clauses, there is exactly one series of amendments bridging them.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total political capital required to adopt a valid subset of amendments.

## Examples

```

```

<RATIONALE>
I completely changed the real-world scenario from a network routing problem to a legislative voting problem. This removes keywords like "tunnels", "vaults", "network", and "routing" which models use to trigger tree/graph templates, forcing them to deduce the tree structure and the minimal subtree requirement from the logical constraints of "clauses" and "amendments".
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The required subset of amendments is mathematically identical to the minimal spanning subtree of the required nodes, so the sum of their weights remains exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains the exact same task of finding the weight of the minimal subtree connecting a set of nodes in a given tree, which is solvable in linear time ($O(R)$).
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might struggle with the definition of "series of amendments that can be traced sequentially", though it is the standard natural language equivalent of a path in a graph.
</RISK>
