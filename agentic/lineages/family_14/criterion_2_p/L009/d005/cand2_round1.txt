<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the objective of "find the minimum subtree" with "find half the length of the shortest closed walk visiting the target nodes". On a tree, the shortest closed walk visiting a set of nodes traverses every edge of their Steiner tree exactly twice. This preserves the exact answer and complexity class while disguising the structural graph problem as a routing/TSP problem, forcing the model to discover the mathematical equivalence rather than pattern-matching tree pruning.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Molecular Routing

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A newly discovered protein structure consists of **N** amino acids, labeled 1 through **N**. 

There are **E** peptide bonds available in the structure. Each bond connects a specific pair of amino acids, **u** and **v**, and requires a folding energy of **w** to traverse. 

The structural bounds are derived from the physical properties of the system:
- The total number of amino acids, **N**, is a positive integer bounded by the number of millimeters in two hundred meters.
- The number of bonds, **E**, is exactly the minimum required to ensure every amino acid is connected to the structure, meaning **E** is exactly one less than **N**. This guarantees there is exactly one unique sequence of non-repeating bonds between any two amino acids.
- The folding energy **w** of any single bond is a positive integer that does not exceed the square of one thousand.
- The number of active sites, **F**, ranges from a single site up to the total number of amino acids in the structure.

A subset of **F** amino acids are designated as active sites. To trigger a reaction, a molecular messenger must travel along the bonds in a continuous sequence of traversals (a closed walk). This walk must start at an amino acid of your choice, visit all **F** active sites at least once, and end at the exact same starting amino acid. 

The total folding energy expended during this walk is the sum of the folding energies of every bond traversal. If a single bond is traversed multiple times, its energy is added to the total for every individual traversal. 

Determine the minimum possible total folding energy expended by a valid closed walk, and output exactly half of this value. (Because of the network's structure, any valid closed walk must traverse each bond it uses an equal number of times in both directions, guaranteeing the minimum total energy is an even number and the final answer is a precise integer).

## Input

The first line contains three integers **N**, **E**, and **F**.
The second line contains **F** distinct integers: the labels of the active sites.
Each of the following **E** lines contains three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**, representing a bond between amino acid **u** and amino acid **v** with a folding energy of **w**.

## Output

Print a single integer: exactly half of the minimum total folding energy required for the messenger's closed walk.

<RATIONALE>
I changed the objective from finding a minimal connecting subgraph to finding half the length of the shortest closed walk visiting the target nodes. Because the network has exactly one unique path between any two points, the shortest closed walk visiting a subset of nodes must traverse every edge of their minimal spanning subtree exactly twice. This logically forces the model to either recognize this mathematical equivalence to the Steiner tree problem or fail by attempting intractable TSP/routing algorithms. I also applied S07 to require deriving the numeric bounds from physical descriptions.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The minimum closed walk visiting a set of nodes in a network with unique paths must traverse every edge of the minimal subtree spanning those nodes exactly twice, making half of its total energy mathematically identical to the sum of weights of that subtree.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains solvable in O(N) time by identifying the minimal subtree connecting the active sites and summing its weights, keeping it in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might mistakenly assume the messenger cannot traverse bonds multiple times if they skim, but the text explicitly states "If a single bond is traversed multiple times, its energy is added to the total for every individual traversal", eliminating this ambiguity.
</RISK>
