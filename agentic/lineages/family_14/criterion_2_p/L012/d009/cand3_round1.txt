<STATEMENT>
# Hardware State Entanglement

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are configuring a quantum-inspired computing architecture with $n$ distinct registers. An adversary intends to destabilize the system by assigning an arbitrary integer "phase state" to each register.

You have access to $m$ hardware locks. Each lock can be engaged between two specific registers, $u$ and $v$, incurring a specific energy cost. When a lock is engaged, the architecture physically enforces that register $u$ and register $v$ must collapse into the exact same phase state. The adversary cannot bypass engaged locks; any phase state assignment they create must strictly respect these physical constraints.

The adversary's goal is to successfully assign phase states to all $n$ registers such that at least two registers in a designated set of $t$ **Primary Registers** end up with *different* phase states.

The complete set of $m$ available locks is known to be fully comprehensive: if you were to engage all of them, the hardware would force all $n$ registers in the architecture to collapse into a single identical phase state. Furthermore, the number of available locks is exactly $m = n - 1$.

Your objective is to determine the minimum total energy cost required to engage a subset of hardware locks that makes the adversary's goal strictly impossible. (If $t=1$, the adversary's goal is inherently impossible, requiring 0 energy).

## Input

The first line contains three integers: $n$, $m$, and $t$.
- $n$ is the number of registers. There is at least 1 register and at most 200,000.
- $m$ is the number of available hardware locks, which is exactly $n - 1$.
- $t$ is the number of Primary Registers, which is at least 1 and at most $n$.

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the indices of the Primary Registers (from 1 to $n$).

Each of the next $m$ lines details an available hardware lock with three integers: $u$, $v$, and $w$.
- $u$ and $v$ are the distinct registers (from 1 to $n$) constrained by the lock.
- $w$ is the energy cost to engage this lock. This cost is a positive integer not exceeding 1,000,000.

The input guarantees that the full collection of $m$ locks is comprehensive as described above.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total energy cost to engage a subset of locks that guarantees all Primary Registers must share the same phase state.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the Primary Registers are 1 and 4. To make it impossible for the adversary to assign different phase states to registers 1 and 4, they must be transitively locked. This can only be achieved by engaging all three hardware locks, since locking (1,2), (2,3), and (3,4) forces registers 1 and 4 to share the same state. The minimum energy cost is therefore 5 + 1 + 2 = 8. 

In Example 2, there is only one Primary Register. Since the adversary needs at least two Primary Registers to have different states, their goal is already impossible. No hardware locks need to be engaged, resulting in a minimum energy cost of 0.
