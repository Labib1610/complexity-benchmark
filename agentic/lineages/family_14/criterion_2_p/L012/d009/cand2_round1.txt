<STATEMENT>
# Regulated Price Synchronization

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An economic simulation models a market with $n$ distinct commodities. The system allows an administrator to impose price-equality regulations. 

You are provided with a catalog of $m$ candidate regulations. Each candidate regulation ties the prices of two specific commodities, $u$ and $v$, legally mandating that their prices must be strictly equal ($p_u = p_v$). Enacting a regulation incurs a known administrative cost.

The full catalog of candidate regulations is known to be globally restrictive and minimally so: if all $m$ regulations were enacted, the only valid pricing state would be one where all $n$ commodities share the exact same price. Because it is minimal, the number of candidate regulations is exactly one less than the number of commodities.

A specific subset of $t$ commodities are designated as *Benchmark Commodities*. 

Your objective is to enact a subset of the candidate regulations. Let $S$ be your selected subset. Your selection must satisfy the following universal property: for any hypothetical assignment of prices $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n$ to the commodities, if every regulation in $S$ is perfectly satisfied, it must logically follow that all Benchmark Commodities share the exact same price. (If $t \le 1$, this property is vacuously true for any subset, including the empty set).

You must determine the minimum total administrative cost required to enact a valid subset of regulations meeting this universal property.

## Input

The first line contains three integers: $n$, $m$, and $t$.
- $n$ is the number of commodities. The simulation models at least a single commodity, and up to a strict architectural limit of two hundred thousand commodities.
- $m$ is the number of candidate regulations. To guarantee exactly one global degree of freedom without redundancy, this is always exactly one less than the commodity count.
- $t$ is the number of Benchmark Commodities. A non-empty subset of up to all commodities are designated as benchmarks.

The second line contains $t$ distinct integers: the indices of the Benchmark Commodities (from 1 to $n$).

Each of the next $m$ lines details a candidate regulation with three integers: $u$, $v$, and $w$.
- $u$ and $v$ are the distinct indices (from 1 to $n$) of the commodities whose prices are tied by this regulation.
- $w$ is the administrative cost to enact this regulation. This cost is a strictly positive integer, bounded by a million units.

These $m$ lines form the exact structure described above, guaranteeing the full catalog restricts the market to a single uniform price.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum total administrative cost required to ensure all Benchmark Commodities are forced to share the identical price under the selected subset of regulations.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 3 2
1 4
1 2 5
2 3 1
3 4 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 4 1
3
1 2 4
2 3 6
3 4 2
4 5 9
```

**Output**
```
0
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 2 3
1 2 3
1 2 4
2 3 5
```

**Output**
```
9
```

## Note

In Example 1, the Benchmark Commodities are 1 and 4. To mathematically guarantee that commodity 1 and commodity 4 have the same price under any valid pricing state, the regulations tying (1, 2), (2, 3), and (3, 4) must all be enacted. If any are omitted, a pricing state could exist where $p_1 \neq p_4$ while still satisfying the enacted regulations. The minimum cost is therefore 5 + 1 + 2 = 8.

In Example 2, there is only one Benchmark Commodity. Since no pairs of Benchmark Commodities exist to diverge in price, the universal property is satisfied without enacting any regulations, resulting in a minimum cost of 0.

<RATIONALE>
The problem was reframed from a linear algebraic vector-spanning problem to a logical implication problem over continuous variables (prices). By asking for a subset of constraints that logically forces a set of variables to be equal, it requires the model to independently realize that this constraint-satisfaction property is perfectly isomorphic to graph connectivity, breaking the explicit graph-path pattern matching triggered by "expressible as a linear combination". Numeric bounds were also embedded into the scenario text (S07) to prevent simple scanning.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The mathematical condition (forcing a subset of variables to be equal via pairwise equalities) is exactly equivalent to requiring the selected edges to connect all target vertices in the underlying graph, so the minimum cost subset remains identically the Steiner tree.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying structure is still finding the minimal subtree connecting a set of terminal nodes in an input tree, which remains solvable in $O(n)$ time via a single traversal, keeping it in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might find the phrase "for any hypothetical assignment... it must logically follow" slightly abstract, though it rigorously and unambiguously defines mathematical implication without edge cases.
</RISK>
