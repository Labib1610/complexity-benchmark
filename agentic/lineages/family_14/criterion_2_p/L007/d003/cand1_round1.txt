<STATEMENT>
# Datacenter Activation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A computing facility contains **n** server racks, numbered 1 to **n**. The facility is equipped with **n − 1** fiber-optic segments. The architecture ensures that if every segment is operational, there is exactly one valid communication path between any two racks. Bringing a segment online requires a specific activation fee.

A client has acquired access to **t** specific racks. Your task is to bring a collection of segments online so that all **t** client racks are mutually connected (meaning data can travel between any pair of client racks using only the online segments). Any rack in the facility can forward traffic if the segments attached to it are online. Every rack used on an active route has a rack activation expense, and this expense must be included in your minimum total calculation. For this specific facility, the rack activation expense is uniformly 0. Therefore, your total network cost is calculated by summing the activation fees of the segments you choose to bring online, plus the rack activation expenses for all racks involved.

Determine the lowest possible total network cost to fully interconnect the client's racks.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ t ≤ n, 1 ≤ n ≤ 200000, m = n − 1).
The second line provides **t** distinct integers, which are the indices of the client's racks.
The next **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1000000). This indicates a fiber-optic segment between rack **u** and rack **v** that costs **w** to bring online. The provided architecture of **m** segments guarantees that exactly one valid sequence of segments exists between any two racks when fully operational.

## Output

Output a single integer: the lowest total network cost to bring the required segments online and activate the involved racks.

<RATIONALE>
Removed the phrase indicating unrented racks are "free relay switches" and replaced it with a rule stating that every rack used on a route has a rack activation expense that must be included in the total cost calculation. To preserve the identical answers, this rack expense is defined as 0. This forces models to parse the cost components rather than relying on the keyword "free" to instantly map the problem to a standard edge-weight-only Steiner tree on a graph.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The total cost adds the segment fees and the rack expenses, but since all rack expenses are uniformly 0, the sum is mathematically identical to just summing the segment fees as in the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains finding the minimal subtree connecting a set of required vertices in a tree, which is solvable in O(n) time using a depth-first search, keeping it in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might be confused by the explicit inclusion of a 0-cost component and attempt to read rack costs from the input, though the text explicitly states the cost is uniformly 0.
</RISK>
