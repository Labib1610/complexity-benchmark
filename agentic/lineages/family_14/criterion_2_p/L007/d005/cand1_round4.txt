# Datacenter Activation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A computing facility houses $n$ server racks, indexed starting from 1 up to $n$. The facility's architectural limit accommodates up to two hundred thousand racks in total. The infrastructure is fully interconnected through pre-wired bidirectional fiber-optic links. To avoid redundancy, the total number of links $m$ is exactly one less than the number of racks, yet this layout still ensures that every single rack can reach any other rack through some sequence of connections.

A customer has rented a specific group of $t$ distinct racks; this group contains at least one rack, and can be as large as the entire set of $n$ racks. Each fiber-optic link has a specific activation expense to power on, which is at least 1 and capped at one million. 

Your objective is to determine the minimum possible total activation expense of a subset of links that must be powered on so that every rented rack can exchange data with every other rented rack. Data can be routed through intermediate racks that the customer has not rented. Any link that is not required to maintain this mutual connectivity among the $t$ rented racks can be left powered off.

## Input

The first line provides three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$.
The second line provides $t$ distinct integers, representing the indices of the rented racks.
The next $m$ lines each contain three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$, indicating a pre-wired bidirectional link between rack $u$ and rack $v$ ($u \neq v$, both being valid rack indices) with an activation expense of $w$.

## Output

Output a single integer: the minimum total activation expense of the links that must be powered on to ensure all rented racks can communicate with each other.
