# Corporate Consolidation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

An industry consists of **n** independent corporations, numbered 1 to **n**. Regulatory approvals have pre-cleared exactly **n − 1** potential bilateral merger agreements. The nature of these pre-cleared agreements guarantees that if all of them were executed, there would be exactly one unique sequence of mergers connecting any given pair of corporations into a single entity. Executing each bilateral agreement requires a specific legal expenditure.

An investment firm has acquired a controlling stake in a specific portfolio of **t** corporations. Your objective is to execute a selection of the pre-cleared merger agreements so that every corporation in the firm's portfolio ends up in the same unified conglomerate. Corporations outside the portfolio may also be included in this conglomerate if their mergers are executed as part of the sequence.

Calculate the minimum total legal expenditure required to execute the necessary agreements to unite the portfolio.

## Input

The first line provides three integers **n**, **m**, and **t** (1 ≤ t ≤ n, 1 ≤ n ≤ 200000, m = n − 1).
The second line provides **t** distinct integers, which are the indices of the corporations in the portfolio.
The next **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v, 1 ≤ w ≤ 1000000). This indicates a pre-cleared merger agreement between corporation **u** and corporation **v** that costs **w** to execute. The given set of **m** agreements guarantees that exactly one valid sequence of agreements exists between any two corporations when all are executed.

## Output

Output a single integer: the minimum total legal expenditure under the rules above.
