# Datacenter Activation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A computing facility houses **n** server racks, indexed from 1 to **n**. The building is pre-wired with a collection of bidirectional fiber-optic links. The infrastructure is designed to provide full network coverage: if every pre-wired link is powered on, it is possible for any rack to communicate with any other rack in the facility. Powering on a link incurs a specific activation expense.

A customer has rented a specific group of **t** racks. Your objective is to select a subset of the fiber-optic links to power on, such that every rented rack can exchange data with every other rented rack (possibly routing through intermediate racks, which may or may not be rented by the customer). 

The total activation expense for your network is the sum of:
1. The activation expenses of all powered-on fiber-optic links.
2. A relay activation fee for every unrented rack that ends up being used in the active communication routes.

At present, the relay activation fee is fixed at 0 per unrented rack. You must determine the minimum total activation expense necessary to fully connect the customer's rented racks.

**System Limits and Capacities:**
- The facility's architectural limit allows a total rack count (**n**) of up to two hundred thousand. There is always at least one rack.
- The total number of installed fiber-optic links (**m**) is strictly one less than the total number of racks.
- The customer's rental group size (**t**) ranges from a single rack up to the entire facility's inventory.
- The expense to power on any single link is a positive integer capped at one million.

## Input

The first line provides the three integers **n**, **m**, and **t**.
The second line provides **t** distinct integers representing the indices of the rented racks.
The next **m** lines each contain three integers **u**, **v**, and **w**. This indicates a pre-wired fiber-optic link between rack **u** and rack **v** that costs **w** to power on. All rack indices provided are valid identifiers within the facility, and no link connects a rack to itself.

## Output

Output a single integer: the minimum total activation expense to power on the required links and racks.

<RATIONALE>
The explicit guarantees of "exactly one unique communication route" and "valid sequence of links" were removed. The solver must now deduce that a fully connected network with exactly n-1 links is structurally a tree. Models relying on pattern matching will likely recognize the objective as the Steiner Tree problem and, failing to deduce the tree structure, will apply an intractable general-graph algorithm.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Since a connected graph with n vertices and n-1 edges is mathematically a tree, the minimal connected subgraph containing the rented racks remains exactly the same unique subset of edges, yielding the same minimum cost.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem class remains P because the underlying graph is still guaranteed to be a tree (by the combination of connectivity and edge count), meaning the optimal solution can still be found in O(n) time via post-order traversal.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
