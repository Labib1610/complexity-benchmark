<METHOD_NAME>Orthogonal Complement Dualization</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the condition "the span of S contains W_perp" with the mathematically equivalent dual condition "the orthogonal complement of the span of S is a subspace of W". This hides the "linear combination of edges forms a path" intuition behind a null-space containment condition, forcing models to explicitly apply orthogonal duality before they can recognize the underlying graph connectivity structure.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>

<STATEMENT>
# Orthogonal Complement Subspace

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are working in the $n$-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the standard inner product.

You are provided with a generator pool of $m = n - 1$ distinct basis vectors. Each vector is defined by a pair of indices $u$ and $v$ ($u \neq v$), representing the vector $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{e}_u - \mathbf{e}_v$ (where $\mathbf{e}_i$ is the $i$-th standard basis vector), and has a specific inclusion cost $w$.

It is guaranteed that the full pool of available vectors spans the entire $(n-1)$-dimensional hyperplane defined by the equation $\sum_{i=1}^n x_i = 0$.

A researcher has specified a target subset of $t$ indices, denoted as $T$. Let $W$ be the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ consisting of all vectors $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n)$ such that $x_a = x_b$ for every pair of indices $a, b \in T$.

Your objective is to select a subset $S$ of the available vectors such that the orthogonal complement of the linear span of $S$ is a subspace of $W$. Note that the orthogonal complement of your selected span may also enforce equalities between coordinates outside of $T$, which is permissible.

Determine the minimum total inclusion cost of a subset of vectors that must be selected to satisfy the researcher's requirement. Any vector not strictly needed to satisfy the subspace condition can be omitted.

## Input

The first line provides three integers $n$, $m$, and $t$ ($1 \le t \le n$, $1 \le n \le 200000$, $m = n - 1$).
The second line provides $t$ distinct integers, representing the indices in the target subset $T$.
The next $m$ lines each contain three integers $u$, $v$, and $w$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$, $1 \le w \le 1000000$), indicating an available vector $\mathbf{e}_u - \mathbf{e}_v$ with an inclusion cost of $w$.

## Output

Output a single integer: the minimum total inclusion cost of the selected subset of vectors.

<RATIONALE>
The previous version used a "span contains" condition, which models easily mapped to finding paths in an incidence matrix graph because linear combinations of difference vectors directly correspond to graph paths. By taking the orthogonal complement of both sides, the condition $W^\perp \subseteq V$ is transformed into $V^\perp \subseteq W$. The problem now asks for the null-space of the selected vectors to be contained in a subspace defined by coordinate equalities. To solve this efficiently, a model must explicitly apply orthogonal duality to recover the original spanning condition before it can recognize the tree structure, breaking superficial pattern matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that the orthogonal complement of the span of $S$ is a subspace of $W$ ($V^\perp \subseteq W$) is mathematically identical to the condition that the span of $S$ contains the orthogonal complement of $W$ ($W^\perp \subseteq V$), which is exactly the spanning requirement of the seed problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
Because the mathematical requirement is strictly equivalent to the original spanning condition, the optimal subset still corresponds to the minimal Steiner tree spanning the target indices, keeping the problem in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
