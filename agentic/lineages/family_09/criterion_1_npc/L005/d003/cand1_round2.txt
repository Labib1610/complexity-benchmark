# Problem A — Productive Assembly

Time limit: 5 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A corporate division consists of **n** staff members (identified by numbers from 1 to **n**). Over time, HR has logged **m** interpersonal conflicts. Each conflict record specifies a pair of staff members who cannot work together. The department operates in an office with a strict capacity of exactly 46 workstations, limiting the staff count **n** to a maximum of 46 (with a minimum of 1). The number of conflicts **m** can range from 0 up to a maximum of **n**·(**n**−1)/2. The target group size **k** must be between 1 and **n**.

Management wants to form a specialized working group. A working group is deemed **productive** if every logged conflict has at least one of its two associated staff members absent from the working group. Equivalently, no two selected staff members may have a conflict logged between them.

You are given a required group size **k**. Determine if it is possible to form a productive working group of exactly **k** staff members. If it is possible, output the members of one such group.

## Input

The first line contains three integers **n**, **m**, and **k** (1 ≤ n ≤ 46, 0 ≤ m ≤ n·(n−1)/2, 1 ≤ k ≤ n).
Each of the next **m** lines contains two integers **u** and **v** (1 ≤ u, v ≤ n, u ≠ v), representing the two staff members involved in a conflict. The input will never contain more than one conflict involving the same pair of staff members (i.e., no pair of staff members appears more than once, regardless of order).

## Output

If a productive working group of **k** staff members exists, print `YES` on the first line, then on the second line print **k** distinct staff member numbers forming such a group, separated by spaces. Any group that satisfies the condition is accepted. Print the **k** group members (not the remaining staff).

If no such group exists, print `NO`.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
4 4 3
1 2
1 3
2 3
3 4
```

**Output**
```
NO
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
5 2 3
1 2
3 4
```

**Output**
```
YES
1 3 5
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3 3 1
1 2
2 3
1 3
```

**Output**
```
YES
1
```

## Note

A productive working group ensures that for every recorded conflict, the group leaves out at least one of the involved staff members. In Example 2, staff members 1, 3, and 5 have no conflicts among them. In Example 1, no three of the four staff members can be selected without including both participants of at least one conflict.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The problem was reframed around "productive assemblies" and "interpersonal conflicts" rather than "harmonious delegations" and "friction events". The core condition was rephrased from "no single event has both included" to "every logged conflict has at least one of its associated staff members absent." This forces models to parse the exclusion logic accurately rather than relying on standard phrasing for the Independent Set constraint. Bounds are integrated into the narrative to follow the strategy, while maintaining strict formal constraints in the input section to satisfy the I/O contract.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that at least one member of every conflict pair must be absent from the chosen group is logically identical to requiring that no conflict pair is entirely present in the group, preserving the exact same valid subsets as the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains equivalent to finding an Independent Set of size k (or a Clique of size k in the complement graph), which is NP-complete.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
