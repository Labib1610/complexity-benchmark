# Problem B — Obstacle Course Design

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are designing an obstacle course consisting of **j** distinct segments. Each segment features an Upper lane obstacle and a Lower lane obstacle. You must choose a single order in which to arrange these **j** segments. The segments are placed sequentially from 1 to **j** in your chosen order.

A competitor will run through your arranged course. However, the competitor wants to maximize their time on the course. They will do this by choosing a single "drop point", which is an integer index **k** (where 1 ≤ **k** ≤ **j**).

The competitor's route is defined as follows:
- They start at the Upper lane of segment 1.
- They proceed along the Upper lane through segments 1, 2, ..., up to segment **k**.
- At segment **k**, after completing its Upper lane obstacle, they drop down to the Lower lane obstacle of segment **k**.
- They then proceed along the Lower lane through segments **k**, **k**+1, ..., up to segment **j**.

The total time the competitor takes is the sum of the times of all obstacles they complete. Because they drop at segment **k**, they complete the Upper lane obstacles of segments 1 through **k**, and the Lower lane obstacles of segments **k** through **j**. Note that for segment **k**, the competitor completes both the Upper and Lower lane obstacles.

You choose the segment order first. After that, the competitor is assumed to choose the drop point **k** that maximizes their total time. 
You must determine the optimal sequence of the **j** segments to minimize the maximum possible total time the competitor can achieve.

What is the minimum possible worst-case total time?

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the following **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000), representing the time required to complete the segment's Upper lane obstacle and Lower lane obstacle, respectively.

## Output

Print a single integer: the minimum possible total time assuming the competitor chooses the drop point optimally to maximize it.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, there is only one segment (j=1). The competitor must choose drop point k=1. They complete the Upper lane obstacle (5 time units) and then the Lower lane obstacle (3 time units). The total time is 8.
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Replaced the explicit two-machine scheduling scenario with an adversarial grid-path problem where an opponent chooses a "drop point" to maximize time. This perfectly preserves the mathematics of the two-machine makespan while stripping away the scheduling vocabulary that triggers Johnson's rule pattern matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The maximum path sum in a 2xN grid where the path goes right then down then right is mathematically identical to the makespan of a 2-machine flow shop, so the optimal sequence yields the exact same value.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem requires finding a permutation to minimize a maximum prefix/suffix sum, which is exactly solvable in O(N log N) using Johnson's algorithm, keeping it in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Solvers might misunderstand that the drop point segment `k` contributes both its Upper and Lower lane times, though the text explicitly states "for segment k, the competitor completes both".
</RISK>
