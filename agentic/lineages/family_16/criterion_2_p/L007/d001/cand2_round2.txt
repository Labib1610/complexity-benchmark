# Security Checkpoints

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are designing a security corridor consisting of **j** sequential checkpoints. You have a set of **j** different scanning devices to install, one at each checkpoint. 

Each scanning device has two components: a primary scanner that emits **p1** units of radiation, and a secondary scanner that emits **p2** units of radiation.

You must choose a single sequence in which to arrange all **j** devices along the corridor. Once you choose the sequence, the primary scanners form a line, and the secondary scanners form a parallel line. The $i$-th device in your chosen sequence provides both the $i$-th primary scanner and the $i$-th secondary scanner.

When a subject walks through the corridor, they start at the first primary scanner. At some point, they will transition to the secondary scanners. Specifically, a subject will choose an index $k$ (where $1 \le k \le \mathbf{j}$). The subject will pass through the primary scanners of devices $1$ through $k$, and then immediately transition to pass through the secondary scanners of devices $k$ through **j**. 

The total radiation exposure for a subject who transitions at device $k$ is the sum of the radiation from the primary scanners they passed, plus the sum of the radiation from the secondary scanners they passed.

The *peak exposure level* of your chosen arrangement is the maximum possible total radiation exposure a subject could experience across all valid transition points $k$.

Your task is to determine the arrangement of the **j** devices that minimizes the peak exposure level. Report this minimum possible peak exposure level.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the radiation units for the primary and secondary scanners of that device, respectively.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible peak exposure level that can be achieved by optimally ordering the devices.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 1, one optimal arrangement is to place the third device first, the second device second, and the first device third. 
The primary scanners in order emit 1, 2, and 3 units.
The secondary scanners in order emit 3, 4, and 2 units.

A subject could transition at:
- $k=1$: passing the 1st primary scanner (1), and the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd secondary scanners (3, 4, 2). Total = $1 + 3 + 4 + 2 = 10$.
- $k=2$: passing the 1st and 2nd primary scanners (1, 2), and the 2nd and 3rd secondary scanners (4, 2). Total = $1 + 2 + 4 + 2 = 9$.
- $k=3$: passing the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd primary scanners (1, 2, 3), and the 3rd secondary scanner (2). Total = $1 + 2 + 3 + 2 = 8$.

The peak exposure level for this arrangement is $\max(10, 9, 8) = 10$. No arrangement can yield a peak exposure level strictly less than 10.

In Example 2, the single device has primary 5 and secondary 3. The only transition is at $k=1$, giving $5 + 3 = 8$.
