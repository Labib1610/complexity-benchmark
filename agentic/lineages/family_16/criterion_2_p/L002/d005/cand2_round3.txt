# Problem B — Cargo Inspection

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A port authority manages a fleet of **j** vessels arriving for cargo clearance. The clearance protocol consists of a Local phase followed by a Federal phase. Every vessel requires both phases. You are given the Local phase duration **p1** and the Federal phase duration **p2** for each vessel.

You are tasked with choosing a single, global processing sequence for the fleet. Both the Local and Federal teams are mandated to process the vessels in this exact sequence.

The completion times for each vessel are determined by the following strict operational rules, evaluated from the first vessel in your sequence to the last:

- The Local team begins work at minute 0. They process the vessels in the chosen sequence continuously, never pausing between vessels. As soon as the Local team completes a vessel, they immediately start the next one in the sequence, and the completed vessel is released to a secure transit zone.
- The Federal team operates independently but must strictly respect the same chosen sequence. They can only handle one vessel at a time. For any given vessel in the sequence, the Federal team begins their work at the exact moment both of the following conditions are satisfied:
  1. The vessel has been released into the secure transit zone (its Local phase is complete).
  2. The Federal team has finished processing all preceding vessels in the sequence.
- If the Federal team finishes a vessel but the next one in the sequence has not yet cleared the Local phase, the Federal team remains idle until it is released.

Your objective is to determine a sequence that minimizes the overall completion time—that is, the minute at which the final vessel in the sequence completes its Federal phase. Output this minimum possible value.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains two integers **p1** and **p2** (1 ≤ p1, p2 ≤ 1000000): the duration required for the Local phase and the Federal phase for that vessel, respectively.

## Output

Print a single integer: the earliest possible minute the final vessel can complete its Federal phase, assuming an optimal sequence is chosen.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 1, one optimal sequence is to process the third vessel, then the second, then the first.
- **First in sequence (third vessel):** The Local team finishes at minute 1. The Federal team starts at minute 1 and finishes at 1 + 3 = 4.
- **Second in sequence (second vessel):** The Local team finishes at 1 + 2 = 3. The Federal team is busy until minute 4, so it starts this vessel at minute 4 and finishes at 4 + 4 = 8.
- **Third in sequence (first vessel):** The Local team finishes at 3 + 3 = 6. The Federal team is busy until minute 8, so it starts this vessel at minute 8 and finishes at 8 + 2 = 10.
The final completion time is 10.

In Example 2, the single vessel finishes its Local phase at minute 5. The Federal team starts at minute 5 and finishes at 5 + 3 = 8.
