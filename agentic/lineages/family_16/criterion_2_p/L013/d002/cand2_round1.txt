<STATEMENT>
# Mountain Railway Ascent

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are coordinating the ascent of a fleet of trains up a mountain railway. The track is divided into two consecutive segments: Zone Alpha followed immediately by Zone Beta. 

Because of strict safety regulations, each zone can accommodate at most one train at any given moment. Furthermore, the single-track layout means that trains cannot overtake one another; they must enter Zone Beta in the exact same sequence they entered Zone Alpha.

You must design a strict departure sequence for the fleet. The movement of trains follows these rules:
- The first train in your sequence enters Zone Alpha at time 0.
- Each subsequent train enters Zone Alpha the exact moment the preceding train in the sequence exits Zone Alpha.
- A train is ready to enter Zone Beta the moment it exits Zone Alpha. However, if the preceding train has not yet exited Zone Beta, the current train must wait at the boundary. It will enter Zone Beta the exact moment the preceding train exits Zone Beta.
- A train completely clears Zone Alpha before waiting at the boundary. This means the next train in the sequence can immediately enter Zone Alpha, even if the current train is forced to wait before entering Zone Beta.

To ensure the summit station begins operations as early as possible, you must find a sequence that minimizes the absolute time at which the final train in the sequence exits Zone Beta.

The bounds for the fleet size and travel times are defined as follows:
- The total number of trains is an integer of at least 1, and will not exceed the total number of seconds in 55 hours, 33 minutes, and 20 seconds.
- The duration any train requires to traverse a zone is an integer of at least 1, and will not exceed the total number of millimeters in exactly one kilometer.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer representing the number of trains.
Each of the subsequent lines describes one train and contains two integers: its travel duration through Zone Alpha, and its travel duration through Zone Beta.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible time at which the final train exits Zone Beta.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, the single train takes 5 to traverse Zone Alpha and then 3 to traverse Zone Beta, exiting at time 8.

<RATIONALE>
The explicit references to "stages", "Lower Track / Upper Track", and "finishes stage 2" were removed because models were using them to pattern-match the problem directly to a two-machine flow shop scheduling task. The mechanics are now described as a continuous-time simulation with explicit boundary-waiting rules, forcing the model to deduce the recurrence relation (and thus the underlying scheduling structure) from the physical movement rules. Additionally, the numeric bounds were hidden behind arithmetic conversions (seconds in 55h 33m 20s, millimeters in a kilometer) to prevent bounds-based pattern matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The boundary-waiting logic and zone capacities mathematically enforce the exact same permutation flow-shop recurrence as the original problem, yielding the exact same minimum completion time for any given sequence.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains equivalent to minimizing the makespan of a two-machine permutation flow shop, which can be solved optimally in O(N log N) using Johnson's rule, keeping it firmly in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
The wording "completely clears Zone Alpha before waiting at the boundary" could theoretically be misread as introducing a third buffer zone, though the text clarifies that this simply allows the next train to enter Alpha immediately, matching standard flow-shop semantics.
</RISK>
