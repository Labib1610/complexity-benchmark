# Problem B — Canyon Journey

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

You are planning a journey through a newly discovered canyon system. The system contains exactly **j** distinct zones. You must choose a permutation of all **j** zones, visiting each zone exactly once in the order you specify.

For each zone, you are provided two values: the descent cost **t1** and the ascent cost **t2**.

During the journey, exactly one zone in your chosen sequence must be designated as the "transfer point". Let **k** be the position of this transfer point in your sequence (1 ≤ k ≤ j). The total cost of the journey, given a transfer point **k** and a sequence **π** (where **π_i** is the zone at position **i**), is calculated as:

$\sum_{i=1}^k t1_{\pi_i} + \sum_{i=k}^j t2_{\pi_i}$

In other words, the cost is the sum of the descent costs for all zones from position 1 up to and including position **k**, plus the sum of the ascent costs for all zones from position **k** up to and including position **j**. Note that the zone at position **k** contributes both its descent cost and its ascent cost.

The transfer point is determined by environmental factors outside your control after your sequence is set, so you must evaluate any sequence by its worst-case scenario. Therefore, the "journey cost" of a specific sequence is the maximum total cost over all possible choices of **k** (1 ≤ k ≤ j).

Your task is to choose the permutation of the **j** zones that minimizes this journey cost.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains exactly 2 integers **t1** and **t2** (1 ≤ t1, t2 ≤ 1000000): the descent cost and the ascent cost of a zone. The input order of the zones has no special meaning; you may arrange them in any sequence.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible journey cost you can guarantee by choosing the optimal sequence of the zones.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, there is only one zone. The only possible sequence has length 1, so the transfer point must be at position **k** = 1. The total cost is the descent cost of the first zone (5) plus the ascent cost of the first zone (3), which equals 8. The journey cost is therefore 8.

This forces the model to mathematically analyze the objective function and independently discover its equivalence to the two-machine flow-shop makespan, rather than triggering Johnson's rule via surface-level scheduling keywords.

The minimax formula over the permutation exactly matches the mathematical expansion of the completion time of the last job in a two-machine flow shop, ensuring all optimal values remain identical.
