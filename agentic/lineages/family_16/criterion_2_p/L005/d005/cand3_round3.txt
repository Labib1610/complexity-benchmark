<STATEMENT>
# Problem B — Canal Convoy

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A fleet of vessels must travel down a narrow canal. You must arrange the fleet into a strict single-file line, assigning each vessel a distinct position $p$ from $1$ to the total number of vessels.

The canal features two locks: Lock Alpha and Lock Beta. The timeline of the transit is strictly determined by your chosen arrangement and the following progression rules:
- The vessel at position $p=1$ begins its Lock Alpha transit at time 0.
- For any position $p > 1$, the vessel at position $p$ begins its Lock Alpha transit exactly when the vessel at position $p-1$ completes its Lock Alpha transit.
- Any vessel begins its Lock Beta transit at the earliest moment when both of the following are true: it has completed its Lock Alpha transit, and (if $p > 1$) the vessel at position $p-1$ has completed its Lock Beta transit.
- Once a transit begins, the vessel takes exactly its specific transit duration to complete that lock.

The canal authority allows a fleet size ranging from a solitary vessel up to a maximum convoy of two hundred thousand vessels. Each vessel's transit duration through a lock is measured in full minutes. A duration is never less than one minute, and the structural limits of the locks cap any single transit at exactly one million minutes.

Your task is to determine the optimal arrangement of the fleet that minimizes the time at which the vessel at the final position completes its Lock Beta transit.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer representing the number of vessels in the fleet.
Each of the subsequent lines describes one vessel with exactly 2 integers: its Lock Alpha transit duration, and its Lock Beta transit duration.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible time at which the final vessel in your arrangement completes its Lock Beta transit.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 2, the single vessel has a Lock Alpha duration of 5 and a Lock Beta duration of 3. It begins Lock Alpha at time 0, completes it at time 5, and begins Lock Beta at time 5 (since it is the first vessel). It completes Lock Beta at time 5 + 3 = 8.

<RATIONALE>
The seed problem overtly described a classic two-machine permutation flow-shop, which models readily recognized to apply Johnson's rule. I removed the triggering phrasing ("two sequential checkpoints", "same chosen sequence", "processes the vessels") and replaced it with a direct formal recurrence on the start times based on the assigned positions. This preserves the exact mathematical structure but forces the model to derive the optimization strategy from the recurrence itself rather than relying on flow-shop pattern matching. The numeric bounds were also shifted to indirect narrative constraints (Strategy S07).
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The start time progression rules mathematically evaluate to exactly the same makespan calculation as the original problem's lock transit rules, meaning the optimal permutations and their resulting times remain identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly equivalent to a two-machine permutation flow-shop makespan minimization, which is solvable in O(n log n) time using Johnson's rule, keeping it firmly in P.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
None. The progression rules are mathematically precise and exhaustive, leaving no room for alternate interpretations of the scheduling timeline.
</RISK>
