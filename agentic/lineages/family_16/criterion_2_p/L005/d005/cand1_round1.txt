# Problem B — Museum Tour

Time limit: 1 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description

A museum is hosting a special exhibition across **j** distinct galleries. You must arrange these **j** galleries into a strict sequence from first to last.

Each gallery features two rooms: a Painting room and a Sculpture room. For each gallery, you are given the time it takes to walk through its Painting room, **t1**, and its Sculpture room, **t2**.

A critic will visit the exhibition starting at the Painting room of the first gallery in your sequence, and must end their tour at the Sculpture room of the final gallery. The critic moves through the galleries according to the following rules:
- **Forward**: The critic may walk from the Painting room of gallery $i$ to the Painting room of gallery $i+1$. Alternatively, they may walk from the Sculpture room of gallery $i$ to the Sculpture room of gallery $i+1$.
- **Downward**: The critic may walk from the Painting room of a gallery to the Sculpture room of the **same** gallery.

The critic will choose exactly one gallery $k$ ($1 \le k \le j$) at which to make a "Downward" move. This means they will visit the Painting rooms of galleries $1$ through $k$, move downward, and then visit the Sculpture rooms of galleries $k$ through $j$. The downward transition itself takes 0 time.

The total duration of the critic's tour is the sum of the times of all rooms they visit. Since the critic is adversarial, they will choose the gallery $k$ that **maximizes** their total tour duration.

Your task is to determine the optimal ordering of the **j** galleries that **minimizes** the critic's maximum possible tour duration.

## Input

The first line contains a single integer **j** (1 ≤ j ≤ 200000).
Each of the next **j** lines contains exactly 2 integers **t1** and **t2** (1 ≤ t1, t2 ≤ 1000000): the time to walk through the Painting room and the Sculpture room of a gallery, respectively.

## Output

Print a single integer: the smallest possible maximum tour duration the critic can achieve, assuming you order the galleries optimally.

## Examples

### Example 1

**Input**
```
3
3 2
2 4
1 3
```

**Output**
```
10
```

### Example 2

**Input**
```
1
5 3
```

**Output**
```
8
```

### Example 3

**Input**
```
3
2 2
2 2
2 2
```

**Output**
```
8
```

## Note

In Example 1, one optimal sequence of the galleries is to place the third one first, then the second, then the first. Their times become:
Gallery 1: 1 3
Gallery 2: 2 4
Gallery 3: 3 2

The critic can choose to move downward at:
- Gallery 1: visits Painting 1, Sculpture 1, 2, 3. Total time = 1 + 3 + 4 + 2 = 10.
- Gallery 2: visits Painting 1, 2, Sculpture 2, 3. Total time = 1 + 2 + 4 + 2 = 9.
- Gallery 3: visits Painting 1, 2, 3, Sculpture 3. Total time = 1 + 2 + 3 + 2 = 8.
The critic maximizes their time, so they choose Gallery 1, resulting in a duration of 10. No other arrangement of the galleries can force the critic's maximum time to be lower than 10.
