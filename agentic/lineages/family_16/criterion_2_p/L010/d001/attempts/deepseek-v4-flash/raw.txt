 We can solve this by sorting the jobs using a greedy strategy.  
The optimal order is to sort by **assembly time in descending order** when preparation times are equal, but more generally, we sort by **assembly time descending** (this is the classic Johnson’s rule for two-machine flow shop scheduling when minimizing makespan, but here we have a special case where preparation is the first machine and assembly is the second).

Actually, for this problem, the optimal ordering is to sort jobs by **assembly time in descending order**.  
Here’s why:  
- The total time is determined by the sum of preparation times plus the maximum “delay” caused by assembly times.  
- If we put a job with a large assembly time early, it can overlap with preparation of later jobs, reducing idle time.

Let’s implement:

```python
n = int(input())
jobs = [tuple(map(int, input().split())) for _ in range(n)]

# Sort by assembly time descending
jobs.sort(key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)

prep_end = 0
assembly_end = 0

for p1, p2 in jobs:
    prep_end += p1
    assembly_end = max(assembly_end, prep_end) + p2

print(assembly_end)
```
