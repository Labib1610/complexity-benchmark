<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Oscillator Tuning

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A control system consists of $n$ precision oscillators, numbered $1$ through $n$. Each oscillator requires a frequency multiplier to operate correctly. You must assign each oscillator a multiplier chosen from the set $\{1, 2, 3\}$.

Within the system, certain pairs of oscillators are connected by coupling links. A coupling link between $u$ and $v$ must be configured as a directed interaction, either from $u$ to $v$ or from $v$ to $u$. When configured from $u$ to $v$, the interaction is governed by the $3 \times 3$ characteristic matrix:

$$
C_{u \to v} = \begin{pmatrix} 
s_u + 1 & s_u^2 - 1 & 3 \\ 
s_v + 1 & s_v^2 - 1 & 3 \\ 
s_u + s_v + 2 & s_u^2 + s_v^2 - 2 & 7 
\end{pmatrix}
$$

where $s_u$ and $s_v$ are their assigned multipliers. 

To ensure system stability, for every link, you must be able to choose a direction (either $u \to v$ or $v \to u$) such that the determinant of its characteristic matrix is strictly positive (i.e., $> 0$). If for any link the determinant is zero or negative in both possible directions, that link undergoes resonance collapse and the entire system fails. The choices of direction for different links are completely independent of one another.

You must determine whether a stable configuration of multipliers exists for the given system.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ — the total number of oscillators and the number of coupling links ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n(n-1)/2$).
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), signifying that oscillator $u$ and oscillator $v$ are linked. It is guaranteed that each unordered pair of linked oscillators appears at most once in the input.

## Output
If there is no way to configure the multipliers without inducing resonance collapse, output a single line containing `NO`.
If a stable configuration is achievable, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, provide $n$ integers $s_1 s_2 \dots s_n$ ($s_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), where $s_i$ represents the multiplier assigned to oscillator $i$. 

If multiple stable configurations exist, you must output the one that minimizes the final system signature $V_n$. This signature is computed sequentially: starting with an initial value $V_0 = 0$, for each oscillator $i$ from $1$ to $n$ in order, the signature updates according to the rule $V_i = 4 \cdot V_{i-1} + s_i$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

<RATIONALE>
The previous matrix determinant evaluated trivially to the symmetric square $(s_u - s_v)^2$, allowing models to algebraically simplify the "non-zero determinant" condition into $s_u \neq s_v$ and map it directly to graph coloring. To remove this affordance, I designed a new matrix whose determinant evaluates to the asymmetric polynomial $(s_u+1)(s_v+1)(s_v - s_u)$. I then replaced the symmetric "non-zero" requirement with a "strictly positive" requirement paired with a free choice of edge direction. This forces models to reason through the orientation logic rather than relying on surface pattern matching of the algebraic expression.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because the determinant evaluates to $(s_u+1)(s_v+1)(s_v - s_u)$ and the multipliers are strictly positive, the determinant is antisymmetric and evaluates to zero if and only if $s_u = s_v$; thus, exactly one direction yields a strictly positive determinant when multipliers differ, meaning the stability condition remains perfectly equivalent to $s_u \neq s_v$.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires assigning values from a domain of size 3 to vertices such that adjacent vertices receive distinct values, preserving the exact proper 3-coloring reduction and NP-complete status.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "directed interaction" as requiring a globally valid directed graph (e.g., a DAG or tournament), though this is heavily mitigated by the explicit statement that "choices of direction for different links are completely independent of one another."
</RISK>

Asymmetric Choice Tautology
</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
Replaced a symmetric non-zero determinant constraint with an asymmetric strictly-positive determinant constraint, paired with a free choice of edge direction. The determinant is antisymmetric and non-zero iff multipliers differ, so exactly one direction is strictly positive. This forces models to reason through the orientation logic to discover it reduces to the same symmetric inequality constraint, defeating models that rely on the algebraic square (s_u - s_v)^2 to trigger their graph-coloring templates.
