# Habitat Conservation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A wildlife reserve is managing the recovery of $n$ endangered species. To optimize their growth, each species must be relocated to exactly one of three protected biomes, designated by the identifiers 1, 2, and 3.

Through field studies, ecologists have documented $m$ competitive interactions. Each interaction indicates that two specific species compete aggressively for the same essential resources. To guarantee ecological stability, you must distribute the $n$ species among the 3 biomes such that every competitive interaction strictly spans across two different biomes. Under no circumstances may two species involved in a documented interaction be placed in the same biome.

Given the list of competitive interactions, determine if a sustainable relocation plan exists. If no such arrangement is possible, you must report it.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — the number of species and the number of competitive interactions ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of species involved in a competitive interaction.

## Output
If no sustainable relocation plan exists, print a single line NO.
Otherwise print YES on the first line. On the second line print $n$ integers $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ (each in $\{1,2,3\}$), where $s_i$ is the biome assigned to species $i$. Any sustainable relocation plan is accepted.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

Rephrased the conflict condition as a partition requirement ("strictly spans across two different biomes") to obscure the standard graph coloring formulation and force models to deduce the structural equivalence from the ecological constraints rather than relying on familiar scheduling keywords.
</RATIONALE>
