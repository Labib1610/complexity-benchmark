# Problem A — Shift Scheduling

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing plant operates three daily production cycles, labeled 1, 2, and 3. Every employee must be allocated to exactly one cycle. Due to interpersonal conflicts, certain pairs of employees cannot be scheduled for the same cycle.

The conflict logs are entirely unrestricted and deliberately contain adversarial configurations. Basic assignment strategies, such as prioritizing individuals based on their total number of conflicts, will not be sufficient to finalize a schedule within the time limit. You must determine an allocation for every employee such that no two conflicting employees share the same cycle, or report that no such allocation is possible.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — the number of employees and the total number of conflict records. The value of $n$ is at least 1 and will not exceed the total capacity of four full ten-person squads. The value of $m$ is at least 0 and will not exceed the theoretical maximum number of distinct pairs of employees.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — indicating that employee $u$ and employee $v$ have a conflict.

## Output
If no valid allocation exists, print a single line `NO`.
Otherwise, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ (each in $\{1, 2, 3\}$), where $s_i$ is the cycle assigned to employee $i$. Any valid allocation is accepted.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

</RATIONALE>

The phrase "theoretical maximum number of distinct pairs of employees" might theoretically be misinterpreted as $n^2$ instead of $n(n-1)/2$, though "distinct pairs" strongly implies combinations rather than permutations in this context.
</RISK>
