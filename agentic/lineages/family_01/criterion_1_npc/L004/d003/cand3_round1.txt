<STATEMENT>
# Resonant Shatter Avoidance

**Time limit:** 2 s  
**Memory limit:** 256 MB  

An automated manufacturing facility employs $n$ robotic arms, identified by integers from 1 to $n$. To synchronize the assembly process, each arm must be calibrated to a base oscillation frequency of exactly 1, 2, or 3 Hz.

The physical framework of the facility relies on a network of structural struts. Each strut forms a direct mechanical link between exactly two robotic arms. Due to the physics of the framework, if the two arms connected by a single strut are calibrated to identical oscillation frequencies, they will induce a resonant shatter that destroys the strut. Consequently, the two arms at the ends of any given strut must always be calibrated to different frequencies.

You are provided with the complete blueprint of all struts connecting the arms. Your task is to determine whether it is possible to calibrate all $n$ robotic arms without inducing a resonant shatter anywhere in the facility.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the number of robotic arms and the number of structural struts, respectively ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that a single strut connects arm $u$ and arm $v$.

## Output
If no safe calibration exists, print `NO` on a single line.
If a valid calibration is possible, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output a sequence of $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ (where each $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) representing the oscillation frequency in Hz assigned to arm $i$. If multiple safe calibrations are possible, output the one that is lexicographically first (evaluating the frequency of arm 1 first, then arm 2, and so on).

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
**Output:**
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
NO
```
