<STATEMENT>
# Harmonic Resonance Mitigation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A robotic manufacturing facility operates $n$ mechanical arms, numbered 1 through $n$. Each arm must be assigned a movement frequency tier to govern its operation speed. The available frequency tiers are identified by the integers 1, 2, and 3.

Certain pairs of arms operate in overlapping physical workspaces. If two arms that share a workspace are assigned the exact same frequency tier, the resulting harmonic resonance will lead to mechanical failure. Consequently, for each of these pairs, the two arms must be assigned different frequency tiers.

Your task is to determine whether it is possible to assign a frequency tier to every arm such that no harmonic resonance occurs in any overlapping workspace.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the number of mechanical arms and the number of overlapping workspaces, respectively. The facility uses a main control board with exactly 4 expansion slots, each capable of connecting up to 10 arms. The system requires at least one arm to be connected, and $n$ will not exceed the board's fully populated capacity. The value of $m$ ranges from zero up to the maximum possible number of distinct pairs that can be formed from the $n$ arms.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ denoting that arm $u$ and arm $v$ share an overlapping workspace.

## Output
If no safe assignment exists, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $b_1, b_2, \dots, b_n$, where $b_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is the frequency tier assigned to arm $i$. If multiple safe assignments exist, output the one that is lexicographically smallest (i.e., compare $b_1$ first, then $b_2$, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The explicit bounds $1 \le n \le 40$ and $0 \le m \le n(n-1)/2$ were removed and replaced with a physical description of a control board's capacity (4 slots of 10) and the combinatorial maximum of distinct pairs. The terminology was shifted from "critical pairs" and "operating states" to "overlapping workspaces" and "frequency tiers" to reduce the immediate surface-level similarity to standard graph coloring descriptions, forcing the model to infer the bounds and recognize the 3-coloring reduction independently.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The requirements for the assignments are mathematically identical (assign values 1, 2, or 3 such that specified pairs have distinct values, breaking ties lexicographically), so the valid configurations and the chosen output for any input remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly equivalent to finding the lexicographically smallest 3-coloring of a graph, which is NP-complete.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
A model might incorrectly interpret "distinct pairs" as ordered pairs rather than unordered pairs, though the context of physical sharing makes the unordered interpretation standard.
</RISK>
