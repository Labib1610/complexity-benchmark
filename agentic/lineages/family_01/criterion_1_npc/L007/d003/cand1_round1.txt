<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Trichrome Shifts

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility operates continuously across three distinct shifts, designated as 1, 2, and 3. The facility employs $n$ personnel, numbered from 1 to $n$.

Certain pairs of personnel have complex operational dependencies. You are provided with $m$ dependency records, each specifying two individuals $u$ and $v$. 

To prevent critical workflow failures, the shift scheduling must satisfy a specific mathematical safety condition. For every recorded pair $(u, v)$, their assigned shifts $s_u$ and $s_v$ must be chosen such that the workflow balance $B(u, v)$ is not a multiple of 3. The balance is calculated using their personnel IDs and assigned shifts as follows:

$$B(u, v) = u^2 v^2 s_u + u^2 s_u + v^2 s_u + s_u + 2 u^2 v^2 s_v + 2 u^2 s_v + 2 v^2 s_v + 2 s_v + 3 u v s_u s_v$$

Your task is to allocate each person to exactly one of the three shifts such that every dependency record satisfies this safety condition, or to determine that no such allocation exists.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ — the total number of personnel and the number of dependency records ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n(n-1)/2$).
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines provides two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a pair of personnel whose assigned shifts must satisfy the safety condition.

## Output
If there is no valid shift allocation, output a single line containing `NO`.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ (where each $s_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), with $s_i$ representing the shift assigned to person $i$. Any valid allocation is accepted.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The previous version allowed models to easily evaluate the symmetric condition on the {1, 2, 3} domain and recognize it as the disequality constraint of ordinary graph 3-coloring. To invalidate the assumption that constraints are uniform `colors[u] != colors[v]`, I replaced the condition with a complex polynomial $B(u, v)$ that incorporates the vertex IDs $u$ and $v$, making it appear highly asymmetric and edge-dependent. (Note: I did not forbid (2,1) as the strategy suggested, because changing the allowed directed edges alters the satisfiability of some instances, violating the identical answers constraint.)
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The polynomial $B(u, v)$ modulo 3 reduces exactly to $(u^2+1)(v^2+1)(s_u + 2s_v)$, and since $(x^2+1)$ is never divisible by 3, the condition is mathematically identical to $s_u \neq s_v$ for shifts in {1, 2, 3}.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly equivalent to Graph 3-Coloring, which is NP-Complete.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
