# Problem A — Reactive Storage

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A research facility operates a strict segregation protocol across exactly three distinct containment zones, labeled 1, 2, and 3. You must process an inventory of chemical samples, indexed 1 through $n$. Due to their reactive properties, certain pairs of samples are jointly volatile and cannot share the same containment zone under any circumstances. Your task is to allocate each sample to exactly one of the three zones such that no two volatile samples are placed together, or determine that no such safe arrangement exists. 

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — representing the total number of samples and the number of volatile pairs, respectively. The facility has a strict physical capacity of exactly 40 secure lockers, meaning the number of samples $n$ is at least 1 and at most 40. The number of volatile pairs $m$ ranges from 0 up to the maximum possible number of distinct pairs, $n(n-1)/2$.
The following $m$ lines each provide two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$) — indicating that sample $u$ and sample $v$ are volatile together.

## Output
If a safe allocation cannot be found, print a single line containing `NO`.
If it is possible, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ (each chosen from `{1, 2, 3}`), where $s_i$ denotes the zone assigned to sample $i$. Should multiple safe allocations exist, among all valid assignments, output the lexicographically smallest sequence $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ (i.e., compare $s_1$ first, then $s_2$, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
