# Problem A — Resonant Loop Prevention

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are configuring a system of signal relays, numbered 1 to $n$. Each relay must be assigned a frequency multiplier chosen from the values 1, 2, and 3. This configuration is expressed as a sequence $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$.

You are provided with a list of $m$ pairs of relays that are in physical proximity. Proximity is a symmetric relationship. 

For the purpose of stability analysis, each multiplier corresponds to a specific large prime number:
- Multiplier 1 corresponds to $P_1 = 998244353$.
- Multiplier 2 corresponds to $P_2 = 1000000007$.
- Multiplier 3 corresponds to $P_3 = 1000000009$.

For any pair of relays $u$ and $v$ that are in physical proximity, we analyze their combined signal by calculating the product of their corresponding prime numbers:
$$X = P_{f_u} \times P_{f_v}$$

Let $\Phi(X)$ denote the count of positive integers $k$ (where $1 \le k \le X$) such that the greatest common divisor of $k$ and $X$ is exactly 1. 
The system is perfectly stable if and only if, for every pair of relays in physical proximity, the following inequality holds:
$$\Phi(X) \le (P_{f_u} - 1) \times (P_{f_v} - 1)$$

Your task is to determine if there exists a valid sequence of multipliers such that the system is perfectly stable. If such a sequence exists, you must output it.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The facility's power constraints limit the network to a maximum capacity of 40 relays, so $n$ is at least 1 and at most 40. The number of proximity connections $m$ ranges from zero up to the theoretical maximum of one connection between every possible distinct pair of relays.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating that relay $u$ and relay $v$ are in physical proximity.

## Output
If there is no valid sequence of multipliers that makes the system stable, print a single line containing `NO`.
If at least one such sequence exists, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print the $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ representing the multipliers assigned to the relays. 
If there are multiple valid sequences, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. (A sequence $A$ is lexicographically smaller than sequence $B$ if, at the first index $i$ where they differ, $A_i < B_i$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
