<METHOD_NAME>Arithmetic Obfuscation of Inequality</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the standard inequality constraint (p_u != p_v) with an arithmetically equivalent condition on the domain {1, 2, 3}: that their product must not be a perfect square. This prevents models from immediately pattern-matching the problem to graph coloring, disabling their graph-specific search heuristics like degree-ordered backtracking and forcing them to either reinvent the heuristic or TLE on generic search.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>
<STATEMENT>
# Antenna Alignment

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are tasked with configuring a network of $n$ communication antennas. Every antenna in the network requires exactly one operational mode, which must be selected from the numeric codes 1, 2, and 3.

The terrain contains $m$ direct line-of-sight links between specific pairs of antennas. When two antennas share a line-of-sight link, their transmitted signals interact. If the product of the numeric codes assigned to these two linked antennas results in a perfect square, the interaction produces a destructive standing wave that destabilizes the network. To guarantee reliable communication, you must ensure that for every line-of-sight link, the product of the two antennas' operational codes is not a perfect square.

Your objective is to determine if there exists a complete assignment of operational modes to all $n$ antennas that avoids creating any destructive standing waves across the $m$ specified links.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ — the number of antennas and the number of line-of-sight links ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n(n-1)/2$). The antennas are indexed from $1$ to $n$.
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines provides two distinct integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$), indicating that antenna $u$ and antenna $v$ share a direct line-of-sight link. Each pair represents a strict standing wave constraint.

## Output
If a safe configuration is impossible, output a single line containing NO.
If a valid configuration exists, output YES on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ space-separated integers $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n$ (where each $p_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), representing the operational mode assigned to antenna $i$. Any valid configuration will be accepted.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The problem was modified by replacing the explicit "different modes" constraint with an arithmetic equivalent: "the product is not a perfect square". Since the domain is restricted to {1, 2, 3}, the product of any two identical modes is a perfect square (1, 4, 9), while the product of any two distinct modes is not (2, 3, 6). This forces the model to mathematically deduce the equivalence to inequality rather than relying on surface-level keyword matching to trigger its degree-ordered graph coloring heuristics.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that the product of two modes from {1, 2, 3} is not a perfect square is logically identical to the condition that the two modes are strictly different, meaning all valid configurations remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying problem is still exactly graph 3-coloring, which is NP-complete, as the arithmetic constraint is merely a restatement of the standard vertex coloring inequality constraint.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
