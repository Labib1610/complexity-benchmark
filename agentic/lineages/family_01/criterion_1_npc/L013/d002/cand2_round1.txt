<STATEMENT>
# Factory Shift Assignment

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A production plant employs a workforce of $n$ individuals. The facility is equipped with exactly 40 workstations, which dictates that the workforce size $n$ is at least 1 and will never exceed this workstation capacity. 

Management has documented $m$ pairs of personnel who experience severe friction when working together. The number of documented pairs $m$ ranges from zero up to the absolute maximum possible number of distinct pairs among the workforce. To maintain a functional environment, workplace rules mandate that the two individuals in any documented friction pair must never be assigned to the same operational period. 

The plant's daily schedule is split into exactly three distinct operational periods: period 1, period 2, and period 3. 

You must establish whether it is feasible to assign every individual to exactly one of these three periods without breaking the friction rules. If a valid configuration is possible, you must output one. You only need to supply one valid configuration, even if several are possible. 

Be advised that the friction pairs may be arranged adversarially to force the exploration of all combinations. Both solvable and unsolvable instances are present, and their structures are designed to undermine step-by-step assignments based on the number of conflicts an individual has.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ — the total number of individuals in the workforce and the number of friction pairs. The limits for $n$ and $m$ are strictly bounded by the physical constraints detailed in the description.
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines provides two integers $u$ and $v$, meaning that individual $u$ and individual $v$ must be assigned to different periods.

## Output
If it is impossible to assign all individuals to the periods without violating the friction rules, print a single line containing `NO`.
If it is possible, print `YES` on the first line. On the second line, print $n$ integers $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ separated by spaces, where each $s_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ indicates the period given to individual $i$. You only need to supply one valid configuration, even if several are possible.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

Removed explicit numeric bounds and replaced them with physical scenario limits (40 workstations, maximum possible distinct pairs) to force the solver to derive the constraints. Added an explicit warning that instances are adversarially arranged to undermine heuristics based on conflict counts, directly targeting the surviving model's assumption that degree-ordered backtracking will be efficient.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The core requirement of assigning all workers to 3 periods such that no conflicting pair shares a period remains completely identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly 3-Coloring on a general graph with up to 40 vertices, which is NP-complete.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
The phrase "maximum possible number of distinct pairs" could theoretically be misunderstood if a model fails to map this to $n(n-1)/2$, though standard combinatorics makes this risk low.
</RISK>
