# Problem A — Independent Audits

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing the annual compliance audit for a corporation with $n$ departments. The company requires each department to be audited by exactly one of three external agencies, labeled 1, 2, and 3.

Because departments collaborate, there are $m$ joint ventures between specific pairs of departments. The audit must be structured to meet a strict regulatory metric across these collaborations.

For each department $i$, let $t_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ be its assigned agency, and let $d_i$ be the total number of joint ventures that department $i$ is involved in. The base operational cost for department $i$ is calculated as:
$C_i = d_i \times (t_i^4 - 5t_i^2)$

For every joint venture between two departments $u$ and $v$, the collaboration overhead is calculated as:
$H(u,v) = 10 t_u t_v + 6 t_u^2 t_v^2 - 4 t_u^3 t_v - 4 t_u t_v^3$

An assignment of agencies to all $n$ departments is legally compliant if and only if the total network metric—defined as the sum of the base operational costs of all $n$ departments plus the sum of the collaboration overheads of all $m$ joint ventures—equals exactly $-4m$.

The corporate network can be highly interconnected, and the list of joint ventures is provided in no particular order. You must determine whether a compliant assignment exists.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$ — the number of departments and joint ventures, respectively ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a joint venture between department $u$ and department $v$. Departments are indexed from 1 to $n$.

## Output
If there is no way to assign agencies to satisfy the compliance metric, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ (where $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is the agency for department $i$). If multiple valid assignments exist, you must provide the one that is lexicographically smallest (comparing the agency of department 1 first, then department 2, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

<RATIONALE>
I expanded the edge-wise dot product into a global polynomial metric, then regrouped the terms into node-specific base costs and edge-specific overheads using a sum-of-degrees equivalence. This removes all explicit vectors and constant uniform edge contributions, forcing the model to either mathematically factor the polynomial to discover the hidden proper 3-coloring condition, or attempt unpruned backtracking on a nonlinear metric which will result in a Time Limit Exceeded error.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The total network metric factors exactly to the sum over all edges of $(t_u - t_v)^4 - 5(t_u - t_v)^2$, which evaluates to -4 if the agencies differ and 0 if they are identical; thus, the sum is exactly -4m if and only if no adjacent departments share an agency, perfectly matching the original valid assignments.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains mathematically identical to finding the lexicographically smallest proper 3-coloring of a graph, preserving its NP-complete complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret $d_i$ as the degree within some induced subset instead of the full graph, though "total number of joint ventures that department i is involved in" strongly implies the static full-graph property.
</RISK>
