<METHOD_NAME>
Obscured Global Identity with Floor-Division Penalty
</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
To prevent models from using simple polynomial algebra to decouple the global sum into independent edge constraints, I replaced the degree-based sum identity with a less common neighbor-sum identity ($\sum f_i^2 \sum_{j \sim i} f_j = \sum f_u f_v (f_u + f_v)$). Furthermore, I replaced the quartic equality-checking polynomial with a floor-division product ($\lfloor f_u/f_v \rfloor \lfloor f_v/f_u \rfloor$) that evaluates to 1 if the variables are equal and 0 if they are unequal. This forces the model to perform multiple non-trivial algebraic and logical deductions to realize the problem reduces to graph 3-coloring.
</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

yes

# Problem A — Independent Audits

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing the annual compliance audit for a corporation with $n$ departments. The company requires each department to be audited by exactly one of three external agencies, labeled 1, 2, and 3.

Because departments collaborate, there are $m$ joint ventures between specific pairs of departments. The audit must be structured to meet a strict regulatory metric across these collaborations.

For each department $i$, its *baseline risk* is calculated as the square of its assigned agency label ($f_i^2$) multiplied by the sum of the assigned agency labels of all departments with which it shares a joint venture. The *total system risk* is the sum of the baseline risks across all $n$ departments.

For every joint venture between two departments $u$ and $v$ (assigned agencies $f_u$ and $f_v$), a *collaborative load* is computed using the following expression:
$$ f_u \cdot f_v \cdot (f_u + f_v) + 8 \left\lfloor \frac{f_u}{f_v} \right\rfloor \left\lfloor \frac{f_v}{f_u} \right\rfloor $$
where $\lfloor x \rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to $x$ (i.e., rounding down to the nearest whole number).

An assignment of agencies to all $n$ departments is legally compliant if and only if the total sum of the collaborative loads over all $m$ joint ventures is exactly equal to the total system risk.

The corporate network can be highly interconnected, and the list of joint ventures is provided in no particular order. You must determine whether a compliant assignment exists.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$ — the number of departments and joint ventures, respectively ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a joint venture between department $u$ and department $v$. Departments are indexed from 1 to $n$.

## Output
If there is no way to assign agencies to satisfy the compliance metric, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ (where $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is the agency for department $i$). If multiple valid assignments exist, you must provide the one that is lexicographically smallest (comparing the agency of department 1 first, then department 2, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```

<RATIONALE>
I targeted the algebraic shortcut that allowed models to easily cancel terms and isolate the equality constraint. I changed the underlying graph identity from a degree-based sum to a neighbor-label sum, which expands to a different cubic base load. I then replaced the explicit quartic polynomial with a mathematically equivalent but computationally opaque floor-division product that checks for equality without using standard polynomial roots. This forces the model to independently derive the non-standard graph identity and logically deduce the properties of reciprocal integer division before it can recognize the 3-coloring reduction.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The total system risk algebraically simplifies to the sum of $f_u f_v (f_u + f_v)$ over all edges, meaning the compliance equality holds if and only if the sum of the floor-division penalty terms is zero, which strictly requires $f_u \neq f_v$ for every joint venture; thus the exact same set of valid lexicographically-ordered assignments is preserved.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly equivalent to finding the lexicographically smallest proper 3-coloring of a graph, which is NP-complete.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
