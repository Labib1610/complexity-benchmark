<METHOD_NAME>Algebraic Triad Collision</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the global equality with a localized constraint on edges: a "compliance triad" generated by three functions (Sum, Product, Square of Maximum) which algebraically guarantee a collision (duplicate values) if and only if the two inputs are equal. This prevents models from using a lookup table's constant offset to bypass reasoning, forcing them to analyze the algebraic properties of the triad to discover it maps to 3-coloring.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem A — Independent Audits

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing the annual compliance audit for a corporation with $n$ departments. The company requires each department to be assigned to exactly one of three external agencies, labeled 1, 2, and 3.

Because departments collaborate, there are $m$ joint ventures between specific pairs of departments. The audit must be structured to meet a strict regulatory metric across these collaborations.

For every joint venture between department $u$ and department $v$, a 3-person oversight committee must be formed. The committee is constituted by generating three "access tokens", calculated based on the agency labels assigned to $u$ and $v$ (let's call them $A_u$ and $A_v$):
- Token 1 is the sum of the two agency labels: $A_u + A_v$.
- Token 2 is the product of the two agency labels: $A_u \times A_v$.
- Token 3 is the square of the maximum of the two agency labels: $(\max(A_u, A_v))^2$.

To pass the regulatory metric, the committee for EVERY joint venture must be "fully diversified". A committee is fully diversified if and only if all three of its access tokens are mutually distinct numbers (i.e., no two tokens in the committee share the same value).

The corporate network can be highly interconnected, and the list of joint ventures is provided in no particular order. You must determine whether a compliant assignment of agencies to all $n$ departments exists.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$ — the number of departments and joint ventures, respectively ($1 \le n \le 40$, $0 \le m \le n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a joint venture between department $u$ and department $v$. Departments are indexed from 1 to $n$.

## Output
If there is no way to assign agencies to satisfy the compliance metric, output `NO` on a single line.
Otherwise, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output $n$ integers $f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n$ (where $f_i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is the agency for department $i$). If multiple valid assignments exist, you must provide the one that is lexicographically smallest (comparing the agency of department 1 first, then department 2, and so on).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
1 4
2 4
```
Output:
```
YES
1 2 3 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
4 6
1 2
1 3
1 4
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
NO
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The previous global equality and lookup table allowed models to subtract a baseline offset and discover a constant difference, trivially bypassing the intended logic. By replacing it with an algebraic triad (Sum, Product, Square of Maximum), models can no longer rely on a static table. They must genuinely reason about the properties of these three functions to realize that they only produce duplicates when the two inputs are identical, mapping the problem back to finding a proper assignment.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The functions are designed such that if A_u = A_v, the Product and the Square of the Maximum are identical, failing the distinctness check; if A_u != A_v (within the domain of 1, 2, 3), the three tokens never overlap, perfectly preserving the proper 3-coloring solution set.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The core requirement remains exactly equivalent to assigning one of three labels to each vertex such that no two adjacent vertices share a label, keeping it firmly NP-complete.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
