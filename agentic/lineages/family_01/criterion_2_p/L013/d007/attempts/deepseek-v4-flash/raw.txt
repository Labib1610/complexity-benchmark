We can model this as a graph coloring problem.  
The key is to figure out when two transformations are compatible.

Let’s check the matrices:

\[
M_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
M_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
\]

We need \( f_u(f_v(V)) = V \) for all \(V\).  
That means \( M_a M_b = I \) (identity matrix).

Let’s compute:

- \( M_1 M_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \neq I \)
- \( M_2 M_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -3 & 5 \end{pmatrix} \neq I \)
- \( M_1 M_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I \)
- \( M_2 M_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I \)

So the only stable links are between nodes with **different** transformations.  
Thus, the problem reduces to: can we 2-color the graph such that adjacent nodes have different colors?  
That’s exactly checking if the graph is bipartite.

If it is bipartite, we assign 1 and 2 to the two partitions.  
For lexicographically smallest assignment, we try to assign node 1 as 1, then BFS/DFS and assign opposite colors, and if conflict → NO.

Here’s the implementation:

```python
import sys
from collections import deque

input = sys.stdin.readline

n, m = map(int, input().split())
adj = [[] for _ in range(n)]
for _ in range(m):
    u, v = map(int, input().split())
    u -= 1
    v -= 1
    adj[u].append(v)
    adj[v].append(u)

color = [-1] * n

for i in range(n):
    if color[i] == -1:
        color[i] = 0
        q = deque([i])
        while q:
            u = q.popleft()
            for v in adj[u]:
                if color[v] == -1:
                    color[v] = 1 - color[u]
                    q.append(v)
                elif color[v] == color[u]:
                    print("NO")
                    sys.exit()

print("YES")
print(" ".join(str(c + 1) for c in color))
```
