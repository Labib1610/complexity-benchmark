# Problem C — Trilingual Focus Groups

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

At an international linguistics summit, there are $n$ distinct dialects, identified by integers from 1 to $n$. You have a roster of $m$ bilingual translators. Every translator is proficient in exactly two distinct dialects.

Your goal is to organize a trilingual focus group. This requires choosing a trio of distinct dialects. To ensure direct communication without intermediaries, the focus group must be supported by three translators from your roster such that every possible pair of dialects in the trio is covered by a dedicated translator.

If several trios meet this requirement, the selection committee mandates a strict preference based on dialect IDs:
1. First, select the trio whose minimum dialect ID is as small as possible.
2. If there are multiple such trios, narrow them down by minimizing their middle dialect ID.
3. If a tie persists, minimize the maximum dialect ID of the trio.

## Input
The first line contains $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The next $m$ lines each provide two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating a translator proficient in dialects $u$ and $v$.
No two translators share the exact same pair of dialects.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid trilingual focus group can be formed.

## Output
Print three integers separated by spaces, representing the dialects of the chosen focus group in ascending order. Follow the committee's preference rules if there are multiple valid groups.

## Example 1
Input:
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4

Output:
1 2 3

## Example 2
Input:
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5

Output:
1 2 4

</RATIONALE>
