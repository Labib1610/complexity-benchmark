# Problem C — Schema Conflict Certification

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A distributed system consists of $n$ databases, numbered 1 through $n$. The hardware limits a cluster to at most two thousand databases, while a minimum of three are required to operate. The architecture specifies that these databases must be divided into exactly two synchronization rings. However, certain pairs of databases have incompatible schemas and cannot be placed in the same ring. The registry tracks $m$ such incompatible pairs. The registry's memory limits it to at most two hundred thousand tracked pairs, and the number of tracked pairs will never exceed the total number of possible pairs among the $n$ databases. There are at least three pairs recorded in the registry.

Your task is not to attempt the division, but to certify that it is impossible. To do this, you must identify a trio of databases such that every pair formed from the trio is listed as incompatible. It is guaranteed that at least one such trio exists, proving that two rings cannot suffice. Your job is to output one such trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the number of databases and the number of incompatible pairs. The values of $n$ and $m$ respect the system limits described above.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of incompatible databases.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the three databases of the trio. If there are multiple valid trios, output the one that is lexicographically smallest (the one with the smallest $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest $b$; and finally the smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
