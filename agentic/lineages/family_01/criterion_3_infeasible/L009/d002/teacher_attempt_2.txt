# Problem C — Ancestral Lineage Refutation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A genetic research facility is analyzing $n$ DNA samples, numbered 1 through $n$. The prevailing theory dictates that all samples must be classified into exactly two ancestral lineages. However, certain pairs of samples exhibit contradictory mutation markers, meaning they cannot possibly originate from the same lineage.

Your objective is not to attempt this classification, but to provide definitive evidence that the two-lineage theory is false. It is guaranteed that the dataset contains at least one trio of samples where every sample in the trio has contradictory markers with both of the other two. Any such trio acts as an irrefutable counterexample to the theory. Your task is to identify one such trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of samples that exhibit contradictory mutation markers.
It is guaranteed that at least one such trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$ $b$ $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that the pairs $a$-$b$, $a$-$c$, and $b$-$c$ are all listed as having contradictory markers. Output the lexicographically smallest such trio (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
