# Problem C — Volatile Mixtures

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A chemical plant stores $n$ reactive compounds, numbered 1 through $n$. Certain pairs of compounds are highly volatile when mixed and must be kept strictly separated.

The safety inspector is looking for a specific hazard: three distinct compounds that form a closed reactive loop. This condition is met when a first compound is volatile with a second, the second is volatile with a third, and the third is volatile with the first. If these three are accidentally stored together, any contact will create a cascading chain reaction.

You are given the list of all volatile pairs. Your task is to identify one such loop.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The number of compounds $n$ is between $3$ and $2000$. The number of recorded volatile pairs $m$ is between $3$ and either $2 \cdot 10^5$ or the theoretical maximum for $n$ compounds, whichever is smaller.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of compounds that are volatile when mixed.
It is guaranteed that at least one such closed reactive loop exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$ $b$ $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$, representing the three compounds that form the closed reactive loop. Output the lexicographically smallest such group of three (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
