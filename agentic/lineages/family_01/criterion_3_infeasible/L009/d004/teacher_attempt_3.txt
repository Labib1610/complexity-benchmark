# Problem C — Volatile Mixtures

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A chemical plant stores $n$ reactive compounds, numbered 1 through $n$. The facility maintains a list of specific two-compound combinations that are highly volatile when mixed.

The safety inspector is looking for a specific hazard: a group of exactly three compounds that feature exactly three volatile interaction records among themselves. If such a trio is accidentally stored together, any accidental contact will create a cascading chain reaction. 

You are given the list of all volatile combinations. Your task is to identify one such trio of compounds.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The number of stored compounds, $n$, is governed by a minimum operational requirement of 3 and a maximum facility capacity of 2000. The inventory system tracks $m$ known unique volatile combinations. The number of such records, $m$, is at least 3, and is capped by a system limit of 200000 (and naturally cannot exceed the theoretical maximum number of distinct pairs among the stored compounds).

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two distinct integers $u$ and $v$ — a unique record indicating that compounds $u$ and $v$ are volatile when mixed.
It is guaranteed that at least one trio of compounds meeting the inspector's criteria exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$ $b$ $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the identified trio of compounds. Output the lexicographically smallest such trio (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
Removed the "every possible pair among the three" phrasing and replaced it with a requirement for exactly three interaction records among the three compounds, forcing the model to deduce that this implies a fully connected trio (a triangle). Replaced explicit mathematical bounds with text describing capacities and limits to satisfy the S07 strategy.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The required trio is still three mutually connected items (since 3 nodes with 3 undirected edges form a triangle), and the lexicographical criteria remains identical, ensuring outputs are unchanged.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle (K3) in an undirected graph, which is typically solved using bitset adjacency intersection in O(n*m/64) time.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
The phrase "feature exactly three volatile interaction records among themselves" could theoretically be misinterpreted if a model fails to realize that three undirected edges among three nodes means every pair is connected, but this is a deliberate structural deduction rather than an ambiguity.
</RISK>
