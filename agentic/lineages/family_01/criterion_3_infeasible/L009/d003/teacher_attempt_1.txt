# Problem C — Ancestral Lineage Refutation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A genetic research facility is analyzing $n$ DNA samples, numbered 1 through $n$. The prevailing theory dictates that all samples must be classified into exactly two ancestral lineages. However, certain pairs of samples exhibit conflicting mutation markers, meaning they cannot possibly originate from the same lineage.

Your objective is not to attempt this classification, but to provide definitive evidence that the two-lineage theory is false. It is guaranteed that the dataset contains at least one set of three distinct samples where all three possible pairs formed among them are known to have conflicting markers. Any such set acts as an irrefutable counterexample to the theory. Your task is to identify one such set of three samples.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The number of samples $n$ is at least the number of samples involved in a single counterexample, and at most the facility's storage limit of two thousand. The number of conflicting pairs $m$ is at least the number of conflicting pairs that make up a single counterexample, and at most two hundred thousand (or the maximum number of unique pairs possible among $n$ samples, whichever is less).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of samples that exhibit conflicting mutation markers.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid counterexample exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$ $b$ $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that the pairs $a$-$b$, $a$-$c$, and $b$-$c$ are all listed as having conflicting markers. Output the lexicographically smallest such set (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
