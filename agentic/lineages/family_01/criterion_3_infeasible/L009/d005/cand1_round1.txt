# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The laboratory protocol requires isolating reactive materials to prevent hazards. We are investigating the fractional stability of small groups of materials. 

Your task is to identify a minimal certificate of instability. A certificate consists of exactly three distinct materials. Suppose we attempt to assign a non-negative fractional stability weight $w \ge 0$ to each of these three materials such that the sum of their three weights is exactly $1$. The safety guidelines dictate that for any mutually volatile pair among these three materials (according to the provided registry), the sum of the stability weights of those two specific materials must not exceed $1/2$. 

A trio of materials forms a certificate if and only if it is mathematically impossible to assign these weights without violating the guidelines. 

It is guaranteed that the provided registry contains at least one such certificate.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of materials and the number of mutually volatile pairs.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — indicating that material $u$ and material $v$ are mutually volatile.
It is guaranteed that at least one certificate of three materials exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the materials of a minimal certificate of instability. Output the lexicographically smallest such certificate (smallest $a$; ties by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The problem originally defined a certificate as a trio with a maximum safe set size of 1, which models easily pattern-matched to finding a triangle (or 3-clique). I replaced this graph-theoretic independent set condition with its continuous fractional relaxation: assigning non-negative weights summing to 1 such that every mutually volatile pair sums to at most 1/2. This forces the model to mathematically prove that the system of inequalities is unsatisfiable if and only if all three edges are present, bypassing surface-level graph heuristics and requiring genuine algebraic deduction before any graph algorithm can be applied.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The continuous weight assignment is mathematically impossible if and only if all three materials are mutually volatile with each other (a triangle), preserving the exact same condition and tie-breaking rules for the output.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying computational task remains finding the lexicographically first triangle in an undirected graph, which maintains the identical algorithmic structure and optimal complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the fractional weight condition as a literal continuous optimization problem to be solved algorithmically, leading to TLE if they attempt to implement an LP solver rather than deducing its equivalence to a triangle. However, the mathematical phrasing is strictly unambiguous.
</RISK>
