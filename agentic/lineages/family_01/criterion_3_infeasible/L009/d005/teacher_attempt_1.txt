# Problem C — Volatile Mixtures

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

An industrial facility maintains an inventory of $n$ reactive substances, indexed from 1 to $n$. Specific pairs of these substances are known to react violently upon contact and require strict isolation from one another.

The facility's hazard team is searching for a critical vulnerability: a group of exactly three substances with a specific dangerous property. The property is that no matter which single substance is excluded from consideration, the remaining two substances in the group are documented to react violently. A breach involving such a group would trigger a self-sustaining chain of reactions.

You are provided with the comprehensive log of incompatible substance pairs. Your objective is to locate one such group of three substances.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ — representing a distinct pair of substances that react violently.
It is guaranteed that there is at least one group of three substances meeting the hazard criteria.

## Output
Output three integers $a$ $b$ $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the substances in the group. If multiple such groups exist, output the one that is lexicographically smallest (the one with the smallest $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest $b$; and finally the smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4

Output:
1 2 3

## Example 2
Input:
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5

Output:
1 2 4
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Changed the description of the trio from "exactly three distinct documented incompatible pairs" to a property based on excluding one substance at a time. This forces the model to deduce that the condition is equivalent to all three pairs reacting (a graph triangle), preventing it from superficially pattern-matching to an edge-based bitset intersection shortcut.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that excluding any one substance leaves a reacting pair is logically identical to requiring all three pairs in the trio to react, keeping all valid groups exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains equivalent to finding a triangle (K3) in a graph, preserving its complexity class and the viability of the bitset adjacency algorithm.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
The phrase "excluded from consideration" could theoretically be interpreted as removing the substance from the entire facility rather than just the trio, but the phrase "the remaining two substances in the group" restricts the scope unambiguously.
</RISK>
