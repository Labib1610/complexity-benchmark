# Stable Ternary Mixture

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A chemical laboratory is analyzing a set of $n$ experimental compounds. Through pairwise testing, they have identified $m$ specific combinations of two compounds that are completely inert when mixed together.

The laboratory needs to formulate a new solution containing exactly three distinct compounds. To guarantee stability, the selected trio must form a complete inert circuit: if the compounds are $X$, $Y$, and $Z$, then $X$ must be inert with $Y$, $Y$ must be inert with $Z$, and $Z$ must be inert with $X$. If this exact mutual inertness is not satisfied, the solution will degrade.

You are provided with the records of the $m$ inert combinations. It is guaranteed that at least one such stable ternary solution can be formulated. Your task is to identify the three compounds.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the number of compounds and the number of inert combinations, respectively. The laboratory's storage facility limits the number of compounds $n$ to at most two thousand, and the minimum required for a mixture is three. The database system restricts the number of logged combinations $m$ to at most two hundred thousand, with a minimum of three logs. The compounds are assigned integer identifiers from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that compound $u$ and compound $v$ are inert when mixed together.

It is guaranteed that at least one valid solution of three compounds can be formulated.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the identifiers of the three compounds. The identifiers must be printed in strictly increasing order ($a < b < c$).

If multiple such valid solutions exist, output the one that is lexicographically smallest (i.e., minimize $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and if still tied, minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
Replaced the phrase "every possible pair of compounds within the mixture is inert" with a structural description of a "complete inert circuit" over variables $X, Y, Z$. This prevents superficial keyword matching on "every pair" and forces the model to deduce the triangle topology. Additionally, the numeric bounds for $n$ and $m$ are now expressed indirectly as physical and system limits (Strategy S07).
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that $X$ is inert with $Y$, $Y$ with $Z$, and $Z$ with $X$ is logically identical to requiring all three pairs in the trio to be inert, so the valid triplets and their tie-breaking remain exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The core task is still to find a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, preserving its computational complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
