# Ternary Quantum Coherence

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A research facility is operating a quantum apparatus containing $n$ trapped ions. During the initial calibration phase, scientists successfully induced $m$ symmetric pairwise entanglements. Each entanglement directly links two distinct ions.

The experiment's second phase requires the extraction of a fully integrated three-ion formation. To maintain stability, this formation must exhibit complete mutual dependence: all three selected ions must share pairwise entanglements with one another.

You will receive the telemetry data detailing the $m$ active entanglements. At least one such three-ion formation is guaranteed to be present. Your task is to pinpoint the exact three ions to be extracted.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the total number of ions and the total number of pairwise entanglements. Ions are designated by unique integers from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating an active entanglement between the ion $u$ and the ion $v$.

The presence of at least one valid three-ion formation is guaranteed.

## Output
Print a single line containing three space-separated integers, representing the identifiers of the extracted ions. The three integers must be sorted in strictly ascending order.

Should the apparatus contain more than one valid formation, you must select the one that minimizes the structural index. For a formation composed of ions $x, y,$ and $z$ (where $x < y < z$), its structural index is computed as $x \cdot 10^8 + y \cdot 10^4 + z$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

Models were relying on the explicit instruction to output the "lexicographically smallest" match (minimizing a, then b, then c) as a cue to write simple nested loops that terminate on the first find. By replacing this explicit phrasing with a mathematically equivalent "structural index" formula, models must now independently deduce that this index corresponds to lexicographical order to safely use an early-exit search, or risk writing exhaustive searches that could time out.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because all ion identifiers are at most 2000 (which is strictly less than 10^4), the structural index formula acts as a base-10^4 representation, meaning minimizing it is mathematically identical to finding the lexicographically smallest tuple.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying computational task remains identifying a 3-clique in an undirected graph, preserving the problem's complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models writing in languages like C++ or Java might attempt to compute the structural index using 32-bit signed integers, which could overflow since the maximum value is roughly 2 * 10^11. However, this is a standard data-type error rather than an ambiguity in the problem text.
</RISK>
