# Problem C — Flight Manifest Rejection

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A cargo company relies on two transport planes to ship various industrial chemicals. Certain pairs of chemicals are logged in a safety database as reactive and cannot share the same flight.

The current shipping manifest cannot be divided between just two planes. To generate an automated safety rejection report, you must provide a minimal proof of this impossibility. You do not need to attempt a safe allocation.

Specifically, you must pinpoint a group of exactly three distinct chemical IDs. The defining feature of this group is that no two chemicals within it are allowed to share a plane—meaning the database contains a safety flag for every possible selection of two chemicals from this group. Because these specific flags prevent any two of the three from flying together, they prove that at least three planes are required.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The number of chemicals $n$ is at least three, bounded above by the physical capacity of the company's main storage, which is exactly two thousand. The number of safety flags $m$ is at least three, bounded above by the system's hard limit of two hundred thousand (or the maximum possible number of unique pairs among the $n$ chemicals, whichever is smaller). Chemicals are identified by IDs from $1$ to $n$.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating that a safety flag exists for chemicals $u$ and $v$.
It is guaranteed that the data contains at least one group of three chemicals satisfying the rejection criteria.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line with $a < b < c$, representing the IDs of the three chemicals that form the proof.
If there are multiple valid groups of three chemicals, output the one that is lexicographically smallest (i.e., smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
