# Problem C — Cargo Prohibition Protocol

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A logistics firm operates a dual-vessel shipping route for hazardous materials. Certain materials are highly reactive and are strictly prohibited from sharing a cargo hold.

The proposed loading schedule is structurally impossible to fulfill. You must produce a definitive proof of this impossibility to trigger an automatic schedule rejection.

To establish this proof, you are required to isolate a "deadlock" configuration. A deadlock is a specific group of exactly three distinct materials that creates an unresolvable placement chain. If you attempt to place the first material of the group in the port vessel, its documented reactivity with the second material of the group forces the second into the starboard vessel. If the second material has a documented reactivity with the third material of the group, the third is forced back into the port vessel. Finally, if the third material also has a documented reactivity with the first material, it cannot be placed in the port vessel either, proving no valid assignment to the two vessels can exist.

Because the automated auditing system requires deterministic reporting, it will not accept an arbitrary deadlock configuration. The system demands the canonical proof. When you identify a deadlock group of three materials, you must sort their identifiers in ascending order to form a sequence. Among all possible deadlock groups in the manifest, you must report the one whose sorted sequence is lexicographically earliest.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$. The integer $n$ represents the number of hazardous materials; the manifest protocol requires at least three materials, while the physical capacity of the loading dock limits this to two thousand. The integer $m$ represents the number of documented reactive pairs; the safety database requires at least three logged interactions, up to a strict storage limit of two hundred thousand records (or the maximum possible number of unique pairs for the given materials, whichever is smaller). The materials are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.
Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$) — indicating that material $u$ and material $v$ are reactive and cannot share a vessel. 
It is guaranteed that the input contains at least one deadlock configuration of three materials.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ ($a < b < c$), representing the identifiers of the materials in the canonical deadlock configuration.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
