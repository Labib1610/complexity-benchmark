<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Flight Manifest Rejection

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A logistics firm uses two cargo aircraft to transport industrial substances. Certain pairs of these substances are incompatible and will cause a hazard if loaded onto the same aircraft. The provided manifest of incompatibilities guarantees that a safe two-aircraft distribution for all substances is impossible. 

Instead of attempting an allocation, your system must generate a definitive proof of failure. To do this, you must find a "critical triplet": a subset of exactly three substances that cannot be partitioned into two safe groups. Any attempt to divide these three specific substances between the two aircraft will inevitably result in at least one incompatible pair sharing a flight. Identifying this triplet provides a localized proof that the entire manifest is invalid.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of substances and the number of incompatible pairs. Substances are numbered from $1$ to $n$.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$) — a pair of substances that cannot share an aircraft. 
It is guaranteed that at least one critical triplet exists in the given data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line with $a < b < c$, representing the IDs of the three substances that form a critical triplet.
If there are multiple such groups, output the one that is lexicographically smallest (i.e., smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I replaced the explicit structural description of a 3-clique ("every chemical in the group reacts dangerously with the other two") with a functional property ("cannot be partitioned into two safe groups"). This forces the model to mathematically deduce that a subset of three items requiring three partitions is structurally equivalent to a triangle, rather than simply translating the sentence into a three-way adjacency check.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
A subset of three elements cannot be partitioned into two independent sets if and only if all three elements are mutually connected (a triangle), so the valid outputs remain identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle (or 3-clique) in a graph, which maintains the exact same time complexity and algorithmic requirements.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "cannot be partitioned into two safe groups" as a requirement to check the entire graph's partitionability rather than just the isolated triplet, though the phrase "divide these three specific substances" strongly clarifies the scope.
</RISK>
