<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Grievance Audit

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory's human resources department is auditing past worker schedules to understand why a complete operational halt occurred. You are provided with a historical archive of filed grievances. Each grievance establishes a mutual incompatibility between two specific employees. Your goal is to process these archives to isolate a trilateral impasse. A trilateral impasse is formed by a subset of exactly three filed grievances from the archive which, when their involved employees are combined, contain exactly three unique employees in total.

We know that during this time, it was strictly impossible to divide the employees into two shifts without internal grievances. By the nature of this scheduling failure, the archive is guaranteed to hold at least one valid trilateral impasse. You must find the employees involved in such an impasse.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), which are the total number of employees and the total number of filed grievances, respectively.
The next $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that employee $u$ and employee $v$ have a mutual grievance.
It is guaranteed that there exists at least one trilateral impasse (a set of three grievances involving exactly three distinct employees in total) in the dataset.

## Output
Print a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, arranged in strictly increasing order ($a < b < c$). These three integers must be the identifiers of the three employees who make up a trilateral impasse. If the archive contains multiple valid impasses, you must output the one whose employee identifiers are lexicographically smallest (i.e., among all valid impasses, output the one with the minimum possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and if still tied, minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I redefined the target structure from a vertex-based description ("three employees with mutual grievances") to an edge-based one ("three grievances involving exactly three employees"). This forces models to genuinely reason about the graph's properties to realize this is still just a 3-clique; models that blindly translate the text into code will attempt an $O(m^3)$ combination search over the grievances and hit the Time Limit.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because the maximum number of edges between three vertices in a simple graph is three, any subset of exactly three edges involving exactly three vertices must form a triangle, meaning the exact same groups of employees are identified.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying task is still to find a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, which remains solvable in the exact same polynomial time complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
