<STATEMENT>
# Arbitrage Hedge

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A financial firm is analyzing $n$ market commodities, labeled with integer IDs from 1 to $n$. A single server rack can hold up to 2000 commodity tracking modules, meaning $n$ will not exceed this capacity, though there are always at least 3. Through historical data, they have verified $m$ distinct exchange parities, each indicating that two particular commodities can be traded directly for one another without slippage. Each verified parity requires one dedicated connection in a fast-access switch that supports exactly 200,000 connections, so $m$ is bounded by this limit, or by the maximum possible number of distinct pairs among the $n$ commodities, whichever is smaller. There are always at least 3 parities.

The quantitative team wants to form a "Hedge Ring". A Hedge Ring is a portfolio consisting of exactly three distinct commodities. A verified exchange parity is considered "internal" to a portfolio if both of its commodities are part of that portfolio. To be perfectly hedged, the portfolio must contain exactly three internal parities.

Market conditions guarantee that at least one valid Hedge Ring exists. Your task is to process the parities and identify one such portfolio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of commodities that have a verified exchange parity.
It is guaranteed that at least one Hedge Ring exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the commodities in the Hedge Ring. Output the lexicographically smallest such set (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
Changed the definition of a valid portfolio from "every commodity must be tradeable with the other two" to "the portfolio must contain exactly three internal parities". This forces the model to realize that three subsets of size two among three items means all pairs are present, rather than pattern-matching the common-neighbor definition of a triangle. Also moved the numeric bounds into the scenario text as physical capacities.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
A set of three distinct items has exactly three possible pairs; requiring exactly three internal parities is mathematically identical to requiring that all three pairs exist in the data.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, preserving its complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "three internal parities" if they fail to realize that a pair of commodities only ever has one parity (guaranteed by "distinct exchange parities").
</RISK>
