# Problem C — Grievance Audit

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory's human resources department is auditing past worker schedules to understand why a complete operational halt occurred. You are provided with a historical archive of filed grievances. Each grievance establishes a mutual incompatibility between two specific employees. Your goal is to process these archives to isolate a trilateral impasse.

For any employee $x$, let $d(x)$ be the total number of grievances in the archive that involve $x$. A trilateral impasse is uniquely defined as a group of exactly three distinct employees, identified by their integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ (arranged such that $a < b < c$), that satisfies the following systemic equation:

The count of grievances in the archive for which *neither* of the two employees involved belongs to the group $\{a, b, c\}$ must be exactly equal to $m - d(a) - d(b) - d(c) + 3$.

We know that during this time, the specific nature of the scheduling failure guarantees that the archive holds at least one valid trilateral impasse satisfying this exact equation. You must find such a group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), which are the total number of employees and the total number of filed grievances, respectively.
The next $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that employee $u$ and employee $v$ have a mutual grievance.
It is guaranteed that there exists at least one trilateral impasse (a set of three distinct employees satisfying the systemic equation) in the dataset.

## Output
Print a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, arranged in strictly increasing order ($a < b < c$). These three integers must be the identifiers of the employees who make up a trilateral impasse. If the archive contains multiple valid impasses, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest (i.e., among all valid impasses, output the one with the minimum possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and if still tied, minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

This forces models to either mathematically prove the equation is equivalent to a 3-clique in order to use their optimized bitset algorithms, or blindly implement the equation which leads to an $O(N^3)$ Time Limit Exceeded failure.
</RATIONALE>

</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
