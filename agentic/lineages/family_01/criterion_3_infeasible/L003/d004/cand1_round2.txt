<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Grievance Audit

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory's human resources department is auditing past worker schedules. You are provided with a historical archive of filed grievances. Each grievance establishes a mutual incompatibility between two specific employees. 

Your goal is to process these archives to isolate a trilateral impasse. A trilateral impasse is a group of exactly three distinct employees (let us call them $x$, $y$, and $z$) such that all of the following conditions are met:
1. There is a grievance on record between employee $x$ and employee $y$.
2. There is a grievance on record between employee $x$ and employee $z$.
3. There is a grievance on record between employee $y$ and employee $z$.
4. Each employee in the group ($x$, $y$, and $z$) is involved in at least two grievance records in the provided archive.

If the archive contains multiple valid trilateral impasses, you must select the one that is lexicographically smallest. That is, if you sort the three employee identifiers of an impasse in strictly increasing order as $a < b < c$, you must output the impasse with the minimum possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, minimize $b$; and if still tied, minimize $c$.

By the nature of the scheduling failure, the archive is guaranteed to hold at least one valid trilateral impasse that satisfies all the above conditions.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), which are the total number of employees and the total number of filed grievances, respectively.
The next $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that employee $u$ and employee $v$ have a mutual grievance.
It is guaranteed that there exists at least one valid trilateral impasse in the dataset.

## Output
Print a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, arranged in strictly increasing order ($a < b < c$). These three integers must be the identifiers of the employees who make up the chosen trilateral impasse.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I redefined the target structure using a strict pairwise conflict narrative and introduced a tautological condition (requiring each employee to have at least two total grievances). This forces models to parse the exact logical requirements rather than blindly pattern-matching to standard triangle-finding, as the extra condition appears to constrain the search space but mathematically changes nothing.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The answers are identical because any three distinct employees with mutual pairwise grievances inherently satisfy the condition of being involved in at least two grievance records, making the added constraint a tautology that preserves all valid groups.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The complexity class is unchanged because the core computational task strictly remains finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, which requires identical algorithmic approaches.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret the tautological degree condition as implying that they only need to consider grievances between employees who have a degree of exactly 2, but the wording "at least two" is standard and unambiguous.
</RISK>
