# Planetary Core Assembly

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A mechanical engineering team is evaluating $n$ distinct gear designs, labeled with integer IDs from 1 to $n$. Through extensive prototyping, they have confirmed $m$ specific meshing compatibilities, each indicating that two particular gears can run together continuously without jamming.

The engineers need to construct a "planetary core". A planetary core is an assembly consisting of exactly three distinct gears. To ensure proper torque distribution, two of the gears in the assembly must have a confirmed meshing compatibility to act as the primary drive, and the third gear in the assembly must have confirmed compatibilities with both of the primary drive gears to act as the stabilizer. 

Physical constraints guarantee that at least one valid planetary core can be constructed. Your task is to process the compatibility records and identify the gears that make up one such assembly.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of gears that have a confirmed meshing compatibility.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid planetary core exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the three gears in the planetary core. Output the lexicographically smallest such set (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The seed problem explicitly stated that removing any one gear leaves a connected pair, which directly triggered 3-clique/triangle recognition patterns in models. The rewrite replaces this symmetric property with an asymmetric mechanical description: a base pair of compatible gears and a third gear compatible with both. This forces the model to logically deduce that this configuration is equivalent to a triangle, rather than relying on the "any 2 of 3" pattern match.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The structural condition requires an edge between two gears and edges from the third gear to both of the first two, which mathematically defines a triangle (a 3-clique); thus the valid sets of three gears are identical to the seed problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
Finding a set of three mutually connected vertices is exactly the Triangle Finding problem, preserving the time complexity and infeasibility class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might mistakenly think they need to output which gears are the primary drive and which is the stabilizer, but the output format explicitly asks for the three gears in the core sorted lexicographically, mitigating this risk.
</RISK>
