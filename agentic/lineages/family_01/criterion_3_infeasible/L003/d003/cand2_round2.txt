# Problem C — Roster Evaluation

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

Factory management is reviewing historical worker rosters to pinpoint the source of a specific scheduling dysfunction. You are given a log of past disputes, where each entry represents an undirected edge in the conflict graph between a pair of workers. Your objective is to analyze this log and identify a three-person deadlock. A deadlock of this type consists of exactly three distinct workers who all mutually conflict—meaning each worker in the trio has a recorded dispute with the other two members of the trio.

Because these logs were taken from a period where the factory was unable to form two conflict-free shifts, any such three-person deadlock serves as a certificate of this impossibility. Your task is to extract one of these groups.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$. The integer $n$ represents the factory's active headcount, which is constrained by a minimum operating requirement of 3 and a maximum facility capacity of 2000. The integer $m$ represents the volume of recorded disputes, which is at least 3, and capped by the lesser of the system storage limit of 200,000 or the physical limit of all possible unique pairs among the $n$ workers ($n \cdot (n-1)/2$).
Following this, there are $m$ lines. Each line contains two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a dispute between worker $u$ and worker $v$. Each unordered pair of workers appears at most once in the records.
It is guaranteed that at least one triple of workers exists such that all three pairs conflict.

## Output
Output a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$, ordered such that $a < b < c$. These integers must represent the workers forming a three-person deadlock. Should there be more than one such group, you must output the sequence that comes first lexicographically (minimizing $a$; then minimizing $b$ in the event of a tie; and finally minimizing $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
