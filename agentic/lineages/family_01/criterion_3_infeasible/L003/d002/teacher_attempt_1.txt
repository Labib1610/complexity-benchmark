# Problem C — Chemical Safety Audit

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A chemical manufacturing plant has an inventory of $n$ distinct reagents, numbered 1 through $n$. Through testing, they have identified $m$ specific combinations of two reagents that produce a highly exothermic reaction when mixed.

The safety protocol requires identifying a "Type-3 Hazard". This hazard manifests when three specific reagents are brought together, provided that the testing log confirms an exothermic reaction for every possible pair formed exclusively from these three reagents. It is a known fact about this inventory that at least one Type-3 Hazard can be assembled.

Your task is to process the list of reactive pairs and identify one such trio of reagents.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The value of $n$ is at least the minimum number of reagents needed for a Type-3 Hazard, up to a strict facility limit of two thousand. The value of $m$ is at least the exact number of reactions that make up a single isolated Type-3 Hazard, and is capped at either two hundred thousand or the theoretical maximum number of distinct pairs for $n$ reagents, whichever is smaller.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of reagents that react exothermically.
It is guaranteed that at least one Type-3 Hazard exists in the given data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$ $b$ $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ identifying the reagents in the hazard. Output the lexicographically smallest valid trio (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
