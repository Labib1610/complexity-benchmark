# Signature Meal Selection

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

At an elite culinary institute, chefs are assembling a tasting menu from a collection of $n$ unique dishes. Years of experimentation have resulted in a registry of $m$ distinct symmetric flavor synergies. Each synergy in the registry denotes that two particular dishes enhance one another.

To graduate, an apprentice must formulate a "masterpiece trio" containing precisely three unique dishes. A trio achieves perfect harmony when the number of synergies in the registry that involve only dishes from this trio is exactly three.

Your objective is to systematically discover a perfectly harmonious masterpiece trio. The provided registries are drawn from historical records and are guaranteed to contain the synergies required to construct at least one such trio.

## Input
The initial line provides the numbers $n$ and $m$ (3 ≤ n ≤ 2000, 3 ≤ m ≤ min(n·(n−1)/2, 2·10⁵)).
Following this are $m$ lines, each listing two integers $u$ and $v$. These represent the identifiers of a pair of dishes that share a flavor synergy.
It is assured that a valid masterpiece trio exists in every test case.

## Output
Provide a single line containing three integers, ordered from lowest to highest, representing the dishes in the chosen masterpiece trio.

In the event that several perfectly harmonious trios can be constructed, you must apply strict selection criteria:
First, compare the smallest dish identifier present in each valid trio; the trio with the lower minimum identifier is preferred.
If a tie persists, evaluate the remaining tied trios by comparing their middle-valued dish identifiers, again preferring the lower value.
If still tied, the trio with the lower maximum dish identifier must be selected.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
Removed the term "lexicographically smallest", which allowed models to blindly rely on the natural iteration order of nested loops to find the required answer. The tie-breaking rule is now described explicitly by comparing the minimum, middle, and maximum elements of the valid sets, forcing models to carefully translate these conditions to realize that a standard ordered search still applies.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The definition of a valid trio (3 dishes with exactly 3 internal pairs present in the input) and the tie-breaking criteria (comparing sorted elements sequentially) are mathematically identical to finding a triangle and returning the lexicographically first one.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The core computational task remains finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, preserving the original time complexity requirements.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
The phrasing "synergies in the registry that involve only dishes from this trio" might require careful reading, though it strictly and unambiguously defines edges whose both endpoints belong to the chosen subset of 3 vertices.
</RISK>
