 We need to find three reports (edges) such that exactly three distinct workers appear, each exactly twice.  
That means the three edges form a triangle: (a,b), (b,c), (c,a).  

So the problem reduces to: find the lexicographically smallest triangle in an undirected graph.  

We can do this by iterating over all edges (u,v) with u < v, and for each, checking if there exists a w > v such that both (u,w) and (v,w) exist.  
We can store adjacency sets for O(1) neighbor checks.  

Here’s the implementation:  

```python
n, m = map(int, input().split())
adj = [set() for _ in range(n + 1)]
edges = []

for _ in range(m):
    u, v = map(int, input().split())
    adj[u].add(v)
    adj[v].add(u)
    if u > v:
        u, v = v, u
    edges.append((u, v))

edges.sort()

for u, v in edges:
    # Find smallest w > v connected to both u and v
    for w in sorted(adj[v]):
        if w > v and w in adj[u]:
            print(u, v, w)
            exit()
```
