<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory still has only two shifts, 1 and 2, but the roster is known to be hopeless. Your job is not to schedule — it is to search the possible worker combinations to certify the impossibility. A deadlock is a specific group of three workers such that, if you try to assign just those three individuals to the two shifts, every possible assignment forces at least one pair with a documented incompatibility to work the same shift. Such a triad provides an undeniable certificate that two shifts cannot suffice. Finding such a deadlock can appear daunting given the sheer number of possible combinations to check.

It is guaranteed that the conflict data contains at least one such deadlock. Output the members of one such deadlock.

## Input
The first line contains two integers n and m (3 ≤ n ≤ 2000, 3 ≤ m ≤ min(n·(n−1)/2, 2·10⁵)).
Each of the next m lines contains two integers u v — a pair of workers who have a documented incompatibility.
It is guaranteed that at least one deadlock exists.

## Output
Print three integers a b c on one line with a < b < c representing the workers in the deadlock. Output the lexicographically smallest such group (smallest a; ties by smallest b; then smallest c).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I replaced the explicit description of a triangle ("one worker cannot work with a second, the second cannot work with a third...") with its functional consequence for 2-coloring: that any assignment of the three workers to two shifts forces an incompatible pair to share a shift. To succeed, the model must deduce that this property holds if and only if all three workers are mutually incompatible, thereby reconstructing the triangle condition rather than pattern-matching it from the text.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that any 2-shift assignment of three workers forces an incompatible pair to share a shift is logically equivalent to the three workers being mutually incompatible (a triangle), so the lexicographically smallest such group remains unchanged for all inputs.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still asks to find a triangle in an undirected graph, which is solvable in polynomial time and retains its infeasibility-certificate nature.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might attempt to literally simulate all 2-shift assignments for all $O(N^3)$ triplets, resulting in a Time Limit Exceeded error if they fail to deduce the triangle equivalence.
</RISK>
