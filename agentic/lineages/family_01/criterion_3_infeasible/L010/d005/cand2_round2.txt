<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory management has a log of incompatibility reports. Each report identifies a pair of workers who cannot share a shift. To prove that their current two-shift system is doomed to fail, management wants to find a specific pattern in the log. Your job is not to schedule the workers, but to search the log to certify this impossibility.

Your task is to identify a "deadlock". We define a deadlock as a subset of exactly three distinct reports from the log such that the modulo-2 sum of their incidence vectors (where each report is treated as an indicator vector over the workforce) is the zero vector. In other words, every worker involved in the deadlock appears in an even number of the three chosen reports.

Finding this configuration provides an undeniable certificate that two shifts cannot suffice. It is guaranteed that the log contains at least one such deadlock.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a report naming two incompatible workers. All reports in the log represent distinct pairs.
It is guaranteed that at least one deadlock exists in the given reports.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the workers who appear in the deadlock's reports. Output the lexicographically smallest such group of workers (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
### Input
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
### Output
1 2 3

## Example 2
### Input
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
### Output
1 2 4

Removed the explicit statement that an empty symmetric difference of three pairs necessarily involves exactly three workers. Replaced it with a formal definition based on the modulo-2 sum of incidence vectors. This forces the model to mathematically deduce that three 2-element sets summing to the zero vector modulo 2 corresponds to a 3-cycle, rather than merely pattern-matching the explicit graph-theoretic reduction provided in the seed.
</RATIONALE>

The modulo-2 sum of three 2-element sets is zero if and only if they form a triangle on exactly three elements, meaning the structural requirement and the resulting output are perfectly unchanged.
