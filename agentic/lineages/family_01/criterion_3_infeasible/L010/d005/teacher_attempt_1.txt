# Signature Meal Selection

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

An elite culinary institute is establishing a new tasting menu drawing from a collection of $n$ distinct recipes. The institute's recipe vault requires at least enough recipes to form a single meal (three), but its physical shelving capacity strictly limits the collection to a maximum of two thousand recipes.

Following rigorous taste tests, the institute has published $m$ pairing certificates. Each certificate lists exactly two recipes that have been verified to taste good together. To justify the publication cost, they print at least three certificates, but the annual printing budget caps the total number of published certificates at two hundred thousand (or the total number of possible pairs, whichever is smaller).

To graduate, a student must curate a "signature meal" consisting of exactly three distinct recipes. To ensure perfect balance, the selection must be fully supported by the published certificates: if you select any pair of recipes from the meal, that specific pair must be listed on a published certificate.

Your objective is to computationally identify the recipes for a perfectly balanced signature meal. The provided certificates originate from prior successful graduations, guaranteeing that at least one valid signature meal can be assembled.

## Input
The initial line provides two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the number of recipes and the number of certificates respectively. The bounds on these values are determined by the vault capacity and printing budget described above.
The subsequent $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$), identifying the two recipes on a published certificate.
All test cases guarantee the existence of at least one valid signature meal.

## Output
Output three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, satisfying $a < b < c$, which identify the recipes comprising the signature meal. Should multiple valid meals exist, provide the lexicographically smallest combination (minimize $a$, then minimize $b$, then minimize $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
