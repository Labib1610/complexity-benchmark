# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory management has a log of incompatibility reports. They want to find a specific pattern in the log that proves their current two-shift system is doomed to fail. Your job is not to schedule the workers, but to search the log to certify this impossibility.

A "deadlock" is formed by choosing exactly three reports from the log. Since each report names two workers, your chosen three reports will yield a combined list of exactly six worker IDs (including any repetitions). This selection of three reports is considered a valid deadlock if and only if it satisfies both of the following conditions:
1. There are exactly three distinct worker IDs present in this list.
2. No single worker ID appears three or more times in this list.

Such a configuration provides an undeniable certificate that two shifts cannot suffice. Finding this configuration might seem daunting given the sheer number of possible three-report combinations to check.

It is guaranteed that the log contains at least one such deadlock. You must output the IDs of the three distinct workers who make up this deadlock.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a report naming two incompatible workers.
It is guaranteed that at least one deadlock exists in the given reports.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the three distinct workers who make up the deadlock. Output the lexicographically smallest such group of workers (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The explicit frequency counts ("exactly three workers will have a tally of exactly 2, and all other workers will have a tally of 0") were removed. They are replaced with a condition on the size of the set of distinct workers (3) and a maximum frequency bound (<3) for a list of exactly 6 items. This mathematically forces the frequencies to be exactly 2, 2, and 2 via integer partitioning, preserving the exact triangle structure of the original problem while forcing the model to deduce this equivalence instead of pattern-matching it.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The only way to partition 6 items into 3 distinct groups with no group having 3 or more items is 2+2+2; thus, the chosen three reports must be the three edges connecting exactly three workers, which is exactly the same triangle structure as the seed.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The complexity class is unchanged because the true underlying condition remains the existence of a 3-cycle (triangle) in the undirected graph of reports.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
