<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory management maintains a log of incompatibility reports. To analyze these mathematically, each report is represented as a state vector of length $W$ over the finite field GF(2), where each component corresponds to a worker ID from 1 to $W$. In the vector for a given report, exactly the two components corresponding to the two incompatible workers are set to 1, and all other components are 0.

They want to find a specific pattern in the log that proves their current two-shift system is doomed to fail. Your job is not to schedule the workers, but to search the log to certify this impossibility.

Your task is to identify a "deadlock". A deadlock is formed by choosing exactly three reports from the log such that their vector sum modulo 2 yields the zero vector. 

Such a configuration provides an undeniable certificate that two shifts cannot suffice. Finding this configuration might seem daunting given the sheer number of possible three-report combinations to check.

It is guaranteed that the log contains at least one such deadlock. By the properties of GF(2) addition on these 2-sparse vectors, any such deadlock will non-trivially involve exactly three distinct workers (i.e., exactly three workers will have a vector component of 1 in at least one of the three chosen reports). You must output the IDs of these three workers.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $W$ and $R$. 
The first integer $W$ is the total number of workers. The workers are assigned sequential IDs starting from 1. $W$ is at least 3, and will not exceed the factory's maximum worker capacity. The factory was established in the year 2000, and by a strange coincidence, this capacity is exactly equal to the establishment year.
The second integer $R$ is the total number of reports in the log. $R$ is at least 3, and will never exceed one hundred times the absolute maximum worker capacity, nor will it exceed the total number of distinct pairs that can be formed from the $W$ workers.
Each of the next $R$ lines corresponds to a report and contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — the IDs of the two workers whose corresponding vector components are 1.
It is guaranteed that at least one deadlock exists in the given reports.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the IDs of the three workers involved in the deadlock. Output the lexicographically smallest such group of workers (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The explicit tally condition was replaced with a mathematically equivalent condition over GF(2) where three 2-sparse vectors must sum to the zero vector. This phrasing completely removes any direct description of a graph triangle while preserving the exact same structural certificate. Models that rely on surface-level pattern matching will fail to map the vector null-space condition to a triangle-finding algorithm, whereas models that genuinely reason will deduce that the only way for three 2-sparse vectors to sum to zero mod 2 is if they form a triangle. S07 was also applied to encode the numeric bounds indirectly.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition that three 2-sparse vectors sum to zero modulo 2 is uniquely satisfied by exactly three vectors forming a triangle, making it mathematically identical to the original tally constraint, so every answer remains exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The underlying operational task remains finding the lexicographically smallest triangle in an undirected graph, preserving the certificate-style INFEASIBLE complexity class and its precise algorithmic requirements.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
