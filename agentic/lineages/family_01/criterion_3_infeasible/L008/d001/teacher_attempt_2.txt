# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The management committee is trying to divide the personnel into two factions, but they already know it cannot be done. Your task is to provide the evidence of this failure. You are given a log of grievances; each entry indicates that two specific individuals refuse to be in the same faction.

To demonstrate the impossibility, you must identify three distinct individuals such that each one has a recorded grievance with both of the other two. It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three exists.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The integer $n$ represents the total number of personnel. The company requires at least enough personnel to form the necessary group of three, but the facility capacity strictly limits the headcount to two thousand individuals. The integer $m$ represents the number of grievances. There are at least three grievances filed, up to a filing limit of two hundred thousand (or the absolute maximum of all possible pairs among the $n$ personnel, whichever is more restrictive).

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), representing a grievance between individual $u$ and individual $v$. Each pair is listed at most once.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$, representing the identified individuals. If there are multiple valid groups, minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
