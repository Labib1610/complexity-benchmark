# Problem C — Unstable Orbital Systems

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a catalog of $n$ celestial bodies in a newly discovered star system. Observations have yielded a list of interference reports. Each report indicates that two specific bodies exert dangerous tidal forces on each other if their orbits align. 

To prove that the star system is fundamentally unstable, you must identify a system-wide collapse trigger. A collapse trigger consists of exactly three celestial bodies where every possible pair drawn from these three has a corresponding interference report. 

It is guaranteed that the catalog contains at least one such group of three bodies. Your task is to find and output one such group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of celestial bodies and the number of interference reports.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of celestial bodies that exert dangerous tidal forces on each other.
It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three bodies exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that all three pairs $a$–$b$, $a$–$c$, and $b$–$c$ are present in the reports. Output the lexicographically smallest such group (smallest $a$; ties by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
