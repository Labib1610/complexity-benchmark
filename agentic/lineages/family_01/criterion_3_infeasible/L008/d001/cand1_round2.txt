# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   
Memory limit: 256 MB

The manufacturing plant operates with only two shifts. However, the personnel department has compiled a list of grievances. Each grievance indicates that a specific pair of employees cannot be assigned to the same shift. 

The situation is so severe that it is guaranteed you can find a trio of employees who all have grievances against each other (i.e., employee X cannot work with Y, Y cannot work with Z, and X cannot work with Z). Such a trio mathematically proves that a two-shift schedule is unachievable, as at least two of them would have to share a shift. 

Your task is to identify and report one such trio to the personnel department to demonstrate the scheduling failure. 

## Input
The first line contains two integers n and m (3 ≤ n ≤ 2000, 3 ≤ m ≤ min(n·(n−1)/2, 2·10⁵)), representing the total number of employees and the number of grievances, respectively. Employees are identified by integers from 1 to n.
Each of the following m lines contains two integers u and v, indicating a grievance between employee u and employee v.
It is guaranteed that at least one trio of employees, all mutually incompatible, exists.

## Output
Output three integers representing the IDs of the employees in the trio, separated by spaces. The three IDs must be printed in ascending order. 

If there are multiple valid trios, you must output the one that wins the following comparison: first, compare the lowest ID of each trio, preferring the smaller value. For trios tied on the lowest ID, compare their middle IDs, again preferring the smaller value. Finally, for any remaining ties, prefer the trio with the smaller highest ID.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
