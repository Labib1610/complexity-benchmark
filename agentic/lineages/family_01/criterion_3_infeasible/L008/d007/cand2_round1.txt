# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

Management insists on dividing the workforce into exactly two non-overlapping shifts. However, the HR department knows this plan is doomed. You must provide undeniable proof of this failure.

HR has a collection of incident files, where each file lists two specific workers who cannot be placed on the same shift. 

Management's plan can be proven impossible if you can find a 'deadlock' in the records. A deadlock is formed by a specific trio of distinct workers. To verify a deadlock, HR extracts all incident files where *both* listed workers belong to the trio. The trio is confirmed as a deadlock if, within this extracted set of files, each of the three workers' names appears exactly twice.

It is guaranteed that at least one such deadlock exists. Your task is to identify the three distinct workers involved in such a deadlock and report them to HR.

## Input
The first line provides the workforce size $n$ and the total incident count $m$. The workforce size $n$ is bounded below by 3 and bounded above by the facility capacity of 2000. The incident count $m$ is at least 3, and is capped by either the theoretical maximum of $n \cdot (n-1)/2$ distinct combinations, or the archival capacity of $2 \cdot 10^5$, whichever is smaller. Workers are identified by integers from 1 to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), identifying the two workers involved in a single incident. No two incident files list the exact same pair of workers.

It is guaranteed that at least one valid deadlock exists.

## Output
Output three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ representing the IDs of the three workers involved. They must be printed such that $a < b < c$.

To ensure a unique valid response when multiple such deadlocks exist, you must select the one that minimizes the value of $a$. If there is a tie, minimize the value of $b$ among the tied triplets, and if a tie still persists, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The previous wording explicitly mentioned "exactly three incident files that... involve exactly three distinct workers," which is the classic definition of a triangle (K3) in a graph. I removed this direct structural affordance. Instead, the problem now defines a deadlock as a trio of workers where, if you restrict the files to only those internal to the trio, each worker appears exactly twice. This requires the model to deduce that a sum of 6 internal endpoints among 3 workers implies exactly 3 internal edges, which is a triangle, rather than just pattern-matching the textbook description.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Since a trio of workers has at most 3 possible internal files, the only way all three workers can appear exactly twice within these files is if all 3 files exist, making it mathematically identical to the original triangle condition.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
