# Problem C — Ternary Reaction Hazard

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a laboratory protocol involving $n$ distinct chemical reagents. Through preliminary testing, you have compiled a log of documented precipitate reactions. Each entry in the log lists two reagents that react when combined.

A specific reaction hazard, known as a ternary complex, is triggered by a trio of distinct reagents. This hazard occurs if and only if, for every possible selection of two reagents from the trio, their combination appears in the preliminary testing log. 

Your task is to identify a trio of reagents that creates this hazard. The provided testing data guarantees that at least one such ternary complex can be formed.

## Input
The first line provides two integers, $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of reagents and the number of documented precipitate reactions, respectively. Reagents are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.

The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that the log contains an entry for reagent $u$ and reagent $v$.

It is guaranteed that the provided records contain at least one ternary complex.

## Output
Output three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the three reagents that comprise the ternary complex. If there is more than one valid trio, you must output the one that minimizes the hazard score, calculated as $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

I replaced the edge-centric double negative ("no two ... are safe to mix") with a subset-centric condition ("for every possible selection of two reagents from the trio"). This removes the framing that suggests picking two reagents (an edge) and looking for a third (the common neighbor), forcing the model to independently deduce the graph structure and the efficiency of the common-neighbor bitset optimization from a holistic definition of the trio.
</RATIONALE>

Any trio of reagents where all three possible pairs are in the log is exactly a set of three reagents where no two are safe to mix under the original definition, meaning the exact same outputs are correct.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
