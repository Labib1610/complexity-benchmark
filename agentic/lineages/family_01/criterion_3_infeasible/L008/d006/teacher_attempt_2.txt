# Problem C — Ternary Reaction Hazard

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a laboratory protocol involving $n$ distinct chemical reagents. Through preliminary testing, you have compiled a list of two-reagent mixtures that produce a precipitate. 

A critical instability occurs when a trio of distinct reagents is assembled such that every possible pair drawn from the trio is documented to yield a precipitate.

Your task is to identify a trio of reagents that creates this instability. The provided testing data guarantees that at least one such trio exists.

## Input
The first line provides two integers, $n$ and $m$, representing the number of reagents and the number of documented precipitate reactions, respectively. Reagents are identified by integers starting from one up to the total number of reagents. The laboratory protocol requires a minimum of three reagents to be analyzed, while the storage vault capacity strictly limits the inventory to at most two thousand reagents. The minimum number of documented reactions is three, and the testing log can hold a maximum of two hundred thousand entries, or the maximum possible number of distinct pairs among the available reagents, whichever is smaller.

The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing the identifiers of the reagents. These identifiers are distinct and valid within the inventory range, indicating that the two reagents produce a precipitate when mixed. Each pair of reacting reagents is recorded at most once.

It is guaranteed that the provided records contain at least one such trio of reagents.

## Output
Output three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the three reagents that comprise the instability. If there is more than one valid trio, you must output the one that minimizes the hazard score, calculated as $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
