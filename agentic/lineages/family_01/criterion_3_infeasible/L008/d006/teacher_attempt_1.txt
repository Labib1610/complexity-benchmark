# Problem C — Ternary Reaction Hazard

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are evaluating a chemical protocol that uses $n$ unique substances. Previous experiments have identified specific pairs of these substances that react to form a solid precipitate.

Your safety review focuses on a particular instability involving exactly three distinct substances. This instability arises only when the three substances are mutually reactive—meaning that any pair chosen from the three is documented to form a precipitate.

You need to find three substances that exhibit this instability. The experimental logs ensure that at least one such group exists.

## Input
The first line contains two integers, $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), denoting the total number of substances and the number of known precipitating pairs. Substances are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n, u \neq v$), showing that combining substance $u$ with substance $v$ yields a precipitate.

The data guarantees the presence of at least one group of three mutually reactive substances.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, sorted so that $a < b < c$, identifying the three substances responsible for the instability. If multiple such groups exist, output the one that yields the lowest severity rating, defined by the formula $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
