# Problem C — Ternary Reaction Hazard

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are evaluating a chemical protocol that uses various distinct reagents. Past experiments have yielded a catalog of binary mixtures that result in a solid precipitate.

A dangerous condition called a ternary complex occurs if three distinct reagents are combined, provided that every possible pair among these three reagents is known to form a precipitate.

You must find a set of three reagents that triggers this condition. The catalog is guaranteed to contain the necessary interactions to form at least one ternary complex.

## Input
The first line contains two integers: the total count of reagents, followed by the count of known precipitate-forming pairs. The inventory contains a minimum of three and a maximum of two thousand reagents. The recorded pairs number at least three and at most two hundred thousand (limited naturally by the maximum number of possible pairs). Reagents are labeled with integers from $1$ up to the total count of reagents.

Each of the following lines provides two integers $u$ and $v$ ($u \neq v$), showing that mixing reagent $u$ and reagent $v$ yields a precipitate.

The records will always allow for the formation of at least one ternary complex.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, sorted in ascending order ($a < b < c$), denoting the reagents forming the ternary complex. In the event of multiple valid triples, select the one that yields the lowest hazard score, evaluated by the formula $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
