# Problem C — Ternary Reaction Hazard

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a laboratory protocol involving $n$ distinct chemical reagents. Through preliminary testing, you have compiled a list of $m$ two-reagent mixtures that produce a precipitate. 

A specific reaction hazard, known as a ternary complex, is triggered when a trio of reagents is mixed together, provided that the testing data confirms a precipitate forms for all three possible pairs within that trio.

The centrifuge used for this protocol limits the total batch size to two thousand vials, and you are analyzing at least enough reagents to form the hazard (three). Thus, the number of reagents $n$ is bounded by these physical limits. A hazard requires a minimum of three documented reactions to exist, so the number of records $m$ is at least this amount. To save storage, the central database caps the log at two hundred thousand entries, though $m$ will naturally not exceed the total number of unique pairs possible among the $n$ reagents.

Your task is to identify three reagents that create this hazard. The provided testing data guarantees that at least one such ternary complex can be formed.

## Input
The first line provides the two integers $n$ and $m$. Reagents are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ (which are distinct valid reagent identifiers), indicating that reagent $u$ and reagent $v$ produce a precipitate when mixed together.
It is guaranteed that the provided records contain at least one ternary complex.

## Output
Output three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the three reagents that comprise the ternary complex. If there is more than one valid trio, you must output the one that minimizes the hazard score, calculated as $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
