# Problem C — Ternary Corroboration

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a collection of $n$ ancient clay fragments. Through digital scanning, you have identified $m$ pairs of fragments that share at least one identical rare symbol, indicating a potential shared origin.

To establish a definitive historical link, researchers require a "ternary corroboration." This condition is met when a set of exactly three distinct fragments is discovered where all three fragments mutually share symbols with one another. Specifically, if the three fragments are chosen, the first must share a symbol with the second, the second must share a symbol with the third, and the first must share a symbol with the third, according to your scanned records.

Your task is to identify three fragments that form this corroboration. The scanned data guarantees that at least one such set of three fragments exists.

## Input
The first line provides two integers, $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of fragments and the number of documented pairs sharing a rare symbol, respectively. Fragments are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n, u \neq v$), indicating that fragment $u$ and fragment $v$ share a rare symbol.
It is guaranteed that the provided records contain at least one ternary corroboration.

## Output
Output three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the three fragments that comprise the ternary corroboration. If there is more than one valid set of three fragments, you must output the one that minimizes the evaluation score, calculated as $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
