# Legislative Committees

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A legislative body consists of $n$ elected delegates, identified by integers from 1 to $n$. Through prolonged negotiations, $m$ distinct pairs of delegates have established open diplomatic channels.

To establish a new "Triumvirate", the legislative charter requires identifying exactly three distinct delegates, labeled $a$, $b$, and $c$ (where their identifier numbers satisfy $a < b < c$), such that every pair among the three maintains an open diplomatic channel. In other words, a channel must connect $a$ and $b$, another must connect $b$ and $c$, and a third must connect $a$ and $c$.

The assembly hall contains exactly two thousand seats, which acts as a strict physical ceiling on the number of delegates $n$. Since a Triumvirate requires three members, the headcount will never fall below this minimum. The administrative registry of diplomatic channels $m$ is capped at exactly two hundred thousand entries, and is naturally no larger than the total possible number of distinct pairs among the $n$ delegates. Because a Triumvirate requires three connections, the registry will always contain at least three entries.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, bounded by the physical and administrative limits outlined above.
Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, representing that delegate $u$ and delegate $v$ share an open diplomatic channel.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid Triumvirate exists in the provided records.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered $a < b < c$, representing the members of the Triumvirate. 
If multiple such groups exist, output the one with the lowest identifier for $a$. If a tie remains, pick the one with the lowest identifier for $b$, and if still tied, the lowest for $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
