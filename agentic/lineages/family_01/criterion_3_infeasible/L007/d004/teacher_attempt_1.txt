# Corporate Mergers

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A regulatory agency is reviewing $n$ multinational corporations. They have identified $m$ pairs of corporations that are strictly prohibited from merging due to antitrust laws.

The agency is investigating "monopoly triads" to understand market vulnerabilities. A monopoly triad consists of a pair of corporations $a$ and $b$ (with $a < b$) that are prohibited from merging, plus a third corporation $c$ (with $b < c$) that is prohibited from merging with both $a$ and $b$.

With up to 2000 corporations, the dataset is large. It is guaranteed that at least one monopoly triad exists in the records. You must identify it.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ are prohibited from merging. 
It is guaranteed that at least one monopoly triad exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ that form a monopoly triad. 
If multiple monopoly triads exist, output the one with the smallest value for $a$; if there is a tie, choose the one with the smallest value for $b$; and if still tied, the smallest value for $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The previous wording symmetrically enumerated the three prohibited pairs ($a$-$b$, $c$-$a$, $c$-$b$), which allowed models to instantly pattern-match the structure as a graph triangle. By redefining the triad hierarchically—as a prohibited pair $a, b$ and a third entity $c$ prohibited with both—we remove the symmetric "triangle" affordance. This forces models to parse the logical requirements directly, which conveniently aligns with the intended bitset-intersection algorithm without giving away the graph-theoretic name.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The hierarchical condition (a and b prohibited, c prohibited with a, c prohibited with b) is logically equivalent to all three pairs being prohibited, preserving all valid triads.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
