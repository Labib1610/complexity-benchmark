# Corporate Mergers

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A regulatory agency monitors $n$ multinational corporations. Due to antitrust regulations, certain corporate pairs are strictly forbidden from consolidating. The agency has compiled a list of $m$ such forbidden pairs.

To evaluate systemic market risks, the agency searches for a "monopoly triad". This occurs when three distinct corporations, say $a$, $b$, and $c$ (ordered such that $a < b < c$), are mutually restricted: consolidation is forbidden between $a$ and $b$, between $b$ and $c$, and between $a$ and $c$.

The agency's jurisdiction requires reviewing a minimum of 3 corporations, while their charter restricts them to overseeing a maximum of 2000 corporations. The number of forbidden pairs is at least 3, and the agency's database capacity limits this to exactly two hundred thousand records (or the theoretical maximum number of distinct pairs for the given corporations, whichever is stricter). The records are guaranteed to contain at least one monopoly triad. Your task is to find it.

## Input
The first line of input provides the two integers $n$ and $m$. 
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, specifying that corporation $u$ and corporation $v$ are forbidden from consolidating. 
The input is guaranteed to contain at least one monopoly triad.

## Output
Output a single line containing three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ ($a < b < c$) representing the corporations in the monopoly triad. 
If there is more than one such triad, output the one that minimizes $a$. If there is still a tie, minimize $b$, and finally $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
