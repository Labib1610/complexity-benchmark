<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — System Instability

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a distributed monitoring network consisting of $n$ sensors. The network is designed to operate by assigning each sensor to one of two available frequency bands. 

Any pair of sensors is considered compatible unless they are linked by a hardware interlock. The provided configuration lists $m$ such interlocked pairs. A safety rule dictates that if two sensors are not compatible, they must not be assigned to the same frequency band. 

However, the current configuration is fundamentally flawed. To demonstrate this flaw, you must identify exactly three distinct sensors such that no two sensors chosen from these three form a compatible pair. Because these three sensors completely lack any compatible pairs, it is impossible to validly assign them to the two frequency bands.

It is guaranteed that the provided configuration contains at least one such subset of three sensors. Your task is to find and report one.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of sensors and the number of interlocks, respectively. Sensors are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$), indicating that sensor $u$ and sensor $v$ are linked by an interlock. It is guaranteed that at least one such subset of three sensors exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three sensors that form the flaw. 

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid subsets of three sensors, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; if there are ties, minimize $b$ among those ties; and if there are still ties, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
Removed the direct adjacency condition ("remaining two are linked") and replaced it with an exclusion process based on "compatibility". By defining compatibility as the absence of an interlock, and requiring a subset of three sensors where no pair is compatible, the model must deduce that all three pairs are interlocked (a triangle) rather than matching the standard clique definition. Removed explicit simple-graph constraints in the input to satisfy the gate's G3 feedback.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The condition "no two sensors chosen from these three form a compatible pair" mathematically requires all three pairs to be interlocked, which is exactly the 3-clique condition of the seed, preserving all answers.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding a triangle in an undirected graph, which remains solvable in O(nm/64) using bitsets.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might find the removal of "Interlocks are bidirectional" ambiguous, though "linked by" strongly implies an undirected relationship.
</RISK>
