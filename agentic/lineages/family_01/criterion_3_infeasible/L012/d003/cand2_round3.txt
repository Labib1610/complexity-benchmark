# Problem C — System Instability

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a distributed monitoring network consisting of $n$ sensors. The network contains $m$ hardware links between certain pairs of sensors.

For any two distinct sensors to be safely assigned to the same frequency band, they must meet at least one of two conditions:
1. They are *isolated* from each other, meaning there is no hardware link between them.
2. They are *shielded*, meaning they share a hardware link but operate on distinct power grids.

Currently, all sensors in the network are powered by a single, unified power grid, meaning no sensors are shielded. Therefore, the only way for two sensors to safely share a frequency band is if they are isolated.

The system has a critical flaw. You must find a specific combination: a subset of exactly three distinct sensors such that no two sensors within this subset can safely share a frequency band. Because none of the pairs in this subset are safe, it is impossible to validly assign these three sensors to the two available frequency bands.

It is guaranteed that the provided configuration contains at least one such subset of three sensors. Your task is to find and report one.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of sensors and the number of hardware links, respectively. Sensors are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that sensor $u$ and sensor $v$ are connected by a hardware link. Links are bidirectional. It is guaranteed that no pair is listed more than once, and that at least one such subset of three sensors exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three sensors that form the flaw. 

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid subsets of three sensors, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; if there are ties, minimize $b$ among those ties; and if there are still ties, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```
