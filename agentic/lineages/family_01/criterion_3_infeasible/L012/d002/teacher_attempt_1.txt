# Problem C — Hazardous Manifest

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing a logistics facility that recently received a manifest of $n$ hazardous materials. To qualify as a bulk shipment, the manifest always contains a minimum of three materials. The facility's receiving bay has a physical capacity that can hold at most two thousand materials at once. 

Certain pairs of these materials are highly reactive and will combust if stored in the same vicinity. The safety logs define $m$ such reactive pairs. The logs will always contain at least three entries. The legacy database system can store a maximum of two hundred thousand pair records, or the absolute maximum number of unique pairs possible for the $n$ materials, whichever is more restrictive.

The facility is equipped with exactly two isolated storage bunkers. Strict safety protocols require assigning every material to one of these two bunkers such that no two reactive materials are placed in the same bunker. However, the current manifest is fundamentally impossible to store under these conditions.

To justify rejecting the manifest to the safety inspector, you do not need to attempt a full assignment. Instead, you must isolate a specific structural contradiction: a sequence of three distinct materials where the first reacts with the second, the second reacts with the third, and the third reacts back with the first. Because each material in this configuration reacts with the other two, it is impossible to distribute them across only two bunkers without causing a combustion event.

It is guaranteed that the provided manifest contains at least one such configuration of three materials. Your task is to find and report one.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the number of hazardous materials and the number of reactive pairs, respectively. It is guaranteed that $n$ and $m$ respect the facility's physical limits and the database capacity described above. Materials are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$. These represent valid material identifiers (between $1$ and $n$ inclusive, with $u \neq v$), indicating that material $u$ and material $v$ are reactive. Reactivity is bidirectional. It is guaranteed that no pair is listed more than once, and that at least one such configuration of three materials exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three materials. 

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid configurations, you must output the one whose identifier triplet is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; if there are ties, minimize $b$ among those ties; and if there are still ties, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```
