<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the explicit structural definition (three mutually connected nodes) with its semantic consequence (a subset of three items that cannot be validly partitioned into two sets). This forces the model to logically deduce the Pigeonhole Principle mapping (3 items in 2 bins requires all 3 pairs to be constrained to guarantee a collision) before it can recognize the problem as triangle finding.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>

<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Processor Allocation Audit

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are auditing a task scheduler that distributes $n$ processes across two identical processors. The scheduler receives a list of $m$ mutual exclusion constraints. Each constraint specifies a pair of processes that cannot be assigned to the same processor due to resource conflicts.

The current system configuration is flawed, making it impossible to safely schedule all processes on just two processors. To demonstrate this flaw, you must extract a minimal failing subset consisting of exactly three processes. 

Specifically, you need to find a group of three distinct processes such that, if the scheduler were to assign only these three processes to the two available processors, every possible allocation would inevitably violate at least one of the listed exclusions between them.

It is guaranteed that the provided logs contain at least one such group of three processes. The constraint distribution can be highly skewed: in some configurations, the processes with the smallest identifiers will have a dense web of mutual exclusions that never form a failing group of three, while the only valid failing subset consists entirely of the largest identifiers. Your task is to find and report the correct subset.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of processes and the number of mutual exclusion constraints. Processes are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$), indicating a mutual exclusion constraint between process $u$ and process $v$. It is guaranteed that at least one such failing group of three processes exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three processes that form the failing subset. 

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid groups of three processes, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; if there are ties, minimize $b$ among those ties; and if there are still ties, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I replaced the explicit instruction to find three mutually excluded processes with the indirect requirement that "every possible allocation of these three processes to two processors violates an exclusion". This forces models to deduce that, by the Pigeonhole Principle, three processes fail on two processors if and only if all three pairwise constraints are present.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Three processes cannot be assigned to two processors without violating a constraint if and only if all three pairs of processes are mutually excluded, which is exactly the same condition as the original triangle-finding problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding the lexicographically first triangle in a graph, which maintains its complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
