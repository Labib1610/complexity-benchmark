# Problem C — System Instability

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a security system consisting of $n$ sensors. Certain pairs of sensors are linked by a hardware interlock. 

The system is designed to operate by assigning each sensor to one of two alternating active phases. A safety rule dictates that if two sensors are linked by an interlock, they must not be active in the same phase. However, the current configuration is fundamentally flawed. 

To demonstrate this flaw, you do not need to attempt a full assignment. Instead, you must find a specific structural failure: a subset of exactly three distinct sensors which, when considered in isolation, cannot possibly be assigned to the two phases without violating the safety rule.

It is guaranteed that the provided configuration contains at least one such subset of three sensors. Your task is to find and report one.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of sensors and the number of interlocks, respectively. Sensors are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that sensor $u$ and sensor $v$ are linked by an interlock. Interlocks are bidirectional. It is guaranteed that no pair is listed more than once, and that at least one structural failure of three sensors exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three sensors that form the structural failure. 

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid subsets of three sensors, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should first minimize $a$; if there are ties, minimize $b$ among those ties; and if there are still ties, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```

This forces the solver to deduce that the only 3-vertex configuration lacking a valid 2-coloring is a mutually connected triangle (K3), bridging the gap between the phase constraints and the required graph structure.
