<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Chemical Volatility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing safety protocols for a chemical storage facility. The facility houses a number of distinct chemical compounds. Certain pairs of these compounds are known to cause a volatile reaction if they come into contact.

The original safety plan was to divide all stored compounds into two isolated containment zones. A rule states that if two compounds react with each other, they must be assigned to different zones. However, the current inventory makes this two-zone division impossible.

To prove this impossibility, you must identify a specific hazardous configuration: a group of three distinct compounds that mutually prevent this two-zone assignment. This configuration consists of three distinct compounds where every compound in the group reacts with both of the other two.

You are guaranteed that the inventory contains at least one such group of three compounds. Your task is to find one.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The facility's capacity limits the number of compounds $n$ to at most two thousand, while the hazardous configuration requires at least three. The database of reactions $m$ contains at least three entries, and is limited by either a system maximum of two hundred thousand or the physical limit of all possible pairs among the $n$ compounds, whichever is smaller. Compounds are referenced by integer identifiers starting at $1$ up to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two identifiers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that the compound $u$ and the compound $v$ react upon contact. These reactions are symmetric. No pair of reacting compounds is listed more than once. It is guaranteed that at least one hazardous group of three compounds exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, representing the identifiers of the three compounds that form the hazardous group.

The identifiers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$). If there are multiple valid groups of three compounds, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. This means you should prioritize a smaller value for $a$; in case of ties for $a$, prioritize a smaller value for $b$; and if there are still ties, prioritize a smaller value for $c$.

## Example 1
**Input:**
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
**Input:**
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
**Output:**
```
1 2 4
```

The numeric bounds were stated indirectly through facility capacities and physical limits rather than raw inequalities, forcing the reader to derive the actual limits.
