# Spectrum Allocation Certificate

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are configuring a network of $n$ radio beacons, numbered 1 through $n$. To avoid signal jamming, certain pairs of beacons that are in close proximity must be assigned different transmission frequencies. You are given a list of $m$ such interfering pairs.

You only have two distinct frequencies available for the entire network. The given interference constraints make it impossible to assign a frequency to every beacon without causing a conflict. Your task is to generate a minimal proof of this impossibility to justify requesting a third frequency from the regulatory authority. 

Specifically, you must identify a group of exactly three beacons such that every beacon in the group interferes with both of the other two. Because only two frequencies are available, any such group guarantees that at least two of its beacons would be forced to share a frequency, violating the constraints. Output one such group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of beacons and the number of interfering pairs.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of beacons that interfere with each other.
It is guaranteed that at least one group of three mutually interfering beacons exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that all three pairs $a$–$b$, $a$–$c$, and $b$–$c$ interfere. Output the lexicographically smallest such group (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
