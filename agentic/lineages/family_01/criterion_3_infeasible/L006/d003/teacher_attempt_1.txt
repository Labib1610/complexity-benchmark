# Hazardous Cargo Certificate

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are managing the logistics for transporting a shipment of hazardous chemical containers, numbered sequentially starting from 1 up to $n$. You are provided with a manifest detailing $m$ combinations of two containers that will react catastrophically if stored in the same cargo hold. 

The shipment size, $n$, consists of a minimum of three containers, but never exceeds the vessel's absolute weight limit which corresponds to exactly 2000 standard containers. The number of listed combinations, $m$, is at least three, and will not exceed the administrative cap of 200,000 records (or the total possible pairs of containers in the shipment, whichever is more restrictive).

Your transport vessel currently has only two isolated cargo holds. The provided safety regulations make it impossible to load all the containers into just two holds without risking a reaction. Your task is to generate a minimal proof of this impossibility to justify requisitioning a vessel with a third cargo hold.

A valid proof consists of selecting exactly three containers such that placing any pair chosen from these three containers into the same hold violates the manifest. Because only two holds are available, any such selection guarantees that at least two of its containers would be forced to share a hold, proving that two holds are insufficient. Output one such trio.

## Input
The first line provides the shipment size $n$ and the manifest length $m$.
The subsequent $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, identifying a pair of containers that cannot share a hold.
It is guaranteed that at least one valid proof trio exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ representing the containers in your proof. If multiple valid trios exist, output the lexicographically smallest one (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
