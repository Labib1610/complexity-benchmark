# Spectrum Allocation Certificate

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are configuring a communication array consisting of $n$ radio beacons, which receive integer identifiers starting from 1 up to $n$. A region's infrastructure can support a maximum of two thousand beacons, and a minimum of three are required to establish an array.

To prevent signal degradation, certain pairs of beacons that are physically close are documented in a log of $m$ bidirectional interference events. The logging system has a capacity limit of two hundred thousand events, though it will never exceed the total number of distinct two-beacon combinations possible for the given $n$. The log is guaranteed to contain at least three events.

The entire array must operate using only two distinct frequencies. Because of the logged events, it is impossible to assign every beacon one of these two frequencies without causing a conflict. Your objective is to produce a minimal proof of this impossibility to secure a third frequency from the regulators.

Specifically, you need to pinpoint three beacons that form a mutually conflicting triad. This means that for any two beacons selected from your chosen three, there must be a corresponding interference event in the log. Such a triad proves that a two-frequency assignment is impossible, as two of the three would inevitably share a frequency. Output the identifiers of one such triad.

## Input
The first line provides the beacon count $n$ and the event count $m$.
The subsequent $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ — representing a logged interference event between beacon $u$ and beacon $v$.
It is guaranteed that at least one mutually conflicting triad exists in the log.

## Output
Produce three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$, representing the members of the triad. If multiple triads exist, provide the one that is lexicographically earliest (the lowest possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, the lowest possible value for $b$; and finally the lowest for $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
