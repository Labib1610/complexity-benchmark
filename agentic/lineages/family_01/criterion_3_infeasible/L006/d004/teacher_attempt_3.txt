# Stable Ternary Blend

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are formulating a new liquid solution from a catalog of $n$ distinct chemical compounds, identified by integers from 1 to $n$. You are provided with experimental data documenting $m$ specific pairs of compounds that are miscible (they mix perfectly without separating).

Your goal is to create a stable ternary blend, which must consist of exactly three distinct compounds. For the entire blend to remain stable, the three selected compounds must be completely mutually miscible. This means that within your chosen three compounds, every possible pair must be listed in the experimental data as miscible.

You must identify a valid combination of three compounds to form this blend. If multiple valid combinations exist, you must choose the one that is lexicographically first.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the total number of chemical compounds and the number of miscible pairs.
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that compound $u$ and compound $v$ are miscible.
It is guaranteed that at least one stable ternary blend can be formed.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$. These must represent three compounds where the pairs $a$–$b$, $a$–$c$, and $b$–$c$ are all present in the experimental data. If there are multiple valid blends, output the one with the smallest $a$; if there is a tie, the smallest $b$; and finally the smallest $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
