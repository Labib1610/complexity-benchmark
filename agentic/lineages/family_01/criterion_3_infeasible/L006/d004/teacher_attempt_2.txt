<STATEMENT>
# Stabilized Component Assembly

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

You are assembling a space frame from distinct structural struts, which are numbered consecutively starting from 1 up to the total inventory size $n$. These struts are connected using specialized joints. 

To construct a primary rigid node, you must select exactly three distinct struts. One strut acts as the foundation. A second strut is attached to the foundation's left socket, and a third strut is attached to the foundation's right socket. Finally, to stabilize the structure and prevent articulation, the remaining free ends of the second and third struts must be joined together. You can only make an attachment between two struts if their specific pair is listed in the approved testing log.

Your task is to identify a valid combination of three struts to form this stabilized assembly. 

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The inventory size $n$ is constrained by a minimum viable frame requirement of three struts and a maximum warehouse capacity of two thousand distinct strut types. The testing log contains $m$ entries; the testing facility guarantees a minimum of three records and a maximum throughput limit of two hundred thousand records (or the total possible combinations of the available struts, if that is lower).

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a pair of struts that have been tested and can be attached to each other.

It is guaranteed that the provided log contains at least one valid combination of three struts capable of forming the stabilized assembly.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, ordered such that $a < b < c$. These must be the identifiers of the three struts used in the stabilized assembly. If there are multiple valid combinations of three struts, output the combination that is lexicographically first (i.e., the combination with the smallest $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest $b$; and then the smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

This forces the model to deduce both the mathematical constraints of the problem (a 3-clique) and the exact bounds from the scenario context rather than relying on explicit keywords or standard mathematical notation.
</RATIONALE>
