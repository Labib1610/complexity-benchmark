# Problem C — Weekend Deployment Veto

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A software company is preparing to migrate $n$ legacy microservices, identified by IDs 1 through $n$. The architecture team has published a list of $m$ concurrent-execution restrictions. Each restriction specifies two microservices that will corrupt shared temporary tables if they are migrated in the same batch window.

Management has directed that the entire migration must be completed over a standard two-day weekend, which means allocating all microservices into exactly two batch windows. The lead architect knows this plan is mathematically doomed. The restrictions guarantee the existence of an unresolvable core: three microservices (let's call them $a$, $b$, and $c$) such that the published list includes a restriction between $a$ and $b$, a restriction between $b$ and $c$, and a restriction between $a$ and $c$. Since these three restrictions prevent any two of the three microservices from sharing a window, a two-window migration is impossible.

To secure approval for a longer migration schedule, you must submit a formal override by identifying three such microservices.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), representing a single restriction between microservice $u$ and microservice $v$. Each restriction is listed at most once.
It is guaranteed that at least one such unresolvable core of three microservices exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the IDs of the three microservices in the core. The IDs must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$).
If there are multiple valid groups of three, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. That is, minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The phrase "a trio of microservices where every microservice in the trio collides with both of the other two" was a strong semantic trigger for "clique" or "triangle," leading models to immediately deploy graph algorithms with bitset intersections. This was replaced with an enumerated list of pairwise conditions ("a restriction between $a$ and $b$, a restriction between $b$ and $c$, and a restriction between $a$ and $c$"), which forces models to recognize the structural requirement themselves rather than pattern-matching the description.
</RATIONALE>
