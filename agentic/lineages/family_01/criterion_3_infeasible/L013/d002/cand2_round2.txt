# Problem C — Transmission Lockup

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A heavy-duty transmission system is built from $n$ sprockets, labeled 1 through $n$. The assembly blueprint specifies $m$ engagement points, where the teeth of two distinct sprockets interlock. In this system, any two interlocking sprockets are mechanically forced to rotate in opposite directions.

During testing, the entire system is completely locked up and refuses to turn. The engineers have diagnosed a fundamental design flaw: there exists a group of exactly three sprockets configured such that every sprocket in the group interlocks directly with the other two. Because each sprocket must rotate in the opposite direction of any sprocket it interlocks with, this trio cannot move without tearing the teeth off the components.

To authorize a redesign, you must write a diagnostic tool to pinpoint such a locked trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the quantity of sprockets and the quantity of engagement points. The system requires at least 3 sprockets to exhibit this flaw, and the chassis capacity limits it to 2000 ($3 \le n \le 2000$). The number of engagement points is at least 3, and is bounded by both the theoretical maximum of $n(n-1)/2$ and the documentation limit of $2 \cdot 10^5$ ($3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).

The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating that sprocket $u$ and sprocket $v$ interlock. No interlocking pair is listed more than once.
It is guaranteed that the blueprint contains at least one locked trio.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the labels of the three sprockets that form a locked trio. The labels must be printed in strictly ascending order ($a < b < c$).
If the blueprint contains multiple locked trios, you must output the one that is lexicographically earliest. Specifically, you must minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
