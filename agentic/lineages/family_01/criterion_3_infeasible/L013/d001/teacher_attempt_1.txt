<STATEMENT>
# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing plant is undergoing a scheduling audit. The workforce consists of $n$ employees, assigned IDs from 1 to $n$. The HR department maintains a list of $m$ conflict reports. Each report specifies a pair of employees who must not be assigned to the same workstation.

The management wants to split the employees into exactly two distinct daily shifts. However, the audit has flagged that this is unworkable. The reason is the existence of a three-person deadlock: a situation where a first employee has a conflict with a second, the second has a conflict with a third, and the third has a conflict with the first. Because every pair within this specific trio cannot work together, they cannot be successfully distributed across only two shifts.

To finalize the rejection of the two-shift proposal, you must provide a formal certificate of its failure. Your task is to identify one of these three-person deadlocks.

## Input
The first line contains two integers, the workforce size $n$ and the number of conflict reports $m$. To maintain operations, the plant requires a minimum of three employees, while strict fire safety codes cap the building's maximum occupancy at exactly two thousand individuals. The HR reporting system requires a minimum of three reports to trigger an audit. The database is strictly capped at two hundred thousand entries, or one for every possible pair of employees, whichever is lower.

The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a conflict report between employee $u$ and employee $v$. Both IDs are valid employee numbers from 1 to $n$, and no report involves an employee conflicting with themselves. Each specific pair is reported at most once.

It is guaranteed that the HR records contain at least one three-person deadlock.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the IDs of three employees who form a deadlock. The IDs must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$).
If there are multiple such groups, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. That is, minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The direct mentions of "triads" and "mutually refuse" were removed and replaced with a "three-person deadlock" defined mechanically by pairwise chaining (first with second, second with third, third with first). This forces the model to synthesize the structural relationship rather than triggering a simple keyword match for a clique. Additionally, all bare inequalities for the constraints were replaced with scenario-derived bounds (e.g., minimum operations, fire safety codes, database limits), requiring the model to extract the numeric limits from the text.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The required output remains the lexicographically smallest set of three employees where all three possible pairs conflict, which is exactly identical to the original problem.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The core computational task is still finding a triangle ($K_3$) in an undirected graph, preserving the certificate-style infeasibility complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
