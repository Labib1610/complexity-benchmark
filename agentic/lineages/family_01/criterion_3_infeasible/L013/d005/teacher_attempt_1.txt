# Problem C — Habitat Allocation Veto

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A conservation park is preparing to house a collection of rescued animal species, which are assigned identification numbers sequentially starting from 1 up to the total headcount. The park's main registry system is designed with a hard limit of exactly 2000 species records. The registry will always contain a minimum of 3 species.

The veterinary team has compiled a logbook of species pairs that exhibit severe territorial aggression. If two species are documented together as a pair in this logbook, they are strictly forbidden from sharing the same habitat enclosure. The logbook has a physical capacity of 200000 pair entries, and it will never contain more entries than the maximum possible number of unique pairs for the registered species. There will always be at least 3 entries in the logbook.

The park management initially proposed constructing exactly two large enclosures to house all the species. However, this proposal is fundamentally unworkable. The behavioral data guarantees the existence of a specific structural flaw: a set of three species such that any attempt to distribute them across just two enclosures will inevitably force at least one documented aggressive pair to share an enclosure. This happens because each species in the set is documented in the logbook as aggressive toward both of the other two species in that same set.

To finalize the veto of the two-enclosure proposal, you must provide a formal certificate of its failure by identifying one such set of three species.

## Input
The first line contains two integers representing the total number of species and the total number of logbook entries, respectively. These values are guaranteed to respect the registry limits and logbook capacities described above.

The following lines, one for each logbook entry, each contain two valid species identification numbers representing a pair of species that are forbidden from sharing an enclosure. Each pair is listed at most once, and the two species in a pair will always be distinct.

It is guaranteed that at least one such set of three mutually aggressive species exists.

## Output
Print three integers on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the numbers of the three species in the set. The numbers must be printed in ascending order.

If there are multiple such sets, you must output the one that minimizes the first integer. If there is a tie, minimize the second integer, and if there is still a tie, minimize the third integer.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The explicit mention of "inherently requires at least three enclosures" was removed, as this framing acted as a strong trigger for recognizing the chromatic number implication (a triangle requires 3 colors). Instead, the structural flaw is described through its direct mechanics: a trio where every species is aggressive towards the other two, forcing the model to independently deduce that this pairwise relationship represents a 3-clique. Numeric bounds were also extracted from the input section and woven into the scenario description as physical constraints, adhering to the S07 strategy.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The required output remains the lexicographically smallest set of three mutually conflicting species, keeping the solution exactly the same.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

The problem still requires finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, maintaining its nature as a subgraph isomorphism / boolean satisfiability certificate task.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
