# Problem C — Habitat Allocation Veto

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A conservation park's registry tracks $n$ rescued animal species, numbered 1 through $n$. The registry requires a minimum of 3 species to be active, and its maximum capacity is exactly 2000 species.

The veterinary team has logged $m$ unique incompatibility reports, each indicating that two specific species cannot cohabitate. The database contains at least 3 such reports, and its storage is strictly capped at a maximum of 200,000 entries (or the total number of possible distinct pairs of active species, whichever is smaller).

The park management initially proposed separating all species into exactly two large enclosures. This proposal was vetoed. The veto was triggered because the database contains at least one unresolvable trio: a group of three distinct species such that the first is incompatible with the second, the second is incompatible with the third, and the third is incompatible with the first. Whenever this overlapping pattern occurs, it is impossible to divide the three species into two enclosures without placing an incompatible pair together.

To formalize the veto, you must provide a certificate of failure by identifying one such trio.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. Their values respect the registry and database limits described above.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$. These represent an incompatibility report between species $u$ and species $v$. Both $u$ and $v$ will always be valid, distinct species identifiers from the registry. Each pair of species is listed at most once.
It is guaranteed that the database contains at least one unresolvable trio.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the numbers of the three species in the unresolvable trio. The numbers must be printed in ascending order, from smallest to largest identifier.
If there are multiple such trios, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. That is, minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
