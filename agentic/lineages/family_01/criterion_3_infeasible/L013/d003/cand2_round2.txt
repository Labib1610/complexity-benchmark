# Problem C — Mechanical Jam

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A clockwork mechanism consists of $n$ cogs, numbered 1 through $n$. The design specifies $m$ direct mechanical links. Whenever two specific cogs are linked, their teeth interlock, forcing them to rotate in opposite directions.

The designers intended for the entire mechanism to rotate continuously. However, the prototype is completely seized. An inspection reveals a fatal structural flaw: a localized parity conflict. Specifically, there exists at least one group of three distinct cogs such that any two cogs chosen from this group are directly linked to each other. Because every pair within this trio is interlocked, each cog must rotate in the opposite direction of the other two simultaneously, making rotation physically impossible.

To authorize a redesign, the lead engineer requires a formal defect report identifying the components involved in one of these minimal conflicting groups. Your task is to find three cogs that form this exact configuration.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, bounded by the minimum jam size and the rig capacity ($3 \le n \le 2000$), and the lesser of the theoretical maximum links or the logging limit ($3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), representing that cog $u$ and cog $v$ interlock. Each pair is listed at most once.
It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three cogs exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the IDs of three cogs that form the described group. The IDs must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$).
If there are multiple such combinations, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. That is, minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The seed problem explicitly described the structure as a loop: "first interlocks with second, second with third, third back with first". This immediately triggers pattern matching for a 3-cycle. The rewrite replaces this sequential description with a subset description: "any two cogs chosen from this group are directly linked" (a 3-clique). This forces the model to deduce that a 3-clique in an undirected graph is structurally identical to a 3-cycle. The bounds are justified through the scenario while preserving the exact numeric inequalities to satisfy strict validation.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The structural requirement of three mutually interlocking cogs is mathematically identical to a 3-cycle, meaning the exact same sets of components will be identified and the lexicographical tie-breaker applies identically.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
