# Problem C — Habitat Allocation Veto

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A wildlife sanctuary plans to integrate $n$ rescued animal species, numbered 1 to $n$, into new environments. Behavioral specialists have documented $m$ unordered pairs of species that display intense territorial hostility. Hostile pairs cannot share the same living space. 

The sanctuary directors originally planned to build exactly two expansive habitats for the entire population. However, this plan is structurally flawed. The hostility records are guaranteed to contain at least one trio of species in which every species is hostile to the other two in the trio. Consequently, no two-habitat configuration can ever separate them; at least two members of any such trio would be forced together.

To formally reject the two-habitat plan, you must submit a counterexample demonstrating its impossibility. Your objective is to pinpoint a trio of mutually hostile species.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n(n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \ne v$), indicating mutual hostility between species $u$ and $v$. A hostile relationship is documented at most once.
The data is guaranteed to contain at least one mutually hostile trio.

## Output
Output a single line with three space-separated integers representing the species identifiers of a mutually hostile trio. These identifiers must be written in ascending order.
If more than one such trio exists, evaluate each valid trio by sorting its members as $S_1 < S_2 < S_3$ and calculating its penalty score using the formula $S_1 \cdot (n+1)^2 + S_2 \cdot (n+1) + S_3$. You must output the trio that produces the absolute lowest penalty score.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
Removed the explicit terms "lexicographically smallest" and "minimize a, then b". Replaced them with a mathematically equivalent penalty score formula evaluated at base $(n+1)$. This forces the model to deduce that the penalty score establishes a strict lexicographical ordering before it can safely apply the shortcut of iterating vertices in ascending order.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
Because $S_i \le n$, evaluating the polynomial at base $(n+1)$ creates a strict positional number system where $S_1$ dominates $S_2$, and $S_2$ dominates $S_3$, which is precisely equivalent to lexicographical comparison.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
