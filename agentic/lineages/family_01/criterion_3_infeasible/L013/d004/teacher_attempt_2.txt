# Problem C — Habitat Allocation Veto

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A conservation park is preparing to house several rescued animal species, numbered sequentially starting from 1. The veterinary team has compiled a list of specific pairs of species that exhibit severe territorial aggression. If two species appear together as a pair on this list, they cannot be housed in the same habitat enclosure.

To finalize the park's safety protocols, you must identify a critical group. A critical group consists of three distinct species (let's call them $X$, $Y$, and $Z$) where the veterinary list contains an aggression record for $X$ and $Y$, a record for $Y$ and $Z$, and a record for $X$ and $Z$. 

You must provide a formal certificate of this critical group by identifying its three species.

## Input
The first line contains two integers: the total headcount of species $n$ and the number of documented aggression records $m$. The headcount $n$ is at least 3, and the park's facility design imposes a maximum capacity of exactly two thousand species. The record count $m$ is at least 3, and is limited by either a database cap of two hundred thousand or the maximum possible number of unique pairs among the $n$ species, whichever is smaller.

The following $m$ lines each contain two distinct integers $u$ and $v$. These represent valid species numbers (from 1 up to the headcount $n$) that cannot be housed together. Each pair is listed at most once.
It is guaranteed that at least one such critical group of three species exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the numbers of the three species in the critical group. The numbers must be printed in ascending order ($a < b < c$).
If there are multiple such groups, you must output the one that is lexicographically smallest. That is, minimize $a$; if there is a tie, minimize $b$; and if there is still a tie, minimize $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
