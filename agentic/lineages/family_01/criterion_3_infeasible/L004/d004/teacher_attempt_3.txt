# Environment Allocation

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A system administrator is trying to partition $n$ software modules (numbered 1 through $n$) into two isolated virtual environments. The system's compatibility registry contains $m$ records of module pairs. If both modules from a record are placed in the same environment, they will trigger a critical system panic.

The hardware architecture limits the maximum installation to exactly two thousand modules, though at least three are required to operate. The registry has a hard storage capacity of two hundred thousand records, starting with a minimum of three records, and will naturally never exceed the theoretical maximum number of unique module pairs for the given $n$.

The administrator needs to formally justify provisioning a third environment by demonstrating that a two-environment deployment is impossible. You must provide a minimal configuration that proves this: select three distinct modules, let us call them $A$, $B$, and $C$. To guarantee a panic in any two-environment setup, placing $A$ and $B$ in different environments must leave no safe option for $C$. This occurs when the registry explicitly forbids $C$ from sharing an environment with $A$, forbids $C$ from sharing with $B$, and also forbids $A$ from sharing with $B$.

## Input
The first line provides the two integers $n$ and $m$.
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a pair of modules that cannot share an environment. 
It is guaranteed that the registry contains at least one such group of three modules.

## Output
Output the three integer identifiers of your chosen group on a single line, ordered from lowest to highest. 
If more than one such group exists, evaluate them by their sorted identifiers. Choose the group that minimizes the first (lowest) identifier. If there is a tie, choose the one that minimizes the second (middle) identifier. If a tie still remains, minimize the third (highest) identifier.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```
