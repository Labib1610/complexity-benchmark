# Portfolio Risk Limits

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A quantitative hedge fund is evaluating a universe of $n$ tradable assets (numbered 1 through $n$). The risk management system has flagged $m$ pairs of assets as being highly co-dependent. 

The fund's risk parameters mandate that the active assets must be partitioned into a primary and a secondary portfolio. A valid partition must ensure that no two co-dependent assets end up in the same portfolio.

The chief risk officer wants to block the current trading universe by proving that a safe allocation is impossible. You must provide a minimal counterexample: a set of exactly three distinct assets that are mutually incompatible with one another. Because every asset in this trio is co-dependent with both of the other two, they inherently cannot be safely divided between just two portfolios without triggering a volatility breach.

## Input
The first line provides two integers $n$ and $m$. 
The number of assets $n$ is at least 3, and at most the total capacity of forty database pages that hold exactly fifty assets each. 
The number of co-dependent pairs $m$ is at least 3. It will not exceed the total number of possible unique pairs among the $n$ assets, nor will it exceed a hard system limit of exactly two hundred blocks of one thousand flags each.
Each of the subsequent $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$, representing a pair of assets that are highly co-dependent. 
It is guaranteed that the universe contains at least one such trio of assets.

## Output
Output the three integer identifiers of your chosen set on a single line, ordered from lowest to highest. 
If more than one such set exists, evaluate them by their sorted identifiers. Choose the set that minimizes the first (lowest) identifier. If there is a tie, choose the one that minimizes the second (middle) identifier. If a tie still remains, minimize the third (highest) identifier.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```

Changed the explanation of the impossibility condition to focus on the mutual incompatibility of the trio (each being co-dependent with the other two) rather than emphasizing the permutations of assigning two strategies to three assets. This removes the pigeonhole-principle framing that models rely on for surface pattern matching, forcing them to recognize the structural requirement directly. Numeric bounds were also replaced with physical limits (database pages and blocks) to satisfy the indirect bounds strategy.
</RATIONALE>
