We need to find a triangle in the graph, and among all triangles, output the one with the smallest lexicographical order of its vertices.

A direct approach:  
- Store adjacency as sets for fast lookup.  
- Iterate over all triples `(i, j, k)` with `i < j < k` and check if all three edges exist.  
- Since `n` can be up to 2000, checking all triples naively is `O(n^3)` which is too slow (8e9 operations).  

We can do better:  
- For each edge `(u, v)` with `u < v`, check if there exists a vertex `w` such that `w > v` and both `(u, w)` and `(v, w)` are edges.  
- If found, that gives a triangle `(u, v, w)`.  
- Since we iterate edges in order of `u` then `v`, the first triangle found will be lexicographically smallest.  

Complexity:  
- For each edge, we check intersection of adjacency sets of `u` and `v` for vertices greater than `v`.  
- Using sets, intersection is fast.  
- Worst-case: `O(m * n)` but with sets it's usually much faster.  

Here’s the implementation:

```python
n, m = map(int, input().split())
adj = [set() for _ in range(n)]
edges = []

for _ in range(m):
    u, v = map(int, input().split())
    u -= 1
    v -= 1
    adj[u].add(v)
    adj[v].add(u)
    if u > v:
        u, v = v, u
    edges.append((u, v))

edges.sort()

for u, v in edges:
    # find w > v such that w in adj[u] and w in adj[v]
    common = adj[u] & adj[v]
    for w in sorted(common):
        if w > v:
            print(u + 1, v + 1, w + 1)
            exit()
```
