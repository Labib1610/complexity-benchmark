<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
Instead of describing the required relational structure (a clique of size 3) through mutual connections or impossibility conditions (which models easily map to odd cycles or non-2-colorability), the requirement is framed as an exact integer density condition on the induced subgraph (exactly 3 internal connections within a subset of size 3). Models relying on surface pattern matching will blindly translate this into an O(n^3) subset enumeration and fail via Time Limit Exceeded, whereas models that genuinely reason will deduce that the density condition mathematically necessitates a triangle and deploy the optimal O(m√m) or bitset intersection algorithm.

<STATEMENT>
# Structural Isolation

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A proposed structural framework consists of a set of load-bearing pillars. The pillars are identified by consecutive integers starting from 1 up to the total number of pillars. The design requires at least 3 pillars, and the foundation's maximum capacity limits the total to exactly 2000 pillars.

To provide stability, certain pairs of pillars are connected by bidirectional steel tie-rods. The number of tie-rods is at least 3, and the inventory system can track up to a maximum of exactly two hundred thousand tie-rods (or the maximum possible number of pairs, whichever is smaller). 

The framework's current configuration contains a critical structural loop. Your objective is to isolate the root cause of this loop. Specifically, you must locate a subset of exactly three pillars that form a "fully locked" configuration. 

We define the *internal reinforcement score* of a subset of pillars as the number of unique, unordered pairs of pillars $\{u, v\}$ such that both $u$ and $v$ belong to the subset, and there is a tie-rod connecting $u$ and $v$. 

A subset of exactly three pillars is considered "fully locked" if and only if its internal reinforcement score is exactly 3.

It is guaranteed that at least one fully locked subset of three pillars is present in the framework.

## Input
The first line contains the total number of pillars and the total number of tie-rods.
Each of the subsequent lines describes a single tie-rod by listing the integer identifiers of the two pillars it connects.
At least one fully locked subset of exactly three pillars is guaranteed to exist.

## Output
Provide the identifiers of the three pillars forming the fully locked subset, printed on a single line in ascending numerical order.
If there are multiple fully locked subsets, you must output the one whose identifiers, when sorted in ascending order, form the lexicographically smallest sequence.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The problem was rewritten to describe the triangle condition as an exact edge-density requirement on a subset of three nodes, rather than an impossibility of 2-coloring or mutual interference. This "Combinatorial Density Obfuscation" forces the models to either genuinely recognize the mathematical equivalence to a triangle (and use efficient set intersections) or fall back to blindly enumerating O(n^3) subsets, which will result in a Time Limit Exceeded error. Numeric bounds were also embedded into the scenario text (S07) to prevent surface-level pattern matching on standard graph constraints.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
A subset of exactly three pillars with an internal reinforcement score of 3 means all three possible pairs within the subset are connected, which is mathematically identical to a triangle, ensuring the exact same subsets are valid and the lexicographical tie-breaker yields the same result.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem remains exactly the task of finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, preserving its computational complexity and intended optimal algorithms.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "unique, unordered pairs of pillars" if they fail to understand basic set theory, but the phrasing is mathematically precise and standard, leaving no genuine ambiguity.
</RISK>
