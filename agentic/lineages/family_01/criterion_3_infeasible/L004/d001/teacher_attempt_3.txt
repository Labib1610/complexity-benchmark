# Market Manipulation

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A cryptocurrency exchange is attempting to segregate $n$ automated trading accounts (numbered 1 to $n$) into two distinct trading pools. However, forensic analysis has flagged $m$ specific pairs of accounts that have engaged in reciprocal "wash trades." Regulatory rules dictate that any two accounts involved in a wash trade together cannot be assigned to the same pool.

The exchange's separation plan is known to be fundamentally impossible to execute. Your task is to provide a certificate of this impossibility. You must identify a set of three accounts such that every possible pair among the three has engaged in a wash trade. Any such group of three mutually conflicting accounts clearly demonstrates that two pools are insufficient. It is guaranteed that at least one such set of three accounts exists in the forensic data.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a pair of accounts that have executed a wash trade with each other. 
It is guaranteed that at least one set of three mutually conflicting accounts exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three accounts, such that all three pairs ($a$ and $b$, $a$ and $c$, and $b$ and $c$) have wash traded. 
Output the lexicographically smallest such set (smallest $a$; in case of a tie, smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```
