# Critical Interference

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A proposed communications standard attempts to allocate $n$ transceivers (labeled 1 through $n$) into a network. However, testing has revealed $m$ specific bidirectional interference links between pairs of transceivers. Each link connects two distinct transceivers, and no two links connect the same pair.

To diagnose the core issue in the network's topology, engineers analyze the resonance of transceiver triplets. For any chosen subset of exactly three transceivers $U$, we consider it *critically flawed* if it is possible to assign a real-valued tuning weight $w_v$ to each transceiver $v \in U$ such that both of the following conditions hold:
1. Not all transceivers in $U$ are assigned a weight of zero (i.e., at least one $w_v \neq 0$).
2. For every transceiver $v \in U$, the sum of the tuning weights of its neighbors *strictly within $U$* is exactly equal to $2 \cdot w_v$. (A transceiver $u \in U$ is a neighbor of $v$ if there is an interference link between them).

Your objective is to isolate a subset of exactly three transceivers $U$ that is critically flawed. The existence of such a subset proves a severe vulnerability in the network. It is guaranteed that at least one such subset is present.

## Input
The first line contains the transceiver count $n$ and the interference link count $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
The following $m$ lines each detail a pair of transceivers $u$ and $v$ that share a mutual interference link.
At least one critically flawed subset of exactly three transceivers is guaranteed to exist.

## Output
Provide the labels of the three transceivers forming the subset, printed on a single line in ascending numerical order.
In the event that several such subsets exist, you must resolve the ambiguity by selecting the subset that includes the transceiver with the absolute lowest label. Should multiple subsets share this lowest-labeled transceiver, further narrow your selection to those whose second-lowest label is as small as possible. If ambiguity still persists, choose the subset whose highest label is minimal.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```
