We need to find a triplet of vertices such that the trace of \( Z^5 \) equals 3, where \( Z \) is the 3×3 matrix with 0 for edges (interference) and 1 for non-edges (including diagonal).

Let’s analyze the condition.

For a triplet \( (a,b,c) \), the matrix \( Z \) is:

\[
Z = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & x_{ab} & x_{ac} \\
x_{ab} & 1 & x_{bc} \\
x_{ac} & x_{bc} & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where \( x_{uv} = 0 \) if edge exists, else 1.

We need \(\text{tr}(Z^5) = 3\).

Since \( Z \) is symmetric with 1s on diagonal, we can compute the trace of powers in terms of the number of edges in the triplet.

Let’s denote:
- \( e \) = number of edges among the 3 vertices (0, 1, 2, or 3).

We can compute \( Z^5 \) trace for each case.

**Case e=0**: All off-diagonal are 1.  
\( Z = J \) (all ones).  
\( J^2 = 3J \), \( J^3 = 9J \), \( J^4 = 27J \), \( J^5 = 81J \).  
Trace = \( 3 \times 81 = 243 \).

**Case e=1**: One edge (0), two non-edges (1).  
Let’s label:  
\( Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \).  
We can compute trace of \( Z^5 \) by diagonalization or direct multiplication.  
Let’s do direct:  
\( Z^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \).  
\( Z^3 = Z^2 Z = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 3 & 5 \\ 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 5 & 5 & 7 \end{pmatrix} \).  
\( Z^4 = Z^3 Z = \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 8 & 12 \\ 8 & 9 & 12 \\ 12 & 12 & 17 \end{pmatrix} \).  
\( Z^5 = Z^4 Z = \begin{pmatrix} 21 & 20 & 29 \\ 20 & 21 & 29 \\ 29 & 29 & 41 \end{pmatrix} \).  
Trace = \( 21+21+41 = 83 \).

**Case e=2**: Two edges, one non-edge.  
\( Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \) with one off-diagonal 1? Wait, two edges means two zeros, one one.  
Example: edges (1,2) and (1,3), non-edge (2,3):  
\( Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \).  
Compute:  
\( Z^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \).  
\( Z^3 = Z^2 Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 & 4 \\ 0 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \).  
\( Z^4 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 8 & 8 \\ 0 & 8 & 8 \end{pmatrix} \).  
\( Z^5 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 16 & 16 \\ 0 & 16 & 16 \end{pmatrix} \).  
Trace = \( 1+16+16 = 33 \).

**Case e=3**: All edges.  
\( Z = I \) (identity).  
\( Z^5 = I \), trace = 3.

So the condition \(\text{tr}(Z^5)=3\) happens **only when the triplet is a triangle** (all three edges present).

Thus the problem reduces to: find the lexicographically smallest triangle in the graph.

We have \( n \le 2000 \), \( m \le 2\cdot 10^5 \). We can iterate over vertices in increasing order, and for each vertex, check pairs of its neighbors to see if they are connected.

We need the smallest triangle by (first, second, third) labels.

Algorithm:
- Store adjacency as sets for O(1) edge check.
- For each \( u \) from 1 to n:
  - For each pair of neighbors \( v, w \) with \( v < w \):
    - If edge \( v-w \) exists, we found a triangle.
    - Since we iterate \( u \) increasing, and for each \( u \) we check neighbors in increasing order, the first found is lexicographically smallest.
- Output.

Complexity: For each vertex, checking all pairs of neighbors could be O(deg^2). Worst-case deg ~ n, so O(n^3) too big. But we can break early: we only need the smallest triangle, so we can stop as soon as we find one.

We can also optimize: For each \( u \), sort its neighbors, then for each \( v \) in neighbors, check if any \( w \) in neighbors with \( w > v \) is also neighbor of \( v \). We can do this by iterating over smaller of the two neighbor lists.

Given constraints, a simple O(n * m) might be okay if we break early. But worst-case could be large. However, since we only need the first triangle, we can do:

For u from 1 to n:
  For each v in adj[u] with v > u:
    For each w in adj[u] with w > v:
      if w in adj[v]: found.

This is O(sum deg(u)^2) worst-case, but with m=2e5, average deg ~ 200, so sum deg^2 ~ 2000*40000 = 8e7, which is okay in Python if optimized.

We can also use bitsets for speed, but not necessary.

Let's implement.

```python
import sys

def main():
    input = sys.stdin.readline
    n, m = map(int, input().split())
    adj = [set() for _ in range(n+1)]
    for _ in range(m):
        u, v = map(int, input().split())
        adj[u].add(v)
        adj[v].add(u)
    
    for u in range(1, n+1):
        # neighbors sorted
        neigh = sorted(adj[u])
        for i in range(len(neigh)):
            v = neigh[i]
            if v <= u:
                continue
            for j in range(i+1, len(neigh)):
                w = neigh[j]
                if w <= v:
                    continue
                if w in adj[v]:
                    print(u, v, w)
                    return

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```
