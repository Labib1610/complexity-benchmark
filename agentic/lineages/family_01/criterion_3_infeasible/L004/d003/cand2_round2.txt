# Frequency Conflict Certification

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A frequency management system is configuring $n$ radio towers (indexed 1 through $n$) to operate on one of two available frequency bands. However, a site survey has identified $m$ specific pairs of towers where simultaneous transmission causes severe interference.

The system's configuration requirements are fundamentally impossible to fulfill. Your task is to certify this impossibility. You must identify a group of three distinct towers such that all three towers mutually interfere with each other (i.e., an interference condition exists between every pair of towers in the group).

Any such group of three towers demonstrates that two frequency bands are insufficient. It is guaranteed that at least one such group exists.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — representing an interference condition between tower $u$ and tower $v$.
It is guaranteed that at least one group of three mutually interfering towers exists.

## Output
Print three integers on one line, representing the identifiers of the three towers that form the mutually interfering group. The three identifiers must be printed in ascending order.

When more than one valid triplet of towers is found, you must break ties by selecting the triplet that contains the absolute lowest tower identifier among all valid triplets. Should multiple triplets share this lowest identifier, break the remaining tie by comparing the middle identifiers (when sorted ascendingly) and choosing the lowest one. If a tie still remains, choose the triplet with the lowest highest identifier.

## Example 1
### Input
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
### Output
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
### Input
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
### Output
```
1 2 4
```
