# Production Line Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility operates two parallel assembly tracks. Certain pairs of manufacturing steps share a delicate calibration instrument. Because the instrument cannot be in two places at once, these specific pairs of steps must never run on the same assembly track. An automated scheduler has just reported a complete allocation failure: it is impossible to distribute the current workload across the two tracks without triggering at least one instrument conflict.

To isolate the root cause, your diagnostic tool must pinpoint a triad of three distinct steps that mutually conflict. Specifically, you must find three steps such that any two of them share a calibration instrument. Because none of the three can share a track with any other in the triad, and there are only two tracks, they form an inherent bottleneck that guarantees an allocation failure.

To maintain deterministic logs, if there are multiple such triads, you must report the one with the lowest step identifiers. When comparing two triads, sort their step identifiers in ascending order: the triad with the smaller first identifier is chosen; if they tie, compare the second identifiers, and finally the third.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The number of steps $n$ ranges from a minimum workflow of 3 up to a maximum facility capacity of 2000. The number of collision pairs $m$ ranges from 3 up to the smaller of the theoretical maximum pairs ($n \cdot (n-1)/2$) and the database limit of $2 \cdot 10^5$. Step identifiers are numbered from 1 to $n$.
Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ (between 1 and $n$ inclusive, with $u \neq v$), indicating that step $u$ and step $v$ share an instrument and cannot be assigned to the same track.
It is guaranteed that at least one mutually conflicting triad of three steps exists in the provided data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, representing the identifiers of the three steps in the triad. The output must be sorted such that $a < b < c$. Output the triad that is sequentially lowest (the smallest possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest possible value for $b$; and finally the smallest possible value for $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
