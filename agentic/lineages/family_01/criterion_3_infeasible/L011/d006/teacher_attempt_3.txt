<STATEMENT>
# Gearbox Jam Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A mechanical clockwork system contains $n$ distinct cogwheels. For the system to operate, rotational energy is transferred between cogwheels that are directly meshed together. By the laws of mechanics, any two cogwheels that are directly meshed must rotate in strictly opposite directions (one clockwise, the other counter-clockwise).

Currently, the clockwork is completely jammed and cannot turn. A mechanical analysis reveals that the failure is caused by a structural lock. A structural lock consists of exactly three cogwheels configured such that every possible pair among these three is directly meshed. Because all three are mutually interlocked, it is impossible for them to rotate in consistent alternating directions, causing the entire assembly to freeze.

Your task is to identify the three cogwheels that form a structural lock. If there are multiple such locks in the system, you must identify the one with the minimum mechanical trace. For a lock consisting of cogwheel IDs $a, b,$ and $c$ (ordered such that $a < b < c$), the mechanical trace is calculated as $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of cogwheels and the number of meshed pairs. The cogwheels are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that cogwheel $u$ and cogwheel $v$ are directly meshed together. 

The input data is guaranteed to contain at least one structural lock.

## Output
Output a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ ($a < b < c$) representing the IDs of the three cogwheels forming the lock. You must output the group that produces the minimum mechanical trace as defined above.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The scenario was systematically replaced from biological compartments (which hints at 2-coloring/bipartite graphs) to mechanical cogwheels that must rotate in alternating directions. This preserves the structural impossibility of a 3-cycle (triangle) while removing any standard graph terminology like "subsets" or "adjacency", forcing the model to deduce from physical constraints that "mutually interlocked pairs" requires finding a 3-clique.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The problem asks for identical mathematical conditions (a set of 3 elements where all 3 pairs are given in the input) and uses the exact same tie-breaking formula, ensuring the same output for any input.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The core computational task remains finding a 3-clique (triangle) in an undirected graph, which is exactly the same complexity class as the seed.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "every possible pair among these three is directly meshed" as a requirement for the whole system rather than just the subset of three, but the phrasing "A structural lock consists of exactly three cogwheels configured such that..." is highly specific and should prevent this.
</RISK>
