<STATEMENT>
# Engineered Protein Aggregation Triads

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A synthetic biology lab is designing a cell with various distinct engineered proteins. The cell has exactly two isolated compartments for these proteins: the cytosol and the nucleus. Certain pairs of proteins exhibit strong binding affinity and will form inactive aggregates if placed in the same compartment. Therefore, any two proteins with a binding affinity must be isolated from one another.

Currently, the cell design is unviable. Analysis shows that the failure is caused by an aggregation triad. An aggregation triad involves three specific proteins that form a complete interaction loop: the first protein binds to the second, the second binds to the third, and the third binds back to the first. Because of these three simultaneous interactions, they cannot be distributed across only two compartments without triggering aggregation.

Your task is to identify the three proteins that constitute an aggregation triad. If multiple such triads exist, you must identify the one with the minimum sequence trace. For a triad consisting of protein IDs $a, b,$ and $c$ (ordered such that $a < b < c$), the sequence trace is calculated as $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The integer $n$ represents the number of engineered proteins. The lab's reactor requires a minimum of three proteins to function, while its physical volume limits the maximum number of proteins to exactly two thousand. The integer $m$ represents the number of known binding affinities between these proteins. The testing apparatus registers a minimum of three affinities, and its memory bank can store a maximum of two hundred thousand affinity records (or the total possible unique pairs for the given $n$, whichever is smaller). The proteins are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ (where $u$ and $v$ are distinct integers between $1$ and $n$ inclusive), indicating that protein $u$ and protein $v$ have a binding affinity. 

The input data is guaranteed to contain at least one aggregation triad.

## Output
Output a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ ($a < b < c$) representing the IDs of the three proteins forming the triad. You must output the group that produces the minimum sequence trace as defined above.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The description of the 3-clique was changed from "every possible pair chosen from these three" to "a complete interaction loop: the first protein binds to the second, the second binds to the third, and the third binds back to the first." This removes the explicit subset-pairing terminology that strongly hints at a clique, forcing the model to recognize that a 3-step loop in an undirected constraint graph is equivalent to a triangle. Additionally, the bare numerical inequalities for bounds were replaced with physical scenario constraints (e.g., reactor limits and memory banks) that exactly encode the same limits.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The criteria for selecting the three proteins (they all mutually bind) and the tie-breaking metric (the sequence trace formula evaluated on sorted IDs) are mathematically identical to the seed problem, ensuring the exact same output for any input.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still asks to find a triangle (a 3-clique) in an undirected graph, which preserves the O(n * m / 64) boolean adjacency or adjacency-intersection complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "first binds to second, second to third..." as implying the binding affinities are directed rather than symmetric, though the statement "protein u and protein v have a binding affinity" clarifies that the relationship is mutual.
</RISK>
