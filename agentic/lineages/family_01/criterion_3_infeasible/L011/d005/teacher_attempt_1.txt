<STATEMENT>
# Synthetic Cell Compartmentalization

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A synthetic biology lab is designing a cell with $n$ distinct engineered proteins. The cell has exactly two isolated compartments for these proteins: the cytosol and the nucleus. Certain pairs of proteins exhibit strong binding affinity and will form inactive aggregates if placed in the same compartment. Therefore, any two proteins with a binding affinity must be isolated from one another.

Currently, the cell design is unviable. Analysis shows that the failure is caused by a structural deadlock. A deadlock is a subset of exactly three proteins in which no two proteins can safely occupy the same compartment. Because every possible pair chosen from these three proteins possesses a binding affinity, it is impossible to distribute them across only two compartments without triggering aggregation.

Your task is to identify the three proteins that constitute a structural deadlock. If multiple such deadlocks exist, you must identify the one with the minimum sequence trace. For a deadlock consisting of protein IDs $a, b,$ and $c$ (ordered such that $a < b < c$), the sequence trace is calculated as $a \cdot 10^8 + b \cdot 10^4 + c$.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of engineered proteins and the number of known binding affinities. The proteins are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that protein $u$ and protein $v$ have a binding affinity. 

The input data is guaranteed to contain at least one structural deadlock.

## Output
Output a single line containing three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ ($a < b < c$) representing the IDs of the three proteins forming the deadlock. You must output the group that produces the minimum sequence trace as defined above.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
I removed the "tertiary aggregation knot" terminology and the explicit description of "a pair of binding proteins both exhibit an affinity for the exact same third protein". Instead, the problem defines a "structural deadlock" as a set of three proteins where no two can safely occupy the same compartment (i.e., every pair has a binding affinity). This forces the model to deduce the pairwise connectivity constraint from the abstract compatibility rule rather than relying on the evocative "common third protein" phrasing that trivially maps to `adj[u] & adj[v]`.
</RATIONALE>
