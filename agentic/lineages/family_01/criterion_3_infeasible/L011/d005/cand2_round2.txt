# Production Line Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility operates continuously using exactly two production lines. Certain pairs of manufacturing steps require a shared specialized calibration tool. A tool conflict occurs if both steps of such a pair are assigned to the same production line. Currently, the entire batch of manufacturing steps cannot be distributed across the two lines without causing at least one tool conflict.

Your diagnostic software must isolate a root cause of this allocation failure by identifying a specific group of exactly three steps. 

For any manufacturing step $k$, let $D_k$ be the total number of tool conflict pairs that involve step $k$. A subset of exactly three distinct steps $\{a, b, c\}$ is considered an unresolvable bottleneck if the number of tool conflict pairs that involve exactly one step from this subset is equal to $(D_a + D_b + D_c - 6)$. 

To ensure deterministic logging, if multiple such subsets of three steps exist, the system must output the one whose step identifiers are sequentially lowest.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the total number of manufacturing steps and the number of tool conflict pairs. Steps are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that step $u$ and step $v$ require a shared calibration tool.

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three steps exists in the provided data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three steps. Output the combination that is sequentially lowest (the smallest possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest possible value for $b$; and finally the smallest possible value for $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<RATIONALE>
The direct description of the bottleneck (which trivially reduced to finding a triangle) was replaced with a mathematical identity. A subset of 3 vertices forms a triangle if and only if the number of edges leaving the subset exactly equals the sum of the vertices' degrees minus 6. To pass within the time limit, models must algebraically simplify the condition and deduce that it strictly requires the three steps to be mutually conflicting, forcing genuine mathematical reasoning rather than pattern-matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The mathematical condition `E_boundary = D_a + D_b + D_c - 6` is strictly equivalent to the subset having exactly 3 internal edges, meaning the exact same triangles are identified and the tie-breaking rules remain identical.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>

</PRESERVED_CLASS>
