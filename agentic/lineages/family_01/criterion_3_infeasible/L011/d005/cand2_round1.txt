<STATEMENT>
# Production Line Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility operates continuously using exactly two production lines. Certain pairs of manufacturing steps require a shared specialized calibration tool. A tool collision occurs if both steps of such a pair are assigned to the same production line. Currently, the entire batch of manufacturing steps cannot be distributed across the two lines without causing at least one tool collision.

Your diagnostic software must isolate a root cause of this allocation failure by identifying a group of exactly three steps that form an unresolvable bottleneck. A group of exactly three steps forms an unresolvable bottleneck if, no matter which of the three steps is temporarily suspended, the remaining two active steps in the group still require a shared calibration tool and thus cannot share a production line.

To ensure deterministic logging, if multiple such groups exist, the system must output the one that is sequentially lowest (the smallest possible value for the first step identifier; in case of a tie, the smallest possible value for the second; and finally the smallest possible value for the third).

## Input
The facility has a physical capacity limit of exactly two thousand manufacturing steps. The logging system's memory buffer can hold a maximum of two hundred thousand tool collision records.

The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the total number of manufacturing steps and the number of tool collision pairs. The value of $n$ is at least three and cannot exceed the facility's physical step capacity. The value of $m$ is at least three and cannot exceed the logging system's memory buffer limit (or the theoretical maximum number of tool collision pairs for $n$ steps, if that is smaller). Steps are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that step $u$ and step $v$ require a shared calibration tool.

It is guaranteed that at least one such group of three steps exists in the provided data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three steps forming the bottleneck. Output the sequentially lowest combination.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I changed the explicit "8 possible ways" bipartite contradiction to a "leave-one-out" property where suspending any one step leaves two steps that share a tool. This forces the model to logically deduce that all three pairs in the subset must be collisions (a triangle), bypassing heuristics that trigger on 2-coloring assignment text. I also expressed the numeric bounds indirectly via physical capacities.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The requirement that any remaining pair from the three steps shares a tool mathematically necessitates that all three steps are pairwise connected, which is exactly the original triangle condition.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The problem still requires finding the lexicographically first triangle in an undirected graph, keeping the time complexity and algorithm identical.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "temporarily suspended" as applying to the global set of steps rather than just isolating one step from the group of three, though "remaining two active steps in the group" clarifies this.
</RISK>
