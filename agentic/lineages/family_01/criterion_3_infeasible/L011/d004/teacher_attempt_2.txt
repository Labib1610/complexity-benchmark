# Dual-Core Scheduling Conflict

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are debugging a task scheduler for a dual-core processor. The system must assign $n$ concurrent processes to either Core A or Core B. However, due to shared resources, certain pairs of processes conflict and cannot be assigned to the same core.

Currently, the scheduler is crashing because a valid two-core assignment does not exist. The crash logs point to a "tri-core deadlock" as the root cause: exactly three processes that all mutually conflict with one another. Since there are only two cores, any attempt to schedule these three processes will force at least one conflicting pair to share a core.

Your objective is to identify a tri-core deadlock from the scheduler's conflict records. If the records contain more than one such deadlock, you must report the one that generates the smallest error signature. When a deadlock involves three processes with IDs $a, b,$ and $c$ (where $a < b < c$), its error signature is calculated by evaluating the polynomial $E(x) = a \cdot x^2 + b \cdot x + c$ at $x = n^2$ (where $n$ is the total number of processes in the system).

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of processes and the number of known conflicts. The processes are identified by integers from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the subsequent $m$ lines provides two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), meaning process $u$ and process $v$ conflict. 

It is guaranteed that the input contains at least one tri-core deadlock (a set of three processes where every process conflicts with the other two).

## Output
Print a single line with three space-separated integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ ($a < b < c$), representing the IDs of the processes that form the deadlock. You must output the specific deadlock that minimizes the error signature described above.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

<PRESERVED_ANSWER>

</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
