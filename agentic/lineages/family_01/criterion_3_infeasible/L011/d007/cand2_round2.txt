# Spin-Glass Frustration Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A quantum simulation operates continuously using exactly two spin states, Spin Up and Spin Down. Certain pairs of particles share a specialized coupling link. An alignment penalty occurs if both particles of such a pair are assigned to the same spin state. Currently, the entire batch of particles cannot be distributed across the two spin states without causing at least one alignment penalty.

Your diagnostic software must isolate a root cause of this allocation failure by identifying a group of exactly three particles that form a frustrated trio.

Consider any group of three distinct particles. There are exactly $2^3 = 8$ theoretical ways to assign spin states to just these three particles. A group of three particles is classified as a frustrated trio if every single one of these 8 possible assignments results in at least one alignment penalty where both of the aligned particles belong to this group.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the total number of particles and the number of coupling links, respectively. The system requires a minimum of three particles to form a trio, and the simulation hardware limits the maximum particle count to two thousand. The number of coupling links is at least three, and cannot exceed the smaller of the theoretical maximum number of distinct pairs among $n$ particles, or two hundred thousand. Particles are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ (ranging from $1$ to $n$, with $u \neq v$), indicating that particle $u$ and particle $v$ share a coupling link.

It is guaranteed that at least one frustrated trio exists in the provided data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three particles. Output the combination that is sequentially lowest (the smallest possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest possible value for $b$; and finally the smallest possible value for $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
