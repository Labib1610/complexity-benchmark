<STATEMENT>
# Spin Glass Frustration

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are simulating a theoretical spin glass system consisting of a set of particles. Certain pairs of particles are coupled. 

Each particle can be assigned a spin state $S$ which is either $+1$ or $-1$. 
For any pair of coupled particles $u$ and $v$, their interaction energy under a given spin configuration is defined algebraically as $(S_u + S_v)^2$.

A group of exactly three distinct particles is classified as a "frustrated trio" if it satisfies the following condition: for every possible assignment of spin states to these three particles (there are exactly $2^3 = 8$ such assignments), the sum of the interaction energies across all coupled pairs where both particles belong to this group is strictly greater than zero. 

Your task is to identify a frustrated trio. If multiple such trios exist, you must output the one that is sequentially earliest. A trio $(a, b, c)$ with $a < b < c$ is sequentially earlier than $(x, y, z)$ if $a < x$, or if $a = x$ and $b < y$, or if $a = x$ and $b = y$ and $c < z$.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $N$ and $M$ ($3 \le N \le 2000$, $3 \le M \le \min(N \cdot (N-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the number of particles and the number of coupled pairs. Particles are numbered from $1$ to $N$.

Each of the next $M$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le N$, $u \neq v$), indicating that particle $u$ and particle $v$ are coupled.

It is guaranteed that at least one frustrated trio exists in the provided data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the particles in the sequentially earliest frustrated trio.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
I replaced the explicit combinatorial description of a bottleneck (which models recognized as a 2-coloring conflict) with an algebraic property of spin states. The condition that the sum of squared spin sums is strictly positive for all 8 states is mathematically equivalent to the presence of a triangle, forcing the model to algebraically deduce this equivalence to achieve an efficient solution instead of relying on pattern matching.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
A trio has strictly positive internal interaction energy for all 8 spin configurations if and only if it contains exactly 3 coupled pairs (a triangle), perfectly matching the original condition for every input.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying task remains finding the lexicographically first triangle in an unweighted graph, preserving the $O(NM/64)$ bitset or $O(M \sqrt{M})$ time complexity.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret "coupled pairs where both particles belong to this group" as including pairs with only one particle in the group, though the phrasing "both particles" makes this highly unlikely.
</RISK>
