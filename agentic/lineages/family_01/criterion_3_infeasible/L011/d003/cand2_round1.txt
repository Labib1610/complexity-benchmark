# Production Line Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing plant distributes its workload across two parallel assembly belts. Certain pairs of operations are mutually incompatible on the same belt because they both require the same stationary calibration fixture. Currently, it is impossible to partition all operations across the two belts without at least one fixture conflict.

Your diagnostic routine must trace this failure to a fundamental bottleneck: a trio of operations that are completely mutually incompatible. Specifically, you must find three distinct operations where every possible pair chosen from these three requires the same stationary fixture, meaning none of the three can share a belt with any other in the trio.

When multiple such trios exist, the logging mechanism requires a deterministic choice. You must select the trio whose operations have the sequentially lowest identifiers (first minimizing the smallest identifier, then the middle identifier, and finally the largest identifier).

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The total number of operations $n$ can be as low as three, while the physical limit of the factory floor allows up to two thousand concurrent operation bays. The number of logged fixture conflicts $m$ is at least three, and the diagnostic buffer can track up to two hundred thousand collision events, never exceeding the theoretical maximum of all possible pairs among the $n$ operations. Operations are numbered sequentially from $1$ up to $n$.

Each of the next $m$ lines specifies a conflict using two valid, distinct operation identifiers $u$ and $v$, meaning operation $u$ and operation $v$ cannot share an assembly belt.

The provided data is guaranteed to contain at least one trio of operations that are completely mutually incompatible.

## Output
Output the three integer identifiers of the operations in the bottleneck trio, separated by spaces, arranged in increasing order. The required trio is the one that is sequentially lowest according to the logging rules.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
