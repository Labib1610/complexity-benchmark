# Production Line Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility operates continuously. Certain pairs of manufacturing steps require a shared specialized calibration tool.

Your diagnostic software must isolate a root cause of an allocation failure by identifying a group of exactly three steps that form an unresolvable bottleneck.

Consider any group of exactly three distinct steps, which we order by their identifiers such that $s_1 < s_2 < s_3$. We evaluate their mutual dependencies by mapping them to three points in a 3D coordinate system. First, we define a dependency metric $d(u, v)$ for any two steps $u$ and $v$:
- $d(u, v) = u + v$ if step $u$ and step $v$ require a shared calibration tool.
- $d(u, v) = 0$ if they do not require a shared tool.

Using this metric, we define three points for the group:
- $P_1 = (0, d(s_1, s_2), d(s_1, s_3))$
- $P_2 = (d(s_2, s_1), 0, d(s_2, s_3))$
- $P_3 = (d(s_3, s_1), d(s_3, s_2), 0)$

A group of three distinct steps is classified as an unresolvable bottleneck if and only if the tetrahedron formed by the origin $(0,0,0)$ and the points $P_1, P_2$, and $P_3$ has a strictly positive volume.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$. The integer $n$ is at least $3$ and at most the number of cubic centimeters in two liters. The integer $m$ is at least $3$ and at most the theoretical maximum for $n$ steps, capped by a hard limit equal to the number of meters in $200$ kilometers.
These integers represent the total number of manufacturing steps and the number of shared tool requirements, respectively. Steps are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that step $u$ and step $v$ require a shared calibration tool.

It is guaranteed that at least one unresolvable bottleneck exists in the provided data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three steps that form an unresolvable bottleneck. If multiple such groups exist, output the combination that is sequentially lowest: the one with the smallest possible value for $a$; in case of a tie, the smallest possible value for $b$; and finally the smallest possible value for $c$.

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

I replaced the zero-diagonal binary matrix linear independence check with a geometric condition involving the volume of a tetrahedron formed by three points and the origin. The coordinates of the points use a strictly positive weight ($u+v$) for existing edges and zero for missing edges. This forces the model to symbolically evaluate the volume formula (a determinant) to realize that it is strictly positive if and only if all three edges exist (a triangle), thereby defeating surface pattern matching on linear algebra keywords that previously gave away the clique structure. </RATIONALE>
