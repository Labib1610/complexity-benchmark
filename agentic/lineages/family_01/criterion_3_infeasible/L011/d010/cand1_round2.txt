<METHOD_NAME>Weighted Identifier Adjacency</METHOD_NAME>
<METHOD_DESCRIPTION>Replaces the binary adjacency matrix entries with the sum of the step identifiers. The determinant of the resulting 3x3 matrix is still zero if any edge is missing, and strictly positive if all three edges are present, preserving the triangle condition exactly while defeating models that pattern-match zero-diagonal binary matrices to cliques.</METHOD_DESCRIPTION>
<METHOD_NOVEL>yes</METHOD_NOVEL>
<STATEMENT>
# Production Line Diagnostics

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

A manufacturing facility operates continuously. Certain pairs of manufacturing steps require a shared specialized calibration tool. 

Your diagnostic software must isolate a root cause of an allocation failure by identifying a group of exactly three steps that form an unresolvable bottleneck. 

Consider any group of exactly three distinct steps. Let their integer identifiers be $s_1, s_2$, and $s_3$, assigned in any arbitrary order. We evaluate their mutual dependencies by constructing three vectors, $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$, and $\mathbf{v}_3$, in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

For each $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, the vector $\mathbf{v}_i = (x_{i,1}, x_{i,2}, x_{i,3})$ is defined such that:
- $x_{i,j} = s_i + s_j$ if step $s_i$ and step $s_j$ require a shared calibration tool.
- $x_{i,j} = 0$ if they do not require a shared tool, or if $i = j$.

A group of three distinct steps is classified as an unresolvable bottleneck if and only if their corresponding vectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$, and $\mathbf{v}_3$ are linearly independent over the real numbers.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$), representing the total number of manufacturing steps and the number of shared tool requirements. Steps are numbered from $1$ to $n$.

Each of the following $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ ($1 \le u, v \le n$, $u \neq v$), indicating that step $u$ and step $v$ require a shared calibration tool.

It is guaranteed that at least one unresolvable bottleneck exists in the provided data.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$, representing the identifiers of the three steps that form an unresolvable bottleneck. If multiple such groups exist, output the lexicographically smallest such triple (smallest $a$; ties broken by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
</STATEMENT>
<RATIONALE>
The binary adjacency matrix condition was replaced with a weighted formulation where $x_{i,j} = s_i + s_j$. This defeats models that rely on pure pattern matching of zero-diagonal binary matrices to cliques/triangles, forcing them to genuinely evaluate the determinant condition to discover it still evaluates to non-zero if and only if all three edges are present.
</RATIONALE>
<PRESERVED_ANSWER>
The determinant of the 3x3 matrix is $2(s_1+s_2)(s_2+s_3)(s_3+s_1)$ if all three edges exist, which is strictly positive (since $s_k \ge 1$), and 0 if any edge is missing, meaning linear independence still perfectly correlates with the three steps forming a triangle.
</PRESERVED_ANSWER>
<PRESERVED_CLASS>
The underlying problem remains finding a triangle (a 3-clique) in an undirected graph, which is solvable in $O(n \cdot m / 64)$ using bitsets, keeping it firmly in the same complexity class.
</PRESERVED_CLASS>
<RISK>
Models might misinterpret $s_i + s_j$ as literal vector addition rather than scalar components, though the notation $(x_{i,1}, x_{i,2}, x_{i,3})$ and $x_{i,j} = s_i + s_j$ strongly implies scalar entries.
</RISK>
