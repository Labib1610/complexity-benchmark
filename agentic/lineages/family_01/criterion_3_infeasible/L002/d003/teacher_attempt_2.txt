# Problem C — Volatile Complex

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

You are analyzing a sample of synthetic proteins. Your specialized assay has identified instances of stable binding affinity, where a specific pair of proteins share a mutual capability to bond to each other.

A volatile "ternary complex" forms if an unbroken sequence of three distinct proteins can be arranged such that the first bonds to the second, the second bonds to the third, and the third bonds directly back to the first. The sample you are analyzing is guaranteed to contain at least one such three-protein complex. You must identify the components of this complex.

## Input
The first line provides $n$ and $m$, representing the number of synthetic proteins and the number of bonding pairs, respectively.
The physical assay tray requires a minimum load of three proteins and has a maximum capacity of two thousand proteins. The database is configured with a minimum detection threshold of three pairs and an absolute storage limit of two hundred thousand pairs. The number of recorded pairs $m$ will never exceed the theoretical maximum number of unique pairs for the given proteins.
The following $m$ lines each contain two integers $u$ and $v$, indicating that protein $u$ and protein $v$ share a stable binding affinity.
The input will always contain at least one ternary complex.

## Output
Output three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on a single line, separated by spaces, with $a < b < c$. These must represent a set of three proteins that form a ternary complex as described above. If there are multiple such sets, provide the one that is lexicographically first (prioritizing the lowest value for $a$, then the lowest for $b$, and finally $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```

Additionally, numeric constraints were translated into physical scenario limits to satisfy the strategy.
</RATIONALE>
