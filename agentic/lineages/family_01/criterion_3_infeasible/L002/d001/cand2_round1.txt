# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory still has only two shifts, 1 and 2, but the roster is known to be hopeless. Your job is not to schedule — it is to certify the impossibility. The provided conflict data is hopeless because it contains a group of three workers where each worker in the group conflicts with both of the other two. Any such group proves two shifts cannot suffice. 

To standardize the certification, you must output the lexicographically smallest such group. Be aware that the conflict data can be adversarial: the lexicographically smallest valid group might only be found after all earlier candidate triples have been evaluated and rejected.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($3 \le n \le 2000$, $3 \le m \le \min(n \cdot (n-1)/2, 2 \cdot 10^5)$).
Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a conflicting pair.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that all three pairs $a$–$b$, $a$–$c$, and $b$–$c$ are listed as conflicts. Output the lexicographically smallest such group (smallest $a$; ties by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
