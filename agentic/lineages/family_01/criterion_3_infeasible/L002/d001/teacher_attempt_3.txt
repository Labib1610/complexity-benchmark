# Problem C — Proof of Impossibility

Time limit: 2 s   Memory limit: 256 MB

The factory still has only two shifts, 1 and 2, but the roster is known to be hopeless. Your job is not to schedule — it is to certify the impossibility. It is guaranteed that the conflict data contains three conflict records that mutually involve exactly three unique workers in total. Any such configuration proves two shifts cannot suffice. Output the workers involved in one such configuration.

## Input
The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$, representing the headcount and the number of records. The factory employs a headcount of at least the minimum number of people required to form the configuration described above, up to a maximum capacity of two thousand workers. The log contains at least three conflict records, and is capped at two hundred thousand entries or the theoretical maximum where every distinct pair of employees has exactly one record, whichever is smaller.

Each of the next $m$ lines contains two integers $u$ and $v$ — a conflicting pair.
It is guaranteed that at least one such configuration of three records involving exactly three workers exists.

## Output
Print three integers $a$, $b$, and $c$ on one line with $a < b < c$ such that these three workers form the configuration described. Output the lexicographically smallest such set of workers (smallest $a$; ties by smallest $b$; then smallest $c$).

## Example 1
Input:
```
4 5
1 2
1 3
2 3
2 4
3 4
```
Output:
```
1 2 3
```

## Example 2
Input:
```
5 6
1 2
1 4
2 4
3 4
3 5
4 5
```
Output:
```
1 2 4
```
