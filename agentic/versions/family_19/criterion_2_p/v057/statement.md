# Problem B — Pallet Routing Quota

Time limit: 2 s
Memory limit: 256 MB

## Description
Our company's daily logistics pipeline operates in three distinct phases. 

First, the analytics team predicts the day's demand and establishes a strict daily quota of identical pallets that must be moved from a central warehouse to a retail center. 

Your task is to implement the second phase: the routing module. Given the quota generated by the analytics team, you must calculate exactly how many pallets to send along each one-way transport corridor connecting our transfer yards. The system has strict limitations. Each corridor has a maximum daily limit dictated by available vehicle fleets. Furthermore, intermediate transfer yards lack overnight storage and do not produce goods, meaning the total number of pallets entering any intermediate yard must exactly match the total number leaving. The central warehouse must have a net departure (total departures minus total arrivals) exactly equal to the quota, and the retail center must have a net arrival (total arrivals minus total departures) equal to the quota. All pallet assignments must be non-negative whole numbers.

Once your module outputs a valid assignment, the third phase begins: the automated dispatch system will physically assign drivers and vehicles to execute your schedule. If your module determines that it is impossible to route the required quota under the given restrictions, it must simply report the failure so the system can alert the analytics team to revise the quota.

## Input
The first line contains three integers: `n` (the number of yards), `m` (the number of corridors), and `1` (indicating a single pallet type).
The second line contains three integers: `s` (the central warehouse yard ID), `t` (the retail center yard ID), and `d` (the daily quota provided by the analytics team).
The next `m` lines each contain three integers `u`, `v`, and `cap`, indicating that a one-way corridor restricts movement from yard `u` to yard `v` to at most `cap` pallets daily.

## Output
If the routing module can fulfill the quota, output `YES` on the first line. On the second line, output `m` integers representing the number of pallets allocated to each corridor, in the exact order the corridors were provided in the input.
If the quota cannot be met, output `NO`.

## Example

### Input
```
4 5 1
1 4 10
1 2 10
1 3 10
2 3 5
2 4 5
3 4 5
```

### Output
```
YES
5 5 0 5 5
```
