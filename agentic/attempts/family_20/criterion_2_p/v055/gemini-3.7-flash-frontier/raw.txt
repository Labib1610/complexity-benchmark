An efficient and elegant way to solve the **Orbital Telemetry Synchronization** (Bipartite Edge Coloring with $\Delta$ colors) is through divide-and-conquer using Euler trails.

### Method Explanation
By Kőnig's line coloring theorem, any bipartite multigraph with maximum degree $\Delta$ can be edge-colored using exactly $\Delta$ colors. 

To achieve $O(m \log \Delta)$ time:
1. We can split the edges of a bipartite graph with maximum degree $\Delta$ into two subgraphs $G_1$ and $G_2$ such that the maximum degree in $G_1$ is $\lfloor \Delta / 2 \rfloor$ and in $G_2$ is $\lceil \Delta / 2 \rceil$.
2. To perform this split:
   - For any vertex with an odd degree, add a dummy edge to a dummy vertex (or connect odd-degree vertices) to make all vertex degrees even.
   - Decompose the resulting graph into Eulerian circuits.
   - Traverse each Eulerian circuit, placing alternating edges into $G_1$ and $G_2$.
   - Remove dummy edges. Each vertex $v$ will have its degree split into $\lfloor \text{deg}(v)/2 \rfloor$ and $\lceil \text{deg}(v)/2 \rceil$.
3. Recursively color $G_1$ with colors $\{1, \dots, \lfloor \Delta / 2 \rfloor\}$ and $G_2$ with colors $\{\lfloor \Delta / 2 \rfloor + 1, \dots, \Delta\}$.
