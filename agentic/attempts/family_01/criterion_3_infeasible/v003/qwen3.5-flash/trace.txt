The problem asks us to find the lexicographically smallest triple $(a, b, c)$ such that $1 \le a < b < c \le n$ and all three pairs $(a, b)$, $(b, c)$, and $(a, c)$ are edges in a given graph of interference. We are guaranteed that at least one such triangle exists.

The constraints are $n \le 2000$ and $m \le 2 \cdot 10^5$.
A brute-force approach checking all triples would take $O(n^3)$ time. With $n=2000$, $n^3 = 8 \cdot 10^9$, which is too slow for a 2-second time limit.
We need a more efficient approach.

The graph is undirected. We are looking for a triangle.
To find the lexicographically smallest triangle $(a, b, c)$ with $a < b < c$, we can iterate through possible values of $a$ from $1$ to $n$. For a fixed $a$, we want to find the smallest $b > a$ and the smallest $c > b$ such that $(a, b), (a, c), (b, c)$ are edges.

Let's refine the search strategy.
We iterate $a$ from $1$ to $n$.
For a fixed $a$, we iterate through its neighbors $b$ such that $b > a$.
For each such $b$, we want to find a neighbor $c$ of $b$ such that $c > b$ and $(a, c)$ is an edge.
Since we want the lexicographically smallest $(a, b, c)$, for a fixed $a$, we want the smallest $b$, and for that $b$, the smallest $c$.
However, simply checking the first valid $b$ for a fixed $a$ might not yield the global lexicographical smallest if a larger $b$ leads to a valid $c$ but we already found a valid $c$ for a smaller $b$. Wait, lexicographical comparison of triples $(a, b, c)$ vs $(a', b', c')$:
If $a < a'$, the first one is smaller.
If $a = a'$, we compare $b$. Smaller $b$ is better.
If $a = a'$ and $b = b'$, we compare $c$. Smaller $c$ is better.

So, the strategy is:
Iterate $a$ from $1$ to $n$.
  Iterate neighbors $b$ of $a$ such that $b > a$. To ensure lexicographical order, we should process $b$ in increasing order.
    Iterate neighbors $c$ of $b$ such that $c > b$ and $(a, c)$ is an edge. We want the smallest such $c$.
    If we find such a $c$, then $(a, b, c)$ is the answer because we iterate $a$ in increasing order, and for each $a$, we iterate $b$ in increasing order, and for each $b$, we pick the smallest valid $c$. The first such triple we find will be the lexicographically smallest.

     Loop $b$ in `sorted(adj[a])`:
